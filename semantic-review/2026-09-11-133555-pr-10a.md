Watch for: **confirmed** — the recovery conclusion says the backlog falls *by* `min(ε, B_i)`, but the premise only guarantees it falls **by at least** that amount; a larger adaptation surplus may produce a larger one-interval reduction.

The claim boundaries otherwise remain aligned across research, derivation, formalisation, and the fail-closed release gate.

## Conditional recovery at the zero floor

**Confirmed.** The update rule is correctly clamped as `B(t+Δt) = max{0, B(t)+ΔD(t)-A(t)+H(t)}`. Under `A_i-ΔD_i-H_i ≥ ε`, it entails `B_{i+1} ≤ max{0, B_i-ε}`; at the floor, that avoids asserting a final full-`ε` decrement. The termination bound `ceil(B_i/ε)` is sound because every positive-backlog interval reduces the recorded amount by at least the bounded decrement.

The next sentence overstates what that inequality establishes. For example, if `B_i=10ε` and the accounted surplus is `2ε`, the next backlog can be `8ε`, a reduction of `2ε`, not exactly `min(ε, B_i)=ε`. The prose must say “falls by **at least** `min{ε,B_i}`”; the present wording appears in both the formal Paper 10a recovery proposition and the Stage-2 Proposition C2. This is a mathematical precision issue in a load-bearing result, not a zero-floor clamp defect.
