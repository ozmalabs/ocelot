## Agricultural labour compression needs a cost-series boundary

The manuscript calls agriculture “the clearest historical example of a production activity whose economic cost and labour requirement have been transformed,” then cites the Our World in Data series for France, the United Kingdom, Italy, and the Netherlands. The source ledger assigns that source only to “historical majority agricultural-labour-force evidence for named countries.” **Confirmed:** the citation supports the labour-allocation half of the sentence, not an economic-cost time series.

USDA remains useful but distinct: it supplies a U.S. 1948–2023 account of nearly tripled output, falling labour and land, and rising intermediate inputs. It supports scoped productivity and input substitution—and the warning against costless food or vanishing physical inputs—but does not convert the four named-country labour series into a historical economic-cost measure. Rephrase the opening claim to describe transformed labour requirements and production/input structure, reserving economic-cost compression for a named audited or comparable cost series. Make the same change in the Stage-1 record, which repeats the wording; the derivation ledger needs no change.
