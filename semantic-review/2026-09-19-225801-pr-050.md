# Separate GCS-only IAM boundary for operation 4

DISPATCH-050 adds a repository-only IAM provisioning definition for operation 4. It separates the GCS capability from the observed KMS-capable account, narrows the bucket grant to `storage.objects.create` and `storage.objects.get` under the exact `p5-r0/operation-4/rs069r18r13-op4-04a98e3e/` prefix, removes the broad and stale bucket grants, and binds the exact image-digest WIF principal to a token-creator grant. The Python boundary requires a versioned prestate, ordered injected apply, poststate validation, rollback, and a single append-only attempt without cloud SDK or credential access. **Watch for:** **confirmed** the new GCS-only account is not wired into the existing operation-4 capability/runtime, **confirmed** approval and prestate are caller-supplied rather than canonical reviewed inputs, **confirmed** the executor receives unvalidated desired-state shapes and the tests do not inspect them, and **confirmed** the required poststate receipt is not emitted. The packet digest is `sha256:a8c313f6e5b775b4ba48a8e8ff4cc5e2561c560930c4e89ec2e9666fb509c447`; the capability-definition digest is `sha256:dd6d3de76387cacca5f18a9e7881fd461fd4e66d2ed5981532fa12acead88fc2`; this definition digest is `sha256:ddf2efa5dbd55b5ecd61e641e4f536e342a3087cd8154702e97069ffb7bb2798`. **Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

The desired IAM boundary is narrower than the observed state: it creates a separate GCS-only account, grants exact-prefix create/get, removes `roles/storage.objectAdmin` and the old `v3/` WIF bucket grant, and targets the reviewed `sha256:394a0707…` principal. The proposal is not connected to the operation-4 credential consumer, which still names the old account.

The runner’s local control flow is fail-closed, but its authorization input is only a self-digested mapping supplied by the caller. No canonical approval/prestate artifact or external custody check binds the readiness assertion and versioned state to the reviewed apply request.

The IaC boundary has no closed desired-state schemas: the calls pass different definition fragments rather than versioned policy documents, and the fake executor checks only call order. The declared poststate receipt is also absent from the ledger and return value, while rollback/poststate contract fields are not fully validated.

### Separate GCS-only identity stops at the provisioning definition

**Confirmed** the new definition records why the old account cannot be reused: `p5-evaluator` is KMS-capable, while `p5-r0-gcs-only` is the required target (`rs069r18r13-iam-provisioning-definition-v1.json:41-45`). Its WIF binding and token-creator resource point to the new account, and its bucket grant uses the new account under the exact run condition (`:56-63`, `:67-86`).

**Confirmed** the existing operation-4 chain still binds the capability to the old account. `gcs_capability_definition.py` keeps `SERVICE_ACCOUNT = "p5-evaluator@..."` and validates that account in the bucket grant and token-creator endpoint (`:27`, `:223-230`); the runtime chooses `self.config.capability_service_account` for IAM Credentials impersonation (`confidential_space.py:996-1015`), and the deployment template sets that value to `p5-evaluator` (`cloud-run.yaml:87-88`). Applying DISPATCH-050 as written would provision one account while the consumer requests a token for another. Make the GCS-only account the operation-4 capability target across the packet/definition, approval, deployment environment, and credential path, while leaving the existing KMS account on its separate authority path.

### Approval and prestate are not independently bound

**Confirmed** `validate_approval` accepts a mapping supplied directly by the caller and treats `signed_environment_ready: true` as sufficient readiness after checking a self-computed `artifact_digest` (`iam_capability_provisioning.py:224-239`). It does not load a canonical approval file, verify an approval identity/actor, or require a signed-environment evidence digest. `execute_provisioning` similarly accepts `expected_prestate` from the caller, compares the executor’s fresh observation to that value, and binds only the resulting digest (`:305-323`). A caller can construct a valid self-digested approval and prestate without demonstrating that either was the reviewed child approval or approved fresh capture.

**Confirmed** the definition explicitly requires a fresh read-only prestate, exact child approval, reviewed executor, and signed-environment readiness (`rs069r18r13-iam-provisioning-definition-v1.json:127-134`). The safe boundary is a new invocation after canonical, externally authorized approval and prestate artifacts are loaded and their project, bucket, account, versions, definition digest, and attempt ID are bound.

### Injected IaC calls lack a closed desired-state contract

**Confirmed** the protocol declares `update_bucket_policy(policy, prestate)` and `update_target_account_policy(policy, prestate)` but does not define the mapping shape or enforce policy etags/fingerprints at the write boundary (`iam_capability_provisioning.py:266-280`). The implementation passes `definition["bucket_bindings"]` to the first call and the complete `definition["wif_binding"]` object to the second (`:329-333`). Neither is a versioned policy document matching the normalized prestate representation; the latter is WIF metadata rather than a `{bindings, etag|fingerprint}` policy.

**Confirmed** the tests cannot catch this. `FakeExecutor` accepts and discards all arguments (`test_iam_capability_provisioning.py:83-109`), and the success assertion checks only method order (`:170-177`). The definition calls the policy changes “atomic” (`rs069r18r13-iam-provisioning-definition-v1.json:103-109`), but no implementation contract establishes an etag-checked write, transaction boundary, or reconciliation behavior if the bucket update succeeds and the target-account update fails. The adapter needs explicit request schemas, exact desired bindings, prestate version preconditions, and a defined no-op/changed result before it can be called rerunnable.

### Poststate receipt and rollback evidence are thinner than required

**Confirmed** the declared poststate receipt must carry IAM digests plus project, bucket, service account, WIF principal, and role condition (`rs069r18r13-iam-provisioning-definition-v1.json:118-125`). The runner’s common event fields contain only definition/prestate digests and operation metadata; `prestate_verified` records a state digest and `poststate_verified` records a poststate digest (`iam_capability_provisioning.py:315-338`). The successful return value likewise contains only the two state digests and ledger head. No receipt object or authenticated account/condition identity is persisted for a later operation-4 child approval.

**Confirmed** the definition validator leaves this contract mutable: it verifies the top-level digest, selected identity fields, apply order, and a few execution flags (`iam_capability_provisioning.py:193-220`), but does not compare the `prestate_contract`, `rollback`, or `poststate` objects to closed expected structures. Recomputing the definition digest after changing rollback order or receipt text would not be rejected. Validate the complete contract and persist exact pre/post versions and identity fields, including manual-review state when account creation cannot be automatically reversed.

### Focused tests prove sequencing, not apply safety

**Confirmed** `python3 -m pytest services/p5_r0_evaluator/tests/test_iam_capability_provisioning.py -q` passes all 7 tests, but the tests use only an in-memory fake and do not exercise a concrete IAM executor, canonical approval loading, receipt emission, policy argument shape, etag/fingerprint writes, or separate-account integration. They therefore do not establish that mutation requests remove the observed broad/stale grants or install the exact token-creator binding.

The reported broader validation remains repository-only: targeted IAM/GCS/deployment tests passed 85, focused IAM tests passed 7, `py_compile` and `git diff --check` passed. No cloud command, credential creation, IAM apply, GCS upload, master-runsheet edit, or applied-state claim is authorized by this review. The first safe apply boundary is one fresh read-only capture followed by an exact child approval and signed-environment credential readiness, then a single invocation through a concrete executor implementing this definition; stop and enter manual review on any post-mutation mismatch or rollback ambiguity, with operation 4 disabled until its account wiring and authenticated receipt are verified.
