DISPATCH-074 closes the two trust-boundary seams identified in REVIEW-074: the effectful runner no longer accepts a caller-supplied adapter factory, and canonical packet loading is fixed to the reviewed APPROVAL-016 path and source-bound validator. Packet, execution-request, child-approval, receipt, and attempt-ledger identities remain bound by exact digests and the canonical absolute path, while validation and dry-run stop before adapter construction or any effectful call. The focused adversarial suite passes and the canonical request validator still reports an intentionally blocked, zero-cloud-operation request. **Watch for:** **confirmed** — no live authority/IAM/WIP/closure/Confidential Space evidence exists for a Track A launch, and the ignored historical-ledger paths have no Git baseline from which unchanged provenance can be independently established.

**Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

The production runner now owns the effectful trust construction: it creates the reviewed factory internally, which in turn selects the launcher source, Google JWT verifier, Google STS client, and default IAM derivation path. Caller-provided constructor seams for those components, the clock, nonce, and request loader are rejected rather than consulted.

The canonical packet path and loader are fixed before preflight. Exact packet, request, approval, receipt, and ledger bindings are checked across the local path, and validate/dry-run modes do not reserve a ledger or construct an adapter. The one-shot ledger also rejects retries and protects the named canonical and historical ledger paths when the supplied ledger is the built-in file ledger.

The hardening does not materialize the real external prerequisites, by design. The canonical request remains a blocked preparation artifact, so no effectful Track A attempt is admissible until the live authority/KMS, WIP/IAM, immutable closure, child approval, Google attestation, and short-lived capability evidence are independently produced and verified.

## Factory and loader ownership at the effectful boundary

`ReviewedBootstrapRunner` exposes only a caller-owned audit ledger in its constructor. Its effectful branch instantiates `ReviewedConfidentialSpaceAdapterFactory` itself; that factory constructs `LauncherTokenSource`, `GoogleJwtAttestationVerifier`, `GoogleSTSClient`, and a `CredentialOnlyCapabilityFactory` without accepting caller implementations. The runner also derives configuration from the canonical request with the verifier-owned clock, so the former factory, source, verifier, STS, impersonation, nonce, and clock injection points are not part of the production API. The exact-type checks prevent a substituted adapter or copied capability binding from entering the credential path.

The runner rejects a noncanonical `request_path` and calls `_load_canonical_request(DEFAULT_REQUEST)` directly. That loader re-runs `validate_request` against the reviewed source artifacts before parsing and shape validation; a caller cannot replace it through the runner constructor or by assigning a `request_loader` attribute. The test seam used by the focused suite is module monkeypatching, not a production argument, and therefore does not establish a caller-visible injection path.

## Exact packet, approval, and dry-run behavior

The packet self-digest is checked, then image, closure, WIP/provider, authority, KMS, capability, and execution-request bindings are reconstructed from the canonical packet. The child approval must be verifier-issued, live at the verifier-owned clock, and exact for both packet digest/path and execution-request digest. Receipt validation repeats the packet, path, request, and approval bindings before the terminal ledger event; reservation and completion/failure use the same tuple and reject drift. The one-shot ledger prevents a second effectful reservation, including after a failed bootstrap.

`validate` and `dry-run` perform only canonical loading and local preflight. They do not require a ledger, construct the adapter, invoke launcher/STS/IAM, or create credentials. The focused tests cover wrong packet paths, stale request and approval identity, child mismatch, wrong image/provider/audience/zone bindings, and the no-retry ledger behavior.

## External evidence frontier remains fail-closed

The canonical request validator still requires the preparation-only status, null/unmaterialized authority binding, and `reviewed_effectful_runner: false`; it reports `cloud_operations: []`. The runner’s later checks require a non-null authority binding digest, exact authority/KMS relation, verifier-issued capability snapshot, resolved subject and WIP values, reconciled closure, and matching child approval before the fixed factory can be used. This preserves the boundary between repository tests and real evidence rather than promoting local fixtures into Track A proof.

**confirmed** — the remaining launch prerequisites are real, not test substitutes: a materialized self-custodied authority/KMS binding, fresh exact WIP/IAM observation, registry-reconciled immutable closure, verifier-issued approval for that exact packet, a Google-signed Confidential Space attestation, and attestation-bounded STS/IAM capability evidence. No timing, task, native execution, result seal, final KMS result signature, or GCS publication is authorized by this bootstrap boundary.
