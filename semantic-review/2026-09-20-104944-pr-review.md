# Minimal self-custodied attested diagnostic plan

The rewritten plan correctly narrows Track A to one self-custodied, attested diagnostic and keeps release qualification separate. Its mandatory evidence is sufficient in substance: canonical generated-input commitments, exact candidate closure, one real Confidential Space attestation before capability activation, verified timing before seed/input/task derivation, authenticated native/resource evidence including OOM/deadline and cleanup behavior, canonical receipts, and a real KMS signature over the exact final result binding. The plan does not claim secrecy, independent evaluator custody, exact CPU timing, P5-E1/E2, or release readiness. The sequence still needs corrections before it can be accepted because it makes optional pre-run lock signing appear mandatory and broadens the MVP execution to the full control/contrast workload.

Watch for: **confirmed** — the sequence's mandatory-looking lock-signing step conflicts with Track A's explicit optional-lock rule; **confirmed** — its all-arms/all-controls/all-contrasts workload conflicts with the plan's narrow MVP scope. **Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

Track A is properly framed as a self-custodied diagnostic, not an independent evaluation or release gate. The plan explicitly excludes exact CPU timing, independent custody, P5-E1/E2, and upstream release gates, and it preserves fail-closed behavior when real external evidence is absent.

The evidence chain is appropriately ordered: generated commitments and closure precede a real attested launch; verified timing precedes seed derivation; native execution must be bound to authenticated supervisor evidence; receipts and the final KMS signature close the claim. The one-shot output also preserves redaction and distinguishes repository validators from real signed-environment evidence.

The sequence is not yet minimal as written. It treats the optional pre-run lock signature as a required task-release step and describes the reusable native runner as executing every arm, primary case, control, and contrast pair, although broader controls and contrasts are reserved for optional hardening unless they are explicitly part of the selected Track A diagnostic contract.

### Claim boundary and attestation posture

The authority is identified as self-custodied, while independent-evaluator custody, exact CPU timing, P5-E1/E2, and release qualification remain out of scope. Wording such as “independent timing capability” should be qualified as “separate timing substrate, not independent evaluator custody” to prevent readers from over-reading the term.

The sequence also correctly preserves the attestation ordering: launcher claims are verified before capability construction, and local validators, generated artifacts, fake timing, local signatures, and callback booleans are not accepted as signed execution evidence. The remaining native adapter and authenticated supervisor are implementation/external prerequisites, not evidence already present in the repository; the sequence should continue to say that Track A is blocked until those real observations exist.

### Evidence sufficiency for the narrow diagnostic

The proposed mandatory gates cover the required narrow result. In particular, the timing/input chain retains `timing_record_digest`, `input_seed_digest`, and `input_digest`; execution evidence is intended to bind the lock, image, closure, attestation, supervisor/resource observations, receipts, and task/result identity; and the final KMS signature is verified against the bound public key. Failures remain typed impasses rather than partial results.

The distinction between the mandatory final signed result and optional post-sign GCS publication is sound once the sequence's durable-input wording is clarified. The final verified seal/result must still be durably retained at the signing boundary as required by the result contract; optional GCS publication is an additional transport/durability step, not the source of authority.
