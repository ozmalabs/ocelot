Paper 10a uses agriculture only as a historical example of cost/labour-compression pressure, not as proof of the broader transition thesis. The record confines historical labour-force dominance to France, the United Kingdom, Italy, and the Netherlands, and confines the quantitative transformation to U.S. agriculture from 1948–2023. It expressly rejects a reconstructed global historical majority, an all-in economic-cost series, costless food, a universal floor, and an AI/automation causal explanation. ## Country history and U.S. production transformation remain distinct

The paper identifies historical agricultural labour-force majorities only in France, the United Kingdom, Italy, and the Netherlands, and disclaims any reconstructed share for every human population or a single global historical estimate. Its quantitative account is separately bounded to U.S. farm output and inputs from 1948–2023. The research record repeats this distinction and assigns the two claims to their corresponding sources. The steering assertion that agriculture was the primary activity of a majority of all humans is not adopted; the revision retains the required country-level scope (**confirmed**).

## Cost and causal boundaries keep the exemplar illustrative

Both records state that the evidence does not reconstruct all-in economic cost, demonstrate zero-cost food, establish a universal floor, or identify automation/AI as the cause. The paper preserves material and institutional constraints—land, energy, water, biology, capital, distribution, and intermediate inputs—so the historical transformation remains an example of cost/labour-compression pressure rather than a claim that food production became free or uniformly bounded (**confirmed**).
