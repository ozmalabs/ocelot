# Theory revisions — standing input to the papers and the book

**Source:** `~/paper_revision_notes_time_seldon_momentum_photon_eye.md`
(25 items, plus a paper-by-paper priority note). These are revisions to the
*theory*, not to this book, and they arrived after Parts I–VI were drafted.
This document records which are applied, which are pending, and — for two of
them — what they corrected in an already-written chapter.

The notes' own priority: **Papers 1–3 carry the largest conceptual revisions.**
Paper 1 needs the non-primitivity of time, the epistemic boundary, and the
separation of discrete sufficiency from fundamental discreteness. Paper 2 needs
the unknown-count / present / determinability structure and blind spots. Paper 3
should recast momentum as present structure. The photon-graph eye material
belongs in its own later paper; these notes are its theoretical foundation.

## Provenance: four primitives, four modes of arrival

**Applied to paper 01 (scope section and the collapse remark) and to the
introduction.** Not a revision to the theory — a revision to how the theory
presents its own history, which the papers had been silent about in a way that
implied more unity than the record supports.

| primitive | arrived by | note |
|---|---|---|
| **g**, the graph | observation | unrelated engineering problems having one shape |
| **Γ**, admissibility | insight | with the sieve image attached, and it predates collapse |
| **collapse** | constraint | adopted from quantum measurement, the regime where it is forced, and generalised |
| **r**, the rules | implementation | "when you make it work, something has to do the things" |

Two consequences landed with it.

**The quantum bridge's arrow ran the other way**, and paper 01 now says so
(Remark, *Direction of the borrowing*). The papers presented a general
actualisation primitive with quantum measurement as a possible instance, which
reads as independent arrival followed by a match. What happened is that collapse
was taken from the one regime where it is experimentally forced and assumed to
hold everywhere. That is a promoted constraint — the same structure as the
equivalence principle — and it is *why* the bridge was always stated
conditionally: the generalisation is the conjecture, not the compatibility.
Stating the provenance makes the claim more defensible rather than less, since
the alternative reading invites *of course it matches, you copied it*.

**And construction is a source of this theory, not a test of it.** Paper 05's
closing list of distinctions that implementation forced on the theory is not a
coda: `r` is the first entry in that list. This should change how that paper is
read.

## The third sharpening: undetermined is not unknown

**Applied to papers 01 and 02, and to chapters 2, 7 and 17.**

The three-state vocabulary said `NULL ≠ unknown ≠ resolved`, with *unknown*
defined as instantiated-but-unresolved-in-projection. That single word was
carrying two conditions that behave differently under every operation that
matters, and ordinary English collapses a third one into it as well:

- **undisclosed** — a value exists and this frame does not hold it. Epistemic.
  Another frame may hold it, and observation, report or inference can obtain it.
- **undetermined** — **no value exists yet.** Collapse has not occurred, so
  there is nothing to be ignorant of. No amount of observation obtains it.
- **indeterministic** — no structure fixes what it will be. The theory denies
  this, and it is now listed explicitly in order to be excluded.

**Determination is an event, not a fact about a pre-existing value.** That is
the whole of the distinction: *undisclosed* presupposes a realised value merely
unheld; *undetermined* says collapse is what produces one. The word
"undetermined" has been damaged by habitual reading as either the first or the
third, leaving the only sense the theory needs without a term.

Landed as a four-state definition in paper 01 (`NULL ≠ undetermined ≠
undisclosed ≠ resolved`), with the remark that separates them, and with
`U_F = 0` redefined to count **undetermined dimensions only** — a frame with
outstanding undisclosed structure is still at its own present, which the old
wording did not permit.

Two consequences worth keeping:

**It is why quantum measurement is the visible case.** Paper 01 now says so
directly: that regime is the one place where the alternatives provably cannot be
re-read as an undisclosed already-realised classical value, since Bell excludes
the local hidden-variable class and Kochen–Specker constrains noncontextual
assignment. Everywhere else the two readings are interchangeable in practice.
There they are not — which is what makes the bridge a claim rather than an
analogy.

**A manifold and a blind spot are different deficits.** Paper 02 now states it:
a Seldon manifold is a projection over the *undetermined*, a blind spot is a
limitation over the *undisclosed*. Improving access reduces the second and
leaves the first untouched. A projection that mixes them reports either a
manifold width no observation could ever reduce, or an occlusion no observation
could ever resolve.

## The sharpening after the notes

Two clarifications arrived after the notes were applied, and both strengthen
item §1 past what it said. Recorded here because the note's own wording ("time
is not primitive", "a time dimension becomes meaningful") is weaker than the
position and would otherwise be re-derived from the notes later.

**1. Time as a first-class dimension does not exist. Only an agent observes
time.** Not "time is nonlinear", not "time is differently shaped", not
"emergent but still there". The substrate has the **ratchet** — succession
through realised collapses, inducing an order. A *time dimension* exists only
where a frame instantiates one to represent that succession. Landed as
Axiom (*No substrate time dimension*) and a proposition in paper 01
§ "Time is not a dimension of the substrate", with the section retitled from
"Time is not primitive".

**2. Time is an inferred dimension — therefore observing time requires the
capacity for inference.** This is the mechanism under (1). No collapse presents
a temporal coordinate, because succession is a relation *between* realised
states and a relation is not a datum of any one of them. So a frame does not
receive time; it infers it. Landed as *Time is inferred, not observed* with the
corollary that **reactive frames have succession and `T = NULL`**, and nothing
about their dynamics is impaired by it.

Two consequences worth keeping:

- **It orders two capacities usually treated as independent.** Inference is not
  a faculty a temporal agent happens to have; it is the precondition for being
  one. And it grades — how much time a frame holds is bounded by the inferential
  structure it can build, which makes it a question about the frame rather than
  about the world.
- **The empirical instance was already in chapter 1 and had not been read this
  way.** The eye receives ordered frames and no temporal coordinate, and mints a
  time axis as the integer count of applications of an inferred generator —
  using its *general dimension-inference machinery*, chapter 4's third rung, not
  anything temporal. Shuffled frames mint nothing. That is the whole claim, run
  as an experiment, in a system built for seeing.

Applied to: paper 01 (axiom, two propositions, corollary, empirical remark),
paper 02 (the horizon is doubly observer-relative — both the ruler and the thing
measured are constructed), ch. 14, ch. 4 (rung 3 named as where time is minted),
ch. 1 (what the minted axis demonstrates).

### The correction §5 forced

Chapters 4 and 7 both used the phrase **"unknown is absence"**, taken from the
substrate's own working vocabulary. Under the three-way distinction that phrase
conflates two different states:

- **NULL** — the dimension is not instantiated at all;
- **unknown** — the dimension exists, the value is unresolved;
- **resolved** — a realised value.

An unvisited cell is *unknown*, not NULL: the spatial dimensions are
instantiated and what is missing is the value. The two behave differently under
projection, and the difference is operational rather than terminological — an
unresolved value is something an agent can go and resolve; a NULL dimension is
not something it can look for.

Both chapters now say **"unknown is a state in its own right"** and chapter 7
states the three-way distinction where it is first used. The original phrasing
was not wrong about what it was arguing against (a default zero); it was
imprecise about what it was arguing *for*.

  alternative to a resolved value.

  **Seldon manifolds are optional observer/agent machinery** for representing
  reachable unresolved continuations. *Nature does not have to run a Seldon

## Why these revisions all point the same way

Several of these revisions move claims in the direction of *claiming less*: the
substrate is not asserted to be discrete, reality is not asserted to be a graph,
the universe is not said to become more deterministic, and higher machinery is
not a universal requirement.

**That is not modesty. It is MDL, and it is the method that produced the theory
being run on the theory.** The procedure has two steps:

> 1. What assumptions have been made that aren't being said?
> 2. What can be taken away — and what happens if it does?

Every revision above is a completed step 2. *Fundamental discreteness* was
removed and no observable consequence went with it, so it was never load-bearing
— only discrete **sufficiency** was. *Reality is graph-shaped* was removed and
the dynamics still generate everything they generated before, so the claim was
carrying nothing but exposure. *Seldon manifolds as substrate requirement* was
removed and the ratchet still runs, which is how you find out the manifolds were
observer machinery all along.

Where a removal *does* cost something, that is the useful outcome rather than a
failure: it locates what the theory actually needs. The revisions that survived
are the load-bearing ones.

So the direction is worth preserving in the prose wherever a chapter has to
choose — not because the over-strong reading is what a hostile reader will assume
was meant, though it is, but because **a claim that can be removed without
observable loss was description length, not content.**
