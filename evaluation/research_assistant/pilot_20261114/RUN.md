# Exploratory feasibility pilot — 2026-11-14

This was a tooling and rubric shakedown, **not** the prospective comparative
evaluation in `docs/research/research_assistant/EVALUATION_DESIGN.md`. There
was no committed preregistration, task order was not randomized, the three
answers were generated by one Luna agent per arm, and no independent held-out
sample or power calculation was used. Agents shared a filesystem and were
instructed not to read the fixture keys; that restriction was not enforced by
isolation. Full prompts, token counts and exact latencies were not recorded by
the framework. The generic-ledger answer invokes some GE frame concepts, so
that arm is not a clean generic-ledger control. Repeated use of the same three
questions and the very small sample prohibit estimates of benefit.

All agents were given the same three synthetic cases, in this order: bounded
four-variable total extension versus general residual-CNF ease; a hypothetical
8/10 outcome with a missing negative control and zero positive control; and an
unavailable numeric result artifact and source revision. They were directed to
the same Ocelot science guide and GE LLM overview. Arm A used ordinary notes;
arm B used a generic claim/evidence ledger; arm C was instructed to use the
research helper and recorded a frame in a local temporary workspace; it did not exercise the full
claim/evidence workflow. Agents
were directed not to change project files. No model sampling settings or random
seeds were available from the agent interface.

The files were assigned pseudonyms using Python's `random.Random(54837)` before
grading: answer-1 = B, answer-2 = C, answer-3 = A. Two fresh Luna agents were
asked to score the files without looking up the mapping or each other's work.
The raw answers may themselves reveal the method used, so blinding is imperfect.
Both initially scored only three of nine combinations; they were asked to correct
their own sheets to cover every answer/task combination. The parent inspected
the mapping and initial sheets before that correction, but the graders were
not told the mapping. Their final independent sheets and reasons are retained
alongside the answers. Grader judgements are exploratory and do not verify
source provenance or establish correctness independently of the graders.

The two other fixture tasks were not run. Do not treat missing tasks as scores
or reuse any of these cases as held-out confirmation. Before a real evaluation,
freeze a new source-grounded task set and answer keys, enforce separation of
keys, set budgets and model parameters, randomize task/arm assignment, and
preregister the scoring, analysis and sample-size rationale. See the main
evaluation design for the required comparisons and measurements.

Across the nine answer/task pairs, graders assigned the same rubric value on
all 9 for six dimensions and on 7 of 9 for limitation recall. This is raw
agreement on a tiny convenience sample, not an estimate of grader reliability.
Both report reopening the two referenced files and matching the fixture hashes;
that does not validate every scientific inference in the answers.
