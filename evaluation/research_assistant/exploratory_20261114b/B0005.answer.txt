Claim: The only supported observation is that a response was detected in 7 of 12 reported target episodes (7/12, about 58%). This is descriptive. Because the negative control was omitted, the positive control produced no detectable response, and the independent-run count is unspecified, these data do not establish that treatment caused the response, that the measure can distinguish signal from noise, or that the experimental arm was functioning. The silent positive control is a critical counterevidence to interpreting the target count as a valid treatment effect: a floor-like result can arise from a dead arm. (Synthetic case statement; *Doing Science Research on the OCELOT Substrate*, §§3.1 clauses 1–5, 7.1.)

Follow-up before a confirmatory treatment claim: prospectively preregister the two-sided hypothesis, exact readout/statistic and comparison, sample size fixed by power analysis, claimed confirmatory seeds, decision rule (including what a null establishes), planned tests, and expected outcome with load-bearing assumptions. First validate the instrument: show a random-input floor and ideal-exemplar ceiling; positive-control each arm and report how many probe conditions evoke non-zero responses; ensure the negative control models the intended alternative; and, if claiming a mechanism, show it is load-bearing downstream by an on/off comparison. Treat independent runs/seeds as the analysis units, not episodes or coupled units as independent replicates. Then run the preregistered comparison and report failures and nulls with the scope of the conclusion. A measure that passes a dead arm is not a valid gate, and confirmatory work should not proceed until the control failure is diagnosed and the instrument/arms pass these checks. (*Doing Science Research on the OCELOT Substrate*, §§2 steps 1–7, 3.1 clauses 1–5, 4 (statistical analysis rules), 7.1–7.2.)
