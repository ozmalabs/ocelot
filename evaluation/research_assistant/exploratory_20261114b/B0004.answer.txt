The only direct conclusion is descriptive: a response was recorded in 7 of the 12 stated target episodes. This does not establish a treatment effect or even that the measurement arm was functioning. With no negative control there is no baseline comparison; with zero detectable response in the positive control, assay sensitivity or arm viability has not been demonstrated. The independent-run count is also unspecified, so the episodes cannot be treated as evidence of independent replication.

Before making a confirmatory treatment claim, first validate the instrument: show that random input scores at the floor and an ideal exemplar near the ceiling, and obtain a non-zero response under a positive control to show the arm is alive. Include an appropriate negative control so the target can be compared against background. If the claimed mechanism is part of the claim, test whether toggling it changes the downstream measure. Then preregister the two-sided hypothesis, exact readout and comparison, sample size based on power, claimed seeds, decision rule, and planned tests before collecting confirmatory data. Analyze independent runs as the unit where runs are independent, and report failures, nulls, and the scope of the result. Until those checks and a valid prospective comparison succeed, treat 7/12 as an exploratory observation, not confirmation.
