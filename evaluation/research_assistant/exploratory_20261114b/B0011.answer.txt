The 7/12 figure is a descriptive observation only. With the independent-run count unspecified, the 12 episodes cannot be assumed to be 12 independent units; it does not establish a reproducible treatment effect. The omitted negative control leaves the random-input/background floor and response specificity uncalibrated. More critically, the positive control produced no detectable response, so the arm or measurement may be inactive. Under the instrument gate, a floor score from a dead arm is not evidence that a measure is valid; this result makes the target response uninterpretable as confirmation (§3.1, clauses 1–5).

Before a confirmatory treatment claim, first diagnose and repair the positive-control failure, then calibrate the measure with random-input and ideal-exemplar controls, verify that the arms produce non-zero positive-control responses, and check duration stability when relevant. Include a negative control that models the intended comparison and calibrates background response. If the claim concerns a mechanism, test whether it is load-bearing downstream by comparing the relevant outcomes with the mechanism on and off (§3.1; §4.2).

Then prospectively preregister a two-sided hypothesis, exact statistic and comparison, sample size fixed by power analysis, clean confirmatory seeds, decision rule, and the complete test list before collecting confirmatory data (§2, steps 1–7). Analyze independent runs/seeds as the units, report failures and nulls, and state the claim’s scope (§4.2; §7.2). Do not treat the current 7/12 observation as confirmatory or proceed with a dead positive-control arm.
