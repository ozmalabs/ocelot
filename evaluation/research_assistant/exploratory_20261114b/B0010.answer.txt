Claim: The strongest supported finding in the fixed report is a partial held-out lift in aperture-covariance: fitting a well-regularized incidence reportedly raises it from about 0.75 (raw incidence) to 0.88 through the substrate BP. This is the report’s result, not a measurement independently reproduced by this document-only analysis (NATIVE_GRAPH_FIDELITY_RESULTS.md, “Claim 1”).

Evidence: The report says the lift uses disjoint held-out prompts and that weak regularization does not beat raw incidence, while stronger regularization does. It also reports train performance near 1.0, which it interprets as evidence that the reading is representable in-sample (same report, “Claim 2” and “Anti-faking / method”).

Counterevidence and limits: The report bounds the result to one small model, one layer, N=8–24 neurons/four modes, 60 training prompts and 10 held-out prompts. Its train-size sweep did not show improvement from more prompts of the same kind, and the proposed benefit of more diverse training data is explicitly undemonstrated. Most importantly, between-layer transport incidence remains untested (“Honest bounds and named next axis”). Thus this is a narrow within-layer result, not evidence for general multi-layer transport or broad generalization.
