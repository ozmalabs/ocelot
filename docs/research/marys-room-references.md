## Per-reference notes

**1. Parslow (repo).** File: `papers/formal/01f_graphic_equalisation_qualia_and_consciousness_grounding.tex`, with a compiled `.pdf` beside it. The title is written as "The Law of Graphic Equalisation --- Qualia and Consciousness Grounding", with the subtitle "Qualia-Space, Aperture, and the Ill-Posed Hard Problem". Author: Matthew Parslow, Independent Researcher.

The core claims, in three sentences:
- Qualia are admitted as determinate phenomenal contents. Because they are coherent distinctions, they are graph structure: relational in both determinacy and role, and not a second ontological kind, even if granted as a fundamental implementation.
- Qualia-space is an optional optimisation inside a conscious field: a compressed qualitative format that makes selected distinctions cheap to hold, compare and transform. It separates an unavailable dimension from a zero value, and coarse resolution from absence.
- Finer resolution over retained relations can reconstruct only what those relations carry, not a difference whose constituting relation is absent from the frame. On this view the explanatory "bridge from graph to feel" is neither missing nor possible.

The paper is unpublished, so cite it as a manuscript.

**2. CIE 170-1:2006.** CIE Technical Committee TC 1-36 report defining the CIE 2006 LMS cone fundamentals for 1°–10° fields, based on the Stockman & Sharpe (2000) functions; CVRL states the functions are the same. ISBN 3 901 906 46 0, taken from the copy at sdr.si. Using it as the source of the 2° and 10° cone fundamentals is accurate. Per `data/colour/PROVENANCE.md`, the official CIE 5 nm datatable reproduces the pilot values exactly.

**3. Bierman et al. 2011.** The venue is *Journal of Biomedical Optics* 16(6):067011, doi 10.1117/1.3593151. The abstract (via the Mount Sinai and ResearchGate pages) reports two things:
- Mean ± s.d. eyelid optical density over 450–650 nm was 2.1 ± 0.3, with about 1.0 OD range across subjects.
- The lid has a much higher OD at short wavelengths than earlier reports. This supports the long-pass direction.

Corrections to the repo:
- `docs/research/spectral-forward-model-spec.md` (line 88) gives the venue as "Ophthalmic Physiol Opt". That is wrong, but the file is frozen, so the paper should use the correct venue and not repeat the spec.
- The declared piecewise OD (OD_short 3.0, OD_long 1.3) gives a mean OD of about 2.89 over 450–650 nm, as the prereg Amendment A0 itself records. It is therefore "anchored to the direction" of Bierman et al. but does not match the 2.1 mean. The paper should say so.

**4. Kuehni 2004.** Citation confirmed from the PDF header: Vol. 29, No. 2, April 2004, pp. 158–162, DOI 10.1002/col.10237. Table I lists ten data sets; the abstract describes six of them as spectral-light experiments. Recomputing the observer-weighted means of the spectral-light rows (Ayama, Schefrin, Nerger, Jordan & Mollon, Volbrecht, Pridmore) gives values that match the repo:

| Hue | Recomputed (nm) | Repo value (nm) |
|---|---|---|
| UB | 478.04 | 478.0 |
| UG | 514.75 | 514.8 |
| UY | 576.28 | 576.3 |
| Unique red | 495.24c | 495.2c |

The unique-red figure is complementary, from Ayama 497.5c (2 observers) and Pridmore 494.6c.

One point to state in the paper: the 478.0/514.8/576.3 nm values are this study's weighted pooling, not means that Kuehni reports. Kuehni's main claim is large inter-observer variability, with UG spanning roughly 490–555 nm across spectral-light sets. The paper can fairly cite him for that variability.

**5. Berent 2023.** *Open Mind* 7:564–587. Berent argues that the hardness of the hard problem may come from human psychology, specifically intuitive dualism, rather than from ontology, and she supports this with experiments. Citing it for "problem intuitions are a psychological phenomenon, not evidence of an ontological gap" is accurate. Do not cite it as showing that dualism is false.

**6. Hirao et al. 2025.** *Scientific Data* 12, article 389. The paper is an fMRI dataset from 35 participants, each trial a relational similarity judgment between two of nine colour stimuli, with a no-report condition in half the trials. It is accurate to cite as an example of relational/structural approaches to colour qualia (qualia-structure paradigm) and as a source of human data. It does not provide a finding about qualia themselves.

**7. Zimnicki & Rogers.** The abstract was found at cdlib (eScholarship qt675702mb, CogSci proceedings collection); authors at UW–Madison. Method: triadic similarity judgments for 58 colours, embedded into a personal 3-D space per participant. Findings:
- Personal embeddings predicted held-out judgments better than other participants' embeddings and better than CIELAB.
- Individual structures fell into two clusters, one more categorical and one more continuous; the authors call these "motifs".

The year 2025 and the proceedings volume (presumably vol. 47) are UNVERIFIED: the eScholarship page returned 403 and Crossref has no record. Accurate if cited for structured individual differences in colour similarity spaces.

**8. Kind 2024.** *Philosophia* 52(2):211–220, online 18 Jan 2024. Kind examines Alter's "deducibility standard": an in-room route to knowing what red is like counts against the knowledge argument only if it is an a priori deduction from physical facts. She works through cases to clarify what counts as "cheating". This is the right citation for the dialectic over whether an in-room route to the experience, such as an eyelid exposure, is cheating. Do not cite it as endorsing any particular route.

**9. Morris 2025.** The author is Kevin Morris. *Philosophia* 53(4):1513–1528; Crossref gives the issue date as Sept 2025 and online publication as 29 Nov 2025. Morris defends the acquaintance account of Mary's epistemic gain against Alter's criticism and argues that Alter has not shown the gain must be propositional. Accurate if cited for the acquaintance strategy and the current debate.

**10. Jackson.** Both papers are confirmed via Crossref and JSTOR DOIs; Crossref lists start pages (127, 291), and the end pages follow the standard citations. Jackson 1982 presents the Mary/Fred knowledge argument against physicalism, with Mary the complete colour scientist in the black-and-white room. Jackson 1986 restates the argument and replies to Churchland. Both uses are accurate. Note that Jackson later recanted (e.g. 1998/2003), which the paper may want to mention.

**11. Physicalist replies.**
- Lewis: the ability hypothesis. What Mary gains is abilities (to remember, imagine and recognise), not new information. Originally *Proc. Russellian Society* 13:29–57 (1988), reprinted in Lycan (1990).
- Loar: the phenomenal-concept strategy. Mary acquires new phenomenal concepts of old physical properties. *Phil. Perspectives* 4:81–108 (1990), revised 1997.
- Conee: the acquaintance hypothesis. Mary becomes acquainted with the experience, which is neither propositional nor know-how. *AJP* 72(2):136–150.

All three uses are accurate.

**12. Naka & Rushton 1966.** *J. Physiol.* 185(3):536–555. In the range where one cone type drives the response, the S-potential is a hyperbolic (saturating) function of intensity: their eq. (1), p. 545, later known as the Naka–Rushton function. Accurate if cited for the receptor's half-saturation nonlinearity. I did not check that the eye's kernel uses exactly this form. `domains/photon_eye.py` exposes a `half_saturation` parameter per receptor class.

**13. Stockman & Sharpe 2000.** *Vision Res.* 40(13):1711–1737. They derived M- and L-cone spectral sensitivities from observers of known genotype; these became the basis of the CIE 2006 fundamentals. Accurate.

**14. Stockman, Sharpe & Fach 1999.** *Vision Res.* 39(17):2901–2927. The paper is mainly an S-cone spectral-sensitivity derivation; along the way it gives the lens and macular density spectra that CVRL distributes as `lensss` and `macss`. Accurate if worded as "lens and macular pigment density spectra of Stockman, Sharpe & Fach (1999), via CVRL". The lens curve is van Norren & Vos (1974) as adjusted by SSF, per CVRL and PROVENANCE.

**15. Govardovskii et al. 2000.** *Vis. Neurosci.* 17(4):509–528. It gives an empirical template for visual-pigment absorbance (α- and β-bands, A1/A2 chromophores) that is more accurate than Gaussian shapes. It is the right citation for a Limits section. The repo confirms the point: `domains/photon_eye.py` defines receptors as (centre, bandwidth) pairs, e.g. L 564/42, M 534/36, S 420/28 nm, not a pigment template.

**16. DKL 1984 / De Valois.** DKL (*J. Physiol.* 357:241–265) characterised macaque LGN chromatic responses in a cone-based colour space. Cells cluster along an L−M axis and an S−(L+M) axis; the latter is often written S−0.5(L+M), as in the prereg. Accurate. "De Valois" needs a specific work. The classic is De Valois, Abramov & Jacobs (1966) *JOSA* 56:966–977 on spectrally opponent LGN cells. Use it only if that is what was meant.

**17. Webster et al. 2000.** *JOSA A* 17(9):1545–1555. Unique-hue settings vary widely across colour-normal observers, and the variations for different hues are uncorrelated, which is inconsistent with a single common factor such as cone sensitivity. Accurate for inter-observer variability. Their stimuli were monitor colours (dominant wavelength), not spectral lights, which is why the repo's loci exclude this data set (Kuehni "Webster I", UG 544 nm).

**18. Data sources.**
- BabelColor: Danny Pascale's *ColorChecker RGB and spectra*. The "BabelColor Avg." rows are the mean of 30 measured charts, 380–730 nm in 10 nm steps.
- CVRL: Stockman & Sharpe's UCL database.

Both are correctly described in `data/colour/PROVENANCE.md`. The CIE datatables are CC BY-SA 4.0; credit BabelColor and Pascale as in other users' notices.
