# Thoughtexperiment predecessor fanout node scale v2 results

The locked 12-worker sweep passes all 14 independent checks at 200k, 400k, and 1m unrelated nodes, with two fresh-process replicates per size/backend cell. Every worker selects the unique correct supplied candidate, preserves primary isolation and active rules, matches the monolithic oracle, rejects stale receipts, and reaches quiescence.

At 1m nodes, shared graph pages reduce median candidate-fork time to 1.66 ms from 508 ms for full copy, and retained allocator growth to 21.8 MiB from 725.9 MiB. The end-to-end lifecycle remains 21.85 s versus 22.96 s: only about a 5% reduction, because graph export and canonical digest together take roughly 21.2 s for both backends. That lifecycle evaluates four candidates, or about 0.18 candidate branches/second end-to-end in this setup. From 200k to 1m, lifecycle time rises about 4.9×/4.9× for full-copy/shared-page; export rises 5.0×/5.0×, and digest rises 4.9×/4.9×. Peak RSS at 1m is about 4.29 GiB / 3.58 GiB. These two-replicate, one-host timings are descriptive; they do not establish a general complexity law or concurrent throughput.

The result moves the engineering bottleneck beyond fork cost: reducing graph-wide export/digest work is necessary before cheap CoW forks translate into materially faster experiments. This is still a sparse synthetic graph with four supplied candidates; it does not measure candidate generation or natural frame locality.
