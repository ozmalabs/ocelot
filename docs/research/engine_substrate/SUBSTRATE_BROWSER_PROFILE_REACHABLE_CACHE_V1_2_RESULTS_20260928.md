# Browser profile ReachableFrom cache v1.2 results

The preregistered 2×2 comparison crossed cache off/on with audit off/on for the five-seed `WorldAdapter.tick()` profile at N=2/8/32/128 affordances and B=1/4 wrappers, with three fresh workers per cell (24 workers, 96 ticks). The cache implementation stores each result and adjacency footprint behind a shared `Arc`, avoiding per-hit `SmallVec` footprint copies; footprint access events are replayed only while access tracing is enabled.

All 13 runner gates and all 12 independent analyzer checks passed. Source/native provenance stayed stable across workers; the loaded rules matched seed manifests; all four arms had identical graphs, progress, mirror counts, and zero-work terminal ticks. The audited cache-on/off traces matched exactly with no dropped keys; matcher counters and reachability hit/miss counts also matched the preregistered invariants. At N=128, audited traversal work remains 2,097,280→257 for B=1 and 587,776→260 for B=4.

After the same-pass invalidation fix, the source-locked panel was rerun. Quiet-arm median tick times at N=128 were 137.5 ms off versus 11.2 ms on (12.34× off/on) for B=1, and 45.5 ms versus 6.4 ms (7.10×) for B=4. At N=32, the quiet ratios are 4.15× and 2.15×. At N=2, B=1 is approximately even (1.01× off/on), while B=4 is slower with cache on (0.69×); the cache therefore has measurable fixed overhead on tiny profiles. Audit-on times show larger benefits but are not the primary performance estimate because tracing instrumentation is active.

At N=128, peak estimated cache payload is 8,512 bytes over two entries for B=1 and 9,028 bytes over five entries for B=4. These are structural payload estimates, excluding hash-table buckets, allocator overhead, capacity slack, and process RSS. They describe this synthetic DOM-shaped profile, not browser-page prevalence or live Chromium latency. The remeasurement after the correctness fix shows the observed gains remain large for N=32/128, but no general CPU complexity claim follows from one rule family and one adapter workload.
