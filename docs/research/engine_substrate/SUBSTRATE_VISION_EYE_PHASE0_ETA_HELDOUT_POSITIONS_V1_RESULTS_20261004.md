# Phase-0 eta-rescue replication on held-out positions v1 results

The source-locked capture passed all runtime and schedule checks for 12 workers: three new initialization seeds (3, 4, 5), two coordinate pairs, and two eta values. Each worker completed 8,000 one-step alternating presentations with exactly 4,000 exposures per location and 399,975 input writes. The independent analyzer recomputed centers and RMS spreads from all captured weights; source, runner, and analyzer integrity checks pass.

At baseline `ph0_eta=0.0005`, both locations localized in 2/3 seeds on the original coordinate pair and 2/3 on the held-out pair. At `ph0_eta=0.005`, both localized in 3/3 seeds on both pairs. The held-out pair uses different retinal coordinates `(3,12)` and `(12,4)` and therefore demonstrates spatial transfer within the same rule family, though its site separation differs from the original pair. This replicates the direction of the higher-eta benefit on new seeds and coordinates, with a smaller baseline-to-high-eta difference than the first panel; three seeds per cell leave considerable uncertainty.

Median closure was 7.22 ms/step at baseline versus 8.07 ms/step at high eta on the original pair, and 7.31 versus 6.94 ms/step on the held-out pair. Input writes are schedule-matched, while input update and closure times remain descriptive single-host measurements. The result supports a local learning-rate sensitivity and spatial transfer claim; it does not establish a stable global optimum, transfer to new visual features, natural-image capability, or self-directed parameter selection.
