# Materialized proposal resolver comparison v1 results

All correctness, idempotence, source-lock, and worker-replication gates pass for three fresh workers, N=2/4/8/16/32/64, and unique/tied maxima. The staged graph-rule construction caches the maximum once on the target, marks top proposals, counts those marks once, then records ambiguity or selects only when the count is one. It preserves the same output semantics as the v1.2 nested resolver.

Every one of the 12 measured cells uses fewer total Term evaluations than the matched v1.2 cell on the same native extension/build. For unique maxima, work falls from 80/198/578/1,914/6,890/26,058 to 53/93/173/333/653/1,293 at N=2/4/8/16/32/64. For ties, it falls from 58/140/400/1,304/4,648/17,480 to 43/75/139/267/523/1,035. At N=64, this is a 95.0% reduction for unique maxima and 94.1% for ties. The staged resolver evaluates `MaxOver` and `Count` once each per target in every measured cell; remaining work grows linearly in this panel.

This is evidence that the earlier quadratic cost was largely caused by recomputing nested terms per candidate, and that materialization is a practical existing-substrate solution for a stable proposal set. It is not a general complexity theorem. The panel does not test proposal mutation after caching: stale `max_score`, `is_top`, `top_count`, `ambiguous`, or `selected` values may need explicit invalidation/retraction or revision-scoped state. Production integration, latency/memory, natural tie rates, and learned proposal quality remain unmeasured.
