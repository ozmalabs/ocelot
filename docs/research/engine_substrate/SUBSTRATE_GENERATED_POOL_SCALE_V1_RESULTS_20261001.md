# Generated-pool scale ladder v1 (2026-10-01)

## Result

A source-locked, preregistered scale ladder completed 120 fresh workers across three authored tasks, four grammar size ceilings per task, five probe-prefix sizes, and two repetitions per cell. All scheduled rows were recorded; there were no worker errors or parent timeouts, and all analyzer gates passed. The full analysis is in [`analysis.json`](../../../evaluation/reports/substrate_generated_pool_scale_v1_20261001_analysis.json), with raw rows in [`raw.json`](../../../evaluation/reports/substrate_generated_pool_scale_v1_20261001_raw.json) and the source lock in [`lock.json`](../../../evaluation/substrate_capability/substrate_generated_pool_scale_v1_20261001_lock.json). Protocol: [`preregistration`](SUBSTRATE_GENERATED_POOL_SCALE_V1_PREREG_20261001.md).

The workload remains small and deliberately synthetic. `max_size` is a grammar-size ceiling, not a graph/frame size. Each cell uses an 8-second internal synthesis timeout, a 15-second parent timeout, a 10,000 behavior cap, and held-out target-support checks. No cell hit either timeout. Pool collection stopped at its declared size ceiling; therefore “complete” here means the bounded grammar enumeration completed, not that the open-ended hypothesis space is complete.

Median exploration and synthesis wall time show a sharp grammar-size step for triangular and Euler tasks, while square remains much cheaper in this grammar. At triangular ceiling 7, 8,709 terms are explored and median synthesis wall time ranges from 0.32 s (3 probes) to 0.75 s (10 probes); at ceiling 5 it explores 905–945 terms in 0.01–0.06 s. Euler at ceiling 5 explores 3,229 terms with no probes in 0.06 s, versus 10,053 terms and 0.80 s at 10 probes. Square at ceiling 5 explores 905–945 terms in 0.01–0.07 s. These are two-repeat medians, not stable tail estimates.

Probe count changes deduplication as well as cost: additional probe inputs distinguish more candidate behaviors. At the largest triangular ceiling, median pool size grows from 1 at zero probes to 9 at three or more probes; for Euler ceiling 5 it grows from 1 to 32 by six probes. The target is retained in both repetitions for triangular at ceiling 7 with at least three probes and for Euler at ceiling 5 with at least six probes. Square retains the target at ceiling 3 and above for every probe count. Thus probe diversity can improve target retention while increasing the generated version space and enumeration cost. Zero-probe pools are not meaningful completeness checks for behavioral distinctions.

Peak RSS high-water deltas are usually zero because workers share the same interpreter baseline and the measurement uses process high-water RSS; two Euler cells show +476 KiB and +1,102 KiB. Treat this as a coarse resource indicator, not per-cell incremental memory. CPU and wall times closely track in the recorded cells, consistent with this small serial CPU-bound workload.

## Interpretation and next gate

This confirms that generated-pool cost can rise sharply within tiny authored grammars, and that probe-set diversity affects both version-space size and whether the target behavior is retained. It does not support extrapolating a complexity exponent, establish natural-task frame locality, or show scalable induction. The preceding v1.5 comparison already found full-pool entropy slower end-to-end than iterative resynthesis on three small tasks; this scale ladder strengthens the case for incremental/version-space construction and explicit budgets before production-loop integration.

Next, implement or measure incremental pool construction under a fixed total learner-work budget. Compare it with full enumeration and current resynthesis on independently authored task families, and report exact held-out success, target retention, incomplete-pool rate, charged CPU/wall time, peak memory, and cost as input size grows. Preserve probe inputs across arms and include at least one family where the target is absent from the chosen grammar, so rung-three escalation is measured rather than assumed.
