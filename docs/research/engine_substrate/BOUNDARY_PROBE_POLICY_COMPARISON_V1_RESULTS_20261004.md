# Boundary-probe policy comparison v1 — results

The source-locked comparison completed all 30 cells: five frozen panels in each of two arithmetic task families, crossed with thresholds 3.75, 5, and 8. Every held support point was transformed deterministically into a target-valid example for its task family. Independent analysis passed source/lock binding, candidate behavior replay, query membership and label checks, survivor filtering, abstention checks, and seeded wide-domain scoring.

The unchanged coverage policy returned the restricted candidate alongside the two exact Terms in all 30 panels. Independent wider scoring found one false candidate acceptance per panel. Candidate-directed probing selected a threshold-adjacent example, removed the restricted candidate with one label, and retained both exact candidates in all 30 panels. It used 270 candidate/probe evaluations total, versus 1,530 for the fixed 17-point grid; median selector time was 0.145 ms versus 0.538 ms. Both probing policies made zero wide-domain errors across 61,800 candidate/case checks each. Abstention triggered in all 30 panels and made no false acceptance, but did no task-solving work.

This result supports a narrow repair: extracting a supplied numeric comparison bound can guide useful probes across additive and product relations in this authored setup. It does not establish a generic Term-bound extractor, autonomous relation discovery, representative natural-task performance, or end-to-end runtime benefit. The wide scoring set is deterministic and finite (1,030 examples per panel); the exact candidate grammar and labels are supplied by the harness.
