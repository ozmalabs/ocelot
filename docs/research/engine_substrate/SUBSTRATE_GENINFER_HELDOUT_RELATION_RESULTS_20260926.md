# GenInfer held-out relation results

The 90-call, 18-cell capture passes every construction, held-out separation, independent scoring, repeat determinism, and provenance gate. Each call gives GenInfer only six training rows; two withheld rows are scored afterward by applying the returned 2×2 matrix and predicate-local transforms. The three clean-training/clean-holdout geometries recover `[1,1,0,2]` and predict both withheld rows in all 15/15 calls.

The remaining outcomes expose the role of residual generalization. With a uniform +1 shift on p0 training rows, the matrix is recovered and one `p0 shift(+1)` is returned in every geometry. It predicts both holdouts in 15/15 calls when the withheld p0 also has +1, but only the p1 row when the withheld p0 is clean (15/30 held-out rows correct). Thus the residual is consistently applied as a rule for that predicate, even when it describes training noise rather than a stable task property.

Inconsistent +1/+2 training perturbations lead to refusal in the `adjacent_12` and `opposite_03` geometries. In `adjacent_01`, all five calls retain the true matrix plus a p0 `shift(+1)` residual: this predicts only 5/10 rows per clean holdout pair, and both rows under the +1 holdout condition. This is direct evidence that a training decomposition can recover the generator while still carrying a residual that fails on clean held-out cases.

All held-out predictions are computed independently from the returned matrix and residual transforms; `explained` alone is not used as a correctness score. Five repeats confirm deterministic behavior on these fixtures, not statistical uncertainty or natural-task performance.
