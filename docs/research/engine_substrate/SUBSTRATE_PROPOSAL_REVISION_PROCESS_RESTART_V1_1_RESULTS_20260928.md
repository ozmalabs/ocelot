# Proposal revision recovery across process restart v1.1 results

The confirmatory v1.1 panel passes all gates in three workers for N=2/16/64 and cuts after zero, one, two, or all three max/count/decision stages. Each cell uses distinct producer and resume PIDs. Thirty-six fsync'ed JSON checkpoints are retained, with byte length and SHA-256 verified against the producer manifest. A fresh Substrate restores graph and grammar exactly, imports the rule store with semantic parity, resumes precisely the missing stages, commits revision 1, and awakens the guarded consumer. Every final state matches the score oracle; each checkpoint keeps one active revision node.

Rule export is semantically, not byte-for-byte, identical after import: the importer canonicalizes the order of match clauses and recalculates the derived `content_hash`. Rule order/slot, active state, effects, guards, and behavior are preserved by the normalized comparison. The process boundary exposed a separate stale-read hazard: the old `consumed_label` remains in the graph while revision 1 is uncommitted. v1.1 adds `consumed_revision`; the old value remains stamped 0 until the new commit, after which label and stamp both advance to 1. Consumers must check the revision stamp as well as the label.

This demonstrates clean-process recovery from a JSON checkpoint written and fsync'ed on the current local filesystem. It does not establish power-loss durability, process termination during a closure, torn writes, schema migration, concurrent updates, or machine-restart guarantees across filesystems. The initial v1 capture is exploratory: its exact-rule-byte and no-old-consumed-value gates did not account for rule canonicalization and versioned prior results. v1.1 is the confirmatory run.
