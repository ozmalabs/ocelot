# Thoughtexperiment revision/receipt validation v1 results

The locked 16-worker panel passes all independent gates. Each full-digest worker performs five graph exports and five canonical digests in its candidate lifecycle. Each revision/receipt worker performs none there, verifies that exploration left the primary at the captured semantic revision, rejects a stale receipt without revision change, and then performs a timed exact graph export/digest comparison of the returned primary and selected branch. All 16 rows preserve the unique correct candidate, primary rules, quiescence, and exact primary/branch parity with the independently built monolithic oracle.

At 200k nodes, lifecycle-only median speedups are 28.2× for full-copy and 122.8× for shared graph pages. At 1m they are 33.7× and 319.8×. These omit the deferred exact branch/primary audit. Adding that audit back gives paired median speedups of 2.33×/2.42× at 200k and 2.47×/2.50× at 1m. At 1m, exact branch/primary audit takes about 9.0 s for full-copy and 8.7 s for shared pages; the revision/receipt lifecycle itself takes 0.71 s and 0.069 s. The audit-inclusive result is the fairer estimate when exact parity is required after every batch.

This result supports an engineering path: use the O(1) graph/rule revision and exact mutation receipt during candidate exploration, then pay for exact whole-state parity at a separate audit cadence. It depends on all semantic writes advancing revision and on complete journal coverage. The 17-case mutation matrix is finite and single-threaded; it does not prove every interior mutation or concurrent safety. This is one sparse synthetic workload, two replicates per cell on one host—not a general production speedup or natural-task throughput claim.
