# Thoughtexperiment polar-conflict guard v1 results (2026-10-03)

## Result

The source-locked runner completed 21 rows: seven conditions × three fresh repeats. Integrity passed, the baseline matched its expected behavior in every row, and the candidate matched all seven candidate behavior oracles in every repeat. Candidate-only conflict outcomes were explicitly returned in 6/6 contradictory-claim rows; control rows returned none. Before each return, the primary graph and rule state matched their captured baselines. Returned conflict nodes matched their candidate branch nodes, and candidate rule installation remained branch-local. Native build freshness reported no stale components.

The candidate preserved `yes` for one accepted affirmative claim, `no` for one accepted negative claim, and `yes` for the accepted claim when its opposite was retracted, proposed, or about another subject. For both insertion orders of contradictory accepted claims, the baseline emitted `yes`; the candidate instead set support to `conflict`, recorded a `PolarConflictOutcome`, and emitted no polar answer plan. The capture is [`raw JSON`](../../../evaluation/reports/thoughtexperiment_polar_conflict_guard_v1_raw_20261003.json), generated by the [`runner`](../../../evaluation/substrate_capability/thoughtexperiment_polar_conflict_guard_v1.py) against this [`source lock`](../../../evaluation/substrate_capability/thoughtexperiment_polar_conflict_guard_v1_lock_20261003.json).

## Interpretation and limits

This is positive evidence for the requested thought-experiment lifecycle: same-snapshot baseline/candidate comparison, isolated rule changes, branch-local execution, sparse graph residual capture, and explicit graph-delta return. It establishes functional behavior on a small unary proposition fixture; it gives no speedup estimate and does not test concurrent branches, stale-revision rejection, multi-relation propositions, event/speaker provenance, or larger graph costs.

Most importantly, the guard was written by the experiment designer and injected into a candidate branch. The substrate neither inferred the conflict concept nor proposed the rule. Therefore this advances the substrate's capacity to test and retain a proposed improvement, but does not demonstrate that it can build itself. The next gate should test candidate-generation provenance and selection: provide multiple generated repairs, withhold held-out conflict cases from proposal construction, and measure whether substrate-owned evidence ranking selects a valid rule while preserving unique-answer and non-conflict controls. Keep proposal generation, test selection, branch evaluation, residual return, and adoption as separately scored stages.
