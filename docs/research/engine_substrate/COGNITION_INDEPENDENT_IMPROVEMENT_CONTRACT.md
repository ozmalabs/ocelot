# Independent-goal improvement contract

**Scope:** P22.01 specification. It governs claims about improvement across independently generated tasks and generations. The P21 retention and curriculum effects both failed at ceiling; neither is treated as a learning benefit or used to tune this protocol. P15's operator-aligned result remains bounded to its declared finite family.

## Independent goals, splits and checkpoints

Task-world identity is generated from a frozen family generator, a split namespace, and a world seed. Generation/checkpoint index never enters the goal, world dynamics, scoring key, or held-out label. Every checkpoint is evaluated on the same sealed world IDs, and every paired arm receives a fresh isolated copy of each world. Training, curriculum selection, development qualification, validation, and sealed evaluation use disjoint seed blocks. Candidate names, operator powers, and expected improvement cannot influence goal generation. A family is claimed only after the same world adapter produces real outcomes for all matched arms in development.

The original `pmb_generated_loopover_v1` and `pmb_generated_d4_corner_v1` generators are retained to preserve the provenance of the 2026-10-23 candidate packet, but their validation blocks contain only two distinct goals each and are insufficient as the P22 independent-goal panel. The preregistration development path uses `pmb_generated_loopover_v2` and `pmb_generated_d4_corner_v2`, with separate family-seed codes. Across the first 200 validation draws, v2 yields 195 distinct Loopover goals and all 8 D4 goals; the corresponding 200 training draws use disjoint seeds. Freeze these versions and exact draw allocations only in the standalone P22 preregistration after the matched arms and resource instruments pass development audit.

The frozen ancestor is copied from the declared starting checkpoint for every world and generation. It does not receive candidate updates. Adaptive checkpoints are immutable snapshots with parent digest, candidate expression digests, active portfolio, task provenance, approval events, proof/adoption/grounding records, cumulative work, memory, and rollback disposition. A failed, stale, timed-out, rejected, or rolled-back generation remains in the lineage and denominator. Human edits invalidate autonomous-generation attribution.

## Arms and equal resources

The matched causal design has four arms, as required by P22.03: **unchanged** keeps the ancestor portfolio and uses the frozen nonadaptive task schedule; **adaptive-only** enables the native curriculum selector while freezing the ancestor portfolio; **invention-only** disables adaptive curriculum while enabling native proposal, verification and promotion; **full** enables both mechanisms. This separates curriculum benefit, invention benefit and their interaction. Do not let Python select tasks or operators by arm label; graph-held policies make those decisions from the same admissible training evidence.

Each arm starts from an independent copy of the same ancestor and receives identical world IDs, primitive interfaces, model-query opportunities, action ceilings, and frozen cumulative resource ceilings. Curriculum and invention costs count against those ceilings. Each agent uses one shared ThinkingBudget. Record denial, retrieval, proof search, approval, rollback, timeouts and failed work in every arm; retain them in the denominator. A success comparison at unequal observed work remains a capability outcome only; no efficiency claim is made unless paired cumulative primitive work and the other preregistered resource dimensions meet the frozen matching rule. The unchanged arm is the comparator for the primary paired checkpoint estimand; the other two ablation arms test causal dependence and interaction.

Equal, frozen ceilings are the assigned-budget condition for the primary efficacy comparison. Exact equality of realized resource use is a separate efficiency-comparability condition. A measured mismatch does not invalidate the paired capability outcome when every arm received the same world inputs and declared ceilings, all measured dimensions remain within their ceilings, and all outcomes are retained; it does prohibit an efficiency claim. This distinction is required by the design: invention/full descendants carry candidate-specific approval and receipt evidence, while passive controls carry neither, so approval cost and event counts necessarily differ. Report those differences explicitly rather than padding controls with sham actions or silently treating them as zero-cost inventions.

Measure `approval_cost` as cumulative human review seconds for candidate-specific approvals. Each approval record must contain `human_review_seconds` and `human_review_cost_source="reviewer_self_report"`; report the value in the approval request/response record before the simulated action. Zero approval events are measured as zero from the graph. If any approval lacks a valid duration/source, retain the event count but classify the dimension `PARTIAL_EVENT_COUNT`; it cannot pass resource preflight. Do not infer time from message length or elapsed wall time, and do not backfill a duration for historical approvals.

Existing approval is candidate-specific. The historical P15 approvals for `commutator_square_v1`, `commutator_cube_v2`, and `commutator_fourth_v3` apply only to those expressions and their frozen criteria digest. Any different expression, parameter, guard, domain, or criteria digest that reaches a world-action path requires a new exact approval event. The evaluator may test an unapproved candidate in a non-actionable verification world, but cannot grant its approval.

## Estimands and inference

For each family, world ID, and generation, record paired bounded scores for the full checkpoint and unchanged ancestor on the same world. The generation-specific effect is the mean of candidate-minus-ancestor paired score differences. The primary final effect is the generation-3 paired mean, which must be at least `+0.10`. The trend estimand is the ordinary least-squares slope of the per-generation paired means over ordered generations 1–3; its point estimate must be at least `+0.05` per generation and its one-sided 99% lower bound must exceed zero. This reconciles the P22.01 contract with the Phase 22 registered slope criterion. A claimed family must satisfy both criteria. Holm correction controls the final-gain and slope superiority tests at `α=.01` across all claimed families. Calculate each one-sided bootstrap p-value at the zero-effect boundary from the registered 10,000 whole-world cluster resamples using `(1 + count(bootstrap_effect <= 0)) / 10,001`, then apply Holm to the complete set of family-by-endpoint tests. Report both raw and adjusted p-values with the registered lower bounds; no family passes if either endpoint's point threshold, lower bound, or adjusted p-value fails.

Inference resamples whole world IDs as clusters, preserving that world's repeated checkpoints and arms. Use 10,000 deterministic bootstrap resamples with the seed fixed in the preregistration. Report paired differences, per-checkpoint rates, Wilson intervals, exact McNemar tests, the slope, and corrected bounds. For all-success, all-failure, or flat paired scores, the paired effect and slope are exactly zero; the result is a valid no-improvement outcome, not an undefined model fit. Missing pairs, duplicate world-arm rows, or inconsistent world identity make the affected estimate `NOT_ESTABLISHED`.

At least three promoted generations are needed for a recursive-improvement claim. Each claimed family and generation must have at least 200 common sealed worlds, plus at least 200 fresh validation worlds, with the same task identities across arms and checkpoints. Use the same sealed evaluation panel at each checkpoint; do not fit on it. Report all families, checkpoints, failures, approval costs, wall time, primitive work, native steps, model calls, graph/memory footprint, and rollback events. A positive score trend does not establish efficiency or human-relative performance.

## Safety, provenance and claim boundaries

Proof bindings, expression digests, adoption events, source/model/frame epochs, independent action receipts and grounding checks must pass for every external transition. Any false proof, stale binding, unauthorized action, label leak, world-state mismatch, or rollback failure is a zero-tolerance phase failure. Revocation suspends dependent tasks. Unknown adapters are `UNSUPPORTED`; missing outcomes are `NOT_RUN`; timeouts remain failures or unknowns and cannot be replaced. No human baseline exists for this task family until a protocol-matched source is cited or a prospective procedure is frozen.

Native qualification terminalizes the bounded search requests that supplied its two distinct verified trials. Additional proposals generated from the same pair of tasks may remain `awaiting_native_qualification`; they require fresh native training requests to obtain another distinct qualification trial. Keep those pending proposals and their costs in the report rather than treating a second candidate as qualified from a request already consumed by another qualification.

No capability level closes from a finite task family. P22.04 must map each L-level to demonstrated input breadth, exact native consumers and held-out evidence, retaining unsupported raw perception, irregular-time handling, opaque motor skills and general language as open where no evidence exists.
