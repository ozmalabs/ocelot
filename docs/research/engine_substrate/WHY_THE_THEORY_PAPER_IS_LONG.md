# A minimal theory has a maximal justification burden

**2026-08-28.** Recorded because the size of paper 01 will be raised by every reader, and the
answer should be measured rather than asserted.

## The measurement

Paper 01, body only, sections classified as *theory proper* (the minimal graph, admissible
possibility, collapse and realised history, the Law, the antecedent-state principle, residual)
against everything else:

```
  theory proper     1,803 words   11%
  everything else  13,900 words   89%
```

The theory is 1,803 words. The paper is 16,068.

## Why that ratio is not a defect

The claim of a minimal substrate is *"this is all you need."* That quantifies over everything not
included, so every candidate has to be named, removed, and shown not to break anything on the way
out. Parent pointers, world models, affordance catalogues, self-models, a privileged standpoint,
time, a universal cost, a privileged carving — each of those is a removal, and a removal is one
sentence to state and a section to demonstrate.

**So the paper is long because the theory is small.** The relationship is not incidental: the fewer
primitives kept, the more candidates must be defeated, and each defeat costs pages that the theory
itself does not. A larger theory would need a *shorter* paper, because it would assert more and
remove less.

That is the same structure as the contribution's own shape — a removal is tiny to state and large in
consequence — with the page count as one of the consequences.

## What is tractable and what is not

**Tractable, and already done.** Groundings and futures separate cleanly, because they are
*applications* rather than justification: physics (01a), mind and self (01b), Seldon manifolds and
their psychology (02, 02a), Narrative momentum (03), Narrativium (04), the constructive realisation
(05), the metaphysics (06). Nine papers, ~65,000 words, cut along that seam.

**Not tractable.** What remains in 01 is substrate plus removal argument, and a removal argument does
not split. Taking out part of it does not shorten the claim — it weakens it, because the unremoved
candidate is then simply unaddressed. Any part removed leaves the paper incomplete in the specific
sense that a reader can ask "but what about X" and find no answer.

## The option nobody has named

Splitting 01 is the wrong instrument, but a **short version** is not. A reader who grants the
minimality claim does not need the removals demonstrated; they need the substrate, the frame
machinery, and the falsifiers — perhaps 3,000 words, with 01 standing as the reference for anyone who
wants a specific removal shown.

That is additive rather than subtractive, which is the right shape here: the length is load-bearing
for one audience and dead weight for another, and the fix for that is a second document, not a
shorter first one.

## The honest risk

89% justification is defensible and is also a real exposure. It means most of the paper is
addressed to an objection the reader may not have, and a reader who never doubted the minimality
claim will experience the bulk of it as padding. That is a presentation problem rather than a
content problem, and it is what the short version would solve.
