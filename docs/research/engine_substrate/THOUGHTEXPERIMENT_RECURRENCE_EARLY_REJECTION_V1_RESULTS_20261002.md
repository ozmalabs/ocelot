# Thoughtexperiment recurrence early-rejection v1 — results

**Paired semantics and selection gates pass: 14/14.** The current evaluator and the new 200-rule-call-per-stage evaluator were compared on the same 256 candidate episodes (128 training, 128 held-out). Their pass/fail outcome matched on every pair. Both graph selectors chose `dedup_identity_fix` in all four workers; the chosen candidate reached fixed point and passed every held-out episode. No selected episode hit the bounded evaluator's work budget. Every first-stage budget rejection occurred before the second recurrence candidate was added; primary rules stayed unchanged and only the compact residual returned from candidate scoring branches.

The bounded evaluator reduced measured rule-work units from 176,288 to 32,288 (5.46× fewer) and evaluator wall time from 116.4 s to 5.64 s across the paired panel (20.6× faster on this fixture). Mean candidate-episode runtime fell from 455 ms to 22.0 ms. Candidate enumeration remained about 104 ms per worker and is reported separately; graph-Term decisions were under 1 ms. Five losing proposals still hit the bounded budget in all 160 train/held-out episodes, but were rejected during their first stage. The cap establishes budget exhaustion on this workload, not nontermination. This is a harness-level budget policy around native rule execution, not a general speed theorem or a change to engine closure semantics.

The earlier recurrence graph-selection v1.1 runtime values are corrected from 115–117 ms to 459–467 ms per candidate episode. The previous divisor counted all four workers while the recorded timing was already aggregated per worker. The locked raw values are unchanged; see the [analysis amendment](THOUGHTEXPERIMENT_RECURRENCE_EARLY_REJECTION_V1_AMENDMENT_20261002.md).
