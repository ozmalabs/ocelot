# Full-seed GenMint four-relation held-out transfer results v2

The amended analysis passes all integrity and clean positive gates on 180 full-seed sequences. The larger held-out set contains four relations (two p0, two p1) and removes the two-row `scale2` ambiguity from the prior probe's clean positive case. In all three clean-training/clean-heldout geometries, five enabled runs each mint `[1,1,0,2]` during training, re-decode training with zero quanta, and select that training-acquired matrix to predict all four held-out relations: 15/15 sequence-level exact transfers, 60/60 held-out rows. All 15 paired disabled controls mint a quantum on training and on the clean heldout, with no expected matrix acquisition.

Uniformly shifted training also acquires the expected matrix with zero quanta in 15/15 sequences and transfers it exactly to clean heldouts (60/60 rows). This reinforces the direct probe's distinction: a predicate-local residual on one training signature is not stored inside the matrix generator and is not automatically applied to a separate clean base. If the heldout target itself carries the p0 shift, the system does not use the acquired matrix in any of the 45 enabled sequences across training conditions: other held generators are selected in most layouts, while the opposite layout either has no generator transform or mints a quantum. These outcomes are not credited to learned-linear transfer.

Under inconsistent +1/+2 training perturbation, the enabled full-seed path acquires the expected matrix in 10/30 sequences (the same layout under both heldout conditions); the other 20/30 mint a quantum. In the five clean-heldout sequences from that acquired layout, the matrix transfers exactly (20/20 rows). In other layouts the heldout path may recognize by a transform minted only at that later stage; v2.1 classifies these separately and does not count them as transfer.

The single capture's median training closure times were 24.69 ms enabled vs 0.80 ms disabled for clean data, 24.94 vs 0.81 ms for uniform training shifts, and 13.15 vs 0.81 ms for inconsistent shifts. Total probe time was 9.93 s. These timings are descriptive, show a substantial acquisition premium, and omit memory attribution and repeated-use amortization. On this small authored family the enabled arm trades one quantum per training observation for a reusable graph-resident generator on positive cases; broader economic benefit remains unmeasured.

Next vary learned matrices and held-out geometries and increase reuse count to estimate acquisition/application break-even; preserve the current predicate and residual cases as known boundary conditions.
