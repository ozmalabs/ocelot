# Phase-0 site-count × schedule factorial v1 results

All 12 source-locked workers passed schedule and runtime gates. Each location received exactly 4,000 training exposures; two-site workers ran 8,000 steps and four-site workers 16,000. The frozen test matrix contains 360 probes. Independent analysis reconstructs every native activation vector and winner from captured weights, with 360/360 matches; weights remain exactly unchanged during probes.

**Two sites.** Both one-step interleaved and blocked schedules localized all trained sites across all three paired seeds (18/18 probes per schedule). Neither schedule localized held-out positions under the strict field-center criterion (0/18), although the coarse nearest-site region score was 18/18 when ties between equally near sites were allowed.

**Four sites.** Blocked training localized all four trained sites in every seed (36/36 trained-site probes; 4/4 unique sites covered in each worker). Interleaved training localized 18/36 trained-site probes overall, with per-seed coverage of 0/4, 4/4, and 2/4 sites. Neither schedule localized held-out positions (0/108 each). Coarse nearest-region scores were 27/108 for blocked and 24/108 for interleaved, so the four-site fields did not provide reliable continuous or categorical interpolation at the sampled points.

Median native closure time stayed similar across the four cells: 7.31 ms/step (two-site interleaved), 7.00 ms (two-site blocked), 6.91 ms (four-site interleaved), and 6.80 ms (four-site blocked). Input update time followed transition volume: median training writes were 143,991 vs 27 for the two-site interleaved/blocked schedules, and 287,991 vs 63 for four sites. Thus the four-site representation failure under interleaving is schedule-sensitive, not explained by a measured closure slowdown. Blocking rescues four-site acquisition in this task, while the four-site map still fails zero-shot held-out localization.

This is evidence for schedule-dependent acquisition and retention in one authored phase-0 learner with four units. It does not prove a general capacity limit, a universal benefit of blocked schedules, or that a larger training set would improve held-out interpolation. The next gate should replicate blocked four-site acquisition across more seeds, then add a denser trained map or alternate representations to measure interpolation directly.
