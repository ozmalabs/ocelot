# Proposal revision and resolver invalidation v1 results

The source-locked mutation panel ran three fresh workers at N=2/8/32/64. Its preregistered lifecycle gates fail, consistently across all workers and sizes. The initial unique state and addition of a strictly higher proposal resolve correctly. Adding an equal-scoring proposal yields `top_count=2` and clears `selected_label`, but `ambiguous` remains false. Removing that tie restores the unique selected label and false ambiguity. Lowering the higher proposal's score then performs zero counted Term evaluations and leaves the prior winner (`p_high`) selected, although the independent graph oracle says the original `p{N-1}` proposal is now best.

The panel therefore demonstrates that a fixed-set materialized resolver does not by itself provide correct truth maintenance. One derived field can lag another within the closure, and a score mutation that is not represented as an explicit graph dependency does not awaken the resolver. A correct mutable lifecycle needs both dependency ordering across derived stages and explicit revision signaling for proposal-attribute changes. The tested path demonstrates that the current resolver, as authored, does not satisfy those requirements; it does not show that every possible staged/event-driven construction must fail.

For the smallest and largest fixtures, Term evaluations across initial / higher-added / tie-added / tie-removed / rescored phases are 51/62/57/62/0 at N=2 and 981/992/739/992/0 at N=64. The zero on rescoring is not an efficiency success: it is the stale-state failure. All three workers reproduce integer counts. The analyzer reports the semantic gates that fail rather than masking them.
