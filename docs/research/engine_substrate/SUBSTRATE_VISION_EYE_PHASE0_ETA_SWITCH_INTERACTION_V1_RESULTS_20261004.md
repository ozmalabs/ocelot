# Phase-0 visual plasticity-rate × switching interaction v1 results

All source-locked capture gates passed for 15 workers, with three seeds in each of five conditions, exact 8,000-step schedules, equal 4,000 exposures per location, expected transition/write counts, positive closure progress, and nonempty Argmax cache occupancy. The independent analyzer recomputed receptive-field centers and spreads from all saved weights; its source hash is recorded in the analysis JSON.

At one-step switching, baseline `ph0_eta=0.0005` retained both locations in 1/3 seeds. Increasing eta to 0.001 and 0.002 remained at 1/3; eta 0.005 retained both in 3/3. The 100-step switching reference at eta 0.0005 also retained both in 3/3. All one-step arms had exactly the same stimulus sequence, switch count, and 399,975 input writes, so their difference cannot be attributed to a change in transition schedule. This supports plasticity magnitude as a real contributor to the failure in this authored setup, with a candidate rescue near 10× baseline eta. The threshold is provisional because each arm has only three seeds.

Median closure time was 6.90, 6.59, 6.70, and 7.37 ms/step for the one-step eta values 0.0005, 0.001, 0.002, and 0.005; the 100-step baseline was 7.01 ms/step. One-step input update time was about 0.11–0.13 ms/step across eta values, compared with 0.005 ms/step in the 100-step reference. The high-eta arm shows no broad closure slowdown in this workload, though this small panel cannot establish stability outside the measured horizon or on other patterns.

The result identifies a potentially tunable learning-rate/switching interaction; it does not establish adaptive rate selection, broad visual learning, natural-image capability, or autonomous self-improvement. Follow up with a denser eta sweep around 0.002–0.005, more seeds, and held-out patch locations while tracking per-step winner assignment and weight-change magnitude.
