# Substrate assessment against the tractability report

**Purpose:** provide an evidence-based starting point for a follow-on plan. This document records what the substrate already supports, what remains a hypothesis, and the work needed to turn the tractability argument into bounded, measurable claims. It is an assessment, not an implementation plan or a claim that the architecture is tractable in general.

**Scope:** rule closure and minting; inference; rung 1–2 recognition and transform; rung 3–4 induction and minting; frame locality and scheduling; runtime evidence and the LLM comparison. Original inventory evidence is from 2026-09-24; dated updates incorporate structural measurements through 2026-09-28.

## Executive assessment

The substrate has a real exact-inference solution for a narrow, favorable case: CAN networks with sufficiently low treewidth. It also has asynchronous rule execution, dependency/read-write metadata, domain-specific shape decoding and mint guards, and empirical performance at small task scales. These are useful components of a tractability story.

The central premise in the report, however, is not yet an engine-wide result. The substrate does not generally discover and enforce a hierarchy of frames with bounded separators, nor does it report such bounds for representative workloads. Consequently, the conditional argument—small separators imply tractable inference, matching, closure, and bounded induction inputs—has only been partly instantiated. The open-ended rung 3–4 problem remains open; rung ordering is a search policy, not a bound on induction cost.

The planning target should therefore be a sequence of scoped, falsifiable results, not a single “tractable substrate” claim:

1. Specify and measure frame/cut structure on representative workloads.
2. State and enforce termination and size conditions for rule closure.
3. Extend bounded-width exact inference beyond the current CAN case where justified.
4. Bound recognition/transform work within declared frame limits.
5. Measure induction frequency and cost separately from held-structure operation.
6. Replace unsupported scale/performance comparisons with reproducible benchmarks.

## Findings and evidence

### 1. Closure and minting

The Rust rewrite engine in `runners/dsl/src/rewrite.rs` provides rule execution and fixpoint iteration. Minting is a first-class capability in rule effects and in domain-specific shape systems. Individual paths do control duplication or repeated work with guards, keys, epochs, or state transitions. That is valuable engineering discipline, but it is not a termination theorem for the complete rule language.

The report’s proposed weak-acyclicity result is a suitable planning item, with a prerequisite: first define exactly which rule effects count as fresh-name invention, and whether rules may create terms/edges that enable additional existential witnesses. Then decide whether the engine will restrict the rule set, statically analyze it, or expose a bounded-closure mode. A proof for a restricted declarative subset should not be generalized to arbitrary substrate rules.

**Updated status (2026-09-28):** general fixpoint execution and selected local mint guards: present. For a conservative finite-key monotone insertion fragment, a formal finite-progress bound, executable reject-on-unknown analyzer, native generated-grammar parity, and one-schema scale evidence through N=64 are present. The bound is `R + E(N+R)^2 - M` absent concurrent graph mutation; for fixed rules this is O(N²) abstract progress, with no uniform program-size claim. This is a restricted theorem/result, not weak acyclicity and not an end-to-end runtime guarantee. Arbitrary fresh-name termination, construction-time admission, rule-access completeness and least-fixed-point correctness remain open. See [finite-key closure statement and evidence](SUBSTRATE_FINITE_KEY_CLOSURE_TERMINATION_20260926.md), [checker/source limits](SUBSTRATE_MINT_TERMINATION_FRAGMENT_ANALYSIS.md), and [generated grammar v1.2](SUBSTRATE_FINITE_KEY_GENERATED_GRAMMAR_V1_2_RESULTS_20260926.md).

**Admission-path confirmation (2026-09-28):** a fresh-mint rule rejected by the finite-key checker is accepted in 3/3 workers by direct `add_rule`, checkpoint `import_rules`, rule-patch revision, and explicit graph-rule self-mint. The self-minted rule is installed but its absent trigger edge prevents the forbidden effect from firing. This closes the question of whether those tested routes enforce the checker: they do not. It does not show that the program fails to terminate, and it does not cover every possible Rust construction path. V1's default-cycle setup error and the unscored v1.1 setup pilot are preserved and disclosed ([v1.1 results](SUBSTRATE_MINT_FRAGMENT_ADMISSION_PATH_PROBE_V1_1_RESULTS_20260928.md), [source audit](SUBSTRATE_FINITE_KEY_CLOSURE_FRAGMENT_SOURCE_AUDIT_V1_20260928.md), [amendment](SUBSTRATE_MINT_FRAGMENT_ADMISSION_PATH_PROBE_V1_1_AMENDMENT_20260928.md)).

### 2. Inference

The strongest tractability result found is the CAN junction-tree work. `domains/graph_treewidth.py` supplies exact treewidth calculation under a size gate and a min-fill upper bound; `domains/can_junction_tree.py` performs exact min-sum inference for the CAN energy. The reported evaluation checks answers against brute-force enumeration on the five-node Bigville template, cross-checks the energy against the Rust implementation, and refuses a dense, high-width case rather than attempting an impractical exact solve. See [CAN_JUNCTION_TREE_RESULTS.md](CAN_JUNCTION_TREE_RESULTS.md).

This is a concrete bounded-treewidth solution, not just a proposal. It is also scoped: the result is for CAN, on the examined graph shapes, and its refusal behavior matters as much as the low-width success. The separate CAN study found that discrete asynchronous updates converge but may settle at different local minima; sparsity or low treewidth alone does not guarantee a benign optimization landscape. See [CAN_SOLVER_TRACTABILITY_RESULTS.md](CAN_SOLVER_TRACTABILITY_RESULTS.md).

The next inference result should name its input family, maximum width/domain size, exactness or approximation status, refusal behavior, and memory/runtime limits. “Polynomial in frame size” is not sufficient by itself: the exponent depends on width/domain size and the measured cost of constructing or obtaining the decomposition.

**Current status:** exact inference in a bounded-width CAN regime: demonstrated. General graph exact inference: not claimed. Frame-hierarchy factorisation: not demonstrated.

### 3. Rung 1–2: recognition and transform

`runners/dsl/src/shape_decode.rs` contains substantial concrete shape-decoding machinery, including recognition, transform/dimension inference and guarded minting paths. This makes “apply held structure before acquiring more structure” more than a paper-only idea for those paths. It does not establish that all graph recognition is polynomial under the report’s suggested restrictions. Complexity depends on the particular candidate generation and matching algorithm, the representation of a frame, and the bounds placed on patterns and observations.

The planning task should inventory the active recognition/transform paths and classify them by algorithm, input size, worst-case bound, operational cap, and fallback. Then select a narrow supported subset—for example, typed patterns with bounded treewidth and frame size—and make that restriction explicit in code and documentation. Do not infer a general anti-unification bound from a domain-specific recogniser.

**Current status:** domain-specific implementations: present. Engine-wide bounded-pattern guarantee: open.

**Current-source task result (2026-09-28):** the selected 20-way Omniglot run-37A contrastive classifier was replayed under a complete source/data lock. Its global arm exactly matches all 400 locked run-26 baseline predictions (322/400, 80.5%); the top-two difference-region arm returns 256/400 (64.0%), with 16 global errors fixed and 82 correct predictions broken (exact paired p=`7.38e-12`). The preregistered stop is triggered. Exploratory post-hoc strata show degradation in both unmatched (60.3% vs 80.2% global) and matched (69.6% vs 81.0%) branches, so the current evidence does not localize harm to stroke-count mismatch alone. This is a strong negative result for this host-side focused classifier on this task, not evidence against all focused perception or structural induction. It demonstrates why rung 1–2 availability and a structural cue do not guarantee useful task performance. Next design a source-locked mechanism diagnostic without outcome-tuned thresholds, then preregister any revision on an independent held-out panel. See [current-source replay](SUBSTRATE_OMNIGLOT_CONTRASTIVE_CURRENT_V1_RESULTS_20260928.md) and its [independent analysis](../../../evaluation/reports/omniglot_contrastive_current_v1_independent_analysis.json).

**Current matcher cost boundary (source audit, 2026-09-28):** `match_rule_each` calls `extend_each` recursively in the rule's stored `match_edges` order. An unbound edge scans the edge-type index; with one endpoint bound it walks that endpoint's typed adjacency; with both endpoints bound it still enumerates the source's typed neighbors and filters the target. The distinct-node check occurs at a complete binding. A failed `where` condition is normally evaluated at that leaf, except for eligible interpreter-path conjunction pushdown; JIT where-clauses remain leaf-evaluated. Optional `IndexJoinHint` can narrow candidates when its bound key and declared index are available, otherwise matching falls back to the ordinary scan.

For a connected pattern with `q` match edges in an order where each edge after the first touches a bound variable, let `M` be the number of edges of the first edge type and let `Δ` bound typed degree. The current enumeration has a conservative candidate upper bound `O(M·Δ^(q−1))`; leaf distinctness and where evaluation add work proportional to the number of reached partial/full bindings and their predicate cost. This is polynomial in graph size for fixed `q` (and bounded-degree data), but its exponent depends on pattern length. It is **not** a bounded-treewidth guarantee: the engine does not generally reorder joins using query width, disconnected/unbound clauses can trigger additional full edge-type scans, and a low-width query with a poor stored order can still create large intermediate joins. The measured bidirected-tree sweep confirms this cost sensitivity: at N=128 and equal node/edge counts, the 2-edge star rule visits 33,020 candidate pairs versus 1,520 on a path; the 3-edge star visits 48,768 candidates despite having no injective complete match. That is a counterexample to predicting matcher cost from N/E or final match count alone, not evidence about natural-task prevalence ([native matcher source](../../../runners/dsl/src/rewrite.rs), [degree-matched results](SUBSTRATE_FINITE_KEY_DEGREE_MATCHED_RESULTS_20260926.md), [analysis](../../../evaluation/reports/mint_termination_degree_matched_v1_1_analysis_20260926.json)).

The three practical constraints for a polynomial claim therefore need to be explicit: bound pattern size or use a width-aware join order; bound or measure typed-degree/intermediate-join growth; and charge predicate evaluation and fallback scans. A future bounded-pattern result should compare the current authored order with a semantics-preserving width-aware order on matched workloads, include high-degree and disconnected controls, and report complete candidate work and where evaluations. Until then, anti-unification complexity for a particular term/graph representation must not be presented as the engine matcher's complexity.

**Task-derived order evidence (2026-09-27–28):** native panels show that measured order effects depend on rule/task structure. For the four-clause cyclic `discourse_goal_select` rule on the frozen common-ground fixture, all 24 authored permutations preserve the independently checked task output and final graph; the current installed order costs a median 106 candidate tests, versus 36 for the cheapest realized order and 139 for the most expensive (3.86× spread). These results demonstrate order sensitivity on one cyclic and one linear task pattern plus one controlled path pattern; they do not test learned ordering, varied optimal orders across held-out tasks, production frequency, or scaling. See [four-edge common-ground replay](SUBSTRATE_TASK_DERIVED_JOIN_ORDER_V1_RESULTS_20260927.md), [anaphora replay](SUBSTRATE_TASK_DERIVED_ANAPHORA_JOIN_ORDER_V1_RESULTS_20260928.md), [three-edge path replay](SUBSTRATE_TASK_DERIVED_PATH_JOIN_V1_RESULTS_20260927.md), and the [bounded estimator study](SUBSTRATE_BOUNDED_THREE_EDGE_ORDER_V1_RESULTS_20260927.md). ### 4. Rung 3–4: induction and primitive minting

The substrate includes real induction/minting behavior in several domains. Some decode loops defer minting until recognition/transform does not explain an observation, and some mint paths require recurrence, support, or a discriminative residual. These mechanisms can reduce unnecessary induction and make mint events auditable.

They do not bound the cost of induction after it begins. The repository has no general hypothesis-space contract that caps candidate count or depth, nor an architecture-wide report of how often residuals reach rung 3 or mint at rung 4. Thus “rare expensive minting” remains an empirical claim that should be measured per workload and distribution. It must not be treated as a guarantee supplied by Occam ordering.

**Current status:** guarded, domain-specific induction/minting: present. General cost bound and frequency evidence: open. Capability/general-learning result: explicitly out of scope for the restricted tractability results.

### 5. Frame locality, cuts, and scheduling

The engine derives rule read/write sets and contains scheduling and batching machinery (including `area_scheduler.rs` and `wave_stencil.rs`). These can identify some dependencies and enable safe local execution in particular cases. The Stage A reassessment documents the interpretation of read/write overlap as a coupling signal and cautions against imposing synchronous batches without the corresponding coupling. See [STAGE_A_REASSESSED_AGAINST_V23.md](STAGE_A_REASSESSED_AGAINST_V23.md).

This is not yet the report’s full cheap-cut mechanism. A scheduler’s dependency relation is not itself a learned partition of the world into frames, a bound on each separator’s state, or proof that inference and matching factor across those frames. Nor does a low edge count imply small treewidth. The needed evidence is a repeatable measurement on representative tasks: frame counts and sizes, separator dimensions, cross-frame factor/rule dependencies, decomposition width, and the cost of finding and maintaining the hierarchy as the graph changes. The existing CAN dependency keys, runtime ownership projections, visual global-fold dependencies, and CAN inference treewidth use different structural objects and must not be pooled. A common field/metric contract and crosswalk is now recorded in [the frame-locality measurement contract](SUBSTRATE_FRAME_LOCALITY_MEASUREMENT_CONTRACT_V1_20260928.md).

**Current status:** dependency/coupling infrastructure and several separately measured structural projections: present. Complete typed-edge node cuts have been measured in three live task families, and connected-frame controls exist. Task-relevant variable interfaces, complete operation-dependency coverage, partition discovery without ownership hints, and a certified bounded-boundary hierarchy remain open.

### 6. Runtime and commodity-hardware claims

The flight-control report explicitly distinguishes the simulator’s 240 Hz physics cadence from a substrate compute limit. It is evidence that a particular loop integrated with that simulator at that cadence; it is not evidence of a 240 Hz general-purpose cognition guarantee. A source-locked storage panel builds 10⁷ nodes and just under 10⁷ typed edges in 15.66 seconds at 2.15 GB incremental RSS on one host. A separate attributed-ring panel settles one monotone transition rule at 10M nodes in 8.85 CPU seconds audit-off; its 20M candidate tests and 10M writes are exact for this workload. Peak process RSS is 6.13 GB and includes the N-entry terminal-oracle readback. These are useful single-host engine measurements, not representative full-workload scaling. Claims about general 10⁷–10⁸ behavior still need interacting rules, topology variation, realistic graph/rule density, mutation rate, cache/memory profile, and inference work.

The comparison with LLMs is therefore currently qualitative. To support “orders of magnitude below a forward pass,” define the task and output quality, include acquisition/induction cost and amortisation, and compare matched workloads and deployment assumptions. Held-structure runtime and learning cost must be reported separately.

**Current status:** workload-specific real-time integration: reported. General throughput/scaling and matched LLM cost comparison: open.

### 7. Episodic query matcher attribution

A pre-locked paired audit measured one exact episodic retrieval tick on fresh checkpoint readers at 6, 24, 78 and 192 Episodes, with three replicates per size. The current uninstrumented median rises from 0.488 ms at N=6 to 12.003 ms at N=192. Native per-rule attribution isolates `episodic_mark_overlapping_cues_for_conservative_completion`: its candidate tests are exactly `2*N*(N+1)` per query on this workload, and at N=192 it accounts for 74,112 of 75,278 candidate tests. The all-unique cue workload contains no actual collisions, so the work is spent establishing that absence through pairwise comparison. Exact answers and provenance match between instrumented and control readers. Per-rule instrumentation adds measurable overhead (2.87× median at N=192), so timing claims use the uninstrumented arm.

This is a concrete negative tractability result for one authored memory rule, not a counterexample to every structural-locality argument. It shows that locality must be reflected in the actual matching plan, not only in the task data: the current rule enumerates across the full Episode set and does not narrow work to the requested cue or a changed frame. An incremental or cue-indexed variant should be evaluated against the existing collision semantics before any performance claim. ## Planning backlog

The following work items are deliberately phrased as planning inputs rather than a prescribed implementation sequence.

### T1. Build a substrate tractability inventory

**Work:** enumerate the rule runner, inference engines, matching/transform paths, induction/mint paths, frame abstractions, and schedulers actually used in representative workloads. Record algorithm, input measure, known restriction, operational guard, and current evidence for each.

**Acceptance evidence:** a reproducible inventory linking each claim to source, tests/results, and explicit “unknown” entries; distinguish default paths from opt-in or experimental paths.

### T2. Operationalise cheap-cut/frame locality

**Work:** use the [frame-locality measurement contract](SUBSTRATE_FRAME_LOCALITY_MEASUREMENT_CONTRACT_V1_20260928.md) to define object-specific frames, boundary interfaces, cut cost, and hierarchy for at least two representative workloads. Measure whether a low-cost cut yields bounded task-relevant interfaces and preserves cross-frame dependencies needed for correctness. Determine whether cuts are authored, inferred, or both.

**Acceptance evidence:** preregistered workload/sample selection; declared structural graph and partition provenance; cut/decomposition algorithm and cost; frame size, incidence, distinct-boundary-unit, interface-domain and width distributions; failure cases; matched high-boundary controls; correctness comparison against monolithic execution. Do not pool unlike graph objects or call the hypothesis validated from selected small fixtures.

### T3. Specify closure termination for a restricted rule subset

**Work:** formalise fresh-name-producing effects and choose a restriction such as weak acyclicity/stratification for the subset to be covered. Decide how unsupported rules are rejected, flagged, or run under a resource budget.

**Acceptance evidence:** formal statement with assumptions; analyzer or construction rule; tests for accepted and rejected examples; termination and round/size bound stated for that subset only.

### T4. Extend and delimit exact inference

**Work:** establish supported graph families and limits for the CAN junction-tree path, then decide whether shared decomposition infrastructure benefits other inference subsystems. Include factor arity, domain size, width, decomposition cost, memory, and refusal thresholds.

**Acceptance evidence:** exact answer checked against exhaustive ground truth on feasible cases; explicit refusal on over-limit cases; benchmark scaling across width and node count; no silent approximation.

### T5. Bound a rung 1–2 subset

**Work:** select frequently used recognition/transform paths and give each a declared supported pattern/frame envelope. Optimize or refuse outside that envelope rather than assuming all matching is bounded.

**Acceptance evidence:** algorithmic cost statement under named restrictions, worst-case and typical benchmark, and correctness tests for typed/bounded-width cases plus adversarial cases.

### T6. Measure induction as a separate cost center

**Work:** instrument residuals, rung transitions, candidate counts, search depth, CPU time, memory, mint counts, and post-mint reuse. Evaluate on a preregistered distribution that includes ordinary, novel, and adversarial inputs.

**Acceptance evidence:** distribution-level rates and tail costs; cost of held-structure operation separated from induction; explicit unresolved cases where the search exceeds budget. This measures “rare”; it does not prove general induction tractability.

### T7. Establish a credible CPU scaling envelope

**Work:** benchmark end-to-end fixed-point operation on controlled graph sizes and shapes, varying rule density, locality, mutation, and cache behavior. Revisit the 240 Hz and node-scale statements as separate experiments.

**Acceptance evidence:** reproducible hardware/software profile, workload definition, latency/throughput/memory curves, and attribution of time to matching, mutation, inference, and allocation. A scale estimate should not be labelled measured until run.

### T8. Make the LLM comparison workload-matched

**Work:** choose concrete tasks where both systems can be evaluated; report answer quality, latency, energy/cost, adaptation data and compute, and amortisation horizon. Include the full induction/acquisition expense for the substrate.

**Acceptance evidence:** documented matched protocol and cost accounting. Avoid claiming a general cost advantage from held-structure microbenchmarks or unmatched model-forward-pass figures.

## Suggested order for a follow-on plan

T1 is prerequisite bookkeeping. T2 and T3 address the central structural and closure assumptions. T4 and T5 convert existing local mechanisms into scoped engine contracts. T6 tests the claimed economic profile of rare induction. T7 and T8 should use the workload definitions and instrumentation established by earlier work.

This order is provisional: the plan should choose workloads and owners, dependencies, resource limits, and stop conditions. In particular, if T2 finds that representative tasks do not exhibit small separators, the next plan should revise the tractability claim and investigate alternative structural parameters rather than continue to assume cheap-cut locality.
