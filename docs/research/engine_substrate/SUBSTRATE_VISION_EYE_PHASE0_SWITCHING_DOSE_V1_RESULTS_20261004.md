# Phase-0 visual switching-dose v1 results

The source-locked capture passed all schedule and runtime gates for 15 workers: five switching intervals crossed with three initialization seeds, exact 4,000 exposures per location, balanced starting locations, active-count and progress checks, and nonempty Argmax cache occupancy. Independent scoring recomputed receptive-field centers and RMS spread from every captured 256-weight field.

Both locations met the preregistered localization criterion in 1/3 workers at 1-step blocks and 1/3 at 10-step blocks. At 100-, 1,000-, and 4,000-step blocks, all three workers per dose localized both locations. The two failures at the fastest intervals (seeds 1 and 2) ended with best spread near 5.33 cells and centers displaced several cells from each target. The result suggests a switching-interval boundary between 10 and 100 steps for this small authored setup; three seeds per dose make the observed threshold provisional.

Input-transition work tracks schedule density: median switches per worker are 7,999 / 799 / 79 / 7 / 1, with 399,975 / 39,975 / 3,975 / 375 / 75 fact writes at block lengths 1 / 10 / 100 / 1,000 / 4,000. Median input update time falls from 0.119 ms/step at block 1 to roughly 0.003–0.004 ms/step at blocks 100 and above. Median closure time stays near 6.7–7.0 ms/step across doses. This separates host input-update cost from rule-closure time, but the learning contrast still co-varies with transition count; it cannot identify whether failures arise from forgetting, transient sensory dynamics, or their interaction. A follow-up should hold the delivered feature sequence fixed while varying the learner update cadence, or compare against a transition-matched nonlearning control.

The claim is limited to two fixed locations, four WUnits, one 16×16 visual family, and three seeds. It does not establish broad memory capacity, natural-image learning, or autonomous structural induction.
