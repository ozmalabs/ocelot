# Phase-0 four-site schedule replication v1 results

The preregistered six-seed replication passed all capture and independent-analysis gates. Twelve source-locked workers crossed seeds 18–23 with blocked and one-step-interleaved schedules; each trained four 3×3 sites for exactly 4,000 exposures/site, then received 48 frozen probes. The analyzer independently reconstructed all 576 activation vectors and winners from the captured fields, confirmed exact frozen-weight parity, and verified the source lock.

**Blocked schedule.** All six workers localized all four trained sites: 72/72 trained probes and 24/24 distinct worker-site slots. Held-out exact localization remained 0/216; nearest-trained-region correctness was 69/216. Median closure was 6.82 ms/step and median training input writes were 63 per worker.

**Interleaved schedule.** Trained-site localization was 39/72 probes. Per-seed distinct-site coverage was 4, 4, 2, 1, 0, and 2 of four; thus full four-site acquisition occurred in two of six workers, with partial acquisition in three and none in one. Held-out exact localization was again 0/216, with nearest-region correctness 69/216. Median closure was 6.70 ms/step and median input writes were 287,991 per worker.

This strengthens the evidence that blocked presentation reliably acquires four site-specific fields in this authored learner and schedule, while rapid interleaving often does not. The failure of held-out probes under both schedules shows the acquired representation does not interpolate to the tested untrained positions. Closure costs are nearly equal; the large input-write difference is an expected consequence of schedule transitions, not a measured closure slowdown. These results do not establish general visual capacity, natural-task utility, or a universal schedule advantage.
