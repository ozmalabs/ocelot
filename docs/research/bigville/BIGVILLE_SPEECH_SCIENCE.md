# BIGVILLE — Emergent Speech: Science Adjudication

**Purpose.** Ground a graph-native "emergent speech" faculty for bigville:
residents should produce utterances as an EMERGENT ACTION (driven by affect,
relationship, norms, goals, encounter) rather than firing on a fixed schedule.
This document adjudicates how utterance INITIATION and KIND-SELECTION actually
work biologically and psychologically, from the real literature, so the design
is grounded rather than naive. **It is adjudication + design only — no code.**

**Method.** Every claim below is grounded in a real, cited source (author,
year, finding), located via live web search. Where the literature is settled I
say so; where it is contested or where I am extrapolating I flag it explicitly.
Sources are listed at the end. I did NOT rely on memory for the empirical
numbers — the Dunbar fraction, the Rimé sharing rates, the Mehl word counts,
and the neuro-locus claims were each checked against sources.

## 1. What the science says (grounded synthesis of areas 1–7)

### 1.1 Speech-production architecture — where an intention to speak is formed (SETTLED, with a contested monitor)

Levelt's model (Levelt 1989, *Speaking: From Intention to Articulation*; Levelt,
Roelofs & Meyer 1999, the WEAVER++ model in *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*)
decomposes production into three feed-forward stages plus a monitor:

- **Conceptualizer** → generates a **preverbal message** (the "communicative
  intention"). This is split into *macroplanning* — deciding **what to say**,
  selecting the **speech-act intention**, and ordering the information — and
  *microplanning* (propositional shape, perspective, information structure).
  The conceptualizer draws on world knowledge and the **model of the current
  communicative situation**.
- **Formulator** → grammatical + phonological encoding (lemma access, then
  wordform), turning the message into a phonetic/articulatory plan.
- **Articulator** → motor execution of the plan.
- **Self-monitoring loop** → the speaker parses their own inner/overt speech and
  can halt/repair. (The *mechanism* of monitoring — perceptual-loop vs.
  production-internal — is genuinely CONTESTED; not load-bearing here.)

**Adjudication for our question.** The **decision to speak, to whom, and what
KIND of act** is a *conceptualizer / macroplanning* event — it lives at the
message level, upstream of any words. Levelt's own subtitle, "From Intention to
Articulation," names the ordering: the *intention* is the correct locus for
"the decision to speak." Everything downstream (lemmas, prosody, articulation)
is *how*, not *whether/what*. This is settled and directly usable: **the faculty
should decide at the intention layer, and only the intention layer.** (The
substrate already encodes this decomposition as concepts — see §5.)

### 1.2 Speech-act theory & pragmatics — how the KIND is selected (SETTLED taxonomy; the *selection rule* is theory-underdetermined)

Austin (1962, *How to Do Things with Words*) established that utterances *do
things* (locution / **illocution** / perlocution). Searle (1969, *Speech Acts*;
1976, "A classification of illocutionary acts") gives the standard five-way
illocutionary taxonomy, which is the settled backbone:

| Kind | Does | Direction of fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Assertive** | commits speaker to truth (state, report, claim) | word→world |
| **Directive** | tries to get hearer to act (request, order, ask) | world→word |
| **Commissive** | commits speaker to future act (promise, offer, threaten) | world→word |
| **Expressive** | expresses a psychological state (thank, apologise, greet, condole) | none |
| **Declarative** | changes the world by saying so (pronounce, name, resign) | both |

Grice (1975, "Logic and Conversation") adds the **Cooperative Principle** and
its four maxims (Quantity, Quality, Relation, Manner) — a normative frame for
how much/what to say and how, and the engine of *implicature* (meaning conveyed
by flouting a maxim).

**Adjudication.** The taxonomy tells you the *menu* of kinds; it does NOT by
itself tell you *which* kind fires when — that is fixed by the **situation +
the speaker's goal + the adjacency context** (§1.3), not by the taxonomy. A
greeting is an *expressive*; answering a question is an *assertive/directive*
determined by the first pair-part; a request born of a need is a *directive*.
So KIND is **derived** from (goal × prior-turn × relationship), and Grice
governs *quantity/manner* (how much, how bluntly) rather than *whether*.

### 1.3 Conversation analysis — turn-taking, adjacency pairs, and the normativity of NOT speaking (SETTLED, and the single most important input)

Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson (1974, "A simplest systematics for the
organization of turn-taking for conversation," *Language*) established the
turn-taking system (one-at-a-time, transition-relevance places, allocation).
Schegloff & Sacks (1973, "Opening up closings") and Schegloff (1968,
"Sequencing in conversational openings") give the two facts most load-bearing
for us:

- **Adjacency pairs**: a *first pair-part* (FPP) makes a *second pair-part*
  (SPP) of a specific type **conditionally relevant** — greeting→greeting,
  question→answer, summons→answer, request→grant/refuse, offer→accept/decline.
- **Conditional relevance ⇒ non-response is itself an ACT.** If the SPP does not
  occur, it is not merely missing — it is **"officially / noticeably absent"**
  (Schegloff 1968), an accountable event the other party *notices and infers
  from* (a snub, a slight, deafness, hostility). **Silence is a move.** This is
  the deepest correction to a naive "speech is an optional output" view.
- **Preference organization** (Pomerantz 1984; Schegloff 2007): the two SPP
  options are not symmetric — *preferred* responses (acceptances, agreements)
  come fast and unmarked; *dispreferred* ones (refusals, disagreements) are
  delayed, hedged, accounted-for. Response *shape* is structured, not free.
- **Recipient design**: speakers design turns *for this particular recipient*
  (what they know, their relationship) — Sacks & Schegloff. Talk is addressee-
  relative, not broadcast.

**Adjudication.** This is the layer a scheduled/forced speech faculty is missing
most. Two consequences bind the design: (a) an open FPP addressed to a resident
creates a *standing obligation* to produce the matching SPP — the strongest,
most norm-driven trigger to speak; (b) **choosing NOT to respond is a
first-class, meaningful output** that must itself be representable and legible to
others, not the mere absence of a scheduled event.

### 1.4 Emotion & speech — affect gates WHETHER, and shapes WHAT/HOW (WHETHER: well-supported via social-sharing; HOW: settled)

Two distinct, well-attested channels:

- **Emotion drives INITIATION (social sharing).** Rimé and colleagues (Rimé et
  al. 1991; Rimé 1998, 2009, *Emotion Review*) document the **social sharing of
  emotion**: after an emotional event people feel a strong pull to *talk about
  it*. Empirically **88–96% of emotional episodes are socially shared**, usually
  soon after (≈60% the same day), and — critically — **the amount of sharing
  rises with the intensity of the emotion**. This is direct evidence that
  affect *initiates* talk, not merely colours it. (SETTLED that sharing is
  near-universal and intensity-scaled; the *mechanism* — coping, meaning-making,
  bonding — is debated but not load-bearing here.)
- **Emotion shapes WHAT/HOW (prosody, content, disfluency).** Arousal reliably
  raises speech rate and amplitude and reshapes pitch contour/rhythm (emotional
  prosody; Scherer 2003; Bänziger & Scherer 2005). Valence and arousal are
  partly separable in the acoustic signal and in listener processing (Paulmann &
  Kotz; the P200 arousal effect). Verbalising an emotion *lowers* arousal vs.
  stating a fact (Mauss/Gross-adjacent work), i.e. talking is partly
  arousal-regulatory. (SETTLED.)

**Adjudication.** There is genuine evidence emotion **initiates** utterances
(social sharing), so "affect drives *whether*, not just *how*" is defensible —
but the strong evidence is for *emotionally salient events prompting sharing to
confidants*, NOT for a blanket "high arousal → more talk to anyone." Arousal's
robust effect is on *delivery* (rate/loudness) and on *lowered thresholds*
(irritability → a snappier directive), not on a simple monotone "aroused people
talk more." So: **affect gates initiation for emotionally salient content and to
appropriate recipients; affect gates loudness/rate/register robustly; affect
lowers the threshold for certain KINDS (e.g., hostile directives).**

### 1.5 Social motivation — why humans talk at all (bonding/gossip SETTLED-ish; exact fractions CONTESTED)

- **Gossip / social-bonding hypothesis** (Dunbar 1996, *Grooming, Gossip and the
  Evolution of Language*; Dunbar, Duncan & Marriott 1997, "Human conversational
  behaviour," *Human Nature*; Dunbar 2004, *Review of General Psychology*):
  language may have evolved as a cheap substitute for social grooming; in freely
  forming conversations **~two-thirds of talk is about social topics** ("gossip"
  in the broad sense — the doings, relationships, and standing of present and
  absent third parties), roughly equal across sexes. A recent revisit ("How much
  conversation content is actually social," *Language and Cognition*, Cambridge
  Core) argues the social fraction is **higher still (~85%)** under a broader
  coding of "social." (The *two-thirds* figure is the citable classic; the exact
  fraction is CONTESTED — small original N=45, coding-dependent — but the
  qualitative claim "most talk is social/relational, not instrumental" is
  robust.)
- **The functions of talk** are plural: **affiliation/bonding**, **status /
  reputation management** (gossip polices free-riders), **information exchange**,
  and **emotional sharing** (§1.4). Talk is overwhelmingly *relational*.
- **Politeness / face** (Brown & Levinson 1987, *Politeness*): every
  face-threatening act (a request, a criticism, a refusal) carries a weight
  **Wx ≈ D(S,H) + P(H,S) + Rx** — social **Distance** + relative **Power** +
  **Rank/imposition** of the act — and the *heavier* the weight the more the
  speaker uses indirectness, redress, or **declines to perform the act at all**.
  (The three-variable structure is settled and widely used; its universality and
  additivity are CONTESTED cross-culturally.)
- **Individual differences** (Mehl et al. 2007, *Science*, ~16k words/day, no
  reliable sex difference; a large 22-sample replication, Mehl et al. 2024,
  ~11.9k men / 13.3k women with **enormous** individual range <100 to >120,000):
  talkativeness varies by ~2–3 orders of magnitude across people; extraversion
  predicts *conversational* word count but context-dependently. **Baseline
  loquacity is a per-agent trait, not a constant.**

**Adjudication.** Most talk is social/relational and *reputationally* loaded;
whether one performs a face-threatening act is governed by relationship (D, P)
and the act's imposition (R); and how much one talks at all is a stable
individual trait. All three are directly parameterisable.

### 1.6 Neurobiology — be honest: there is NO "decision-to-speak center" (SETTLED where stated; the framing is where overclaiming happens)

- **Broca's / Wernicke's areas are articulation-planning / comprehension**, not
  the *decision* to speak. Do not locate "the will to talk" there.
- **Volitional initiation** of vocalisation engages **mediofrontal cortex —
  supplementary motor area (SMA) and anterior cingulate cortex (ACC)** (Jürgens
  2002, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, on primate vocal pathways;
  recent intracranial-EEG work on SMA in speech initiation, e.g. 2024).
  SMA/pre-SMA + ACC handle *motor initiation and volitional gating* of an
  utterance — the "go" — but this is the initiation of a *motor act*, general to
  voluntary movement, NOT a semantic "decide to converse" module.
- **There is no single neural locus for "the urge to communicate."** The drive
  to talk is distributed (limbic/affective input, prefrontal goal
  representation, ACC/SMA gating). Claiming a "speech-decision center" would be
  an overclaim.

**Adjudication.** The neurobiology *supports the layering* (an affective/goal
"want" → a mediofrontal "go/no-go gate" → motor execution) but *refuses* a tidy
single decision node. Our design should mirror the layering (a value/threshold
gate on initiation) and must NOT be described as modelling a "speech center."

### 1.7 Proximity/encounter → talk — people do NOT reliably talk on encounter (SETTLED; the key caveat against a naive rule)

- **Greetings are access rituals** (Goffman 1971, *Relations in Public*;
  Goffman 1967, *Interaction Ritual*). Opening an encounter with a known/ratified
  other is a ritual obligation: a greeting *obliges* a greeting (an adjacency
  pair, §1.3), and openings use "opening moves" / "clearance signs" (glances,
  nods) to establish mutual access. Passing acquaintances exchange a nod/smile —
  a *presentation ritual*.
- **BUT the default among strangers in public is CIVIL INATTENTION** (Goffman
  1963, *Behavior in Public Places*): co-present strangers deliberately grant
  each other a brief acknowledging glance and then *withhold* further attention —
  a *positive* ritual of non-engagement, not a failure to interact. Most
  co-presence does **not** yield talk.

**Adjudication.** Encounter is a *necessary occasion* for talk but not a
sufficient cause. Whether an encounter opens into talk depends on
**ratification/relationship** (known other, standing to speak), **an occasion**
(a goal, an emotional prompt, a pending FPP), and **norm** (civil inattention is
the strangers' default). A rule that makes residents talk *whenever* they are
co-present would be biologically and sociologically wrong — the caveat is
first-order, not a footnote.

## 2. The load-bearing model for "the decision to speak"

Synthesising §1, an utterance is the *product of a communicative INTENTION
formed at the conceptualizer* (§1.1), and initiation is a **gated value
decision**, not a schedule. Precisely:

**An utterance is minted when a resident forms a communicative intention whose
initiation value clears a threshold. The intention is a function of five inputs,
which jointly also select the KIND; delivery (loudness/register) is a separate,
affect-driven gate.**

```
INITIATE(speaker, target?) fires when
  init_value  =  occasion            # an encounter (§1.7) OR a pending FPP
                                      # addressed to me (§1.3, strongest) OR an
                                      # emotionally-salient event to share (§1.4)
               × standing/relationship  # ratified to speak to target? D, P (§1.5,§1.7)
               × goal_pressure        # an active need/goal this act would serve (§1.1)
               × affect_gain          # arousal/mood raises or lowers the threshold (§1.4)
               − face_cost            # Wx = D + P + R for the act's imposition (§1.5)
               − civil_inattention    # default suppressor among non-ratified others (§1.7)
     >  speaker.loquacity_threshold   # per-agent trait baseline (§1.5, Mehl)

KIND  = f(prior-turn FPP | goal | affect)     # adjacency first (§1.3), then goal/affect (§1.2)
LOUDNESS/REGISTER = g(arousal, target distance, face) # delivery gate (§1.4)
```

**What DRIVES initiation, in priority order (this ordering is the adjudication):**

1. **A conditionally-relevant obligation** — an unanswered FPP addressed to me
   (greeting, question, summons, request). This is the *strongest, most
   norm-compelled* trigger; producing the SPP is near-obligatory and *withholding
   it is itself a legible act* (§1.3). Speech here is *responsive*, not
   spontaneous.
2. **An emotionally-salient event to share** to a *confidant-class* relationship,
   scaled by intensity (§1.4 Rimé).
3. **A goal/need the utterance would serve** — a request (directive) born of a
   need, a status/reputation move (gossip), an information query (§1.1, §1.5).
4. **A ratified encounter opening** — meeting a *known* other creates the
   *occasion* for a greeting access-ritual (§1.7), gated by relationship, NOT an
   automatic firing.

**What SUPPRESSES initiation:** civil inattention toward non-ratified others
(§1.7); a high face-cost act toward a high-D/high-P target (§1.5) — the resident
may *decline to speak*; low arousal / low goal pressure below the per-agent
loquacity threshold. **The model must be able to decide NOT to speak, and that
non-decision must be meaningful when an obligation existed.**

This is *emergent* precisely because `init_value` is computed from the resident's
live affect, relationships, goals, and the current interactional state — not from
a clock. Two residents in the same tick can differ (different affect, different
relationship, different pending FPPs, different loquacity), and the *same*
resident talks or stays silent depending on state.

## 3. Design implications for the faculty (design ONLY — do not implement)

The substrate already has the pieces (§5). The faculty should **compose** them,
not rebuild them. What follows is what the DECIDING rule should read and how the
structure must be shaped.

### 3.1 The initiation rule reads (all five inputs)

An `init_utterance`-style rule (in the exact family as the existing
`af_choose_action` affect-Argmax and `nd_choose_good`) should compute an
initiation value over a menu that INCLUDES a "speak/stay-silent" action, reading:

- **Affect** — the resident's `AffectiveState` (mood/arousal/distress/hostility)
  as a `affect_gain × eff_distress`-style term (mirrors `af_choose_action`).
  High arousal → higher init_value + louder delivery + threshold-lowered KINDS.
- **Relationship** — the directed edge to the target (affinity/closeness = D,
  standing/rank = P). Governs *whether ratified to open*, *whether to share*
  (confidant class), and the *face cost* of the act.
- **Norms / adjacency** — a *pending FPP addressed to me* is the highest-weight
  term. This should be a graph fact the rule matches on (an open first pair-part
  with `expects_response` to me), so responding is near-obligatory and its
  absence is representable.
- **Goals** — an active `Goal`/need the act would serve (request, query,
  status move). Ties initiation to the resident's live goal graph.
- **Occasion** — co-presence/encounter (there is already an earshot/sight model
  via `lo_observe_act`), used as the *occasion* term, gated by relationship —
  never as an automatic trigger.

### 3.2 What makes it EMERGENT, not forced

- **No clock term.** Nothing in `init_value` reads a tick counter or a schedule.
  Firing is entirely a function of state (affect × relationship × norm × goal ×
  occasion). This is the whole point of the reframe.
- **A per-agent loquacity trait** as the threshold (Mehl: 2–3 orders of magnitude
  of individual variation) — so a taciturn resident and a garrulous one diverge
  from the *same* situation. Seed it as a distribution over residents (consistent
  with "population = distribution over seeds").
- **The KIND is derived, not fixed.** Adjacency-first: if a pending FPP exists,
  KIND is the conditionally-relevant SPP (question→answer). Else KIND comes from
  the goal (need→directive) or affect (grief→share/expressive; hostility→snap/
  directive). Reuse `speech_act_classify`'s kind vocabulary.

### 3.3 The normative structure (non-response is a move)

- **A greeting obliges a greeting; a question obliges an answer.** Model an open
  FPP as a graph fact carrying conditional relevance to a specific target; the
  target's initiation rule gets a strong term from it.
- **Non-response must be representable and legible.** When an obligation exists
  and the resident's init_value stays below threshold (or a face-cost/hostility
  term suppresses it), the *silence itself* should mint a legible fact (a
  "noticeable absence" / snub) that OTHER residents' ToM and affect rules can
  read (Schegloff's official absence; §1.3). Do NOT model non-response as the
  mere non-firing of a rule — that loses the social meaning.
- **Preference organisation** (optional, higher-fidelity): dispreferred SPPs
  (refusals) carry delay/redress; a fast bare "no" reads as hostile. Can be a
  later refinement, but the hook (dispreferred = marked) should exist.

### 3.4 How emotion gates initiation, KIND, and loudness (three distinct effects)

- **Initiation:** emotionally-salient events raise init_value *toward confidant-
  class targets*, scaled by intensity (Rimé). Not a blanket "aroused → talk to
  anyone."
- **KIND:** lowered thresholds under high negative arousal shift the KIND toward
  hostile directives / snaps (this already exists as the grief→snap affinity in
  `af_choose_action`; speech is the verbal sibling of that action menu).
- **Loudness/register:** arousal → louder/faster delivery. This maps directly
  onto the existing `lo_choose_loudness` (loudness = the Chebyshev hearing
  radius) — arousal should be a term in the chosen loudness.

### 3.5 Trade-as-social-act

Economic exchange is *also* an occasion for talk, not a silent transfer. A trade
event is an encounter with a *ratified* counterpart and a *shared goal*, so it
should raise init_value (greeting/negotiation/expressive — "pleasure doing
business"). Fits the model as a high-occasion, high-ratification, goal-present
case: exactly the configuration that reliably opens talk. Reuse the existing
`bigville_econ_exchange` event as the *occasion* the initiation rule reads.

### 3.6 The civil-inattention caveat (the rule must sometimes stay silent)

**People do not talk on every encounter.** The default among non-ratified
co-present others is civil inattention (Goffman 1963). The initiation rule MUST
include a suppressor term for non-ratified encounters, so that co-presence with a
stranger/low-affinity resident yields *acknowledgement at most* (a glance/nod),
not talk. A faculty that fires an utterance on every proximity event is wrong.
The falsifier for "emergent" is precisely that some encounters produce silence
and some produce talk *from the same rule*, depending on relationship/affect/goal.

## 4. Honest cautions — where naive designs go wrong

- **"Emotion → template" is too shallow.** Affect does not map to a canned
  sentence. Affect gates *whether* (via salience/sharing), *which KIND* (via
  lowered thresholds), and *how* (loudness/register). The message itself is a
  conceptualizer product built from the goal + recipient + prior turn — not a
  lookup keyed on mood. Building "grief → emit 'I am sad'" would be the naive
  error the literature warns against.
- **Do not claim a neural "speech-decision center."** §1.6: SMA/ACC gate *motor
  initiation* generally; Broca/Wernicke are articulation/comprehension. The drive
  to talk is distributed. Describe the faculty as an affect/goal/norm *value gate
  on initiation*, never as modelling a brain "talk center."
- **Do not overstate "arousal → more talk."** The strong, citable evidence is
  (a) social sharing of *emotionally salient* events to *confidants* (Rimé), and
  (b) arousal → delivery changes. A blanket "high-arousal residents talk more to
  everyone" is NOT well supported; encode the *targeted* and *delivery* effects,
  and treat generic loquacity as a *trait* (Mehl), not an arousal readout.
- **Do not make co-presence sufficient.** §1.7 civil inattention. Encounter is an
  occasion, gated by relationship/norm.
- **The Dunbar fraction is contested in exact value.** Cite "~two-thirds social"
  as the classic and note the ~85% revisit; do NOT report a single precise number
  as settled. The robust claim is *qualitative*: most talk is social/relational.
- **Non-response is not "no output."** Modelling silence as a mere non-firing
  discards its social meaning (snub/slight/hostility). When an obligation exists,
  silence must be a legible act.
- **Speech acts don't select themselves.** The taxonomy is a menu; KIND selection
  comes from adjacency + goal + affect. Don't hard-wire a kind per emotion.
- **Preference/culture universality is contested.** Brown & Levinson's D+P+R and
  its cross-cultural universality are debated; use the three variables as a
  *weighting*, not a law.

whose value reads (`AffectiveState`) × (relationship edge) × (pending FPP /

science says is missing when speech is merely scheduled. Same falsifier discipline
as `af_choose_action`: an `affect_gain`/`norm_gain`-style coupling knob that,
zeroed, collapses the decision back to a schedule — proving the affect/norm layer
is doing the work.

# ADDENDUM — Relationship as HELD FEELING + BELIEF (not a bare edge)

**Why this addendum.** The build also refactors the RELATIONSHIP
REPRESENTATION. Today a relationship is a bare world-level label
(`A ─hates→ B`). That is the wrong primitive: emotion-driven speech (§2–3
above) reads *the speaker's feeling TOWARD this interlocutor*, and a flat label
cannot carry a *subjective, asymmetric, remembered, updatable* feeling. This
section grounds, from the literature, how humans actually represent
relationships and feelings about specific others, so the refactor is principled.
Same discipline: real citations, consensus vs contested flagged, no fabrication.

## A1. How humans mentally REPRESENT relationships (SETTLED that they are held, updatable, dual self+other structures)

- **Relational schemas** (Baldwin 1992, "Relational schemas and the processing
  of social information," *Psychological Bulletin* 112:461–484). A relationship
  is stored as a **cognitive structure with three parts**: a *schema of the
  other*, a *schema of the self-in-that-relationship*, and an **interpersonal
  script** — an *if-then* expectation ("if I get angry, my partner rejects me").
  Crucially, schemas are **relationship-SPECIFIC**: people hold *different*
  representations for different partners, not one global setting. This is
  precisely "a held, updatable model of the other + a script for interaction."
- **Internal working models / attachment representations** (Bowlby 1969/1982;
  Bartholomew & Horowitz 1991, *JPSP*). Attachment is carried as **internal
  working models** built along **two dimensions — model of SELF (worthy of care
  or not) × model of OTHER (trustworthy/available or not)** — yielding the
  four-way secure / preoccupied / dismissing / fearful typology. The load-bearing
  point for us: a relationship representation is fundamentally a **model-of-other
  paired with a model-of-self-toward-them**, learned from experience.
- **General vs relationship-specific** (CONTESTED balance, both real). People
  hold BOTH a *generalized* working model (a disposition/prior) AND
  *relationship-specific* models that can diverge from it (Baldwin; Collins &
  Read; Overall et al.). The generalized model is a **prior**; specific
  relationships are *elaborations updated by that partner's behaviour*. (What is
  contested is the relative weight and how they interact — not that both exist.)

**Adjudication.** The literature strongly supports modelling a relationship as a
**held, per-agent, per-target representation** = *(a model of the other) + (one's
affect/stance toward them) + (learned interaction expectations)*, sitting on top
of a *generalized prior*. A bare world-level label captures none of this.

## A2. The structure of interpersonal AFFECT — many specific relational emotions, not one valence (SETTLED that emotions are discrete & agency-attributed; dimensional-vs-discrete is the classic open debate)

- **Appraisal theory** (Lazarus 1991, *Emotion and Adaptation*; Scherer 2001,
  component-process model). Emotions arise from *appraisals* of an event against
  goals; Lazarus's **core relational themes** make each emotion a specific
  *person–environment relationship* (anger = "a demeaning offence against me and
  mine"; gratitude = "appreciation of an altruistic gift"). **Agency /
  who-caused-this is the dimension that separates anger from guilt from
  gratitude** — i.e., emotions toward a person are individuated by *attributed
  responsibility*, not by valence alone.
- **Specific relational emotions.** Trust, resentment, gratitude, affection,
  contempt, admiration, fear, jealousy are *distinct states with distinct
  action tendencies* (contempt → withdrawal/derogation; gratitude → reciprocate;
  anger → confront). Reducing them to a single like/dislike scalar discards the
  action tendencies that drive *what kind of utterance* fires (§1.2/§3.4).
- **One axis or many? (CONTESTED — the core affective-science debate).**
  Dimensional models (Russell's valence×arousal circumplex) vs discrete/basic
  emotions (Ekman; appraisal-based discrete emotions). Consensus middle ground:
  a low-dimensional *core affect* (valence, arousal) underlies experience, but
  *interpersonal* emotions are individuated by appraisal (agency, fairness,
  certainty). **So: model affect as BOTH a low-D core AND a set of specific
  relational emotions with action tendencies — do not collapse to one axis.**

**Adjudication.** Liking/disliking is NOT one axis. The relational-affect slot
should carry the *specific* emotion(s) toward the target (with action tendency),
grounded in *who did what to whom* — which is exactly what appraisal theory says
generates them.

## A3. How feelings toward another UPDATE from experience (the update ASYMMETRY is well-supported; some pop-numbers are soft)

- **Reciprocity / equity as the update engine.** The norm of reciprocity
  (Gouldner 1960, *American Sociological Review*) and equity theory (Walster,
  Walster & Berscheid 1978) say relational affect updates toward those who
  benefit us fairly and away from those who exploit us. Benefit → gratitude/
  liking; imbalance/exploitation → resentment.
- **Kin altruism is a PRIOR, not a fixed bond** (Hamilton 1964, inclusive
  fitness, *rb > c*). Relatedness *biases* baseline altruism/affection upward —
  but it is a **weighting on the prior**, not a guarantee; kin relationships
  still update from behaviour (siblings feud, estrange). Model kin as an
  *elevated starting affinity the agent then elaborates*, never a locked label.
- **Familiarity / mere exposure** (Zajonc 1968, *JPSP* monograph). Repeated
  encounter *by itself* raises liking (robust, well-replicated; strongest on
  early exposures). So co-presence history is a mild positive prior on affinity
  — relevant because bigville residents accrue encounters.
- **Positivity/negativity ASYMMETRY (SETTLED direction; exact ratios SOFT).**
  Bad is stronger than good (Baumeister, Bratslavsky, Finkenauer & Vohs 2001,
  "Bad is stronger than good," *Review of General Psychology*; Rozin & Royzman
  2001, negativity bias). A betrayal is weighted far more than an equivalent
  kindness; **trust builds slowly and breaks fast**, and a betrayal triggers a
  *global re-evaluation* of the relationship, not a single data point. (The
  *direction* is settled. The popular "5 positives to offset 1 negative" —
  Gottman's marital ratio — and "3–5× harder" figures are **contested / context-
  specific**; cite the asymmetry qualitatively, do NOT hard-code a magic ratio.)
- **Grudges & forgiveness.** Trust repair is possible but slow and requires
  repeated positive evidence + often an account/apology (McCullough on
  forgiveness; Slovic on trust asymmetry). So the update should be *stateful and
  hysteretic*: fast down on betrayal, slow up on repair.

**Adjudication.** Update rules should be **event-driven, reciprocity-based, and
asymmetric** (negative events move affect more than positive; trust drops faster
than it rises; betrayal can trigger a wholesale re-appraisal). Kin/familiarity
set *priors*, not fixed values.

## A4. Theory of mind as the BELIEF layer — separable from the FELT layer (SETTLED that cognitive & affective ToM dissociate)

- **Mentalizing** (Premack & Woodruff 1978; Frith & Frith; Baron-Cohen): holding
  a model of another's **beliefs, desires, intentions, dispositions** and using
  it to predict behaviour. A relationship is *partly* a running ToM model of the
  other ("she's proud," "he can't be trusted with money").
- **Cognitive vs affective ToM DISSOCIATE** (Shamay-Tsoory; Kalbe et al.).
  *Cognitive* ToM (what they think/intend — a **belief about the other**) is
  neurally and behaviourally separable from *affective* ToM/empathy and from
  **one's own felt affect toward them**. This directly licenses the refactor's
  two-slot design: **a HELD BELIEF/disposition-model of the other (ToM) is a
  DIFFERENT thing from the FELT emotion toward them.** You can believe someone
  is competent (belief) yet resent them (affect); believe someone means well yet
  fear them.

**Adjudication.** The per-agent relationship representation needs **both** a ToM
disposition-model (what I believe the other is/wants/will do) **and** a distinct
relational-affect slot (what I feel toward them). They update from the same
events but are not the same variable.

## A5. Asymmetry & (in)accuracy — the representation is SUBJECTIVE and can be WRONG (SETTLED)

- **Egocentric bias & social projection** (Krueger 1994; Ross, Greene & House
  1977 false-consensus). People weight their own perspective disproportionately
  and project it onto others; A's model of B is systematically *biased*.
- **Informational asymmetry.** A has far more data on their own contributions/
  intentions than on B's, so A over-credits self and mis-reads B (effort/
  contribution studies, e.g. the overclaiming literature). A's model of B and
  B's model of A are built from *different, asymmetric information* — they need
  not agree, and typically don't.
- **Accuracy is real but partial** (Funder's *realistic accuracy model*; Kenny's
  SRM/PERSON perception): person perception is *above chance but far from
  veridical*, and it is *directional* (A→B) not symmetric.

**Adjudication.** The representation MUST be **per-agent and directed**: A's
feeling+belief about B is A's *private, biased, information-limited* construct.
It can diverge from reality, and from B's reciprocal model of A. **Asymmetry is a
feature, not a bug** — modelling relationships as shared world-edges (symmetric
by construction) is scientifically wrong.

## A6. Design implications: relationship as held feeling + belief (design ONLY)

The refactor is well-grounded. Each agent should hold, **per target and
directed** (A→B, distinct from B→A):

1. **A model of the other (ToM belief slot, §A4).** Believed dispositions/
   traits/intentions ("trustworthy," "means me harm," "generous") — a running,
   updatable disposition-model, NOT ground truth. This is the *cognitive* layer.
2. **Relational affect toward the other (§A2).** The *specific* relational
   emotion(s) with their action tendencies (trust, resentment, affection,
   contempt, fear, gratitude) — plus, if desired, a low-D core-affect summary
   (valence/arousal) for cheap reads. NOT a single like/dislike scalar. This is
   the *felt* layer the speech faculty reads (§3.1: "affect toward interlocutor").
3. **Grounding events (§A3).** A memory trace / accumulator of the interactions
   that produced the current stance (who did what — a benefit, a betrayal, an
   encounter). This is what makes the feeling *grounded in remembered
   experience* and what the update rules read. (Consistent with the substrate's
   "relationship = held belief updated by events" over a bare edge.)
4. **A prior (§A1, §A3).** A generalized disposition + kin/familiarity priors
   that set the *starting* stance before events elaborate it.

**How it updates (event-driven rules, §A3):**
- Each interpersonal event is **appraised** (who caused it, benefit vs harm,
  fairness) and moves the relational-affect + ToM slots accordingly (benefit →
  gratitude/trust↑; exploitation/betrayal → resentment/trust↓).
- **Asymmetric & hysteretic:** negative events move affect *more* than positive;
  trust rises slowly, falls fast; a betrayal can trigger a *re-appraisal* of the
  whole relationship (not a single decrement). Do NOT hard-code a magic ratio —
  encode the *direction* of the asymmetry and leave the magnitude a tunable knob.
- **Familiarity drift:** repeated benign co-presence nudges affinity up mildly
  (mere exposure).
- Updates are **per-agent**, so A→B and B→A diverge naturally.

**Why bare `kin`/`hates` edges are the WRONG primitive:**
- They are **symmetric and world-level** — but relationships are *directed,
  subjective, and asymmetric* (§A5). A shared edge cannot represent A loving B
  while B is indifferent.
- They are **flat labels with no affect structure** — but the speech faculty
  needs the *specific* felt emotion + action tendency (§A2), not a category name.
- They are **static** — but feelings are *updated from experience* (§A3); a label
  has nowhere to put the grounding events or the update history.
- They **conflate belief and feeling** (§A4) — "hates" fuses a disposition-belief
  and an affect that the literature says dissociate.
- **Correct role for kin/hates edges:** treat them as **PRIORS / seeds** the
  agent *elaborates* into a held feeling+belief — an elevated (kin) or negative
  (feud) *starting* affinity that subsequent events move. The bare edge becomes
  the *initial condition*, never the *representation*.

## A7. Honest cautions (relationship refactor)

- **Do not over-formalize fuzzy emotions.** Appraisal theory individuates
  emotions, but the boundaries between resentment/contempt/anger, or
  affection/trust/love, are *fuzzy and overlapping* in the literature. A small
  set of relational emotions with action tendencies is defensible; a precise
  taxonomy with sharp thresholds over-claims. Prefer graded/blended affect over
  crisp categories.
- **Kin is a prior, not a guarantee** (§A3, Hamilton). Do not let a `kin` edge
  lock affection; kin relationships must still update (and can go negative).
- **Don't hard-code the negativity ratio.** The *asymmetry direction* is settled;
  the "5:1" / "3–5×" magnitudes are contested/context-specific — make them knobs.
- **Cognitive ≠ affective, but they're coupled.** Keep belief and feeling in
  separate slots (§A4), but note they usually co-move; don't model them as
  independent.
- **Accuracy is partial, not zero.** Person perception is biased but *above
  chance* (§A5, Funder). The subjective model should *correlate* with reality
  (events do inform it), not be arbitrary noise.
- **Dimensional-vs-discrete is unsettled.** Support both a core-affect summary
  and specific emotions; don't commit the design to one side of an open debate.

## A8. Map to the substrate (relationship refactor)

The pieces to compose already exist; the refactor *moves the representation
inward and directional*:

| Layer (this addendum) | Substrate hook |
|---|---|
| Held affect (§A2, §A6.2) | `affective_state_core.json` (`AffectiveState`) — instantiate a *directed, per-target* relational-affect node, not just a global mood |
| ToM belief-of-other (§A4, §A6.1) | `psychology.json` / `psy_social.json` (mental-state vocabulary, ToM); `bigville_drama_social.json` `bd_mary_infers` already infers a disposition — that's the belief slot in embryo |

| The consumer (§3.1) | the emergent-speech initiation rule reads *this held relational affect toward the interlocutor* (D, P, confidant-class) — the whole reason for the refactor |

**Synthesis for the builder:** replace the bare `A ─hates→ B` edge with a
**per-agent, directed, held representation** = { ToM disposition-belief about the
other } + { specific relational affect toward the other, with action tendency } +
{ grounding events } over a { kin/familiarity prior }, updated by **appraised,
asymmetric, event-driven rules** (reciprocity up, betrayal down-harder,
re-appraisal on betrayal, mild familiarity drift). This is what lets
emotion-driven speech read a *real feeling toward this specific interlocutor*
instead of a flat label — and it is exactly the layer the science says a bare
edge is missing.

# ADDENDUM 3 — The SOCIAL STRUCTURE the dyads live in (spectrum + small-community dynamics)

**Why this addendum.** Passes 1–2 grounded the DYAD (representing and talking to
one other). This pass grounds the SOCIAL STRUCTURE those dyads sit inside. The
current `kin`/`hates` primitive only sees *intimates + enemies* — but the
literature is unanimous that **most of a person's social life is neither**: it is
weak-tie, peripheral, "consequential-stranger" contact. A village where every
relationship is kin-or-enemy is missing the bulk of real social life (the daily
greetings, the acquaintance you nod to, the neighbour who is also your creditor).
Same discipline: real citations, consensus vs contested flagged, no fabrication.

## B1. Weak ties & consequential strangers — the MAJORITY of social life is peripheral (SETTLED)

- **The strength of weak ties** (Granovetter 1973, "The strength of weak ties,"
  *American Journal of Sociology* — one of the most-cited papers in social
  science, >70k citations). Acquaintances ("weak ties"), NOT intimates, are the
  **bridges** that connect otherwise-separate clusters and carry **novel
  information** (the classic finding: people find jobs through weak ties, not
  close friends, because close friends' information is redundant with one's own).
  Strong ties live inside tight redundant clusters; weak ties reach across gaps.
- **Consequential strangers** (Fingerman 2009, "Consequential strangers and
  peripheral ties," *Journal of Family Theory & Review*; Blau & Fingerman 2009,
  *Consequential Strangers*). The relationships *between* intimates and strangers
  — the barista, the regular at the market, the neighbour you chat with — are
  **"consequential strangers."** The empirical headline: **most individuals
  report a FEW core ties (mostly family) and HUNDREDS of peripheral ties.** Their
  functions: information, novel resources/behaviours, diversion, a *sense of
  familiarity and daily social texture*, identity/positioning, and **buffering**
  (they cushion strain on intimate ties). They differ from intimates in emotional
  quality, stability, and density.

**Adjudication.** The **weak-tie / consequential-stranger band is numerically the
majority** of meaningful social contact and carries distinct, real functions. A
representation that only encodes kin+enemies literally cannot represent where
most village talk happens. This is the single strongest reason for the refactor
in this pass.

## B2. The relationship SPECTRUM — a graded, layered structure, not a binary (layered structure SETTLED; exact layer sizes CONTESTED)

- **Dunbar's layered ego-network** (Hill & Dunbar 2003; Zhou et al. 2005;
  Dunbar 2020 review). Relationships nest in **concentric layers of decreasing
  closeness**, each ~3× the one inside it: **support clique ~5** (contacted
  ≥weekly, turned to in crisis) ⊂ **sympathy group ~15** ⊂ **~50** (band) ⊂
  **~150** ("casual friends"/active network — Dunbar's number, the max actively
  maintainable) ⊂ outer acquaintance layers (~500 acquaintances, ~1500
  recognisable faces). Inner layers = strong ties; outer = weak ties.
- **What orders the spectrum.** Granovetter defined **tie strength** as a
  combination of **(1) time / interaction frequency, (2) emotional intensity,
  (3) intimacy, (4) reciprocal services**. Add **multiplexity** (how many
  distinct role-relations the tie carries — B4). Closeness is *graded* along
  these, not a label. Interaction *frequency* is the cheapest strong proxy and
  the one that most sharply distinguishes the layers (inner = frequent contact).
- **CONTESTED:** the *specific numbers* (5/15/50/150) and even the existence of a
  hard ~150 cognitive limit are actively debated (e.g. Lindenfors et al. 2021
  challenged the statistical basis of "150"). What is NOT seriously contested is
  the *qualitative* claim: relationships form a **graded, layered spectrum**
  scaled by tie strength, with far more weak ties than strong.

**Adjudication.** Model relationships on a **graded tie-strength continuum**
(driven by interaction frequency × intimacy × reciprocity × multiplexity),
optionally bucketed into **bands** (intimate/kin · close friend · friend ·
acquaintance · consequential-stranger · rival · stranger). Do NOT hard-code the
Dunbar layer *sizes* as if settled — use the graded structure, treat the numbers
as a soft prior.

## B3. Small-community / tight-knit dynamics — CLOSURE, density, multiplexity, low anonymity (SETTLED; village nostalgia is the contested part, see B6)

- **Gemeinschaft vs Gesellschaft** (Tönnies 1887). The ideal-typical contrast:
  *Gemeinschaft* (community) = bonds arising organically from kinship,
  neighbourhood, and shared tradition, with affective/customary regulation;
  *Gesellschaft* (society) = impersonal, rational-instrumental ties. A village is
  the Gemeinschaft pole — regulation runs through custom, kin, and reputation
  rather than contract. (An *ideal type*, not a literal description — flagged.)
- **Network closure** (Coleman 1988, "Social capital in the creation of human
  capital," *AJS*). In a *closed* network (everyone knows everyone, ties loop
  back), behaviour is **observable**, norm violations can be **collectively
  sanctioned**, and norm-compliance rewarded — so **closure is what makes trust
  and norms enforceable**. A small village is a high-closure network; that is
  precisely what makes reputation a *real force* (B4).
- **Multiplex ties** (Coleman; Gluckman). In a small community one alter is
  *simultaneously* neighbour + creditor + kin + rival + fellow-congregant. A tie
  is a **bundle of role-relations**, not one label — and multiplexity *raises the
  stakes* of each interaction (a quarrel with your creditor is also a quarrel
  with your neighbour).
- **Low anonymity + repeated interaction + triadic closure.** In a small-n,
  high-closure world you interact with the same people repeatedly (shadow of the
  future → cooperation), you cannot be anonymous, and **triadic closure** (your
  friends become each other's friends; a friend-of-a-friend tie tends to form)
  densifies the graph. These are the structural facts that make village social
  life qualitatively different from urban strangership.

**Adjudication.** The village's small-n, high-closure, multiplex, repeated-
interaction structure is *exactly* what turns reputation and gossip into
effective forces (B4) and what makes each dyadic tie carry multiple roles. The
representation must support **multiplex, directed ties in a dense observable
graph**, not isolated pairwise labels.

## B4. Gossip, reputation & informal social control — the INDIRECT (third-party) update path (SETTLED as mechanism; content/honesty CONTESTED)

- **Gossip as social control** (Gluckman 1963, "Gossip and scandal," *Current
  Anthropology* 4:307–316). In close-knit groups gossip is a mechanism of
  **informal social control**: it polices deviance by threat of reputational
  damage, marks group membership (you must *belong* to be allowed to gossip), and
  bonds the in-group. Merry (1984, "Rethinking gossip and scandal") develops
  gossip as social control specifically in *close-knit, low-anonymity*
  communities where exit is costly.
- **Gossip as grooming** (Dunbar 1996/2004; §1.5 above). Most talk is social;
  gossip services the network AND transmits reputational information.
- **Indirect reciprocity / reputation** (Nowak & Sigmund 1998, 2005, *Nature*;
  "image scoring"). Cooperation can propagate through **reputation** even without
  repeated dyadic contact: a bystander observes A's act toward B, **emits an
  evaluative signal (gossip)**, and that signal joins A's *public reputation*
  that future partners consult. Recent formal work (e.g. a 2024 *PNAS*
  gossip–reputation–cooperation model) makes the gossip channel explicit. **This
  is a relationship-update path that is NOT dyadic experience: C tells A about B,
  and A revises B.**
- **Order without law** (Ellickson 1991, *Order Without Law: How Neighbors
  Settle Disputes* — the Shasta County cattle study). **Close-knit groups**
  (his term: nonhierarchical, informal power broadly distributed, good
  information flow about past/present events, continuing relationships) **develop
  and enforce welfare-maximizing norms informally — via reputation and reciprocal
  self-help, not formal law.** "Small wrongs are entered into ranchers' mental
  ledgers for repayment in kind." Ostrom (1990, *Governing the Commons*) shows
  the same: communities devise their own monitoring + graduated-sanction
  enforcement, sustained by reputation and repeated interaction.

**Adjudication.** There are **TWO update paths** for a relationship, and the
current primitive supports neither well: (1) **DIRECT** — A updates B from A's own
experience of B (Addendum 2, §A3); (2) **INDIRECT** — A updates B from *what a
third party C says about B* (gossip/reputation). In a high-closure small village
the indirect path is powerful and fast, and it is the substrate of informal
social control. (CONTESTED: gossip's *veracity* and whether it is net
prosocial vs. manipulative — it can carry false information and be weaponised;
model it as *evidence weighted by the source*, not ground truth.)

## B5. (folded into B4) Reputation as an emergent, graph-level object

Reputation is not a private dyadic feeling — it is a **partially-shared,
graph-level summary** of an agent built from many observers' gossip (Nowak &
Sigmund image score; the *PNAS* gossip model). It is *approximately* shared
(closure makes reputations converge) but never perfectly (private information,
biased transmission — Addendum 2 §A5). The substrate already has a reputation
loop (`bigville_loop_reputation.json`) — the design point is that **reputation
feeds the INDIRECT update path** into each agent's *private* held representation.

## B6. Village-scale dynamics HONESTLY — support AND surveillance, do NOT romanticize (SETTLED that both sides are real)

The literature is explicit that tight-knit community is **double-edged** — the
*same* closure that enables trust and support also enables control and harm:

- **The dark side of social capital** (Portes 1998, "Social capital: its origins
  and applications," *Annual Review of Sociology*; "The Dark Side of
  Gemeinschaft," rural-criminology literature). High closure produces
  **surveillance, enforced conformity, exclusion of outsiders, downward
  levelling** (pressure not to rise above the group), and **restricted individual
  freedom.** Social support and social *constraint* are the same mechanism seen
  from two sides.
- **Feuds, ostracism, scandal.** Gossip that bonds the in-group *excludes* and
  can destroy the target; ostracism is a severe sanction precisely because exit
  is costly in a closed community; multiplex ties mean a feud contaminates every
  role at once. Merry and Gluckman both stress gossip's *coercive* face.
- **Not a golden age.** Tönnies's Gemeinschaft is an *ideal type*, and modern
  work warns against romanticizing it — village life historically combined
  mutual aid with rigid hierarchy, low privacy, and harsh treatment of deviance.

**Adjudication.** Emergent village speech must be able to produce the *dark*
outputs too — a snub, a piece of malicious gossip, an exclusion, an enforced-
conformity rebuke — not only warm greetings. Support and surveillance are one
mechanism; the model should not encode only the pleasant half.

## B7. Design implications: relationship as a SPECTRUM in a small closed community (design ONLY)

Building on Addendum 2's per-agent held feeling+belief, this pass adds the
**structural** requirements:

1. **A graded tie-strength, not a binary (§B1–B2).** Each directed relationship
   carries a **continuous tie-strength** driven by **interaction frequency ×
   intimacy × reciprocity × multiplexity** (Granovetter's four + multiplexity),
   optionally bucketed into a **band**: *intimate/kin · close friend · friend ·
   acquaintance · consequential-stranger · rival · stranger*. Kin and hostility
   are two *points/priors* on this spectrum, not the whole of it. **Interaction
   frequency is the cheapest driver** — ties strengthen with benign contact
   (mere exposure, §A3) and decay without it.
2. **Most emergent speech is consequential-stranger ritual (§B1, ties back to
   §1.7 / §3).** The *modal* utterance in a village is a **frequent, low-stakes,
   ritual exchange with a weak tie** — a greeting, a nod, small talk at the
   market — NOT an intimate disclosure or a hostile confrontation. The initiation
   rule's *base rate* should therefore fire many low-intensity acquaintance
   greetings and *few* intimate/hostile exchanges. A faculty that only speaks to
   kin/enemies would produce a sociologically empty village. (This also supplies
   the "trade as social act," §3.5 — a trade is a canonical consequential-
   stranger ritual occasion.)
3. **Two update paths (§B4).** Keep the **DIRECT** dyadic update (Addendum 2 §A3)
   AND add an **INDIRECT** third-party path: when C tells A about B (gossip), A's
   held representation of B updates — *weighted by A's trust in C and by how the
   report squares with A's own priors* (evidence, not ground truth). This is the
   engine of reputation and informal social control, and it is what a small
   closed community runs on.
4. **Multiplex ties (§B3).** One alter can hold **several role-relations at once**
   (neighbour + creditor + kin + rival). The representation should let a directed
   A→B tie carry *multiple typed role-relations* whose affect can even conflict
   (you can resent your creditor who is also your brother). This is exactly the
   `relation_role_frame` shape already in the substrate.
5. **Closure makes reputation real (§B3–B5).** Because bigville is small-n and
   high-closure (everyone can observe/hear — the earshot model, `lo_observe_act`),
   acts are witnessed, gossip propagates, and reputation is enforceable. The
   design should let **witnessed acts mint reputation signals** that flow (via
   gossip) into others' indirect updates — turning the village into a functioning
   informal-social-control system rather than a set of isolated dyads.
6. **Both faces (§B6).** The graded spectrum + gossip path must be able to
   produce **exclusion, snubs, malicious gossip, and enforced-conformity
   rebukes**, not only affiliative greetings. Support and surveillance share the
   mechanism.

## B8. Honest cautions (social-structure pass)

- **Do NOT hard-code Dunbar's layer sizes.** 5/15/50/150 and the ~150 limit are
  **contested** (Lindenfors et al. 2021). Use the *graded/layered* structure as
  settled; treat the numbers as a soft prior a small village won't even reach.
- **Do NOT romanticize the village (§B6).** Closure produces surveillance,
  exclusion, and conformity pressure as much as support. Model both.
- **Gossip is not ground truth.** It can be false, biased, or weaponised (B4
  contested). The indirect update must weight by *source trust*, never overwrite
  the direct experience wholesale.
- **Weak ties are the majority, but shallow.** Consequential strangers matter *in
  aggregate and for specific functions* (information, texture, buffering) — don't
  over-weight any single weak tie's affective pull; their power is *distributional
  and structural*, not intimate.
- **"Tie strength" is a bundle, and its components can diverge.** Frequency,
  intimacy, reciprocity, and multiplexity usually correlate but not always (a
  high-frequency low-intimacy tie = the barista). Prefer the multi-component
  drive over a single scalar where it matters.
- **Ideal types are not measurements.** Gemeinschaft/Gesellschaft and the
  weak/strong dichotomy are analytic poles; real ties are continuous.

# ADDENDUM 4 — ATTRACTION / REPULSION: the approach–avoidance pull (a THIRD slot)

**Why this addendum.** The relationship representation so far has TWO slots: a
**belief** slot (ToM/appraisal of the other — Addendum 2 §A4) and a
**specific-relational-emotion** slot (resentment/gratitude/trust — §A2). A third,
*dissociable* dimension is missing: **attraction / repulsion** — the raw
**approach–avoidance pull** toward or away from a person. You can *believe*
someone is competent, feel *no particular resentment*, and yet be *drawn to* or
*put off by* them; attraction is not reducible to either other slot. This
addendum grounds it, including its romantic/sexual variant (which must gate
courtship→pairing→fertility instead of a round-robin), and — crucially — how it
**drives spatial behaviour**, turning movement social. Same discipline: real
citations, consensus vs contested flagged, no fabrication.

## C1. Determinants of interpersonal attraction (proximity, familiarity, similarity ROBUST; complementarity WEAK/contested)

- **Proximity / propinquity** (Festinger, Schachter & Back 1950, *Social
  Pressures in Informal Groups* — the Westgate housing study). Physical AND
  *functional* proximity strongly predict friendship: 41% named next-door
  neighbours as close friends vs. 10% for those four doors away; living near a
  stairwell (more passers-by) raised friendship counts. **Proximity is one of the
  most robust attraction findings** — but note it operates largely *via* repeated
  exposure (C1 familiarity) and opportunity.
- **Mere exposure / familiarity** (Zajonc 1968; already cited §A3). Repeated
  encounter *itself* raises liking — the mechanism linking proximity to
  attraction. (Robust; strongest on early exposures. Caveat: exposure to an
  *already-disliked* stimulus can *deepen* dislike — Finkel et al. found
  familiarity can breed contempt when initial evaluation is negative.)
- **Similarity–attraction** (Byrne 1971, *The Attraction Paradigm*; "law of
  attraction" — attraction rises linearly with proportion of similar attitudes).
  Extended to networks as **homophily** (McPherson, Smith-Lovin & Cook 2001,
  "Birds of a feather," *Annual Review of Sociology* — "similarity breeds
  connection," structuring ties of every type; race/ethnicity the strongest
  divide, then age, religion, education, occupation, gender). **Similarity→
  attraction is one of the best-replicated effects in social psychology.**
- **Reciprocity of liking** (robust): people like those who like them; liking is
  self-reinforcing and reciprocal.
- **Physical attractiveness / halo** (Dion, Berscheid & Walster 1972,
  "what is beautiful is good") — real but to be *not over-weighted* (see cautions).
- **Complementarity** ("opposites attract") — **WEAK / largely unsupported**; the
  evidence favours similarity over complementarity for most traits. Flag as
  contested/weak.

**Adjudication.** The robust drivers of the approach pull are **proximity ×
familiarity × similarity × reciprocity** (the first two tightly linked). These
are all *cheaply computable* from a village's spatial + attribute + interaction
state — which is exactly why attraction can drive emergent spatial behaviour (C5).

## C2. REPULSION as its own force (the specific "repulsion hypothesis" is CONTESTED/largely refuted; person-level aversion is REAL)

- **Rosenbaum's (1986) repulsion hypothesis** ("The repulsion hypothesis: on the
  nondevelopment of relationships," *JPSP*): claimed *similarity is irrelevant*
  and only *dissimilarity* moves attraction (downward). **This strong form did
  NOT replicate.** Byrne, Clore & Smeaton (1986) rebutted it; Smeaton, Byrne &
  Murnen (1989) and Singh et al. showed *both* similar and dissimilar attitudes
  affect attraction — so similarity is NOT irrelevant. The settled resolution:
  there is an **asymmetry** (dissimilarity tends to weigh somewhat *more* than
  similarity — consistent with the general negativity bias, §A3), but the strong
  "only dissimilarity matters" claim is rejected. **Do NOT adopt Rosenbaum's
  strong form; the defensible residue is a modest dissimilarity-weighs-more
  asymmetry.**
- **Aversion / disgust toward persons is real** (moral disgust, contempt →
  withdrawal action tendency, §A2). And the **approach–avoidance asymmetry**
  (§A3, negativity bias) applies to persons: a strongly aversive other pushes
  avoidance harder than an equally attractive other pulls approach.

**Adjudication.** Model repulsion as the **negative pole of one signed
approach–avoidance axis**, with a mild "bad-is-stronger" asymmetry — NOT as a
separate mechanism, and NOT via Rosenbaum's refuted strong claim.

## C3. Approach–avoidance as a fundamental, SIGNED affective dimension (SETTLED that it is foundational; it is a DISTINCT layer from appraisal and discrete emotion)

- **Approach–avoidance motivation** (Elliot 2006, hierarchical model; Gray's
  Reinforcement Sensitivity Theory — **BAS** approach/reward, **BIS** avoidance/
  threat). The approach/avoid distinction is one of the **most basic, ancient
  organizing principles of affect and behaviour**: positively-valenced stimuli
  instigate *approach*, negatively-valenced ones *avoidance*. Carver & White's
  BIS/BAS scales operationalize the two systems as *separable* dispositions.
- **Distinct from the other two slots.** Approach–avoidance is a **signed valence
  toward a target** — a *motivational pull*, not a *belief* (cognitive appraisal,
  §A4) and not a *specific discrete emotion* (§A2). It is closely *related* to
  liking (liking ≈ a positive evaluative attitude) but is specifically the
  **motivational/behavioural** vector: *how much do I move toward vs. away from
  this person*. It composes with, and is partly caused by, the affect slot
  (contempt → avoid; gratitude → approach) but is not identical to it (you can be
  drawn to someone you also resent — ambivalence = simultaneous approach+avoid).

**Adjudication.** Attraction/repulsion is best modelled as a **single signed,
directed approach–avoidance valence toward each alter (A→B)** — a distinct THIRD
slot alongside {belief} and {specific relational affect}. Ambivalence (co-active
approach and avoidance) is real and worth allowing.

## C4. Romantic / sexual attraction & mate choice — a DISSOCIABLE system (three-systems model SETTLED as framework; specific mate-preference sex-difference claims CONTESTED)

- **Fisher's three systems** (Fisher et al. 2002, "Defining the brain systems of
  lust, romantic attraction, and attachment," *Archives of Sexual Behavior*):
  three **dissociable** emotion–motivation systems with distinct neural
  substrates and behavioural repertoires — **lust** (initiates mating with any
  suitable partner), **romantic attraction** (focuses courtship energy on a
  *specific* preferred partner — the obsessive, high-dopamine "in love" state),
  and **attachment** (pair-bond maintenance for cooperative parenting). They are
  **separable** — one can feel attachment without lust, lust without romantic
  attraction, etc. This is the key design point: **romantic/sexual attraction is
  its own system, dissociable from platonic attraction (C3) and from attachment
  (§A1).**
- **Mate preferences & assortative mating** (Buss 1989 and after). Long-term
  preferences are complex (kindness, intelligence, health, dependability); Buss
  reports two cross-culturally recurrent sex differences (men weight youth/
  attractiveness more; women weight status/resources more) — but these are
  **CONTESTED** in magnitude, universality, and interpretation (biosocial vs.
  evolutionary accounts; effects shrink with gender equality — Eagly & Wood).
  **Assortative mating** (partners resemble each other — homophily, C1) is robust.
- **Jealousy as mate-guarding** (Buss 2002): jealousy is an evolved response
  that triggers *mate-guarding* behaviour scaling with perceived infidelity risk.
  (The *specific* "sex difference in jealousy type" claim is contested; the
  *existence* of jealousy→guarding is not.)

**Adjudication.** Add a **romantic/sexual attraction variant** that is a
*dissociable* system: it (a) focuses on a *specific* alter, (b) gates
courtship → pair-bond → fertility (replacing any round-robin/scheduled pairing),
and (c) produces **jealousy → mate-guarding** when the bond is threatened. Keep
it *separable* from platonic approach–avoidance and from attachment. Treat the
*directional mate-preference* claims as tunable, culturally-contested priors,
NOT laws.

## C5. Attraction → SPATIAL / affiliative behaviour (proxemics: SETTLED that distance encodes relationship; approach-those-you-like is robust)

- **Proxemics** (Hall 1966, *The Hidden Dimension*). Interpersonal distance is a
  **function of relationship**: intimate (0–18 in), personal (1.5–4 ft), social
  (4–12 ft), public (>12 ft). Distance *encodes* and *is chosen by* relational
  closeness; intrusion into intimate space by a non-intimate causes discomfort
  → withdrawal. (Zone *boundaries* are culturally variable — flag — but the
  *ordinal* mapping closeness→proximity is robust.)
- **Affiliation vs. avoidance in space.** The **affiliation motive** (approach)
  draws people physically *toward* liked/attractive others; aversion increases
  distance from disliked others. Proximity both *causes* attraction (C1) and is
  *chosen by* attraction — a positive feedback loop that, at population scale,
  produces **clustering / cliques** (homophilous, spatially self-sorting groups)
  and **avoidance patterns**.

**Adjudication.** Attraction/repulsion is not just an internal number — it
**selects spatial behaviour**: approach attractors, avoid repulsors, choose
interpersonal distance by closeness. In a spatial village this is the lever that
makes movement *social* (people go where their attractors are), producing visible
cliques, courtship approach, and avoidance on the map.

## C6. Design implications: attraction/repulsion as a signed approach–avoidance slot (design ONLY)

Add a **THIRD slot** to the per-agent, directed relationship representation
(Addendum 2 §A6; Addendum 3 §B7), composing with — not duplicating — the other
two:

1. **A signed, directed approach–avoidance valence A→B** (one scalar, positive =
   attraction/approach, negative = repulsion/avoidance; optionally allow *co-active*
   approach+avoid for ambivalence, C3). This is distinct from the {belief} slot
   (C3/§A4) and the {specific relational affect} slot (§A2), though it is *fed by*
   them (contempt→avoid, gratitude→approach).
2. **Seeded + updated by the robust drivers (C1–C2):**
   - **similarity / homophily** — attribute overlap between A and B raises the
     pull (Byrne, McPherson); **dissimilarity lowers it, weighing slightly more**
     (C2 asymmetry — the *defensible* residue of the repulsion hypothesis, NOT its
     strong form);
   - **proximity × mere-exposure** — benign co-presence frequency raises the pull
     (Festinger, Zajonc; ties to the interaction-frequency driver of tie-strength,
     §B7.1) — with the *familiarity-breeds-contempt* caveat when the current sign
     is already negative;
   - **reciprocity** — B's approach toward A raises A's toward B.
3. **A DISSOCIABLE romantic/sexual variant (C4).** A *separate* signed
   attraction channel toward a *specific* alter that, above threshold and gated by
   availability/mutuality, drives **courtship → pair-bond → fertility** (this
   should REPLACE any round-robin/scheduled pairing with an *emergent* mate choice
   from mutual romantic attraction), and mints **jealousy → mate-guarding** when
   the bond is threatened by a third party. Keep it separable from platonic
   approach (C3) and from attachment (§A1). Mate-preference weightings = tunable,
   culturally-contested priors.
4. **It DRIVES spatial routine (C5) — this is the payoff.** The movement/
   navigation faculty should read the approach–avoidance slot: **seek attractors,
   avoid repulsors, choose interpersonal distance by closeness** (proxemic zones).
   At population scale this yields **emergent cliques, courtship approach, and
   visible avoidance on the map** — movement becomes *social* rather than
   need/errand-only. Attraction also raises the *encounter frequency* with
   attractors, which (via §B7.1) strengthens the tie and (via §3) raises the rate
   of emergent speech with them — closing the loop: attraction → proximity →
   exposure → stronger tie → more talk → (dyadic + gossip) affect updates.
5. **Composition, not duplication.** The three slots interact but stay distinct:
   {belief: what I think B is} × {affect: what specific emotion I feel toward B} ×
   {approach-avoid: how much I move toward/away from B}. The speech faculty (§3)
   reads *affect* toward the interlocutor; the *movement* faculty reads
   *approach–avoid*; both compose with tie-strength/band (§B7).

## C7. Honest cautions (attraction pass)

- **Do NOT adopt Rosenbaum's strong repulsion hypothesis (C2).** It failed to
  replicate; similarity is *not* irrelevant. Keep only the modest
  dissimilarity-weighs-more asymmetry.
- **Complementarity is weak (C1).** Don't build "opposites attract"; similarity
  dominates.
- **Do NOT over-weight physical attractiveness.** The halo effect is real but
  modest and easily overstated; in a village of repeated interaction, proximity/
  familiarity/similarity/reciprocity dominate long-run ties.
- **Mate-preference sex-differences are culturally contested (C4).** Treat
  Buss's directional claims as *tunable priors that shrink with equality* (Eagly
  & Wood), never as hard laws. Keep the *mechanism* (romantic system gates
  courtship; jealousy→guarding) while leaving the *preference weights* soft.
- **Keep the romantic system dissociable (C4).** Do not fuse romantic/sexual
  attraction with platonic approach or with attachment — Fisher's whole point is
  that they *dissociate* (attachment can outlast romantic attraction; lust ≠
  love).
- **Proxemic zone *sizes* are cultural (C5).** The ordinal closeness→proximity
  mapping is robust; the exact distances are not universal — keep them as
  parameters.
- **Attraction ≠ liking ≠ the affect slot.** Related but distinct; model the
  *motivational pull* separately from the *evaluative belief* and the *discrete
  emotion*, and allow ambivalence (co-active approach+avoid).

## C8. Map to the substrate (attraction pass)

| Layer (this addendum) | Substrate hook |

**Synthesis for the builder:** add a **signed, directed approach–avoidance

Give it a **dissociable romantic/sexual variant** that gates **emergent

it **makes movement social**: the navigation faculty reads it to *seek

# ADDENDUM 5 — KINSHIP IS CONSTRUCTED / FELT, not merely genetic

**Why this addendum (the final science pass).** The remaining defect in a
`kin`-edge model is that it treats kinship as a *genetic label*. The literature —
across anthropology, evolutionary biology, and attachment science — is clear that
**kinship is MADE, not merely born**: it is built through care, feeding,
co-residence, and shared history, and a step-brother, an adopted child, or a
foster child can be **more kin than blood kin**, while a neglectful blood tie can
**wither**. Genetic relatedness is at most a *weak, overridable prior* — and even
the evolved mind does not read "blood," it **estimates** kinship from
care/cohabitation cues. This corrects the genetic-label primitive at its root.
Same discipline: real citations, consensus vs contested flagged, no fabrication.

## D1. Fictive / social kinship & chosen family — kinship is MADE (SETTLED in anthropology; the *definition* of kinship is a live theoretical debate)

- **Schneider's critique** (Schneider 1984, *A Critique of the Study of
  Kinship*). The **genealogical grid** — kinship as biological descent — is a
  *Western folk theory*, not a cross-cultural universal; it fails to describe
  most ethnographic cases. Blood-relatedness is one culturally specific idiom of
  kinship, not its essence.
- **Kinship as "mutuality of being"** (Sahlins 2011/2013, *What Kinship Is — And
  Is Not*). Sahlins defines kinship as **"mutuality of being": kinfolk are
  persons who are parts of one another**, such that what happens to one is felt by
  the other. This is a *symbolic/experiential* belonging, explicitly **NOT a
  biological connection by blood** — and it is *acquired* (through shared nurture,
  substance, names, residence), not fixed at birth.
- **Cultures of relatedness** (Carsten 2000, *Cultures of Relatedness*; 2004,
  *After Kinship*). Kinship (Carsten prefers "relatedness") is **processually
  built** — through *feeding, dwelling together, sharing substance and daily
  life* — so relatedness is *acquired and can change over time*, frequently
  centred on food-sharing and co-habitation rather than genealogy.
- **Chosen families** (Weston 1991, *Families We Choose*). LGBTQ+ communities
  build durable families from **love, friendship, and choice** rather than
  biology — "families we choose." Empirically robust that these function as
  kinship (support, obligation, identity). (Also: "voluntary kin"/"intentional
  families," Braithwaite et al.; Muraco.)

**Adjudication (contested boundary flagged).** That kinship is *substantially
constructed* is settled across modern kinship anthropology. What is *contested*
is the strong claim that kinship is *purely* cultural with *no* biological anchor
(Sahlins vs. his critics; and evolutionary anthropologists retain relatedness as
one real input). The defensible synthesis: **kinship is a felt/constructed bond
built by mutuality, care, and co-residence — with biology as one culturally
weighted cue among several, not the definition.**

## D2. Alloparenting & non-genetic care bonds — humans bond parentally with non-kin (SETTLED that humans are cooperative breeders; degree/adaptationist story debated)

- **Cooperative breeding / alloparenting** (Hrdy 2009, *Mothers and Others*).
  Humans are **cooperative breeders**: infants are reared by **alloparents** —
  anyone other than the biological parents (grandmothers, older siblings,
  extended family, godparents, ritual kin, unrelated helpers). Crucially, **"once
  selected to respond to infant cues by helping, caregivers need NOT be close
  relatives"** — the human care system responds to *infant cues and proximity*,
  not to a relatedness readout. Humans are among the very few primates who invest
  alloparental care in *unrelated* young. This is the evolutionary substrate for
  non-genetic parental bonds.

**Adjudication.** The human parental-bonding system is **triggered by caregiving
interaction, not by genetic verification** — which is exactly why adoptive and
foster bonds form at full strength (D3). (Contested: the precise selective story
and how much cooperative breeding shaped human cognition — the *phenomenon* of
extensive alloparenting is not in doubt.)

## D3. Adoption & foster attachment — care builds attachment, genes do not (SETTLED: adoptive/foster attachment forms; timing/early-deprivation caveats real)

- **Adoption attachment meta-analysis** (van den Dries, Juffer, van IJzendoorn &
  Bakermans-Kranenburg 2009, "Fostering security? A meta-analysis of attachment
  in adopted children," *Children and Youth Services Review*). **Children adopted
  before ~12 months are as securely attached to adoptive parents as non-adopted
  peers.** Attachment forms through the **caregiving relationship**, not genetic
  relatedness. Later-placed children show somewhat more insecure/disorganized
  attachment — but this tracks **pre-adoption deprivation/institutional care**,
  NOT the absence of a blood tie, and improves with sensitive caregiving.
- **Adoption as a successful intervention** (van IJzendoorn & Juffer 2005,
  *Current Directions*): adopted children show massive catch-up in IQ, growth,
  and attachment relative to institutional peers — the *caregiving environment*,
  not genes, drives the bond and recovery.
- **Primacy of the caregiving relationship** (Bowlby; attachment forms with
  *whoever* provides sensitive, consistent care — the "attachment figure" is
  defined by care, not biology).

**Adjudication.** **Attachment is built by care, full stop.** Adoptive/foster
parent-child bonds reach the same security as biological ones; where they fall
short it is *early deprivation history*, not lack of genetic relatedness. This is
the strongest single evidence that the kin bond is *earned*, not *inherited*.

## D4. Psychological kinship & the earned bond — the mind ESTIMATES kinship from care/cohabitation cues (SETTLED that cues, not "blood," drive it; cue weights debated)

- **The mind computes a kinship INDEX from cues, not a blood readout**
  (Lieberman, Tooby & Cosmides 2007, "The architecture of human kin detection,"
  *Nature*). The evolved kin-detection system estimates relatedness (and thereby
  regulates altruism + incest aversion) from **two contextual cues**:
  **(1) co-residence DURATION during childhood**, and **(2) maternal perinatal
  association** (seeing a newborn cared for by one's own mother). **There is no
  direct perception of genetic relatedness** — the mind *infers* kinship from
  *care and cohabitation*. This is the key mechanistic result: **even the
  "genetic" kin instinct is actually driven by care/co-residence cues.** So a
  step- or foster-sibling raised alongside you registers as kin *by the same
  mechanism*; a biological sibling raised apart may not.
- **Co-residence outweighs genetic relatedness, measured** (Pollet et al. 2020,
  "Sibling relations in patchwork families: co-residence is more influential than
  genetic relatedness," *Frontiers/PMC*). In blended families, **relationship
  quality tracks childhood CO-RESIDENCE, not r**: higher relatedness improved
  sibling relations *only among those who did NOT live together*; among
  co-resident siblings, actual genetic relatedness did **not** predict relationship
  quality. Directly: *raised-together step/half/adoptive siblings ≈ full siblings*.
- **Kinship psychology extends to non-kin** (Henrich et al.; Yasawa fieldwork):
  humans co-opt the psychology of genetic relatedness and **extend it to
  unrelated others** through culturally-constructed kinship, mediated by *shared
  environment, frequent interaction, care, and nurture* rather than genealogy.

**Adjudication.** The felt kin bond is a function of **accumulated care ×
co-residence duration × shared history × investment** — and genetic relatedness
is a *cue the mind uses only insofar as it correlates with those*, not an
independent switch. (Contested: the *relative weighting* of cues, birth-order
effects, and cross-cultural variation in reckoning — but the *cue-based, care-
driven* architecture is well supported.)

## D5. The dark side / honest bounds — kinship can WITHER; blood is neither necessary nor sufficient (SETTLED)

- **Family estrangement** (Blake 2017; Coleman; Wikipedia synthesis of the
  estrangement literature). Blood relationships **cease or wither**: estrangement
  is common, often *initiated to escape abuse/conflict*, and the initiator's
  wellbeing frequently *improves*. A genetic tie does not guarantee a bond and
  can be actively severed.
- **Neglectful/abusive blood ties.** Maltreatment within biological families is
  the clearest proof that **genetic relatedness is neither necessary nor
  sufficient** for a felt kin bond: blood kin can be the *source* of harm, and the
  bond does not persist on genetics alone.
- **Do not romanticize constructed kinship either.** Chosen/foster/adoptive bonds
  can also fail; the point is *symmetry* — **all** kin bonds are earned and can
  decay, regardless of biology.

**Adjudication.** Genetic relatedness is **neither necessary nor sufficient**.
The bond is *earned and maintained* (by care/co-residence/history) and *decays*
under neglect/betrayal — for blood and non-blood ties alike.

## D6. Design implications: the kin BAND is EARNED (design ONLY)

This **corrects the genetic-label model**. The kin band is not read off a `kin`
edge — it is a *value built and decayed by events*, sitting on the graded
tie-strength spectrum (Addendum 3 §B7) with the other slots (Addenda 2, 4).

1. **Kin-band = EARNED tie-strength, not a label (D1–D4).** The strength of a kin
   bond is an **accumulator** driven by **cumulative care given/received ×
   co-residence duration × shared history × investment** (the Lieberman cues +
   Carsten's feeding/dwelling + Sahlins's mutuality). It *builds* with sustained
   care/cohabitation and *decays* with neglect/absence/betrayal (D5). This is the
   same event-driven, asymmetric update engine as the relational-affect slot
   (§A3) — care up, betrayal down-harder.
2. **Genetic relatedness = a small, overridable PRIOR (D4).** An `r`/`kin` edge
   sets only a **modest starting bias** on the kin-band accumulator (mirroring the
   mind's cue-based estimate), NOT the band itself. It is *overridable*: enough
   accumulated care makes a non-blood tie exceed it; enough neglect makes a blood
   tie fall below a stranger's.
3. **Foster/adopted/step/chosen can reach or EXCEED the strongest blood band
   (D3–D4).** Because the band is earned, a raised-together foster/adopted/step
   relation accrues the same care/co-residence signal as a biological one and can
   reach or surpass the top kin band (Pollet: co-resident step-siblings ≈ full
   siblings; van IJzendoorn: adoptive attachment ≈ biological). The model MUST
   allow this — it is the headline correction.
4. **A neglected blood tie WITHERS (D5).** With no care/co-residence signal (or
   with betrayal events), a genetically-kin tie's band decays toward — or below —
   acquaintance/stranger. Estrangement is representable as a decayed/severed band.
5. **Kin-band, relational-affect, and attraction stay INDEPENDENT slots
   (composition, not fusion).** You can be **strongly kin-bonded yet resentful**
   (high kin-band, negative affect — the sibling you'd die for but can't stand),
   or attracted without kinship, etc. Kin-band (Addendum 5) × relational-affect
   (§A2) × approach-avoid (§C3) × belief (§A4) are four independent, co-composed
   fields on the directed relationship rep. The kin-band biases *obligation/
   altruism* (Hamilton-style helping, mutuality of being — "what happens to one is
   felt by the other"), NOT the momentary feeling.
6. **Alloparenting falls out for free (D2).** Because parental/kin bonds are
   triggered by *caregiving interaction*, an adult who cares for a child (own or
   not — infant-care/rearing seeds) accrues a kin-band toward that child. No
   special "adoption" rule is needed; the earned-band mechanism produces
   alloparental bonds directly.

## D7. Honest cautions (kinship pass)

- **Do NOT romanticize constructed kinship (D5).** Chosen/foster bonds also fail;
  the correct claim is *symmetry* — all kin bonds are earned and can decay, not
  "found family always wins."
- **Keep a SMALL biological prior, don't delete it (D4).** Relatedness is a real
  (if weak, indirect) cue; the evolutionary-anthropology camp retains it. Model it
  as an overridable prior, not zero and not a switch. (The strong "kinship is
  purely cultural" claim is contested — §D1.)
- **Early-deprivation caveat is real (D3).** Later-placed/institutionally-
  deprived children show more insecure attachment — but that is *history*, not
  *genetics*. If modelling, attribute it to deprivation events, not to the
  non-blood status.
- **Cue weights & cross-cultural reckoning are contested (D4).** The exact
  weighting of co-residence vs. perinatal association, birth-order effects, and
  how cultures reckon kin vary. Keep cue weights tunable; don't hard-code one
  culture's kinship algebra.
- **Kin-band ≠ affect ≠ obligation-in-the-moment.** A high kin-band raises a
  *standing* altruism/obligation prior; it does not force positive momentary
  affect. Keep them separate (D6.5).

**Synthesis for the builder:** replace the **genetic `kin` label** with an

sitting on the graded tie-strength spectrum. Genetic relatedness becomes only a
**small, overridable prior** on that accumulator (mirroring the mind's own
cue-based kin *estimate*, Lieberman/Tooby/Cosmides — not a blood readout). This
lets **foster/adopted/step/chosen relations reach or exceed the strongest blood
band** (van IJzendoorn adoption attachment ≈ biological; Pollet co-resident

resident can be **strongly kin-bonded yet resentful** — with the kin-band biasing
*standing altruism/obligation* (mutuality of being) rather than momentary feeling.
