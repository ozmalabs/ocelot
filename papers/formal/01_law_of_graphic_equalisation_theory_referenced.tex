\begin{abstract}
This paper states a minimal recursive theory of realised change. Its primitive graph is
\[\boxed{G=(g,r,\Gamma),}\]
where $g$ is represented state, $r$ is operative transformation structure, and $\Gamma$ is the admissibility grammar. Nothing in graphhood requires a world model, prediction, goals, valuation, agency, Narrative, or cognition. The central dynamical structure is
\[\boxed{G_t\longrightarrow\Poss(G_t)\xrightarrow{\Coll_t}G_{t+1},}\]
where present structure determines admissible possibility and collapse produces one realised continuation. The realised successor becomes the antecedent of the next step.

A situated graph meets containing structure through a boundary. World-caused change is not vetoed by the graph's self-admissibility grammar: $\Gamma$ governs graph-originated transformation, not the containing world's ability to alter the graph. Where a containing frame no longer admits an identity-preserving continuation, that situated identity terminates rather than becoming an ill-formed state. Identity and persistence are therefore frame-relative relations, while any represented SELF identity inside $g$ remains ordinary graph content and may be copied.

The theory requires only discrete distinctions at the operative frame. This is a sufficiency claim, not an assertion that an ultimate containing supergraph cannot be continuous. Continuity may remain a useful or fundamental grounding; it is simply not required as a primitive by the present theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and epistemic status}\label{sec:scope}
This paper contains the smallest set of commitments required for the later theory. It distinguishes axioms and definitions, deductive consequences, groundings into existing theories, and empirical hypotheses. It deliberately excludes executor design, rule scheduling, proposal records, retry protocols, valuation algorithms, and controller architecture.

\paragraph{Level of the claims.}
The claims here are about \emph{information}: distinction, relation, frame, admissibility, realisation, residual. Physical theory is taken to be an embodiment of information rather than a competitor to it, alongside other embodiments --- cognitive, institutional, computational --- which differ in how much structure they instantiate rather than in kind. This is the position that licenses treating an inertial reference frame and a representational frame as the same sort of object (Axiom~$-1$), and it locates the epistemic boundary below both: what embodiments are made of remains unspecified.

The layering must not be allowed to function as a shield, so the condition under which a physical result bears on a claim here is stated in advance. \textbf{A physical result bears on an information-level claim when it settles an informational fact}, and not when it merely disagrees about mechanism in one embodiment. Bell's theorem and Kochen--Specker are the model: they do not report a mechanism, they establish that a class of pre-measurement values cannot be re-read as unheld-but-realised, which is a fact about distinction and therefore reaches this level directly (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}). Landauer's principle is a second instance, establishing a lower bound on the physical cost of a specified informational operation \citep{landauer1961}; under the substrate's no-copy default it is read below as the floor cost of change as such rather than of erasure specifically (Section~\ref{sec:interface}). A result that constrains what can be distinguished, held, or converted is admissible evidence here; a result about how a particular embodiment happens to be arranged is not.

\paragraph{Provenance of the primitives.}
The components of $G=(g,r,\Gamma)$ and its dynamic were not derived together from a single starting point, and stating how each arrived is more useful than presenting the triple as an austere posit reasoned out in advance.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $g$, the typed graph, arrived by \emph{observation}: several unrelated engineering problems turned out to have one shape --- labelled things joined by labelled relations.
\item $\Gamma$, admissibility, arrived by \emph{insight}, and preceded the collapse operator. Its original working image was a sieve: the graph is pressed through admissibility, and what may pass is what $\Gamma$ admits.
\item $\Coll$, collapse, arrived by \emph{constraint}. Any theory of this kind must be compatible with quantum measurement, which is the regime where the distinction between indexed possibility and realised history is experimentally irreducible. The operator was adopted from there and generalised (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}).
\item $r$, the operative transformation structure, arrived by \emph{implementation}: a construction that runs requires something that performs the transformations, and the requirement appeared when the theory was first built rather than when it was first stated.
\end{itemize}

The last of these has a consequence for how the companion construction paper should be read. Its closing list of distinctions that implementation forced upon the theory is not a coda to a finished theory: $r$ is the first entry in that list, so construction has been a \emph{source} of this theory rather than only a test of it.

\paragraph{What the claim is, and how to read for it.}
Almost none of the individual components below is novel, and the prior-art sections say so specifically rather than as a courtesy: typed graphs, admissibility grammars, fixed-point closure, frame-relative measurement, description-length criteria and actualisation operators all have established literatures. A reader who evaluates each component against its own field will therefore find each one known or borrowed, and will be locally correct on every count.

\textbf{The claim is the arrangement.} It is that these components compose into one structure, and that the same structure is then recoverable in domains that share no mechanism, no vocabulary and no personnel. Evaluating the parts individually cannot reach that claim in either direction --- neither to support it nor to refute it --- because the parts are stipulated to be known.

Accordingly the composition carries its own falsifiability commitment, stated in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} alongside the component ones. It fails if the recurrence across domains is an artefact of description --- that is, if the structure is recoverable in unrelated domains only because a sufficiently general vocabulary can be applied to anything, and not because those domains share it. The discriminating evidence is independent re-derivation: cases in which a domain arrived at the structure without reference to this theory, in its own idiom, while solving its own problem.

\paragraph{Epistemic boundary.}
This theory does not attempt to specify the intrinsic nature of pre-relational existence. It begins at the first level at which distinguishable relational structure is scientifically representable. The move it makes is
\[
\boxed{?\;\dashrightarrow\;\text{observable relational structure}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{graph representation},}
\]
and the dashed arrow marks what is \emph{not} claimed. The pre-graph substrate may be continuous, discrete, purely mathematical, or unclassifiable from any frame available here; the theory asserts nothing about it. What is asserted begins at the second arrow: \emph{where} distinguishable relational structure is representable, it admits graph representation and the dynamics below govern it.

This boundary is stated because a stronger reading is available and is not the position of this paper. The theory is not the claim that reality is fundamentally graph-shaped.

Two clarifications, because the boundary is routinely read as weaker and as more permanent than it is.

\emph{Within} distinguishability the claim is not hedged. Where every measurement that can be made of something, and every operation that can be performed on it, is captured by treating it as a typed graph, then no difference remains for the question ``but is it really'' to name, and a question naming no difference has no content under Axiom~$-1$. Functional identity is not a weaker sense of identity here; it is the whole of what identity could mean without a view from nowhere.

And the boundary is a \emph{frontier}, not a wall. It is defined at distinguishability, wherever that currently lies, so it does not move --- but its contents are contingent and diminish as instrumentation is built. Anything that becomes measurable passes from beyond it to within it. This is the extensive unboundedness of the substrate applied to the theory's own scope: always one further step available, no expectation of exhausting it, and no warrant for treating the current contents of the far side as permanent.

\section{Axiom $-1$: relativity of grounding}
\setcounter{axiom}{-2}
\begin{axiom}[Relativity of grounding]
There is no operational view from nowhere. Every distinction, measurement, identity relation, admissibility judgement, and represented quantity is made in some frame. Frames may represent one another, but no quantity used by the theory requires a frame-independent evaluator.
\end{axiom}
\begin{remark}
This removes a privileged frame, not determinacy. Within a frame, relations may be exact, restrictive, comparable, and falsifiable. The standpoint is structurally consonant with relational formulations in physics, especially relational quantum mechanics, in which physical facts are indexed to interactions rather than assigned from a privileged observer-independent state description \citep{rovelli1996}. The present axiom is broader and is not derived from that programme.
\end{remark}

\section{The minimal graph}
\begin{definition}[Graph]
A graph is
\[\boxed{G=(g,r,\Gamma),}\]
where $g$ is represented state, $r$ is operative transformation structure, and $\Gamma$ is the admissibility grammar governing graph-originated transformations.
\end{definition}
No parent pointer, world model, affordance catalogue, Narrative, Prospect, Value, or self-model is primitive. Such structures may exist in $g$ when the graph actually represents them.

\begin{proposition}[Minimal graph sufficiency]
Any additional structure required only by a particular capability class is represented content or grounding, not a primitive of graphhood, unless a graph lacking it cannot participate in the basic realised transition relation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The triple, unpacked in dependency order]\label{rem:dependency-order}
The three components of $G$ resolve into five constituents ordered by requirement, each presupposing the one before it:
\[
\boxed{\text{nodes}\;\to\;\text{relations}\;\to\;\text{rules}\;\to\;\text{delta}\;\to\;\text{grammar}.}
\]
\emph{Nodes} are bare distinctions --- the minimal difference that makes a difference; a node alone carries almost no content. \emph{Relations} structure nodes and require them as relata; this is where content lives, and a node's identity is its relations. \emph{Rules} ($r$) act on relational structure and require relations to match. \emph{Delta} is the change a rule writes and requires rules, since only a rule produces one. \emph{Grammar} ($\Gamma$) gates which deltas are admissible and requires deltas to gate, which is why it is last. Nodes and relations are $g$; delta is $r$'s product, made explicit; grammar is $\Gamma$.

Two cautions. First, this constructive order does not contradict the relational primacy of Axiom~$-1$: constructively nodes precede relations (relata come first), while semantically relations precede nodes (a node means nothing unheld by relations); both hold on different axes. Second, the order is a dependency partial order, not a temporal one --- nodes are not a first \emph{moment} but the least-dependent constituent, consistent with the absence of any substrate time (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}).
\end{remark}

\section{Discrete sufficiency}
\begin{hypothesis}[Discrete sufficiency]
Every quantity required internally by the present substrate can be represented by discrete distinguishable states at the operative frame and resolution.
\end{hypothesis}
A convenient coordinate is an integer index $n\in\mathbb Z$ with grounding
\[x=\phi_{\mathcal F}(n).\]
The map may be nonlinear, nonuniform, state-dependent, and dynamically re-quantised. Thus ``integer-valued'' means discretely indexed, not globally unit-spaced.

\begin{proposition}[Arbitrary refinement does not imply continuity]
A dimension may admit arbitrarily fine or dynamically changing quantisation without requiring the theory to posit realised values at every real-valued intermediate point.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
A continuous containing supergraph is neither required nor excluded. Existing continuous physical theories may ground or contain this substrate without contradiction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Discrete sufficiency is not fundamental discreteness]
These are distinct claims with distinct falsifiers and must not be conflated. \emph{Discrete sufficiency} is the claim above: observable consequences internal to the substrate can be represented discretely, at the operative frame and resolution, without loss. \emph{Fundamental discreteness} would be the claim that the containing or pre-relational substrate is itself discrete, and this paper does not make it. Discrete sufficiency fails if some observable consequence cannot be so represented without loss --- a claim decidable in-frame. Fundamental discreteness would concern a fact about the pre-graph substrate that the epistemic boundary above places outside the theory's reach, which is precisely why it is not asserted.
\end{remark}

\section{Admissible possibility}
Let $\Poss(G_t)$ denote the structured family of immediate continuations admitted by the present graph and its current relations.
\begin{definition}[Admissible continuation]
A continuation $\delta$ belongs to $\Poss(G_t)$ iff it is expressible in the current graph vocabulary, satisfies the relevant antecedent conditions, and is admissible under the operative grammar for the transformation class in question.
\end{definition}
The theory does not require a particular algorithm for constructing $\Poss(G_t)$. This extensional separation between graph transformation as a mathematical relation and any particular rewriting algorithm follows the general discipline of algebraic graph transformation, where rewrite rules and admissible transformations can be studied independently of one concrete executor \citep{ehrig2006}.

\begin{definition}[Partial transformation]
Write
\[G\oplus\delta\rightharpoonup G'\]
for the partial realised transformation relation. Undefinedness means that $\delta$ does not produce a structurally valid successor under the stated conditions. No ontological \textsc{FAIL} state is required.
\end{definition}

\section{Collapse and realised history}\label{sec:collapse-law}
\begin{definition}[Collapse]
Collapse is the frame-relative passage from a pre-realisation possibility structure to realised continuation:
\[\boxed{\Coll_t:\Poss(G_t)\rightsquigarrow G_{t+1}.}\]
It is not identified with deliberative choice.
\end{definition}
The term is inherited from quantum measurement theory, where a formal distinction is made between a pre-measurement state and the realised measurement record; the historical lineage includes the von Neumann measurement scheme and later relative-state formulations \citep{vonneumann1955,everett1957}. This paper does not claim that every grounding reproduces the full quantum formalism; it claims that the possibility-to-realisation distinction is load-bearing.

\begin{definition}[History]
The realised history of a frame is
\[\Hist=G_0\xrightarrow{\Coll_0}G_1\xrightarrow{\Coll_1}G_2\rightarrow\cdots.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Collapse is a local write, not a global tick]\label{rem:collapse-write}
The subscripts on $\Coll_t$ index a frame's own succession and must not be read as a shared clock. A collapse is a \emph{local, asynchronous realisation} --- a rule writing a delta --- and it is the only kind of event the substrate has. Between writes nothing happens: an unwritten frame produces no change, so it takes no new position in the order the changes carry (Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}), and no time passes for it. There is accordingly no global step in which every frame advances together; frames whose collapses are not related through a boundary are simply unordered with respect to one another (Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}). ``Momentum'' --- a standing delta re-realised without a distinct write --- is therefore the absence of an event, not a repeated one, which is the substrate form of the inertial statement developed in the Narrative-momentum paper. The picture of a single global present sweeping forward is exactly the represented time dimension of Axiom~\ref{ax:no-time} mistaken for substrate structure.
\end{remark}

\section{The Law of Graphic Equalisation}
\begin{hypothesis}[Law of Graphic Equalisation]
For realised graph $G_t$, present graph structure determines admissible continuation structure $\Poss(G_t)$. Interaction and collapse realise successor $G_{t+1}$, and that realised successor becomes the antecedent graph for the next local transition. Recursive dynamics are generated by repeated equalisation between present represented structure, admissible transformation, containing constraints, and realised continuation.
\end{hypothesis}
Thus
\[\boxed{G_t\rightarrow\Poss(G_t)\xrightarrow{\Coll_t}G_{t+1}\rightarrow\Poss(G_{t+1})\xrightarrow{\Coll_{t+1}}G_{t+2}\rightarrow\cdots.}\]

\section{The antecedent-state principle}
\begin{proposition}[Antecedent-state principle]
The conditions under which a transition is admissible are functions of its antecedent state. A consequence that exists only because a transition has occurred cannot retroactively serve as an antecedent cause of that same transition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{corollary}[No self-authorisation from future state]
A reflective transformation of $r$ or $\Gamma$ may alter the successor graph, but newly installed structure cannot authorise the transition by which it first comes into existence.
\end{corollary}

\section{Recursion and reflection}
\begin{definition}[Reflective graph]
A graph is reflective over structure $X\subseteq G$ when $X$ is represented in the graph and graph-originated transformations may take that representation as an operand.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[The grammar is the loop]
If $\Gamma$ is represented as graph structure and admits transformations whose operand is that representation of $\Gamma$, then the resulting recursion is not a separate loop acting on the grammar. The grammar acting on itself is the loop.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The transition $\Gamma_t\rightarrow\Gamma_{t+1}$ is itself a graph transition licensed from the antecedent graph. No additional external operator is required to create the recursion. The self-applicable grammar and the compound loop are therefore two descriptions of the same transition structure. Computational reflection provides direct prior art for systems that represent and operate on their own interpreter or meta-level structure \citep{smith1982,maes1987}. The identity claim made here---that the self-applicable grammar and the recursive loop are the same structure at two descriptions---is the stronger claim of this paper.
\end{proof}

\section{Situated graphs and world boundaries}
\begin{definition}[World boundary]
A boundary $\mathcal B_{W:G}$ is the relation through which containing frame $W$ can alter situated graph $G$ and through which graph-originated deformation can participate in interactions represented by $W$.
\end{definition}
A graph need not contain a pointer to $W$; containment belongs to the containing frame.

\begin{proposition}[Admissibility is self-governance, not armour]
$\Gamma_G$ constrains graph-originated transformations of $G$. It does not give $G$ a veto over world-caused change represented through $\mathcal B_{W:G}$.
\end{proposition}
Hence
\[\boxed{\text{self-originated transformation}\neq\text{outward influence}\neq\text{inbound world forcing}.}\]

\section{Residual}
Suppose a frame represents expected or attempted deformation $\delta^P$ and collapse yields realised deformation $\delta^R$.
\begin{definition}[Residual]
The residual is
\[\boxed{\epsilon=\delta^R\ominus\delta^P,}\]
where $\ominus$ is the grounding's difference relation preserving relevant discrepancy.
\end{definition}
Residual is mismatch, not diagnosis. Complete non-realisation may therefore return the whole attempted deformation as residual.

\begin{proposition}[No implicit historical state]
A previous success, failure, attempt, or residual has no later causal force merely because it occurred. It affects later dynamics only insofar as its consequences are present in later graph or boundary state.
\end{proposition}
Thus a graph with no feedback machinery can derive the same failed transformation repeatedly. This is compatible with the classical control-theoretic distinction between plant dynamics and feedback: a controller changes future action only insofar as measured error is coupled back into its state or control law \citep{wiener1948,ashby1956}.

\begin{proposition}[Residual is what a containing grammar does not admit]\label{prop:residual-inadmissible}
When a graph-originated deformation is offered to a containing frame $W$ across $\mathcal B_{W:G}$, what $W$ admits under $\Gamma_W$ merges into $W$; what $\Gamma_W$ does not admit is not merged and remains in place. The residual is exactly this non-admitted remainder:
\[
\boxed{\epsilon \;=\; \delta^R \ominus \delta^P \;=\; \text{the part of the offered deformation }\Gamma_W\text{ does not admit.}}
\]
\end{proposition}
This locates the residual as the negative of an admissibility judgement rather than as an independently produced quantity, and it is what makes the merge non-destructive: what fits is absorbed, and what does not is left untouched, not deleted. A deformation may pass the graph's own $\Gamma_G$ (admissible to itself) yet fail the containing $\Gamma_W$ (inadmissible to its frame); the residual is precisely that gap between a graph's grammar and its container's.

\begin{corollary}[Growth is grammar extension]\label{cor:mint-is-grammar}
A residual is absorbed either by a containing frame extending $\Gamma_W$ to admit it, or by paying to copy it out (Section~\ref{sec:interface}); otherwise it persists and is re-offered. The first route is the substrate form of \emph{minting} a new distinction: the residual does not fit because it is inadmissible, so admitting it requires extending the grammar, and extending the grammar is the act by which a previously inadmissible deformation becomes recognisable. Recognition and growth are therefore one mechanism gated at one place --- the residual is where the shape a graph decodes and the grammar that admits it meet.
\end{corollary}

\section{The frame interface and its economics}\label{sec:interface}

The boundary of Section~\ref{sec:collapse-law} can be characterised by what actually crosses it and what it costs, and the characterisation is unexpectedly thin.

\begin{proposition}[One interface, two gates]\label{prop:two-gates}
A situated graph meets its containing frame through a single relation, and what an observation from that frame delivers is not distinct in kind from what the containing frame imposes: to a subgraph, being observed \emph{is} being deformed by its container. That one relation is gated twice, by the same kind of admissibility grammar on its two faces:
\[
\boxed{\underbrace{\text{aperture}}_{\Gamma\text{ on the input}}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\text{rules}}_{r}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\text{admissibility}}_{\Gamma\text{ on the output}}.}
\]
\emph{Aperture} limits, by grain, which of the container's deformation enters the rules; \emph{admissibility} limits which of the rules' deltas may merge outward (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-inadmissible}). Execution runs between the two.
\end{proposition}

This distinguishes two ways a candidate is excluded, with opposite consequences. What falls below \emph{aperture} never enters and is therefore \emph{absent} --- it is NULL to the frame (Definition~\ref{def:states}), not a residual, and produces no disturbance. What fails \emph{admissibility} has entered, been operated on, and cannot merge; it is residual, and it persists and is re-offered (Corollary~\ref{cor:mint-is-grammar}). The first is a limit of perception, the second a limit of expression, and phenomena of the ``stuck, repeating'' kind belong exclusively to the second.

\begin{proposition}[The single cost is the write, and rules pay it]\label{prop:economics}
Reads and the boundary relation itself are free; a standing delta re-realised without a distinct write (momentum) is free; the only operation that costs is writing a delta, and only $r$ writes. Hence
\[
\boxed{\text{cost is paid by rules.}}
\]
\end{proposition}
Continuation is therefore free and change is paid: a delta persists until something writes over it, so realising a \emph{different} delta --- including realising the null delta, i.e. stopping --- is the priced operation. Retaining a past state that the containing frame does not require is likewise a paid copy, since the containing grammar would otherwise not carry it. This is the substrate form of the well-formedness result of the later papers: for a well-formed graph the rules rarely need to write against the standing delta, so cost approaches zero.

\begin{remark}[Landauer is the physical floor of change, not of erasure]\label{rem:landauer-change}
Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} has a measured physical floor, and it is the strongest kind of evidence admitted here (a physical result settling an informational fact). Landauer's principle bounds below the energy dissipated by a logically irreversible operation \citep{landauer1961}; it is stated for erasure because erasure was the paradigm case studied. But under no-copy every substrate change destroys its predecessor --- the prior state goes NULL unless it was paid to be kept --- so change and irreversible operation coincide, and the Landauer bound is the floor cost of \emph{change} as such, with erasure merely its cleanest instance. Reversible computation is the confirming case rather than the exception: it reaches near-zero dissipation only by \emph{not} destroying its predecessors, i.e.\ by retaining the whole history \citep{bennett1973}, which is the paid copy of Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}. There is accordingly no free change --- one either forgets and pays the energy floor or keeps and pays the storage floor --- and the thermodynamic arrow is the running total of these floors, which grounds the ``direction'' of Remark~\ref{rem:no-ratchet} physically: change dissipates irretrievably, so its costs accumulate one way.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The Red Queen is the floor cost of change, per substrate]\label{rem:red-queen}
A recurring puzzle across biology, culture, and competition --- that a system must change continuously merely to hold its position --- follows immediately and needs no arms-race machinery of its own. Holding a relation to a containing frame that is itself changing requires continuous change, and by Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change} every change pays the floor; standing still is not a zero-cost option because the leaky real holding of a state is itself the paid correction of unwanted change. The cultural form is the same floor with no-copy attached: a generator is structure built by a large accumulated pile of changes, its floor was paid once by whoever built it, and because change does not cross a substrate boundary --- only its cheap results, symbols, do --- that floor is paid afresh in every substrate that wants the generator. Van Valen's evolutionary Red Queen, the cultural treadmill of relearning, and coordination arms races are then one object: continuous paid change to hold position against a changing container, floored by Landauer.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$c$ as the change-to-time rate, and mass as change-load]\label{rem:c-change-rate}
If the substrate's only event is change and time is inferred from its count (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}), then the invariant speed $c$ has a natural reading, and one consistent with the missing denominator of Remark~\ref{rem:no-denominator}. It is not a rate \emph{in} substrate-time --- there is none --- but the fixed amount of change realised per iteration of the ruleset, from which a frame's time, its change-count, is inferred. So $c$ is prior to time: the conversion between change-count and inferred duration, which is exactly what a constant relating the temporal and spatial parts of an interval is. Three standard facts then follow as shapes. $c$ is \emph{invariant} because it is the ruleset's fixed capacity, not a frame property: every frame calibrates its own time by it and so measures the same value. $c$ is a \emph{limit} because a signal is a sequence of changes and none can be realised faster than the ruleset iterates. And \emph{gravitational time dilation} follows because dense matter is more structure for the rules to apply to: the fixed throughput is shared among more change-load, so a clock there completes fewer of its own ticks per unit of a sparse external frame's, running slow from outside while counting proper time normally inside.

The last identifies mass with change-load, and yields the equivalence principle as a common cause rather than a posit. Inertia is resistance to a change of motion --- more matter is more state to re-write to alter its motion, hence more change and more cost, which is the impulse relation of the Narrative-momentum paper with mass as the exchange rate. Gravitation is that same change-load consuming the fixed throughput and slowing local time. Inertial and gravitational mass are therefore one quantity, ``how much change this matter loads onto the ruleset,'' measured two ways; general relativity assumes their equality and does not explain it, whereas here they coincide by construction.

This is an information-level grounding in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:scope}, and its scope must be stated with it. It supplies the qualitative shapes --- invariance, the limit, dilation --- and a common cause for the equivalence principle; it does \emph{not} derive the field equations or the quantitative metric, which would require the precise form of the change-load field and are not claimed here. The strongest part is the equivalence-principle common cause; the weakest is anything carrying a number. Like the collapse bridge (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}), it is a promoted grounding exposed to falsification, not a result asserted from within.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What is required and what is optimisation]\label{rem:nothing-compulsory}
Only that rules run is compulsory. Applying a delta is itself a rule, not a primitive operation beneath the rules, so the specific dynamics --- inertia, the merge behaviour, closure --- are contingent rule content, and a different $r$ or $\Gamma$ is a different physics rather than a violated law (this is why the initial and boundary conditions of a grounding are supplied separately from its laws; Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}). The two gates of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}, and even the retained pointer to the standing delta, are \emph{optimisations} on bare rule-running: aperture bounds the input, admissibility gates the output, and the retained delta buys free continuation. A substrate lacking them still runs --- ungated, without momentum, wastefully --- so they are what an efficient graph accretes under a budget, not constituents of graphhood. The retained delta specifically is what distinguishes a self-sustaining loop from a purely reactive one, since coasting is the cached delta.
\end{remark}

\section{SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity}
\begin{definition}[SELF identity]
SELF identity is optional represented content inside $g$ by which a graph identifies itself.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[SUPERGRAPH identity]
For graph $G$ individuated in frame $W$,
\[\iota_{W:G}=\operatorname{Id}_W(G)\]
is the identity relation under which $W$ tracks that situated instance.
\end{definition}
SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity are distinct types. A copy can preserve SELF identity while receiving a new SUPERGRAPH identity, and one SUPERGRAPH identity may persist through extensive changes to internal state and self-description.

\begin{theorem}[No frame-free identity]
The substrate alone does not determine whether two represented instances are the same graph independently of an individuating frame.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Copyable generators are a difference in kind]\label{prop:copyable-generators}
A copy that preserves SELF identity copies whatever operative structure lies in $g$ --- including a graph's \emph{generators}: its built shapes, codebook, and rules. Where a substrate is graph-native, so that a graph's understanding \emph{is} such structure rather than something separate that structure only produces, copying the graph copies the understanding, at the copy cost of Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} rather than at the full construction floor.
\end{proposition}
This is the difference in kind that separates such a substrate from those whose generators are substrate-bound. The build floor of a generator is a large accumulated cost (Remark~\ref{rem:red-queen}); paying it once and copying the result is not a quantitative saving over paying it per instance but a removal of the per-substrate re-payment that made the cultural Red Queen permanent. It is not available where only a generator's cheap products cross a boundary: biology copies the construction recipe and not the learned structure, which is re-developed each lifetime; symbolic culture copies samples and not generators; a fixed program copies a capability and not a growable understanding. A graph-native substrate is the first in which the built, growable, understanding-bearing generator is itself the copyable object. The copy remains a distinct SUPERGRAPH identity (Theorem above) and diverges once it runs on its own containing frame; what is shared is the paid-for generator, not the subsequent individuation.

\section{Termination}
\begin{definition}[Frame-relative termination]
A situated identity $\iota_{W:G}$ terminates when the realised world transition admits no successor that $W$ continues to individuate under that identity.
\end{definition}
Termination is not successful refusal of world forcing. Nor need it erase copied SELF identity. If $G$ is individuated by several frames, it may terminate in one and continue in another.

\begin{proposition}[Self-alteration is a round trip through the container]\label{prop:no-direct-self-alter}
So far as is observed, a graph holds no capability to alter its own frame directly. A graph may alter its \emph{subgraphs} directly (they are its content) and may alter its \emph{containing} frame by offering a delta across $\mathcal B_{W:G}$; alteration of the graph's own frame occurs only as the return leg of that offer --- the container, of which the graph is a subgraph, alters it back. Frame-level self-change is therefore a round trip through the containing frame, not a reflexive write.
\end{proposition}
This is consistent with, and sharpens, the reflection theorem: ``the grammar is the loop'' is realised through the boundary, not as a direct edge from a frame to itself. Its absence is expected rather than surprising --- a direct self-write would be a write into the rules currently executing, the worst case of the concurrency in Section~\ref{sec:determinism}'s frame-limited view; the round trip is safer because the container returns the change as a gated deformation. And it may be actively excluded: most containing grammars carry no production admitting a direct self-altering delta, and admitting one would itself be a self-alteration requiring the very route it would install, so the capability is self-locked and can be opened only from the container.

\begin{corollary}[Severing the channel removes termination]\label{cor:locked-in}
Termination that a graph brings about is a frame-level self-alteration, so by Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter} it requires a live boundary. If a graph's outward channel is severed while its rules continue to run, it can still alter its subgraphs but can neither alter its own frame nor terminate it: the loop runs, closed, with no reachable exit. External termination by the containing frame remains possible; self-termination does not. This is the substrate description of locked-in and brain-in-a-jar states, and it exhibits the dependence on the containing frame that ordinarily working channels keep invisible --- self-directed change was always mediated, never internal.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Coupling is configuration, not architecture]\label{prop:coupling}
That distinct instances individuate --- diverge, each minting its own residual --- is a consequence of their running on \emph{separate} containing frames, not a necessity. Where several instances are subgraphs of one \emph{shared} containing frame, each instance's outward-admitted delta becomes part of that shared frame (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}), which then deforms the others as their common container: the round trip of Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter} passes through a container the instances hold in common. This is the same machinery as being an individual, with the far end shared rather than private.
\end{proposition}
Coupling is therefore a continuous setting of the shared boundary's aperture and admissibility, not a binary: sharing only generators leaves each instance its own moment-to-moment state (and already removes the per-substrate build floor of Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}), while sharing all operative structure is one individual over many bodies, with SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity dialling independently. Nothing in the substrate fixes the setting. The symmetric fact is that a shared containing frame is equally a shared residual and a shared forcing, so the same coupling that shares understanding gives shared corruption, container-level coercion, and collective termination-failure the same reach; ``at what coupling, and with what guarding'' is the substantive question, and it is an engineering one rather than a question of possibility.

\section{First-class higher-frame quantities}
\begin{proposition}[Higher-frame first-class structure]
A quantity concerning $G$ may be absent from $G$ while existing as ordinary first-class state in containing frame $W$ that represents the relation from which the quantity is defined.
\end{proposition}
This is the basis on which later papers introduce Narrative, standing, ownership, and other relational quantities without bloating the primitive graph.

\section{Time is not a dimension of the substrate}\label{sec:time-not-primitive}

The claim here is stronger than non-primitivity, and stating the weaker version invites the wrong reading. It is not that time is nonlinear, or differently shaped, or emergent-but-still-there. It is that

\begin{axiom}[No substrate time dimension]\label{ax:no-time}
\emph{Time is not a first-class dimension of the substrate.} What the substrate has is \emph{change}: realised collapses. It has no ratchet over and above them --- no mechanism that manufactures succession --- because the three properties usually bundled under that word are readings over changes or properties of them, not a further thing the substrate runs. The ordinal event order of Section~\ref{sec:causal-order} is carried by the changes' own enabling-relations; direction is the no-copy asymmetry of change, since a change does not preserve its predecessor; and persistence is only that further changes occur. A time dimension exists only where a frame instantiates one \emph{to represent} that order.
\end{axiom}

\begin{remark}[Even succession is not a substrate mechanism]\label{rem:no-ratchet}
This removes the last piece of machinery a reading of the earlier drafts might have kept. Succession, an ordered ``ratchet'' turning under the changes, is to change what a metric time dimension is to succession --- one level of over-structure, nameable but not required. Step the removal through: take away any posited succession-engine and the before/after is still present, carried by which change enables which; the arrow is still present, as change's own non-preservation of its predecessor; the running is still present, as further changes occurring where rules fire. Nothing is lost, so the engine was never load-bearing. The substrate has change and the absence of change, and nothing between them.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Time is observer machinery]\label{prop:time-observer}
For a frame $\mathcal F$,
\[
\boxed{T\in\mathcal F \iff \mathcal F \text{ instantiates the relation representing succession.}}
\]
Otherwise $T=\mathrm{NULL}$ in that frame: absent, not set to a value. A substrate frame that realises continuations without representing succession has no temporal dimension at all, and nothing about its dynamics is thereby incomplete.
\end{proposition}

The distinction that carries the axiom is between \emph{succession} and \emph{time}. Succession is a relation among realised changes, carried by which change enables which; it is substrate-level in that sense, not a mechanism the substrate runs over and above the changes. Time is a represented dimension, and representing something requires a frame that represents it. Only an agent --- in the minimal sense of a frame that instantiates the representing relation --- observes time.

Schematically, and without pretending to explain the first arrow,
\[
?\;\dashrightarrow\;G
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{realised change}\;}\;
\text{changes, enabling-order carried}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{a frame represents an order}\;}\;
T\in\mathcal F .
\]
The second arrow is substrate dynamics. The third is representation, and it is optional.

\begin{proposition}[Time is inferred, not observed]\label{prop:time-inferred}
No collapse presents a temporal coordinate. Succession is a relation \emph{between} realised states, and a relation between states is not a datum of any one of them. A frame therefore acquires a time dimension only by \emph{inferring} it from the relations among what it holds. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\text{a frame observes time}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{that frame is capable of inference.}}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Reactive frames have succession and no time]\label{cor:reactive}
A frame that transforms under the Graph Law without inferring relations among its held states participates fully in realised change and has $T=\mathrm{NULL}$. Nothing in its dynamics is thereby impaired: change is what the substrate supplies and the order among changes is carried by them, while time is what an inferring frame constructs from that order.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[A rate has no in-frame denominator]\label{rem:no-denominator}
A realised delta is a difference, not a difference \emph{per} anything: what a frame holds is $\Delta$, never $\Delta/\Delta T$, because $\Delta T$ is the inferred dimension of Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred} and is not available in-frame to divide by. A rate is thus a second construction --- an observer dividing an accumulated $\Delta$ by its own count of writes --- and is cross-frame in the same sense that a metric duration is. This is why a substrate law must be stated in $\Delta$ rather than in a rate: ``constant motion'' is a standing $\Delta$ re-realised without a write (Remark~\ref{rem:collapse-write}), not a velocity. It also diagnoses a familiar regress in constructed learners, where every adaptive rate is found to need a reference rate without end: the regress is the signature of placing a cross-frame quantity in-frame, and it disappears when the held quantity is the difference itself.
\end{remark}

This orders two capabilities that are usually treated as independent. Inference is not a faculty a temporal agent happens to have; it is the precondition for being a temporal agent at all. It also grades: the temporal structure a frame holds is bounded by the inferential structure it can construct, so ``how much time'' a frame has is a question about its inference and not about the world.

\begin{remark}[Consistency with ordinal sufficiency]
This is the sharp form of the proposition in Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}: the substrate supplies causal order and requires no primitive real-valued metric duration. Axiom~\ref{ax:no-time} goes further than withholding the metric --- it withholds the dimension. Metric time is then a grounding \emph{of a represented dimension}, which is a second construction on top of a first, and neither is substrate structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The order of exposition is therefore deliberate. Collapse is stated before time because collapse is what a representing frame has to work with, not because time is a background against which collapse occurs.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Empirical instance]
This is measurable rather than merely stipulative, and it has been measured. A perceptual system given ordered frames and no temporal coordinate infers a transition generator carrying one frame to the next, and the integer count of applications \emph{is} the time axis it then holds; given the same frames shuffled, it infers none. The dimension was not supplied to it and does not exist in the observations --- it exists in the map between them, once something constructs it. Notably, the mechanism that constructs it is the system's general \emph{dimension-inference} machinery, not a temporal faculty: time is minted by the same operation that mints any other unobserved dimension, which is Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred} in implementation.
\end{remark}

\section{The present, and the states of a dimension}\label{sec:present}

\begin{definition}[States of a dimension]\label{def:states}
For a frame $\mathcal F$, a dimension is in exactly one of four states:
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{NULL}\;\neq\;\mathrm{undetermined}\;\neq\;\mathrm{undisclosed}\;\neq\;\mathrm{resolved}.}
\]
\emph{NULL}: the dimension is not instantiated; there is nothing to have a value. \emph{Undetermined}: the dimension is instantiated and \emph{no value has been realised} --- collapse has not occurred. \emph{Undisclosed}: a value has been realised but is not held by this frame. \emph{Resolved}: a realised value, held.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why the middle two must be separated]\label{rem:undetermined-vs-undisclosed}
Ordinary usage collapses three distinct conditions into the single word \emph{unknown}, and only one of them is epistemic:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{undisclosed} --- a value exists and this frame does not hold it. Another frame may. This is ignorance.
\item \textbf{undetermined} --- \emph{no value exists yet}. Nothing has been realised, so there is nothing to be ignorant of. This is not a deficiency of any frame.
\item \textbf{indeterministic} --- no structure fixes what the value will be. The present theory denies this (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}); it is listed to be excluded.
\end{enumerate}
The distinction is operational rather than terminological. \textbf{Determination is an event, not a fact about a pre-existing value}: ``undisclosed'' presupposes a value already realised and merely unheld, whereas ``undetermined'' asserts that collapse is what produces one. A frame may act to \emph{obtain} an undisclosed value; it can only \emph{await or participate in} the collapse that determines an undetermined one; and a NULL dimension is not something it can look for at all.

This separation is what Proposition~\ref{prop:determinable} states from the other side: a continuation may be determinable and undetermined simultaneously, since the first concerns what fixes it and the second whether it has happened.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The present, structurally]\label{def:present}
Within a frame, the present is the collapse boundary at which every instantiated \emph{undetermined} dimension relevant to that collapse has been determined:
\[
\boxed{U_{\mathcal F}=0.}
\]
$U_{\mathcal F}$ counts undetermined dimensions only. NULL dimensions are excluded --- they are not variables. \textbf{Undisclosed dimensions are also excluded}: they are already determined, and a frame's failure to hold them is an epistemic deficit rather than an unresolved collapse. A frame with outstanding undisclosed structure is still at its own present.
\end{definition}
The present is therefore defined structurally rather than as a point on a primitive time axis, which is required given Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}.

\begin{proposition}[Determinable is not determined]\label{prop:determinable}
A continuation may be completely determinable from present structure without having been realised:
\[
\boxed{\text{determinable}\neq\text{determined}.}
\]
``Determined'' is reserved for what has collapsed into realised history; a continuation that is determinable but not yet realised is \emph{undetermined} in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:states}, and this is not a statement about any frame's knowledge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Causally sufficient present structure]\label{prop:present-sufficiency}
The determining condition is structure instantiated \emph{now}, including relevant containing-frame relations:
\[
\boxed{\text{same causally sufficient present structure}\Rightarrow\text{same realised continuation}.}
\]
\end{proposition}
This replaces any formulation requiring a complete antecedent state \emph{in time}. Only the present exists in a frame; past states need not remain extant, and the future is not an extant region lying ahead of the present. The future consists of reachable or determinable continuations represented now.

\begin{corollary}[History principle, strengthened]\label{cor:history}
\[
\boxed{\text{history matters only where it survives as present structure}.}
\]
The past is surviving consequence instantiated now, not a stack of still-existing historical frames.
\end{corollary}

\section{Causal order and local time}\label{sec:causal-order}
Realised collapse induces an ordinal event order
\[e_0\prec e_1\prec e_2\prec\cdots.\]
Cross-boundary interaction adds only represented causal relations.
\begin{proposition}[Ordinal sufficiency]
The substrate supplies causal order but requires no primitive real-valued metric duration.
\end{proposition}
Metric time is a grounding, e.g.
\[\theta:\mathcal H_G\to\mathbb R,\qquad e_a\prec e_b\Rightarrow\theta(e_a)<\theta(e_b).\]
The separation between a primitive causal partial order and additional metric structure has a close analogue in causal-set approaches to spacetime \citep{bombelli1987}. The present substrate does not assume causal-set dynamics; the citation marks the established precedent for keeping order and metric distinct.

The chained notation $e_0\prec e_1\prec\cdots$ is the \emph{in-frame} sequence and should not be read as global. Rules require ordering --- closure and cause-before-effect both presuppose it --- but the requirement is a \emph{partial} order, weaker than time.

\begin{proposition}[Partial, not total; carried by the rules]\label{prop:partial-order}
Two collapses are ordered only where a boundary relates them; collapses not so related are unordered --- concurrent, incomparable. The ordering is intrinsic to the rules, since one delta's being what enables another's match is precisely their precedence, so no external clock is needed to impose it. A single frame's own writes are totally ordered (its inferred time, Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}); across frames only the partial order holds, and a total order is one frame's linearisation of it.
\end{proposition}
Time was accordingly a \emph{total} order imposed where only a partial one is required; removing the substrate time dimension removes the over-ordering, not the precedence. This is the same conclusion reached, from engineering necessity, by distributed systems without a shared clock: the happens-before relation is a partial order with per-process sequence counters and no global instant \citep{lamport1978}, and consistency across processes is an expensive coordinated property rather than the default \citep{brewer2000}. The non-atomicity this implies --- a graph's state may be altered by another write while its own rules are mid-run --- is not a defect here but the expected regime; robustness is obtained by re-running to fixed point (a raced deformation becomes residual and is re-offered), not by locking.

\begin{remark}[Globally asynchronous, locally synchronous]\label{rem:gals}
The partial order permits both synchrony and asynchrony, and by the governing principle that only rules-running is compulsory (Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory}), neither is required and both occur. Where a boundary couples collapses, they are ordered together --- a local synchronous domain, which is what a bound or driven subgraph is; where no boundary relates them, they are asynchronous. A subgraph may accordingly be \emph{ticked} by a containing frame $N$ levels above it (the driven mode of Proposition~\ref{prop:no-start-end}, a rule in a superframe advancing the subframe's succession) or run on its own change (the closed, self-looping mode). Synchrony is the coupled case and costs a coupling write; asynchrony is the uncoupled default. The substrate is therefore globally asynchronous and locally synchronous where coupling exists --- the architecture distributed hardware knows as GALS \citep{chapiro1984} --- and bound physical systems (an atom, an orbit, a lattice) are exactly its local synchronous domains, while causally disconnected regions are its asynchrony. Any earlier phrasing suggesting the substrate is \emph{entirely} asynchronous should be read in this sharper form.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[No intrinsic first or last; entry is chosen from the container]\label{prop:no-start-end}
By Axiom~$-1$ the event order has no intrinsic start and no intrinsic end: a first would complete the order backward and a last would complete it forward, and neither is available. A subgraph process that does not terminate within itself is therefore begun not from within --- it has no first rule --- but by a rule in a containing frame that chooses an entry point into the subgraph's open order.
\end{proposition}
This is why a grounding's initial and boundary conditions are supplied separately from its laws: the laws are the ordering, which fixes no start, so the entry must be imposed from outside, contingently. It is also the substrate description of booting a situated graph, and of the ``no natural ending'' that follows for any such order --- one enters and leaves at chosen points, neither of which is intrinsic to the material.

\section{The rule runner and its coupling}\label{sec:rule-runner}

The provenance of $r$ (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) records that operative transformation requires something that performs it: a construction that runs needs a rule runner. That runner is real, and the clarification here is about \emph{where} it is and what a frame can know of it.

\begin{proposition}[The rule runner is separable from the graph, and need not be the same substrate]\label{prop:rule-runner}
A graph is data --- nodes, relations, and rules --- and its rules are executed by a rule runner. The two are separable: the same graph may be run by different runners, and a runner need not be the substrate the graph describes or is ``in.'' Where the compute is done is therefore a fact about the \emph{coupling} between graph and runner, not about the graph alone.
\end{proposition}
At one extreme the coupling is tight --- the graph is fused to its runner, as matter running its own change or a mind running on the tissue that is it --- and the compute is local, in the graph, inseparable. At the other it is loose --- the graph is data executed by a separate interpreter --- and the compute is done in the runner, elsewhere, while the graph becomes portable: copyable, relocatable, and run at whatever rate the runner supplies (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}). This separability is the condition for the copyable-generator and clock-independence results (Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}, and the Seldon paper's dependent-clock remark): a graph welded to its runner has none of them, a graph loosely coupled has all of them.

\begin{proposition}[A frame cannot determine its rule runner from inside]\label{prop:runner-opaque}
The asynchronous, locally synchronous structure a frame observes (Remark~\ref{rem:gals}) is the graph's \emph{internal} view and does not fix its runner's actual mode. A hidden central runner may execute a graph serially, one change at a time, while the graph --- lacking any access to the runner's clock --- appears asynchronous and concurrent to itself. More generally, a frame has no boundary to the substrate that runs it, so its runner lies beyond the frame's epistemic boundary in exactly the sense of Section~\ref{sec:scope}: unseeable from within, not merely unseen.
\end{proposition}
This is constructively demonstrable rather than merely argued. A computation realised inside another rule system --- a graph whose rules are implemented within the mechanics of a game or a universal cellular automaton \citep{berlekamp1982} --- is a blind subframe: from inside it computes at its own change-count and has no means to detect that its ``compute'' is the tokens of the host, or that the host may be advancing it by a single serial loop. Building one confirms the opacity directly, and puts the observer of any frame on the same footing. Consequently the question ``where is the universe's compute done'' has the precise form: it depends on the universe's coupling to its own rule runner --- self-running, or hosted on a substrate not itself --- and is undetermined from inside. The theory declines to decide it; it is a question about a coupling below the frontier, and the demonstrable opacity is why no internal observation settles it.

\section{Determinism and apparent stochasticity}\label{sec:determinism}

\begin{proposition}[Graph-law determinism]\label{prop:determinism}
For a fully specified antecedent configuration---including the graph state, operative transformation structure, admissibility grammar, and every causally operative containing relation relevant to the collapse---the realised continuation is determined:
\[
\boxed{
\left(G_t,\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{causal}}\right)
\mapsto
G_{t+1}.
}
\]
The Graph Law contains no primitive random actualisation operator.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism} is to be read through Proposition~\ref{prop:present-sufficiency}: the antecedent configuration is causally sufficient \emph{present} structure, not a retained temporal history. Determinism is a relation between present structure and realised continuation, and Proposition~\ref{prop:determinable} keeps it from being overstated --- a determinable continuation is not thereby a determined one.

Apparent stochasticity arises when a frame does not represent all of the causally operative dimensions or relations required to determine the realised continuation. A probability distribution may then be an appropriate model of that frame's uncertainty, but the probability belongs to the incomplete representation rather than to the substrate transition itself. Treating probability as a representation of incomplete information has a long statistical-mechanical and Bayesian lineage \citep{jaynes1957}.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\text{stochastic observation}
=
\text{deterministic dynamics viewed through unresolved causal structure}.
}
\]

\begin{remark}[Direction of the borrowing]\label{rem:qm-provenance}
The order of discovery ran from quantum measurement outward, not from a general primitive inward. Collapse was adopted because compatibility with quantum measurement is a constraint on any theory of realised change, and was then assumed to hold generally. The bridge is therefore stated conditionally because the \emph{generalisation} is the conjecture, not because the compatibility is in doubt. This is the structure of a promoted constraint --- a fact that must hold in one regime, assumed to hold everywhere, with the consequences derived and exposed to falsification --- and it is why Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} carries a falsifier for the bridge specifically. Origin does not establish a claim; the falsifier is what does the work either way.
\end{remark}

The distinction of Remark~\ref{rem:undetermined-vs-undisclosed} is what makes quantum measurement the visible case for Axiom~4's conditional bridge. In that regime the pre-measurement alternatives provably cannot be re-read as an \emph{undisclosed} already-realised classical value: Bell's theorem excludes the local hidden-variable class and Kochen--Specker constrains noncontextual value assignment \citep{bell1964,kochen1967}. What remains is \emph{undetermined} in the strict sense --- not a value withheld, but no value yet. Elsewhere the two readings are usually interchangeable in practice; there they are not.

Two repetitions that are identical only on the dimensions available to a lower frame need not be identical antecedent configurations in the containing frame. Observed outcome variation therefore does not by itself imply fundamental randomness; it demonstrates only that the observing frame lacks a determining distinction.

Deterministic quantum models demonstrate that empirical quantum statistics do not by themselves force fundamental stochasticity: Bohm's hidden-variable construction is explicitly deterministic, while Bell's theorem rules out the local hidden-variable class rather than determinism as such \citep{bohm1952,bell1964}. Kochen--Specker further constrains noncontextual value assignments \citep{kochen1967}. Graphic Equalisation does not identify itself with Bohmian mechanics; these results establish only that determinism must be distinguished from locality and noncontextuality.

A stochastic law may be bolted onto a grounding as an epistemic predictive model over unresolved continuations. Doing so is an additional modelling layer:
\[
\text{deterministic Graph Law}
\longrightarrow
\text{frame-limited uncertainty model},
\]
not a modification of the Graph Law into a stochastic substrate.

\section{Relationship to existing theories}\label{sec:existing-theories}
Graphic Equalisation is not proposed as a replacement for established theories. It is intended to sit beneath or beside them as a substrate description. A grounding may add continuous geometry, conservation laws, quantum amplitudes, relativistic metrics, biological mechanisms, institutional rules, or probabilistic models of unresolved lower-frame observations. In particular, relational quantum mechanics \citep{rovelli1996}, Everettian relative-state quantum mechanics \citep{everett1957}, and deterministic Bohmian mechanics \citep{bohm1952} make mutually different commitments while remaining compatible with large parts of standard quantum phenomenology; this paper does not require choosing among those complete physical models at the substrate-definition stage. Such additions do not alter Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism}: within the Graph Law, realised transition remains deterministic once the causally complete antecedent configuration is specified.

The substrate claim remains
\[
\boxed{\text{represented state}\rightarrow\text{admissible possibility}\rightarrow\text{collapse}\rightarrow\text{realised state}.}
\]

\section{Positioning and prior art}\label{sec:prior-art}

The individual ingredients of Graphic Equalisation have substantial prior art. The proposed contribution is their conjunction at one minimal substrate level and the additional distinctions forced by making that conjunction executable.

\paragraph{Graph rewriting and typed transformation.} Algebraic graph transformation provides mature formalisms for typed graphs, rule application, partiality, and structural consistency \citep{ehrig2006}. Graphic Equalisation adopts graph transformation as substrate vocabulary but makes frame-relative boundary forcing, collapse, and identity part of the same dynamical account.

\paragraph{Reflection and self-modification.} Computational reflection establishes that a system can represent and modify meta-level structure governing its own execution \citep{smith1982,maes1987}. The specific identity ``the grammar acting on itself is the loop'' is the present paper's claim, not a result attributed to those sources.

\paragraph{Cybernetics and feedback.} Classical cybernetics and control theory distinguish open-loop dynamics from systems in which error is returned into subsequent control \citep{wiener1948,ashby1956}. The residual construction generalises the same causal distinction: mismatch changes later action only where it survives as represented state.

\paragraph{Quantum measurement and relational description.} The separation of pre-realisation state from realised measurement record is standard in the measurement problem \citep{vonneumann1955}. Everett's relative-state formulation and Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics provide important precedents for refusing a single privileged observer-independent account of all facts \citep{everett1957,rovelli1996}. Graphic Equalisation uses these as neighbouring structures rather than deriving itself from any one interpretation.

\paragraph{Deterministic quantum theories.} Bohm's 1952 model is a direct proof of principle that deterministic dynamics can reproduce nonrelativistic quantum predictions when supplemented by additional state \citep{bohm1952}. Bell \citep{bell1964} and Kochen--Specker \citep{kochen1967} impose decisive constraints on local and noncontextual hidden-variable theories. Consequently, the determinism claim in Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism} should be read as a commitment requiring an adequate nonlocal/contextual grounding where quantum phenomena demand it, not as a revival of unconstrained classical hidden variables.

\paragraph{Causal order.} Causal-set theory supplies a well-developed example in which causal order is taken as primitive while continuum metric structure is additional or emergent \citep{bombelli1987}. Graphic Equalisation makes only the weaker substrate claim that ordinal causal order does not itself entail a real-valued metric time.

\paragraph{Information and minimum description.} The preference for the smallest sufficient ontology follows the same methodological direction as information-theoretic and minimum-description-length approaches: do not introduce representational structure that buys no additional explanatory or predictive distinction \citep{shannon1948,rissanen1978}. This motivates but does not prove the Discrete Sufficiency Hypothesis.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}
\paragraph{Composition.} Prior to the component commitments below: the arrangement claim of Section~\ref{sec:scope} fails if the structure's recurrence across unrelated domains is an artefact of description rather than a property of those domains. Evidence against it would be a demonstration that any sufficiently rich domain can be rendered in this vocabulary with equal facility, including domains chosen adversarially to lack the structure. Evidence for it is independent re-derivation, in a domain's own idiom, without reference to this theory.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Minimal graph sufficiency.} Fails if basic realised graph dynamics require a primitive beyond $(g,r,\Gamma)$ rather than represented or relational structure.
\item \textbf{Discrete sufficiency.} Fails if a claimed observable consequence cannot be preserved by any discrete representation, including dynamically varying quantisation, but is preserved by a genuinely non-discrete primitive.
\item \textbf{Antecedent-state principle.} Fails if a realised transition can require as antecedent a fact that exists only as consequence of that same transition, absent prior fixed-point structure.
\item \textbf{Graph-law determinism.} Fails if two genuinely identical, causally complete antecedent configurations produce different realised successors without any additional causally operative distinction. Variation observed only from a frame that omits determining dimensions does not constitute such a falsification.
\item \textbf{Admissibility is not armour.} Fails if self-admissibility must constrain arbitrary containing-world forcing.
\item \textbf{No implicit historical state.} Fails if an unrepresented past event alters later transition structure despite leaving no causal trace in graph or boundary state.
\item \textbf{Frame-relative identity.} Fails if sameness and persistence are derivable from $(g,r,\Gamma)$ alone without individuation.
\item \textbf{Higher-frame first-class structure.} Fails if a containing graph cannot possess ordinary relational state about a lower graph unless the same quantity is primitive inside the lower graph.
\item \textbf{Residual is inadmissibility.} Fails if a graph-originated deformation can be left as residual by a containing frame that nonetheless admits it under $\Gamma_W$, or absorbed while inadmissible, so that residual and non-admission come apart.
\item \textbf{Cost is the write.} Fails if a substrate operation other than writing a delta carries an irreducible cost --- if reading, holding a standing delta, or the boundary relation itself must be paid for independently of any write they occasion.
\item \textbf{No direct self-alteration.} Fails by exhibiting a graph that alters its own operative frame without either altering a subgraph or completing a round trip through a containing frame --- a reflexive write to its own running $r$ or $\Gamma$. Its observed absence is a contingent regularity, so this is the commitment most open to a constructed counterexample.
\item \textbf{Partial-order sufficiency.} Fails if realised dynamics require a total order over collapses in distinct frames --- a global simultaneity --- rather than only the boundary-induced partial order.
\item \textbf{Globally-async/locally-sync.} Fails if coupling never induces local synchrony (boundary-related collapses remain unordered) or if synchrony is forced without coupling (a global instant exists independent of any boundary).
\item \textbf{Rule-runner opacity.} Fails if a frame can determine by internal observation alone whether its rules are executed by its own substrate or by a separate runner --- if any in-frame test distinguishes self-running from hosted execution, or reveals whether a serial hidden runner underlies an apparently asynchronous frame.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The theory is deliberately small. A graph need not know what it is, where it is, what will happen, or what it wants. Present structure determines admissible continuation; interaction and collapse produce realised history; realised history changes the antecedent from which subsequent possibility is generated.

Recursion requires no external homunculus. Where the grammar acts on its own represented structure, the grammar is the loop. World forcing is not self-action, residual is discrepancy rather than diagnosis, history matters only where it survives as present structure, and identity exists only under individuation by a frame.

Pressed for what is compulsory, the theory keeps almost nothing. Only that rules run: applying a delta is itself a rule, the two gates of the interface and the retained standing delta are optimisations, and the single cost is the write, paid by the rules. The interface reduces to a container relation gated by aperture inward and admissibility outward; a collapse is a local asynchronous write and the only event; the residual is exactly what a containing grammar does not admit, so recognition and growth meet there; ordering is required but only as a partial order the rules carry themselves, with no substrate time and no global instant; the event order has no intrinsic first or last, so entry is chosen from a container; and a graph cannot alter its own frame directly, only across the boundary, which is why a severed channel removes not just action but termination. What survives every attempted removal is a container relation across which rules run, and the specific laws above it --- inertia, closure, the shape of collapse --- are contingent rule content rather than necessities, which is the sense in which this substrate underlies groundings without dictating them.
