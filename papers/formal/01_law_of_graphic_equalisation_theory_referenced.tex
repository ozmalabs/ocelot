\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes that one minimal structure underlies phenomena usually studied apart --- physical, cognitive, and institutional --- and that the case for it rests not on a new result in any single field but on the recurrence of the same structure across fields that developed it independently, without reference to one another or to this account. The structure is information-level: represented state, the transformations admissible upon it, and the realisation of a single continuation from what was possible. Almost none of its components is individually novel, and that is the point: the claim is the \emph{arrangement} --- that known pieces compose into one substrate, and that the composition is what recurs. It carries its own falsifier, and fails if the recurrence is an artefact of a sufficiently general description rather than a property of the domains themselves.

Read at this level, several things ordinarily treated as background turn out to be constructed rather than given --- a privileged standpoint from which to measure among them, and the substrate that remains once every removable commitment is stripped away is strikingly small, with most of what is called law being contingent upon it rather than necessary to it. Time is one of the constructed things: it is not a dimension of the substrate but a dimension a frame builds from the order among its own changes.

These are claims about information, of which physical theory is taken to be one embodiment rather than a rival. The theory asserts nothing about the nature of pre-relational existence; it begins where distinguishable relational structure can first be represented, and treats the limit of that reach as a frontier instrumentation moves rather than a fixed wall.
Provenance is relational rather than an intrinsic origin label: a frame
infers it from present structure, records, and interactions. Different
histories compatible with the same evidence can prevent unique recovery;
adequate traces can establish a local attribution under declared assumptions.
\end{abstract}

\emph{Why these are separate documents and not one.} The division is a claim and not an editorial
convenience, which matters now that this document is long enough for merging them to look like tidying.
\emph{Is} offers itself \emph{as evidence that transformation and grammar are separable rather than
constitutive}, and that evidence exists only because the two sit in different documents; the same holds
one level up for every boundary here. The ledger of Remark~\ref{rem:inherited} is \emph{what this paper
postulates over} \emph{Is}, and it is checkable because there is a companion to check it against ---
merge them and it becomes an argument about earlier chapters, which is not an accounting. Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} is
a guard here and a commitment in the groundings, and that split needs the groundings to exist. And the
documents fail differently on purpose: a physics grounding can be wrong without the substrate being
wrong, which is the \textsc{[shape]}/\textsc{[detail]} distinction at the scale of a document rather
than of a claim. One text would put every commitment inside one blast radius.

What the division does require is that material sits in the document whose question it answers, and
that is a live failure mode rather than a settled one. The remedy where it has bitten is to
\emph{restore} a boundary, not to dissolve one. The closest case in the other direction is the frames
companion, four pages answering one bounded question and carrying no independent risk; it stays separate
because the question is separate, and that is the weakest instance of this argument rather than a
representative one.

\paragraph{Level of the claims.}
The claims here are about \emph{information}: distinction, relation, frame, admissibility, realisation, residual. Physical theory is taken to be an embodiment of information rather than a competitor to it, alongside other embodiments --- cognitive, institutional, computational --- which differ in how much structure they instantiate rather than in kind. This is the position that licenses treating an inertial reference frame and a representational frame as the same sort of object (Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view}), and it locates the epistemic boundary below both: what embodiments are made of remains unspecified. It also fixes what this account does not attempt, which is worth saying once rather than conceding repeatedly: the constants belong to the embodiments. Where a number appears anywhere in this programme it is borrowed from a grounding and never derived here, because a framework that produced the constants would be a rival physical theory rather than the thing physical theories are stated in. \emph{This is the framework the rules live in, not a re-derivation of the rules.}

The layering must not be allowed to function as a shield, so the condition under which a physical result bears on a claim here is stated in advance. \textbf{A physical result bears on an information-level claim when it settles an informational fact}, and not when it merely disagrees about mechanism in one embodiment. Bell's theorem and Kochen--Specker are the model: they do not report a mechanism, they establish that a class of pre-measurement values cannot be re-read as unheld-but-realised, which is a fact about distinction and therefore reaches this level directly (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}). Landauer's principle is a second instance, establishing a lower bound on the physical cost of a specified informational operation \citep{landauer1961}; it constrains logical erasure, not bijective change or reversible uncomputation (Section~\ref{sec:interface}). A result that constrains what can be distinguished, held, or converted is admissible evidence here; a result about how a particular embodiment happens to be arranged is not.

\paragraph{Provenance of the primitives.}
The components of $G=(g,r,\Gamma)$ and its dynamic were not derived together from a single starting point, and stating how each arrived is more useful than presenting the triple as an austere posit reasoned out in advance.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $g$, the typed graph, arrived by \emph{observation}: several unrelated engineering problems turned out to have one shape --- labelled things joined by labelled relations. Both labellings there are those frames' own, and so is the sorting into kinds --- though not as decoration, since kinds arise in any graph that runs and those frames all ran. What is not supplied at all is that the relations differ (Remark~\ref{rem:types-forced}).
\item $\Gamma$, admissibility, arrived by \emph{insight}, and preceded the collapse operator. Its original working image was a sieve: the graph is pressed through admissibility, and what may pass is what $\Gamma$ admits.
\item $\Coll$, collapse, arrived by \emph{constraint}. Any theory of this kind must be compatible with quantum measurement, which is the regime where the distinction between indexed possibility and realised history is experimentally irreducible. The operator was adopted from there and generalised (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}).
\item $r$, the operative transformation structure, arrived by \emph{implementation}: a construction that runs requires something that performs the transformations, and the requirement appeared when the theory was first built rather than when it was first stated.
\end{itemize}

The last of these has a consequence for how the construction paper should be read: construction has been a \emph{source} of this theory rather than only a test of it. The evidence is $r$'s own provenance immediately above --- a primitive of the substrate that arrived because something had to perform the transformations in a system that ran --- and the construction paper describes eleven further distinctions implementation forced upon the theory, about antecedence, identity typing, residual and absent dimensions, which a reader cannot check and which is offered as an account of the direction of travel rather than as support for it.

\paragraph{What the claim is, and how to read for it.}
Almost none of the individual components below is novel, and the prior-art sections say so specifically rather than as a courtesy: typed graphs, admissibility grammars, fixed-point closure, frame-relative measurement, description-length criteria and actualisation operators all have established literatures. A reader who evaluates each component against its own field will therefore find each one known or borrowed, and will be locally correct on every count. Evaluating the parts individually cannot reach the composition claim in either direction --- neither to support it nor to refute it --- because the parts are stipulated to be known.

Accordingly the composition carries its own falsifiability commitment, stated in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} alongside the component ones. It fails if the recurrence across domains is an artefact of description --- that is, if the structure is recoverable in unrelated domains only because a sufficiently general vocabulary can be applied to anything, and not because those domains share it. The evidence bearing on it is independent re-derivation: cases in which a domain arrived at the
structure without reference to this theory, in its own idiom, while solving its own problem --- which
bears on the artefact reading without settling the composition claim, at the strength
Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} gives.

\paragraph{One worked instance of independent re-derivation, offered only as that.}
This paragraph does one job and no more: it sets out a single dated case in which a field reached a
relata-and-relations arrangement in its own idiom, without reference to this account, and states
exactly what that bears on. \emph{It is evidence for this paper's composition reading and for nothing
above it.} It does not ground \emph{Is}, whose claim is argued there and needs no history; it does not
establish that reality is fundamentally graph-shaped, which Section~\ref{sec:scope} disowns
outright; and it is not a new formal commitment --- it adds no falsifier of its own, and connects only
to the composition falsifier and the independent-re-derivation evidence already stated in
Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} and to Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence}. A reader who
sets it aside loses an illustration and no argument.

Double-entry bookkeeping is the case, and it is usually described as an invention. A transfer has two
sides, and the record that keeps both names two accounts and the movement between them --- relata and a
relation and nothing else. It should be said at once that this is \emph{not} a minimality claim, because
\citeauthor{ellerman2014} refutes the obvious one: a single-sided system using signed numbers records
the same information, and given the same opening equation and the same transactions the two end in the
same place \citep{ellerman2014}. What single-sided recording loses is the trial balance, and that is
the whole of the difference worth having here. Nobody derived the technique from a thesis about what
exists.

The history is what makes it a case rather than an analogy, and it is contested in a way the case has
to carry rather than hide. \citet{pacioli1494} is usually credited with the system, and what he wrote
was a description --- the \emph{Particularis de Computis et Scripturis}, a treatise inside a mathematics
textbook, setting out a method already in use among Venetian merchants. The practice is older than the
account of it by roughly two centuries: \citet{lee1977} reads the Farolfi ledger of 1299--1300 as full
double entry, so the system was found in use and then written down, which is the order a compression
arrives in and not the order an invention does. The counterweight belongs with it. \citet{yamey1949}
argued against the view that double entry was adopted for rational profit calculation, holding that
early merchants did not use it that way; what he found is a \emph{smaller} purpose rather than none ---
tracking debtor and creditor relations, custody, and agency accountability --- and \citet{sangster2016}
names it more precisely: a clear picture of every debtor and creditor \emph{together with the means to
check that the entries between them were complete and accurate}. So the case is not that nobody had a
theory of what the technique was for; it is that the something it was for was checking, which is what
makes its coherence property structural rather than incidental. A ledger that does not balance records
a \emph{residual} in the exact sense of Definition~\ref{def:residual} --- something recorded that does
not cohere with the rest --- and the trial balance is a coherence check, which is why the technique
catches errors at all.

That reading has a mathematical statement and it is not this account's. \citet{ellerman1985,ellerman2014}
shows that double entry implicitly uses the \emph{group of differences} over pairs of unsigned numbers
--- the T-account is the pair --- so a transaction is a difference and its two entries are its two
components rather than one record written twice, balance is the group identity, and an unbalanced ledger
is a residual in that exact sense. And \citet{aryagraph2000} reach the relata-and-relations reading
independently again, characterising the consistent transaction vectors by a \emph{directed graph
representation of the accounting system}, without which recovering transactions from statements
\citep{arya2000} becomes intractable as accounts multiply.

The strength of the case is bounded twice over, and both bounds are the ones this paper's own
falsifiers already impose. It is a single instance, so it bears on the artefact reading without settling
the composition claim, exactly as Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} says any independent re-derivation
does. And it is contested: the identification went unmade for a long interval by capable people looking
from the mathematical side --- \citeauthor{ellerman2014} names De Morgan in 1869 and Cayley, who
published a pamphlet on double-entry bookkeeping in 1894, neither making the ordered-pairs
identification. That the arrangement was reached in the accounting idiom without this theory is what
counts here; that it can be \emph{drawn} as a graph would count for nothing, since almost anything can,
and only re-derivation in a domain's own terms bears on the artefact reading.

\paragraph{Epistemic boundary.}
This theory does not attempt to specify the intrinsic nature of pre-relational existence. It begins at the first level at which distinguishable relational structure is scientifically representable. \emph{The graph is not an axiom of this paper.} What sits at that point is stated separately in \emph{Is}, and it is established there rather than assumed: that to be is to stand in relation, that nothing further --- no sequence, no cause, no rule --- is required for there to be anything at all, and that given relational determinacy and a no-unpaid-structure discipline, \emph{the bare untyped graph is the least ontology such an account may retain}. This paper therefore presupposes a graph, not as a postulate but as a result \emph{within that discipline}, and \emph{its own axioms concern change in that graph, not the graph's existence}. The qualification is not a hedge on a stronger claim, it is the whole of what \emph{Is} licenses: nonrelational structure registering in no relation is not shown impossible there and is not shown impossible here, and what either account says of it is that it is unwarranted rather than unavailable. That companion carries one claim and this paper presupposes it.

\emph{And the relation is dependence rather than temporal genealogy --- which governs how every
sequential sentence below should be read.} The changing graph this paper characterises presupposes the
bare relational condition; the bare condition does not presuppose transformation, grammar or
observation. \emph{The asymmetry is ontological and concerns what each requires}, not a sequence in
which one state becomes another. \emph{Is}'s base therefore comes first \emph{in requirement} and
need not be an earlier state: nothing here asserts that a world passed through a static graph before
becoming a changing one, and the ordering of these accounts mirrors that requirement rather than
constituting it. The exposition here runs in sequence because an argument is written and read in time, and
\emph{no part of that order is an order within what is described} --- \emph{postulate},
\emph{addition}, \emph{derivation} and \emph{later} name relations among commitments, not steps
anything took. This is not a promise to avoid such verbs, the paper being unreadable without them; it
is the instruction for reading them, and \emph{Is} states it at its own length. The move it makes is
\[
\boxed{?\;\dashrightarrow\;\text{observable relational structure}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{graph representation},}
\]
and the dashed arrow marks what is \emph{not} claimed. The pre-graph substrate may be continuous, discrete, purely mathematical, or unclassifiable from any frame available here; the theory asserts nothing about it. What is asserted begins at the second arrow: \emph{where} distinguishable relational structure is representable, it admits graph representation and the dynamics below govern it.

This boundary is stated because a stronger reading is available and is not the position of this paper. The theory is not the claim that reality is fundamentally graph-shaped.

Two clarifications, because the boundary is routinely read as weaker and as more permanent than it is.

\emph{Within} distinguishability the claim is not hedged. Where every measurement that can be made of something, and every operation that can be performed on it, is captured by treating it as a typed graph, then no difference remains for the question ``but is it really'' to name, and a question naming no difference has no content under Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view}. Functional identity is not a weaker sense of identity here; it is the whole of what identity could mean without a view from nowhere.

And the boundary is a \emph{frontier}, not a wall. It is fixed in \emph{definition} --- it sits at distinguishability, wherever that currently lies --- and moving in \emph{extent}, since its contents are contingent and diminish as instrumentation is built. Anything that becomes measurable passes from beyond it to within it. This is the extensive unboundedness of the substrate applied to the theory's own scope: always one further step available, no expectation of exhausting it, and no warrant for treating the current contents of the far side as permanent.

\section{Axiom \texorpdfstring{$-1$}{-1}: relativity of grounding}\label{sec:axiom-minus-one}
\setcounter{axiom}{-2}
\begin{axiom}[Relativity of grounding]\label{ax:no-view}
There is no operational view from nowhere. Every distinction, measurement, identity relation, admissibility judgement, and represented quantity is made in some frame (Definition~\ref{def:frame}). Frames may represent one another, but no quantity used by the theory requires a frame-independent evaluator.
\end{axiom}
\setcounter{axiom}{0}
\begin{remark}[What the axiom does not say]\label{rem:not-observer-talk}
\emph{No view from nowhere} is heard by most readers arriving from physics as a claim about observers,
and it is the reverse of one. \emph{Observation is derived in this account, not primitive.} Making a
distinction is an act; an act needs transformation and a grammar of what may follow; \emph{Is}'s
base has neither, so what merely stands in relation cannot observe --- and, in the case that companion
posits, nothing containing it, cannot be observed either but only modelled (\emph{Is},
Proposition~\ref{IS-prop:one-way}). An observer is late machinery and an expensive kind, and nothing in the substrate
waits on one --- collapse is not measurement, residual is not a failure to be seen, and a frame is a
locus at which distinctions are made rather than something that looks.

What the axiom denies is a \emph{privileged characterisation}: not that quantities need observers, but
that no quantity is available from outside every frame. A world with nothing in it capable of observing
is fully described here, and is the ordinary case rather than a degenerate one.

\emph{Where this becomes a risk.} At the substrate the relativity carries none, being a discipline over
what the account may say. In the groundings it is a commitment about how things are, and that is where
it can fail: the Seldon grounding asks whether a nervous system implements anything of the manifold's
shape and answers that the pieces are implemented while the composite is not
(its Section~\ref{SG-sec:scope}), which is a claim that could turn out false without anything here
turning out false. The exposure lives there, and Remark~\ref{rem:inherited} says so of the axiom generally.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Provenance is relational and its attribution is inferred}\label{sec:provenance-principle}

\begin{definition}[Relational provenance]\label{def:provenance}
Provenance is a relation between a structure or event and the causal
organisation or history that produced it. A provenance attribution is a
frame's account of that relation, constrained by the evidence the frame
can distinguish. Neither an origin nor a source is an additional intrinsic
mark carried independently of the relations and traces that make it
distinguishable.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Status of the principle]\label{rem:provenance-status}
This is a derivational treatment of an established concept. Source-monitoring
theory treats remembered origins as judgments based on memory characteristics
and other available information \citep{johnson1993}. Causal identification
characterises when causal queries can be recovered from observational or
interventional information under stated models \citep{shpitserpearl2008}.
Database provenance formalises relationships between derived outputs,
contributing inputs, and source locations \citep{buneman2001}. These are
distinct precedents with their own scopes; none is being claimed as a
derivation of this entire ontology.

\emph{Provenance is inferred, not inherent} states the relational discipline
for origin. Actual causal relations do not wait for an attribution. What a
frame knows of them comes through its relations to present structure,
records, and effects, under Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view}. The principle adds no
origin primitive or further observer to the substrate. The attribution is
itself represented structure and can affect subsequent action.

A provenance record can be carried explicitly. Such a record is evidence
whose relation to its claimed source must be established in the frame;
writing an origin into a label does not make the label authenticate its own
history. Conversely, reliable recording and controlled intervention can
make a local attribution determinate. The principle does not say that
every origin is unknowable or that actual histories are interchangeable.

Graphic Equalisation's contribution here is derivational backing from its
relational commitments and a common account of the principle's applications.
The dependencies are explicit: the relational ontology supplies the account
of origin as a causal relation; relative grounding places its operational
characterisation in a frame; the declared evidence map then yields the
recovery or non-recovery result. The no-view axiom alone does not prove the
absence of an intrinsic historical property. The backing is conditional on
these foundational commitments and the distinction between actual relation
and represented attribution.
The evidence-relative proposition below states a standard identifiability
argument in that account's terms; neither the underlying concept nor the
argument is claimed as a new discovery. ``Inferred'' also does not require
conscious deliberation: source-monitoring judgments can be automatic or
deliberate \citep{johnson1993}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Provenance recovery is evidence-relative]\label{prop:provenance-inference}
For a declared family of candidate histories \(H_F\), let \(O_F(h)\) be the
evidence distinguishable in frame \(F\), and let \(\pi_F(h)\) give the
provenance distinction being asked about. The compatible provenance set is
\[
P_F(s)=\{\pi_F(h):h\in H_F,\ O_F(h)=s\}.
\]
If two compatible histories have different provenance, the evidence \(s\)
does not determine a unique attribution. A singleton \(P_F(s)\) determines
that distinction only relative to the declared history family and evidence
model. An empty set indicates that the model supplies no compatible history.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A recovery rule receiving the same evidence \(s\) has the same input for
both histories. It cannot return their different provenance values as one
uniquely warranted answer. If every compatible history has the same value,
the requested distinction is fixed within that family. Neither conclusion
establishes that the family includes every actual causal alternative.
\end{proof}

This is the general principle; operator supply in
Section~\ref{sec:rule-runner} is one application. The imagined/perceived
distinction in the mind companion and the deliberate/autonomous distinction
in the decision-making companion are others. An output alone need not
identify which process produced it. Where no relevant history survives in
the evidence available to a frame, that frame cannot recover it by adding
an origin label. Where a trace survives, it constrains the attribution
through its actual relations. Loss of evidence limits inference; it does
not undo the causal event.

\subsection{Further consequences of relative grounding}

\begin{remark}[The axiom in bits]\label{rem:axiom-in-bits}
The axiom is clearer stated as a measure, and stated that way it is the same result as
Remark~\ref{rem:self-edge} at the other end of the same scale.

A view is informative in proportion to the relation between viewer and viewed: what the one tells you
about the other is what a view delivers, and it is measured in bits. A view from nowhere is a
standpoint standing in no relation to what it views. So it does not deliver a partial view, or a
distorted one, or one this account happens to have no access to. \emph{It delivers no bits.}

That inverts the picture the phrase invites. A view from nowhere is ordinarily imagined as the best
view --- unobstructed, unbiased, complete --- and the axiom is heard as denying us something desirable.
The measure says the opposite: seeing is standing in relation, so a standpoint that adds nothing to
what it views also \emph{receives} nothing from it. The view from nowhere is not the complete view but
the empty one, and what the axiom denies is not a vantage we are missing but one that would tell us
nothing if we had it.

The same fact turns up at both ends of one scale. A self-relation has both
ends the same and carries no bits (Remark~\ref{rem:self-edge}); a view from nowhere has no relation at
all and carries no bits. Zero distance and infinite distance are the two ways of getting nothing, and
in between --- standing apart from something, at some remove, from somewhere --- is where information
is. It is also why \emph{Is}'s graph follows --- and what follows is a check passed rather than
evidence gathered, exactly as with the method (Remark~\ref{rem:mdl-ontic}). \emph{Is} argues for
the bare graph on its own ground and needs nothing from here; what this paper's economics supplies is
that it agrees, and were the two to disagree the account would be self-undermining. That is the whole
of what the step claims, and it needs stating with care, since the short version of it is invalid. A richer intrinsic form would have to be read off from nowhere, which
is to say read off at zero bits, which is not reading it off --- and \emph{that} much is about reading
rather than about what is there. What carries the step is this paper's own economics: positing a form no
relation registers is a write that is paid in full and can never be read back --- the fourth and
silent cost of Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}, which returns nothing and does not announce that it
returned nothing. One such posit is not disqualifying, well-formedness being graded and a matter of
how much is carried rather than whether anything is; what the definition supplies is a reason to
decline it absent something asking for it, which is all the step needs. So the graph follows as what
such an account may carry, and not as a demonstration that nothing richer could exist --- that remains
open to anyone willing to posit one and meet the bill.

\emph{And on this reading it is not an axiom.} It follows. To be is to stand in relation; a relation is
what carries information, being what tells you about one end given the other; so a standpoint standing
in no relation to what it views carries nothing about it, and there is no informative view from
nowhere. Nothing is posited there that \emph{Is}'s claim and the measure do not already give. The
axiom's own wording anticipates this and should be read as meaning it: what it denies is an
\emph{operational} view from nowhere --- a view that does something --- and the qualifier was never
decoration.

Two other readings of its status do not survive. It is not a posit among posits, and it is not, \emph{in the groundings}, a costless methodological guard, since the groundings use it to
license positive results and carry it as a commitment that can fail. A consequence has content and can
fail, which is what the guard reading could not accommodate. This one fails if a view can be
informative without standing in relation to what it views, or if relation is not what carries
information here --- both checkable, and the second is \emph{Is}'s own identification of
distinction with relation, so the exposure is real and is where a reader should press.

It keeps its number and its prohibition-form for the reason the next remark gives: what it denies is
imported freely, so a consequence that nobody would otherwise derive has to be stated before the
counting starts. \emph{Not an axiom, and stated first anyway} is what the numbering records.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Two familiar statements of the same zero, and the misreading both invite]\label{rem:everything-nothing}
Two things people already say turn out to be this measure, and both are usually said in a form the
measure corrects.

\emph{Everything is nothing.} Taken as a claim about what there is, it is false and obviously so. Taken
as the measure says it, it is exact: a view of everything from outside everything stands in no relation
to what it views --- there is nothing left over to stand in one --- so it delivers zero, which is what
a view of nothing delivers. \emph{The totality and the empty set meet at zero bits}, which is also
where this programme's own empty limit sits. The equality is between two \emph{views}, not between two
\emph{things}, and the usual version slides from the first to the second: from \emph{no standpoint
distinguishes them} to \emph{they are the same}. That slide asserts an identity from a standpoint the
same measure says would be empty, so it refutes itself in the act, and Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} is what blocks it.
From somewhere --- which is the only place anything is --- everything is emphatically not nothing.

\emph{The pale blue dot} \citep{sagan1994} is the same scale approached rather than reached. Distance
costs bits: at enough remove, everything anyone has cared about is one pixel, and the force of the
image is that information collapse felt rather than computed. The nihilist reading takes the limit to
have been reached and concludes that nothing matters. Sagan's own text does not, and the measure says
why it need not: \emph{that's here, that's home, that's us} is a reading from a frame, and a frame at
zero remove from what it cares about is where the bits are. Scale indexes mattering; it does not
abolish it.

The two share one correction, which is the whole of what this account adds to either. \emph{The zero is
a property of the view and not of the viewed.} A thing does not become nothing by being seen from far
enough away, and everything does not become nothing by being taken all at once. What goes to zero is
what a standpoint at no relation, or at unbounded remove, receives --- and there is no such standpoint
occupied by anyone, which is why neither statement licenses the conclusion usually drawn from it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The method is one of the account's own consequences]\label{rem:mdl-ontic}
Minimum description length appears twice in this programme under one name, and the second appearance is
not a method. In \emph{Is} it is the method --- \emph{prefer the shortest account that suffices} ---
and is imported rather than invented. It is also \emph{derivable}: relation is what carries the
information, a relation with no content of its own adds nothing to what a thing is, so what a thing is
is its shortest sufficient relational description. That is the same principle applied to \emph{things}
rather than to accounts, and it is what the claim that information is everything cashes to. Neither
grip is prior; they are one principle held at two places.

Naming the norm's source makes the size of the second grip visible, and it is larger than it looks. The
companion identifies minimum description length as Occam's razor with the vagueness removed --- a norm
on \emph{accounts}, telling you which of two descriptions to prefer. The constitutive reading is that
same razor turned on \emph{things}: not that a shorter description is the better model of a thing, but
that the shortest sufficient one is what the thing comes to. That is a considerably stronger claim than
the methodological razor anyone would grant, and it is stated here as a separate one rather than
carried in on the back of it.

One extension is required and is named rather than hidden. Remark~\ref{rem:self-edge} settles the case
of a relation carrying no information \emph{at all}; this uses the conditional form --- no information
\emph{given the rest}. A duplicated edge is not zero-bit on its own, only conditional on the edge it
duplicates. The step from the unconditional result to the conditional one is plausible and is not
proved here.

Well-posedness comes from Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} and could come from nowhere else, since description length is
defined only against a vocabulary and \emph{shortest simpliciter} is not a quantity. The standing
objection to any constitutive reading of description length --- that Kolmogorov complexity is universal-machine-relative up
to an additive constant (not a general invariance theorem for practical MDL codes) --- is therefore not answered here but \emph{accepted}: the constant is the
frame. Converting an objection into a thesis is only honest where the thesis was motivated
independently of the objection, which is the case here, Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} having been argued for on other
grounds entirely.

One consequence is easy to miss. \emph{If the method is a consequence of the account, the method cannot
be independent support for the account.} There is nothing vicious in the circle --- a description of
everything describes its own production --- but the most that may be claimed from it is consistency:
were the method and the ontological reading to disagree, the account would be self-undermining, and
they do not disagree. That is a check passed and not evidence gathered, and it is the weaker of the two
things a reader might have taken the agreement to show.

\emph{The method was never evidence}, and calling its loss a cost gets the sign wrong. It is a selection principle: it did its work in choosing what to keep, never in showing that
what was kept is so. Finding it to be a consequence removes it from a column it had no business in, and
by Definition~\ref{def:well-formed} that is not a loss but the thing that definition names --- paying
for nothing that does no work. What the correction does not license is the stronger reading, that
needing no support is better without limit. The account's live commitments are answerable and are
untouched by this. And a description that genuinely needed no support would be the object the frames
companion arrives at from the other direction (its Remark~\ref{FR-rem:empty-limit}): nothing held, no work done, hence nothing to support and
nothing to be supported. \emph{Perfection and well-formedness part company there}, because the criterion
is work done \emph{for} something and at zero work it has no argument to take --- the same collapse as
the self-relation and the view from nowhere, reached a third way. Needing less support is a direction.
Needing none is not the end of that direction but the point at which the measure stops applying.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why it is numbered before the axioms]\label{rem:why-minus-one}
The number is not decoration and it is not a claim that this is a different kind of thing --- it is a
commitment rather than a methodological guard \emph{in the groundings}, the groundings using it to license positive results
and carrying it as something that can fail. The number records something else: \emph{what it denies is imported for
free}. A reader does not arrive neutral on whether there is a view from nowhere; they arrive already
using one, without having decided to, because reading a claim as being about how things are rather
than about how things are from somewhere is the default and costs nothing to adopt. An axiom that
cancels a default has to be stated before the counting starts, since by the time the first axiom is
numbered the import has already happened.

That is also why it is the one thing forbidden rather than one more thing asserted. Everything else
this account does at the base is removal --- take a candidate away and see whether the account still
stands. This cannot be done by removal, because what it removes is not in the text to be taken out; it
is in the reader, and it returns unless it is refused explicitly. A prohibition is the only form that
does that job.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Determinacy is retained; only the privileged frame is removed]\label{rem:determinacy-retained}
This removes a privileged frame, not determinacy. Within a frame, relations may be exact, restrictive, comparable, and falsifiable. The standpoint is structurally consonant with relational formulations in physics, especially relational quantum mechanics, in which physical facts are indexed to interactions rather than assigned from a privileged observer-independent state description \citep{rovelli1996}. The present axiom is broader and is not derived from that programme.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What this paper postulates over \emph{Is}]\label{rem:inherited}
This is the complete list of what this paper assumes beyond \emph{Is}, and the price of each.
The ledger is Table~\ref{tab:ledger}. The prose after it is why each entry sits where it does, and a reader who wants
only the account can stop at the table and the paragraph on how to read the count. The entries are named
rather than counted, because a count can be carried unchanged while the list beneath it grows and a list
cannot. Where an entry was arrived at by withdrawing something the withdrawal is stated with it, since a
ledger whose entries cannot be checked against what they replaced is not checkable; the fuller history is
in the review record.

\emph{Inherited.} \emph{Is}'s claim, that to be is to stand in relation \emph{and to cohere in it}.
Both halves are inherited and the second is not decorative: relation alone is satisfied by a relation to
oneself, which carries nothing, so what is taken over is \emph{Is}'s pair of propositions and not
the first alone (Remark~\ref{rem:cohere}). Nothing is added here by taking the second, since the
companion states coherence structurally, without an information measure.

\emph{Admitted.} That anything \emph{happens}. \emph{Is} is careful to say this is not required for
there to be anything, so it is a real addition, and it is not derived from anything here. It is one of
two unpaid entries, the other being \emph{Is}'s claim that there is anything at all; neither is
grounded here, and an entry not derived from anything here is unpaid whatever follows from it once
granted.

\emph{Taken to follow from the admission, with two candidates open.} The transformations $r$. Something has to do the happening, whatever
does it is a graph, and the least graph that can do anything is a pattern together with a change it
makes --- so a rule is a graph that does something. The candidate
list this minimality claim was tested against is: a brute unconditioned change, a change with no
pattern, an external agency, a primitive not decomposable into match-and-change, and a continuous flow
admitting no step. The first two are rules with an empty match; the third is excluded by the
companion's claim rather than by this argument; the fourth and fifth are open. Allowing an empty match
also makes the minimality claim partly analytic, which is conceded rather than hidden. Admissibility comes with it rather than beside it:
$\Gamma$ names the rules that decide admit-or-residual, and Definition~\ref{def:admissible}'s writing
of $\Poss$ in terms of $\Gamma$ is a definitional dependence and not an ontological one.

One thing the corpus states in several places without naming its origin belongs here.
\emph{Existence is not contents.} A thing holding
nothing is still a thing provided it stands in a relation, so emptiness is not non-existence, and a
dimension holding no value is not a dimension that is not there.

That is derived, and it is derived from \emph{Is}'s claim rather than from admissibility.
Definition~\ref{def:states} already gives it in this paper: \textsc{null} is the edge's non-existence,
where a value is an edge that exists and carries one, so revoking a dimension is the container ceasing
to instantiate the relation. \emph{Seldon}'s own definition of a dimension says the same (its Definition~\ref{SM-def:dimension}) --- a
dimension exists in a frame only where the frame instantiates the relation needed to distinguish values
along it, and if that relation is absent the dimension is undefined rather than present at zero. Both
are relational and neither mentions a grammar.

A rival argument runs instead that an empty set is a set while having no members, so what makes a
thing is that it is \emph{admitted} as one, and concludes that admissibility is forced. It fails three ways. The premise contradicts \emph{Is}, which holds the empty set to
be the one perfect thing and perfection to be unavailable to an existent --- so on that account the
empty set does not stand in a relation and no question about its existence arises. The word
\emph{admitted} then slides: read as \emph{stands in the relation that individuates it} the argument is
\emph{Is}'s claim restated, and read as \emph{a grammar licenses it} it is denied by
Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}, which says relations survive stripping $\Gamma$. And the conclusion would make
existence a matter of what some frame's grammar admits, which by Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} indexes
existence to frames --- reinstalling exactly the reading \emph{Is} disowns when it says a tree
falling unobserved is as related to its forest as one that is watched.

\emph{Postulated.} Two.

A \emph{realisation operator}. $\Coll$ is typed as a map out of $\Poss(G_t)$, and
Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory} says outright that \emph{a collapse needs something to select
from}. An attempt to dissolve this --- what matches writes, two rules matching make a bigger change,
incoherent writes leave residual --- does not survive: it trades collapse for union, which is a
retained-branch treatment with the branches glued rather than a realisation, and the physics companion's
measurement account needs exactly one continuation realised
(its Section~\ref{PH-sec:measurement}). It also mis-locates residual, which is
defined as a \emph{part} of a realised deformation and is per-rule, so it cannot carry a joint
incompatibility between two individually admissible writes; where two such writes are made one after
another, the second is made against the result of the first and what fails is one rule's write, which
is the per-rule case again (Proposition~\ref{prop:partial-order}).

One thing more remains postulated on the economics side.
A \emph{destroy\slash preserve distinction}. Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} exempts evaluation from
cost because it preserves, so the operative criterion is not \emph{a change costs} but \emph{a change
that destroys costs}. The reading that cost simply \emph{is} change does not remove this: an
information-preserving write is a change that destroys nothing --- the physics companion says so of
free continuation (its Section~\ref{PH-sec:noether}) --- so the two criteria are distinct and the account uses the second. The claim that
the discarding class is the general one is empirical and is counted here.

\emph{Imported measure and operational consequence.} The informational rendering adds an information measure over represented variables and a declared distribution \citep{shannon1948}; it is not supplied by the untyped base graph. The weak conclusion below concerns information accessible through a relation. It does not prove that every fact is frame-indexed. The full frame-indexing language is retained as the account's methodological guard.  Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view}. The \emph{of what} is the
load-bearing part of it: it follows from the
companion's claim together with the measure, in the two steps Remark~\ref{rem:axiom-in-bits} sets out
--- to be is to stand in relation, a relation is what carries information, so a standpoint in no
relation to what it views carries nothing about it and there is no informative view from nowhere. What
the axiom denies was always an \emph{operational} view from nowhere, and that qualifier is the whole of
the claim rather than decoration on it. The unqualified frame-indexing reading is a separate discipline and is not a consequence of that derivation. In this paper every use of it refuses
an import --- no frame-free denominator, no absolute threshold, no evaluator standing outside every
frame --- which is a discipline governing what the account may say, and a discipline is not a place
this paper can fail. That is a claim about \emph{this paper} and it does not generalise: in the
groundings the axiom is used to license positive results (that two indiscernible particles are one,
that a context-independent measure is available, that an inertial frame and a representational frame
are the same kind of object), and there it is a commitment that can fail. \emph{Narrativium}
already says so, locating the axiom's exposure in its own falsifier (its Section~\ref{NV-sec:falsifiability}) rather than in the substrate. So
the honest statement is split: guard here, commitment there, and the groundings carry the risk --- which
is what Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} says of this account generally.

\emph{A third tier.} Between postulate and consequence sit the results stated as hypotheses or marked
undischarged: Minimal graph sufficiency (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:minimal-graph}), Discrete sufficiency
(Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:discrete}), Located degradation (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:located-degradation}), and the near-uniqueness of the
cheap cut (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:cheap-cut}). The recurrence schema organises the definitions rather than adding a separate empirical bet; the other entries carry the substantive hypotheses. A two-tier ledger has nowhere to put those bets.

\emph{What is offered as consequence}, and offered rather than proved: the residual's non-admission
reading, the impossibility of complete self-transparency, the conditional that having time requires
closure as well as inference, and the contingency of carvings. The frame interface is not among them,
because this paper calls its two gates optimisations an efficient graph accretes under a budget rather
than constituents of graphhood, which routes them through a selection pressure
Section~\ref{sec:carvings} declines to supply.

\emph{How to read the count.} These reductions are re-descriptions --- $X$ is a special case of $Y$ ---
and the criterion licensing them, that doing something is what a rule is, applies to anything this
account posits. So a count driven to zero would be a theorem of the vocabulary rather than a
measurement of the economy. What a
surviving re-description does buy is narrower and real: it says two things cannot be varied
independently, which constrains what a grounding may do. It does not reduce the number of ways the
account can be wrong.

Nothing here exempts this paper from the rules it states. Where the ledger has been wrong it has been
corrected against those rules and not against a lighter standard, and the record of that is in the
assessment companion (its Section~\ref{AS-sec:turned}).

That is not a further principle. It is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} applied to the text: an account that stated its rules
from outside its own scope would be speaking from nowhere, and there is nowhere to speak from. And it
needs saying for the reason the axiom is numbered where it is (Remark~\ref{rem:why-minus-one}) ---
\emph{the import is free}. A reader defaults to hearing a claim as made about things rather than as
made by one of them, so the standpoint returns wherever it is not refused, and the refusal has to be
repeated at each place it would return rather than stated once and relied on. That the account
describes its own production needs no argument beyond the account --- a description of everything
includes the writing of it --- and is not restated here.
\end{remark}

\part{The substrate}
\section{The minimal graph}\label{sec:minimal-graph}
\begin{definition}[Graph]\label{def:graph}
A graph is
\[\boxed{G=(g,r,\Gamma),}\]
where $g$ is represented state, $r$ is operative transformation structure, and $\Gamma$ is the admissibility grammar governing graph-originated transformations.

Nothing in that is typed, and the omission is the definition's content rather than its brevity. What
the word \emph{graph} denotes here is the floor --- difference, and what differing consists in --- while
the typed object the word ordinarily calls to mind is derived and arrives with anything that runs
(Remark~\ref{rem:types-forced}). A reader supplies the typing regardless, which is why this is said here
and again where it matters rather than left to the definition to carry alone.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Only $g$ is required; the triple is what a \emph{running} world costs]\label{rem:only-g}
The triple is not the minimum. Strip $r$ and
$\Gamma$ and something remains: structure, with relations among its parts. Nothing \emph{happens} in
it --- no transformation, so no collapse, no residual, and nothing it individuates --- but it has not
thereby become nothing, and it has not stopped being individuable by something else. What $r$ and
$\Gamma$ buy is \emph{occurrence}, and everything this account is about --- change, collapse,
observation, cost, experience --- requires them. $\Gamma$ is definitional --- an everything-admitting
grammar is still the grammar $\Poss$ is written over (Definition~\ref{def:admissible}) --- and what
Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory} calls an optimisation is the \emph{restrictiveness} of its two
faces, not its existence. The triple is the price of a world in which anything
happens rather than the price of there being anything, and Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory}'s
``only that rules run is compulsory'' should be read in that narrower sense.

A distinction is doing the work there. Being a
frame is being individuated (Definition~\ref{def:frame}), and individuation is performed by the
container, not by the thing: a boundary belongs to the frame that draws it. So a structure with no
rules of its own is still a frame --- something else instantiated its boundary --- and what it lacks
is not framehood but the capacity to individuate anything itself. Stripping $r$ does not leave ``no frame'', and reading it so would invalidate the mind companion's result, which reasons from a rock's being a frame to the universe's being one (its Proposition~\ref{MS-prop:minimal-universe}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The reference point, and what it is not]\label{rem:rock}
The definitional reference point for $g$ is a rock, and the anchor is ostensive because every
candidate word --- structure, extent, configuration --- needs defining in turn. A rock has parts and
relations among them; nothing about that structure is represented by anything; and at the grain at
which one is a rock it originates no transformation. What happens to it is done by its container, and
what happens inside it happens in subframes at a finer grain. In its own terms it does not change: it
merely is, and then later also merely is.

Three limits on that, because the anchor is more useful than the claim and the claim can be
overstated.

It is a claim about a \emph{grain} and not about a substance. A rock's atoms move and it weathers,
both at grains other than the one at which it is a rock, so what is exhibited is an individuation.

It is not a proof that anything is $r$-free absolutely, and the machinery here suggests nothing is:
Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-recursion} declines a floor, and at fine enough grain a rock's yield
point --- deformation absorbed up to a limit, fracture past it --- is hard to distinguish from a
change grammar sorting what coheres from what does not. The rock is offered as what $g$ looks like,
not as an existence proof that pure $g$ occurs.

And nothing here says that every object has a grain at which nothing in it runs, which would assert a
floor this account elsewhere refuses. What holds is weaker and sufficient: for the objects
one ordinarily handles, there is a grain at which one is not treating them as agents, and $g$ is the
reading of them at that grain.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[An operational test, and what it cannot do]\label{rem:space-test}
There is a test for the reference point that is worth running even though it does not settle
anything. \emph{Throw it into space and see what happens.}

A rock thrown into space does nothing that is not done to it. It is struck by radiation, radiates
heat away, and is altered over long enough --- all by its container, and none of it registered,
because registering requires rules to run. That criterion is the thing being tested, so this
illustrates Remark~\ref{rem:only-g} rather than checking it; the value is in what the test separates.

It separates three cases and not two. Throw an organism and something happens immediately: the rules
it was running assumed conditions its container supplied, and the supply stops. Throw a spacecraft's
computer and it continues, because it carries its supply. Nothing running, running with the
container's support, running on supply brought along.

What it cannot do is distinguish \emph{never ran} from \emph{ran and stopped}. A rock and an organism
killed on the way out are identical under it, so the test does not anchor $g$ and is not offered as
doing so.

The intermediate case is instructive. Tardigrades desiccated before launch survived exposure to space vacuum in low Earth orbit and resumed
on return, though survival fell sharply where vacuum was combined with full-spectrum solar
ultraviolet \citep{jonsson2008}. That is a structure whose rules stop firing and later fire again ---
which is \emph{not} a structure entering $g$ and leaving it, since resuming requires the rules to
have survived as present structure (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-implicit-historical-state}). Rules
present and not firing is a third condition, distinct from $g$ and from running alike, and the
mind companion names it directly (its Section~\ref{MS-sec:minimal}). The slide from \emph{has no} $r$ to \emph{fires no} $r$ is easy to make and is not made here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[On idealising, since this section has done a great deal of it]\label{rem:idealising}
Every construction above is an idealisation, which invites the obvious objection: an account whose axiom
denies a view from nowhere has spent this section reasoning about frictionless rocks in vacuums. The
criterion that answers it belongs to the method rather than to this paper and is stated in \emph{Is}
(its Section~\ref{IS-sec:method}): an idealisation is a frame, no frame is illegitimate, and what makes one cheap rather than
expensive is whether what it removed is named and restorable.

Applied here, the removals are on the page. The rock is $g$ at the grain at which it is a rock, and the
same passage says its atoms move and it weathers at other grains, so the removed structure can be put
back. What Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} rejects is accordingly not an illegitimate simplification but an expensive habit
--- a simplification that has forgotten it made one, and is then read as a claim about the world rather
than about a frame, at whatever that misreading later costs.
\end{remark}

\begin{hypothesis}[Minimal graph sufficiency]\label{hyp:minimal-graph}
No parent pointer, world model, affordance catalogue, Narrative, Prospect, Value, or self-model is
primitive; such structures may exist in $g$ when the graph actually represents them. Any additional
structure required only by a particular capability class is represented content or grounding, not a
primitive of graphhood, unless a graph lacking it cannot participate in the basic realised transition
relation.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}[The triple, unpacked in dependency order]\label{rem:dependency-order}
The three components of $G$ resolve into five constituents ordered by requirement, each presupposing the one before it:
\[
\boxed{\text{nodes}\;\to\;\text{relations}\;\to\;\text{rules}\;\to\;\text{delta}\;\to\;\text{grammar}.}
\]
\emph{Nodes} are bare distinctions --- the minimal \emph{``difference which makes a difference''} \citep{bateson1972}; a node alone carries almost no content. \emph{Relations} structure nodes and require them as relata; this is where content lives, and a node's identity is its relations. \emph{Rules} ($r$) act on relational structure and require relations to match. \emph{Delta} is the change a rule writes and requires rules, since only a rule produces one. \emph{Grammar} ($\Gamma$) determines which deltas are admissible and requires deltas to act on, which is why it is last. Nodes and relations are $g$; delta is $r$'s product, made explicit; grammar is $\Gamma$.

Two cautions. First, this constructive order does not contradict the relational primacy of Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view}: constructively nodes precede relations (relata come first), while semantically relations precede nodes (a node means nothing unheld by relations); both hold on different axes. Second, the order is a dependency partial order, not a temporal one --- nodes are not a first \emph{moment} but the least-dependent constituent, consistent with the absence of any substrate time (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The triple is a functional decomposition with nothing underneath it]\label{rem:one-substance}
$g$, $r$, and $\Gamma$ are not three kinds of thing. Rules are graph data (nodes and relations), and admissibility is enforced by rules that decide admit-or-residual, so $r$ and $\Gamma$ are themselves graph data playing roles --- transformation and gating --- over the state $g$, which is graph data too. The triple decomposes function, and there is nothing beneath the functions for them to be modes of:
what makes the three one is that each is graph data doing a job, not that each is a mode of some stuff
that has jobs. Saying instead that \emph{there is one substance, graph data} imports the bearer \emph{Is} removes by name (substance or intrinsic nature is on its candidate list, and the
removal is reported as successful there), and does so in the sentence claiming to have decomposed it
away. One substance is not the correction to three kinds of thing; \emph{no} substance is. Three things follow. Admissibility is \emph{mintable} like any rule, so what is admissible is not a fixed law but a growable set of rules a graph carries and extends. That the grammar acting on itself is the loop (Proposition~\ref{prop:grammar-is-loop}) is the special case of this homogeneity (the grammar, being rules, can act on itself), and it does not tower into a stack of meta-grammars, because gating and being-gated are one activity closing on itself rather than two. And the irreducible form of the substrate is \emph{graph data that runs} --- where the running is supplied by structure that is, in turn, graph data that runs (Section~\ref{sec:rule-runner}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Whether a node may have an edge to itself]\label{rem:self-edge}
The question is basic, and the answer is not one thing.

\emph{Nothing forbids the notation}, and the graph definition above says nothing either way.

\emph{A self-edge cannot do the work of individuating.} \emph{Is} holds that to be is to stand in
relation because a thing standing apart from nothing differs from nothing, and a single relatum with
nothing to relate to is a failed case rather than a minimal one. A loop supplies no second term, so a
node whose only edge is to itself is that failed case with an extra mark on it.

\emph{And a self-edge does not cohere, and carries no information --- which are one fact and not two.}
Nothing stops a frame writing one; the point is what the write does. A relation holds two things apart,
and with the same thing at both ends there is nothing to hold apart, so nothing coheres into a
distinction. Correspondingly there are no bits: a relation is informative in the ordinary sense, as
what it tells you about one end given the other, and this one gives you the thing given the thing, at
conditional uncertainty zero. The failure to cohere and the absence of information are the same failure
seen twice, because carrying information is what cohering into a distinction \emph{is} here.

So a self-edge is not forbidden and not a cheaper relation. It is a write that is made and returns
nothing into the frame, which is the decoherence case at its smallest, and it is the exact zero of the
discipline \emph{Is} states with its prior art --- do not introduce structure that buys no
additional distinction. Definition~\ref{def:well-formed} prices it accordingly: paid for in holding and
search, returning nothing.

\emph{In one place it is excluded rather than idle.} Axiom~\ref{ax:no-time} makes enablement
asymmetric --- if one change makes another possible, the second does not thereby make the first --- so
in the enabling order a self-edge is the degenerate cycle and is ruled out with the rest.

\emph{And it is not what self-reference means here.} A reflective graph
(Definition~\ref{def:reflective}) holds a \emph{representation} of itself, which is distinct structure
from what it represents; Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-recursion} has an operator be some other graph's
running exposed. Self-reference in this account is always across a grain or a frame, never a loop at
one node --- which is why the regress goes downward without end rather than closing.

And the information reading says why that is not a limitation to be regretted. A model related to its
subject by a self-edge would carry no bits; what makes a self-model informative is exactly that it is a
\emph{different and lesser} object than what it models, so that having it tells you something you did
not have. \emph{The incompleteness of a self-model is the condition of its being informative at all},
not a defect alongside its usefulness --- which is the sharper form of the incompleteness results the
mind companion reports (its Proposition~\ref{MS-prop:selfmodel}), and is why a frame that could hold a complete model of itself would gain
nothing by holding it.

\emph{Relative to what the ends are the same} is the part left unsaid above, and it is not a detail. A
self-relation has both ends the same \emph{as drawn by whatever individuates them}, and individuation is
performed by the container (Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}). So a loop at one node is a verdict of the frame
holding it, not a property the structure carries on its own: a container that discriminates more finely
sees two things where the inner frame saw one, and what was a self-relation there is an ordinary
relation here, carrying bits. The zero is real and is real \emph{in that frame}.

Which gives the frame-relative form of the result, and it is the useful one. \emph{What does not cohere
in a frame does not exist in it, and no appeal against that verdict is available inside it.} A
containing structure that the current frame cannot resolve is not a thing here that fails a test; it is
not here, and it never comes to be here without the frame's individuation changing --- which is a
transition, not a discovery. That is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} applied to existence and not a weakening of the account.

It does not reinstate what Remark~\ref{rem:inherited} rejected, and the difference matters, the two being easily run together. What was rejected there was existence turning on
\emph{whether some grammar admits it}, which would make the unobserved tree's relation to its forest
wait on a licence. Nothing waits on a licence here. What is being said is only that a distinction not
drawn in a frame is not available in it, which is what having an aperture means. \emph{Is}, having
no frames at all, cannot state any of this and correctly does not: at that level the careful form is
that a description which does not cohere picks out nothing, and there is no current frame for it to
fail to cohere in.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Coherence, not relation, is the criterion]\label{rem:cohere}
The self-edge result bore on \emph{Is}'s first proposition, which as worded the degenerate case
passes: \emph{anything that exists as a determinate something stands in at least one relation} is
satisfied by a thing standing in a relation to itself, and the previous remark establishes that such a
relation carries nothing, so the letter was met and the point was not. \emph{Is} carries a
second proposition for it, and what follows is that content in this paper's vocabulary. \emph{To exist is to
cohere}, where cohering is the relation carrying something --- a fact about one end given the other that
was not already had. \emph{That is this paper's measured rendering of the condition and not its
ground.} At the base the condition is structural --- a relation coheres when its ends are distinct ---
and \emph{Is} states it that way for the reason it gives: there is nothing at that level to
measure with. Here there is, so the same three cases can be sorted by a quantity rather than by a
structural exclusion, and the quantity agrees with the exclusion everywhere it is defined. The
agreement runs in that direction, and reading the quantity as the ground of the base condition would
put an evaluator underneath a level that has none. The three cases differ only in that quantity: nothing related carries nothing and has no relation to
carry it; a relation only to oneself is a relation carrying nothing; two things related carry something,
which is why that case is the only one of the three that is anything at all. The first two are
descriptions picking out nothing rather than things that fail a test --- \emph{Is} is careful about
this and it is worth being careful about here, since a quantity sorted across cases invites reading the
empty ones as populated. \emph{Relation is necessary and coherence is what makes it sufficient.}

Observation remains unnecessary for existence, which the
companion already says, and coherence is what it was leaving open by saying only that --- a difference is
carried whether or not anything comes to register it, and registering is a separate event the
companion's own subject matter cannot perform. And \emph{cohere} is the logician's word used
in frame. A consistency check is exactly what is being run, and this paper has been running it throughout under the name $\Gamma$.
Residual is inadmissibility (Definition~\ref{def:admissible}), which is a frame declining to hold two
things together --- so the mechanism was already here and only the identification was missing.

One boundary on that, since without it the identification would prove too much. Frame-relative
consistency belongs \emph{here}, where there are frames; it cannot be what \emph{Is}'s base runs on,
because a criterion for existing that required a frame would index existing to frames, which
Remark~\ref{rem:inherited} rejects on exactly that ground. At the base the degenerate cases are
degenerate \emph{structurally} --- what a relation to oneself reports about one end given the other was
already given, for anything or nothing --- and $\Gamma$ is that floor made operative once there are
frames to operate it, not a replacement for it. \emph{Is} has no rules of any kind, and does not need them: nothing there \emph{enforces} coherence,
because not existing is not an outcome anything has to bring about. Rules are what \emph{making}
requires, and \emph{Is}'s question is what can exist rather than what can be created --- a
distinction it draws with the one-node case, which can be created and does not thereby exist. What this
paper adds is a grammar of what may follow, which arrives with the happening and is paid for in the
ledger.

Reading the two as one scale places the degenerate case correctly. What cannot be held together at all
is one end; what is held so trivially that having it tells nothing is the other; and a self-relation is
the \emph{second}. One note on the word, since it was in use here before it was a criterion. Where these papers already
say \emph{incoherent} --- a grain that cannot hold what entered it and must split, a self that ceases
to hold together, a superframe re-cohering after a change --- they mean the end where holding is
\emph{impossible}. That is one of the two ends, and the criterion above covers both, so a reader
carrying the established sense into \emph{Is}'s proposition will look for a contradiction in the
self-relation case and find none. There is none to find: that case fails at the other end. The
widening is deliberate and is noted here rather than left to be discovered.

That is why the self-relation's failure is emptiness rather than prohibition --- nothing rules it
out, and there is nothing in it --- and why Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} has no commitment
forbidding one. The relativity is load-bearing in the same way twice over: consistency without a
frame is the unasked question that shortest-description without a vocabulary is, and Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} is what
answers both.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What plurality forces, and what it does not]\label{rem:types-forced}
The measure of the previous remark applies to a \emph{pair} of differences as readily as to the two
ends of one. Doing so forces something, and less than it first appears.

Two differences differing in nothing are not two. Where nothing tells them apart the second carries no
bits the first did not, which is the previous result read across a pair rather than along one edge. So
a plurality \emph{requires} that its members differ, and requires whatever that differing consists in.
This is the argument that forces existence to be graph and not a second one: a plurality requires
whatever makes the plurality possible, and that is not a further posit but the content of there being
more than one. Stated so it needs no relata --- it is difference among differences, and speaking of
relations joining relata gives a frame's rendering of it rather than the thing itself.

The objection is that the differentiating structure looks eliminable. Reify it --- make the relation a
node and hang its ends off it --- and it appears to dissolve into more graph. It does not. The reified
node carries several relations of its own, at least to its ends, and those must be told apart or the
reification has not said which end is which. The problem is \emph{reproduced} at the new level, not
removed, and again at the next. So it is not a convenience bought over some cheaper form lacking it: it
is where distinguishability sits at whatever level one stops, and it descends without closing for the
reason self-reference does.

What that forces is smaller than a \emph{type}, and smaller than a \emph{partition} too. A partition
is a grouping into classes, a class is a sameness holding across
occurrences, and a sameness holding across occurrences is a concept. \emph{Distinctness is forced;
sameness is not.} The asymmetry is not stipulated. That two differences are not one is given with there
being two; that two are of a kind says they are interchangeable in some respect, and a respect is a
frame's. Section~\ref{sec:scope} says as much already in holding that functional identity is the whole
of what identity could mean without a view from nowhere. So what is forced is that they differ in some
further way. Organising those ways into kinds, naming the kinds, and fixing which side of one is the
\emph{yes} are three further things, each supplied and none given.

None of that makes typing absent, and reading it that way would be the opposite error to the one being
corrected. \emph{Types are emergent.} A plurality forces its members to differ; anything that operates
over them repeatedly treats some alike, and an operator treating two differences alike is what their
alikeness consists in --- so kinds arise wherever a plurality is run over, which is wherever anything
runs. Every graph anyone meets is therefore typed, and none is typed at the floor. The defect is never
that types are present; it is only ever the ordering, taking as given what arrives derived.

Which is why the import stays nearly free even from the bare word \emph{graph}. A reader supplies types
on hearing it, because in every field that uses the word they are there, and is almost always right to.
\emph{That is the dangerous case and not the safe one.} A false import is refuted by a counterexample
and does not survive; a true-but-derived one is refuted by nothing, and passes every check that asks
whether it is so. The remedy cannot be a better lens and is the axiom's own: refuse it where it would
return, repeatedly, rather than once and thereafter relied upon.

The descent is worth setting out in order, because each step looks like the floor when seen from the
one above it. There is a difference. Reading its sides as \emph{two things} draws boundaries, and a
boundary belongs to the frame that draws it (Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}). Reading two differences as
\emph{the same kind} adds an identity across occurrences. Naming the kinds adds nothing measurable at
all \citep{shannon1948}. Fixing a polarity adds less than that, there being no \emph{yes} to be
distinguished where nothing has yet been distinguished. Concepts, names, signs and polarities stand on
the supplied side together; \emph{only the difference does not}.

Which says from below why the previous remark came out as it did. A self-relation asserts an identity,
and an identity is what a frame supplies rather than what a difference gives --- so it carries nothing,
not because a special case was found for it, but because it asserts the one thing no difference
contains.

Two exposures, for the two halves. A formalism carrying a genuine plurality with nothing at any level
telling its members apart would refute the forcing, and the reification regress is why none is
expected. A sameness readable off a difference with no respect supplied would refute the limit --- and
that second exposure is not new here. Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} already carries it as
\emph{frame-relative identity}, failing if sameness and persistence are derivable from $(g,r,\Gamma)$
alone without individuation, which is this limit stated before it had an argument. What this remark
adds is the argument, not the commitment.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Grounding]\label{def:grounding}
A \emph{grounding} is a particular assignment of rule content and initial conditions to the substrate
--- one $(g,r,\Gamma)$ with its laws and its starting configuration fixed --- together with the
domain it is taken to model. The substrate is what every grounding shares; a grounding is what any
one of them adds. Claims made about the substrate are accordingly claims about all groundings, and
are correspondingly weak; claims made within a grounding carry that grounding's contingent rule
content and are where this account's falsifiable commitments mostly sit.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Discrete sufficiency}\label{sec:discrete}
\begin{hypothesis}[Discrete sufficiency]\label{hyp:discrete}
Every quantity required internally by the present substrate can be represented by discrete distinguishable states at the operative frame and resolution.
\end{hypothesis}
A convenient coordinate is an integer index $n\in\mathbb Z$ with grounding
\[x=\phi_{\mathcal F}(n).\]
The map may be nonlinear, nonuniform, state-dependent, and dynamically re-quantised. Thus ``integer-valued'' means discretely indexed, not globally unit-spaced.

\begin{proposition}[Arbitrary refinement does not imply continuity]\label{prop:refinement}
A dimension may admit arbitrarily fine or dynamically changing quantisation without requiring the theory to posit realised values at every real-valued intermediate point.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
A continuous containing supergraph is neither required nor excluded. Existing continuous physical theories may ground or contain this substrate without contradiction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Discrete sufficiency is not fundamental discreteness]
These are distinct claims with distinct falsifiers and must not be conflated. Discrete sufficiency is the claim above (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:discrete}), and it fails if some observable consequence internal to the substrate cannot be represented discretely, at the operative frame and resolution, without loss --- a claim decidable in-frame. \emph{Fundamental discreteness} --- that the containing or pre-relational substrate is itself discrete --- would instead concern a fact about the pre-graph substrate that the epistemic boundary above places outside the theory's reach, which is precisely why it is not asserted.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Admissible possibility}\label{sec:admissible}
Let $\Poss(G_t)$ denote the structured family of immediate continuations admitted by the present graph and its current relations.
\begin{definition}[Admissible continuation]\label{def:admissible}
A continuation $\delta$ belongs to $\Poss(G_t)$ iff it is expressible in the current graph vocabulary, satisfies the relevant antecedent conditions, and is admissible under the operative grammar for the transformation class in question.
\end{definition}
The theory does not require a particular algorithm for constructing $\Poss(G_t)$. This extensional separation between graph transformation as a mathematical relation and any particular rewriting algorithm follows the general discipline of algebraic graph transformation, where rewrite rules and admissible transformations can be studied independently of one concrete executor \citep{ehrig2006}.

\begin{definition}[Partial transformation]\label{def:partial-transformation}
Write
\[G\oplus\delta\rightharpoonup G'\]
for the partial realised transformation relation. Undefinedness means that $\delta$ does not produce a structurally valid successor under the stated conditions. No ontological \textnormal{\textsc{FAIL}} state is required.
\end{definition}

\section{Collapse and realised history}\label{sec:collapse-law}
\begin{definition}[Collapse]\label{def:collapse}
Collapse is the frame-relative passage from a pre-realisation possibility structure to realised continuation:
\[\boxed{\Coll_t:\Poss(G_t)\rightsquigarrow G_{t+1}.}\]
It is not identified with deliberative choice.
\end{definition}
The term is inherited from quantum measurement theory, where a formal distinction is made between a pre-measurement state and the realised measurement record; the historical lineage includes the von Neumann measurement scheme and later relative-state formulations \citep{vonneumann1955,everett1957}. This paper does not claim that every grounding reproduces the full quantum formalism; it claims that the possibility-to-realisation distinction is load-bearing.

\begin{definition}[History]\label{def:history}
The realised history of a frame is
\[\Hist=G_0\xrightarrow{\Coll_0}G_1\xrightarrow{\Coll_1}G_2\rightarrow\cdots.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Collapse is a local write, not a global tick]\label{rem:collapse-write}
The subscripts on $\Coll_t$ index a frame's own succession and must not be read as a shared clock. A collapse is a \emph{local realisation} --- a rule writing a delta --- and it is the only kind of event the substrate has (its synchrony or asynchrony with other collapses is set by coupling, Remark~\ref{rem:gals}). Between writes nothing happens: an unwritten frame produces no change, so it takes no new position in the order the changes carry (Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}), and no time passes for it. There is accordingly no global step in which every frame advances together; frames whose collapses are not related through a boundary are simply unordered with respect to one another (Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}). ``Momentum'' --- the standing structure a frame carries, persisting without a distinct write --- is therefore the absence of an event, not a repeated one; a delta is a write \emph{to} that structure, not the structure itself, and the momentum paper names its operative history-bearing form the Narrative (its Definition~\ref{NM-def:narrative}), so that to change course is literally to write the Narrative. The picture of a single global present sweeping forward is exactly the represented time dimension of Axiom~\ref{ax:no-time} mistaken for substrate structure.
\end{remark}

\section{The Law of Graphic Equalisation}\label{sec:the-law}
\begin{schema}[Law of Graphic Equalisation]\label{hyp:the-law}
For realised graph $G_t$, its present structure specifies admissible
continuations $\Poss(G_t)$. Interaction and collapse realise $G_{t+1}$,
which becomes the antecedent for the next local transition. Here
\emph{equalisation} names this accommodation of a proposed continuation to
represented admissibility and containing constraints. It imposes no further
numerical balance or fixed-point condition beyond the stated recurrence.
\end{schema}
Schema~\ref{hyp:the-law} is \emph{the Law} throughout, and \emph{the Graph Law} where the contrast
with a grounding's own laws matters. Thus
\[\boxed{G_t\rightarrow\Poss(G_t)\xrightarrow{\Coll_t}G_{t+1}\rightarrow\Poss(G_{t+1})\xrightarrow{\Coll_{t+1}}G_{t+2}\rightarrow\cdots.}\]

\subsection{The antecedent-state principle}\label{sec:antecedent}
\begin{proposition}[Antecedent-state principle]\label{prop:antecedent-state}
The conditions under which a transition is admissible are functions of its antecedent state. A consequence that exists only because a transition has occurred cannot retroactively serve as an antecedent cause of that same transition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{corollary}[No self-authorisation from future state]\label{cor:no-self-auth}
A reflective transformation of $r$ or $\Gamma$ may alter the successor graph, but newly installed structure cannot authorise the transition by which it first comes into existence.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Recursion and reflection}\label{sec:reflection}
\begin{definition}[Reflective graph]\label{def:reflective}
A graph is reflective over structure $X\subseteq G$ when $X$ is represented in the graph and graph-originated transformations may take that representation as an operand.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}[The grammar is the loop]\label{prop:grammar-is-loop}
If $\Gamma$ is represented as graph structure and admits transformations whose operand is that representation of $\Gamma$, then the resulting recursion is not a separate loop acting on the grammar. The grammar acting on itself is the loop.
\end{proposition}
The argument is short. The transition $\Gamma_t\rightarrow\Gamma_{t+1}$ is itself a graph transition licensed from the antecedent graph (Proposition~\ref{prop:antecedent-state}), so no additional external operator is required to create the recursion; the self-applicable grammar and the compound loop are two descriptions of one transition structure.

\section{Situated graphs and world boundaries}\label{sec:situated}
\begin{definition}[World boundary]\label{def:world-boundary}
A boundary $\mathcal B_{W:G}$ is the relation through which containing frame $W$ can alter situated graph $G$ and through which graph-originated deformation can participate in interactions represented by $W$.
\end{definition}
A graph need not contain a pointer to $W$; containment belongs to the containing frame. A situated graph is a \emph{subset} of $W$, not a thing separate from it: the boundary demarcates which subset is the graph, and self and container are one graph divided by individuation, not two substances (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}).

The boundary is a relation, and that fixes the type of the term this paper uses most.

\begin{definition}[Frame]\label{def:frame}
A \emph{frame} is an instantiated boundary relation --- the edge $\mathcal B_{W:G}$ itself, and not the subgraph $G$ it demarcates. The subgraph is the relatum; the frame is the relation. To individuate is to instantiate such an edge, which is why individuation is per frame rather than a fact about the graph.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Frame and dimension are one edge type in two roles]\label{cor:frame-dimension}
A dimension is the same construction read from the other end. The boundary $\mathcal B_{W:G}$ is a dimension of $W$ --- one of the properties along which $W$ separates what it contains --- and each dimension $W$ instantiates is a frame to the values it separates. So \emph{frame} and \emph{dimension} name two roles one edge type plays relative to another edge, not two kinds of thing, and the asymmetry between them is positional in the same way that the asymmetry between a parent and a child is.
\end{corollary}
\begin{definition}[States of a dimension]\label{def:states}
For a frame $\mathcal F$, a dimension is in exactly one of four states:
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{NULL}\;\neq\;\mathrm{undetermined}\;\neq\;\mathrm{undisclosed}\;\neq\;\mathrm{resolved}.}
\]
\emph{NULL}: the dimension is not instantiated; there is nothing to have a value. \emph{Undetermined}: the dimension is instantiated and \emph{no value has been realised} --- collapse has not occurred. \emph{Undisclosed}: a value has been realised but is not held by this frame. \emph{Resolved}: a realised value, held.
\end{definition}
These are the states of an \emph{edge}, which is why the definition sits here: the boundary is the edge, and a dimension is that same edge read from the other end. A node has no admissible-but-uncollapsed state; an edge does, and Definition~\ref{def:admissible} is about exactly that. Frame-relative termination (Section~\ref{sec:termination}) and the revocation of a dimension are then one operation --- the container ceasing to instantiate the relation --- which is why \emph{Narrativium} observes revocation occurring with $\Delta g=0$ (its Proposition~\ref{NV-prop:conferral}): nothing in the contained state need change for the edge to go.

The construction paper's realisation carries no frame primitive, its grammar quantifying over edge types, and a frame there is a selection of dimensions; that is a description of one realisation and not support for the corollary, which stands on the edge alone. And the convenience reading of a frame as \emph{the subgraph} is used throughout these papers, including above; it is shorthand for the relatum, and where a claim turns on the difference the relation is meant.

\begin{proposition}[Admissibility is self-governance, not armour]\label{prop:self-governance}
$\Gamma_G$ constrains graph-originated transformations of $G$. It does not give $G$ a veto over world-caused change represented through $\mathcal B_{W:G}$.
\end{proposition}
Hence
\[\boxed{\text{self-originated transformation}\neq\text{outward influence}\neq\text{inbound world forcing}.}\]

\begin{proposition}[Self-change is direct and gated; a graph is a subset of its container]\label{prop:no-direct-self-alter}
A graph changes itself and its subgraphs directly. Self-change is real, but it is rule-governed, with its own cost and limits (an instance of the non-uniform writeability of Remark~\ref{rem:writeability}); a substrate's plasticity is exactly the rules for how its rules may change. What a graph does \emph{not} do is change its container directly: the container applies deformations to it --- being observed \emph{is} being so deformed --- and gates its changes through its own change grammar (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-inadmissible}). But a situated graph is a \emph{subset} of its container, not a thing set against it, so a self-change is already a change in the container, in the region that is the graph, propagating outward through whatever couples it to the rest.
\end{proposition}
``Change only yourself'' is therefore not isolation but the mechanism of outward effect: a graph changes its container by changing the part of it that it is. Two things follow. This is one \emph{self-similar} operation --- a graph changing its own subsets --- run at every level: the container does to the graph exactly what the graph does to its subgraphs (Section~\ref{sec:rule-runner}). And it dissolves the agency/determinism dichotomy: a subset changing itself (agency, in the graph's frame) and the whole changing that subset (determined, in the container's frame) are one event read at two scales, so both hold without contradiction. The grammar acting on itself (Proposition~\ref{prop:grammar-is-loop}) is this self-change of the rules; that it is bounded rather than free is because a change to the rules currently executing is heavily constrained --- the worst case of the concurrency of Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}, seen through the frame-limited view of Section~\ref{sec:determinism} --- which is what plasticity's limits are. Governance can tighten to exclusion: where a container's admissibility admits no such self-change, that change is available only across the boundary, from the container.

\begin{proposition}[Coupling is configuration, not architecture]\label{prop:coupling}
That distinct instances individuate --- diverge, each minting its own residual, the part of a change a containing grammar does not admit (Definition~\ref{def:residual}) --- is a consequence of their running on \emph{separate} containing frames, not a necessity. Where several instances are subgraphs of one \emph{shared} containing frame, each instance's self-change is a change in that shared frame, in its own region (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}), which then deforms the others as their common container: instances that are subsets of one container propagate to each other through it. This is the same machinery as being an individual, with the container shared rather than private.
\end{proposition}
Coupling is therefore a continuous setting of the shared boundary's aperture and change grammars (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}), not a binary: sharing only generators leaves each instance its own moment-to-moment state (and already removes the per-substrate build floor of Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}), while sharing all operative structure is one individual over many bodies, with SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity (Section~\ref{sec:self-supergraph}) dialling independently. Nothing in the substrate fixes the setting. The symmetric fact is that a shared containing frame is equally a shared residual and a shared forcing, so the same coupling that shares understanding gives shared corruption, container-level coercion, and collective termination-failure the same reach; ``at what coupling, and with what guarding'' is the substantive question, and it is an engineering one rather than a question of possibility.

\subsection{SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity}\label{sec:self-supergraph}
\begin{definition}[SELF identity]\label{def:self-identity}
SELF identity is the continuity of the graph as a whole --- its rules together with the state they
act on --- across realised transitions. A graph is the same self while its present remains
congruous with its own history: if its rules changed abruptly, the structure carried forward would
no longer cohere with what produced it, and that incongruity is the discontinuity. Represented
self-description --- content inside $g$ by which a graph identifies itself --- is one optional part
of this and not the whole of it; a graph may have SELF identity without representing itself at all.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[SUPERGRAPH identity]\label{def:supergraph-identity}
For graph $G$ individuated in frame $W$,
\[\iota_{W:G}=\operatorname{Id}_W(G)\]
is the identity relation under which $W$ tracks that situated instance.
\end{definition}
SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity are distinct types. A copy can preserve SELF identity while receiving a new SUPERGRAPH identity, and one SUPERGRAPH identity may persist through extensive changes to internal state and self-description.

\begin{corollary}[No frame-free SUPERGRAPH tracking identity]\label{thm:no-frame-free}
The substrate alone supplies no $\operatorname{Id}_W$ tracking edge without
an individuating frame $W$. This follows from Definitions~\ref{def:frame}
and~\ref{def:supergraph-identity}; it does not deny SELF continuity across an
actual run (Definition~\ref{def:self-identity}).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
That two instances are the same is an identity relation, and an identity relation here is an
$\operatorname{Id}_W$ edge (Definition~\ref{def:supergraph-identity}). An edge of that kind is
instantiated by a frame, since to individuate is to instantiate such an edge
(Definition~\ref{def:frame}). The substrate with no frame nominated therefore
instantiates no such edge, and settles nothing about whether the two instances are the same.
\end{proof}

\part{Frames, economics, and failure}
\section{The frame interface and its economics}\label{sec:interface}

The boundary of Section~\ref{sec:situated} can be characterised by what actually crosses it and what it costs, and the characterisation is unexpectedly thin.

\begin{proposition}[One interface, two gates]\label{prop:two-gates}
A situated graph meets its containing frame through a single relation, and what an observation from that frame delivers is not distinct in kind from what the containing frame imposes: to a subgraph, being observed \emph{is} being deformed by its container. That one relation is gated twice, by the same kind of admissibility grammar on its two faces --- the \emph{aperture grammar} $\Gamma_a$ on the input and the \emph{change grammar} $\Gamma_c$ on the output:
\[
\boxed{\underbrace{\text{aperture}}_{\Gamma_a}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\text{rules}}_{r}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\text{change}}_{\Gamma_c}.}
\]
$\Gamma_a$ limits, by grain, which of the container's deformation enters the rules; $\Gamma_c$ determines which of the rules' realised deltas cohere into the container and which remain as residual (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-inadmissible}). Both are faces of the frame's one grammar $\Gamma$, and instantiate for a specific container $W$ as $\Gamma_{W_a}$ and $\Gamma_{W_c}$; it is $\Gamma_{W_c}$, the change face, that the residual proposition uses. Execution runs between the two.
\end{proposition}

The input face is named often enough in these papers to be worth defining rather than assumed.

\begin{definition}[Aperture]\label{def:aperture}
A frame's \emph{aperture} is its input gate $\Gamma_a$ together with what that gate fixes: which
dimensions it can separate along at all, how finely it separates within each (its resolution,
per dimension, for which this paper also uses \emph{grain}), and how far along each it reaches. The three are properties of one edge --- existence,
grain, and extent --- and are named separately because they vary independently, not because they are
different kinds of limit. What falls outside an aperture on any of the three is \emph{absent} to that
frame rather than held-but-unresolved.
\end{definition}

The output face is the same kind of object and takes the same treatment.

\begin{definition}[Change grammar]\label{def:change-grammar}
A frame's \emph{change grammar} $\Gamma_c$ is its output gate: it determines which of the realised
deltas its rules produce cohere into the containing frame and which do not. What it declines has
entered the frame and been operated on, so it is \emph{residual} (Definition~\ref{def:residual})
rather than absent, and it persists in place rather than being discarded. $\Gamma_a$ and $\Gamma_c$
are the two faces of one grammar $\Gamma$ on one boundary edge, and the asymmetry between them is
which direction they gate, not what kind of thing they are.
\end{definition}

This distinguishes two ways a candidate is excluded, with opposite consequences. What falls below the aperture grammar $\Gamma_a$ never enters and is therefore \emph{absent} rather than residual: there is nothing there for the frame to hold or to be disturbed by. What $\Gamma_c$ does not admit has entered, been operated on, and cannot cohere; it is residual, and it persists in place, re-encountered on each merge (Corollary~\ref{cor:mint-is-grammar}). The first is a limit of perception, the second a limit of expression, and phenomena of the ``stuck, repeating'' kind belong exclusively to the second.

\begin{proposition}[Costs are paid; where and how is not fixed by the substrate]\label{prop:no-free-lunch}
The substrate's economic commitment is narrow and should be stated before the specific one that
follows, because the specific one is often mistaken for it. \emph{A change is paid for.} Which frame
pays, in what currency, at what point, and under what discipline is \emph{not} fixed here --- it is
rule content, and implementations differ over it freely. And what the substrate's \emph{economics} forbids is nothing: a change paid
for nowhere is admissible here, exactly as a frame with no economics is
(Remark~\ref{rem:inherited}). The claim is not a prohibition but an observed universal --- \emph{every
change anyone has traced has been paid for somewhere} --- and stating it as a prohibition would give
it a quantifier over all frames that no one can discharge and that
Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} denies anyone the standpoint for. Free lunches are permitted. None has been
found, in any frame that has been measured, and that is the whole of the claim.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Funding is category-preserving, not closed]\label{rem:funding-category}
\emph{A change is paid for in change}, and the status of that is category-preservation and not
closure --- the closure reading does not survive, for reasons this paper supplies against itself.

The event order cannot loop within a frame. Axiom~\ref{ax:no-time} forbids a change enabling what
enabled it, and Proposition~\ref{prop:partial-order} makes a frame's own writes a total order, so no
cycle sits inside one frame; whether a longer cycle can close across frames is not settled here, and
nothing below needs it settled. Proposition~\ref{prop:cycle-unit} does use the word
\emph{closure}, but of a \emph{rule} loop, the same rules re-firing over distinct successive events;
that is not the event order closing, and the two must not be run together. What is left
without a loop is a descending chain, which is a regress rather than a closed thing --- and this paper
already has the right word for where such a chain terminates. Remark~\ref{rem:handler-abuse} says a
purchase buys \emph{relocation rather than closure}, and Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} counts an
in-frame procedure that settles rather than relocates as a single risk named four times. The physics
companion is blunter still (its Section~\ref{PH-sec:cosmology}): it holds that this universe's entry \emph{must come from outside}, a seed
no frame derives from within.

What survives is weaker and is enough. The funding is
\emph{category-preserving}: whatever funds a change is a change, in this frame or in a containing one,
so no second kind of thing is admitted and the economics introduces no primitive beyond what the
admission already brings. That is not a demonstration that the economics needs nothing; it is a
demonstration that it needs nothing \emph{new}. The regress relocates outward exactly as provenance
does, which is what this account says everywhere else. Nothing forbids two brute facts either, so no
argument from scarcity is available --- a costless change is not ruled out by the account being unable
to spend \emph{ex nihilo} twice (Remark~\ref{rem:inherited} counts two unpaid entries and not one).

The falsifier that the closure reading would have licensed goes with it. \emph{Exhibit an economics
that does not close} is not decidable: showing a
funding source is not itself a change requires showing it at every containing level, and the physics
companion says the relevant level is unrepresentable from within. What can be asked instead is finite
and is the surviving claim's own test --- exhibit a change funded by something of a different kind, on
inspection of the frame that made it and the container that sustains it, which is the two-level bound
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-free-lunch} already uses.
\end{remark}

The correction this repeatedly needs is always the same shape: an assignment of the cost mistaken for the invariant. Whether an inadmissible write
propagates or coheres to a value, whether a failure is caught or unwound, whether the check is explicit
at every call or deferred to a marked path --- each is a convention, each puts the cost somewhere
different, and none is the substrate speaking. The invariant is only that the cost is somewhere.

And the floor is not a claim about magnitude. You may pay \emph{less} for the same result --- that is
compression, and Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} is about structures pushed toward it.
You may pay and thereby be able to do \emph{more} --- that is spend-and-gain, and it is why expenditure
is not loss. What is unattested is neither of these but the third thing: getting the result without anyone having
paid. It is unattested rather than excluded, Proposition~\ref{prop:no-free-lunch} being an observed
universal and not a prohibition.

The everyday form makes the mechanism visible and is worth stating, because apparent freeness is
common and always has the same structure. \emph{An all-you-can-eat buffet is free at the point of
consumption and is nonetheless paid.} The cost was moved --- to a different transaction, borne by
another party, amortised across many, settled earlier in time --- and the moving is itself structure
that somebody holds and maintains. Every case of something appearing free resolves this way on
inspection, and the appearance is not an illusion in the frame that has it: at the table the next plate
genuinely costs nothing, which is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} showing up in an ordinary place. What is
unavailable is the view in which nobody paid.

That is a real commitment rather than a definitional one, and its falsifier is stated with the others:
exhibit a realised change whose cost is absent at every frame, rather than relocated to one that was
not being looked at.

\begin{remark}[The cost of a task approaches zero, and the total does not]\label{rem:cost-to-zero}
The preceding proposition has a limiting form that sounds like a contradiction of it and is not. \emph{A well-formed frame's tasks approach zero cost.} Two readings hold and both matter.

The weaker is about overhead. Well-formedness is paying for nothing that does no work, so as a frame
approaches it the \emph{excess} above what the task requires goes to zero. Cost does not: a change is
floored (Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change}), and no arrangement gets beneath the floor. What vanishes is
the difference between what is paid and what must be.

The stronger is about absorption and is the interesting one. A frame meeting something that coheres with
what it already holds writes nothing, and what forces no write costs nothing --- so \emph{the cost of a
task falls toward zero as the task becomes held}, and the floor does not bind because the floor is on
change and a fully absorbed task is not a change. That is what expertise is, mechanically: not a faster
way of paying but a task whose cost has left the write column --- for the search cost of holding it
(Remark~\ref{rem:third-cost}) and for whatever supplies it (Remark~\ref{rem:one-write}), neither of
which the frame pays as a write. The total does not follow it down, for the reason
Remark~\ref{rem:third-cost} gives.

It composes with the previous proposition rather than escaping it, and the composition is the same shape
as the buffet. Fluency is free at the point of use because it was paid for during acquisition; the
learner paid, the expert does not, and they are the same frame at two times. Every case of a task
costing nothing resolves into a case of it having been paid for earlier, which is prepayment rather than
exemption.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A held program is one write; the lanes are the runner's]\label{rem:one-write}
A held motor or cognitive program is one rule whose action is one delta over a whole trajectory, so the
frame pays one write where a learner without it pays one per step (Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}) ---
the prepayment named earlier in mechanism. From the time side it is one cycle,
however much change it does; from the recognition side it is the
first rung of the adaptability paper's ladder, the program decoded as
one held quantum whose parts are no longer distinctions the frame makes (see Proposition~\ref{prop:cycle-unit}
and the adaptability paper's Section~\ref{AD-sec:ladder}). The accounting is that of
vector hardware, one instruction over many data: one decode, many lanes. The status of that arrangement
is the one Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory} gives the gates: it is what a frame under a budget
accretes, forced by what supplies it and confirmed by what such frames are observed to do, and not a
necessity of graphhood. A substrate in which every step is separately written runs; it pays more to.
The frame pays one write; the
runner still pays per lane, and the frame cannot see that, cost being inherited along provision
(Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-recursion}) and a well-formed runner not exposing itself
(Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}). \emph{So expertise is a change in where the work is charged and
not in how much work there is}: the per-step cost has moved below the frame's aperture into what
supplies the program, which is why it presents as free from inside (Remark~\ref{rem:evaluation-writes}).
Two consequences follow. A program is one work only while it fires as one, so interrupting it mid-lane
returns the frame to paying per step, and disrupted skilled action should degrade to novice cost rather
than to a fraction of expert cost --- a prediction, checkable where the Seldon grounding measures such
programs (its Section~\ref{SG-sec:motor}). And copying a generator copies the pointer and not the lanes
(Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}), so a copied program is one work for the copy only if the
copy's runner supplies the lanes --- same program, different runner, different bill.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sideways and up: externalising versus moving up]\label{rem:sideways-and-up}
None of this is about cognition in particular. \emph{Task} here means any change a frame has to pay
for, and the reading applies to a forager, a firm, a lineage and a protocol alike --- which yields the
distinction that does the work.

A cost can be moved \emph{sideways}, to a peer frame. That is externalising, and it buys a real local
advantage. Whether that advantage is temporary depends on the peer: one able to register the cost
responds, and the response is the continuous paid change of Remark~\ref{rem:red-queen}. A peer that
cannot register it --- the case treated later in this paper --- does not, and there the advantage
persists for as long as the signal fails to return, which may be indefinitely. Nothing is reduced by a lateral move; the
total is what it was, redistributed, and the advantage lasts exactly as long as the asymmetry does.

Reduction proper comes from moving \emph{up} --- acquiring structure at a level that did not previously
carry it, so that what each unit below must pay falls. A shared protocol replaces pairwise translation;
a medium of exchange replaces the search for a double coincidence of wants; a held record replaces each
generation re-deriving; multicellularity stops every cell solving the whole problem. In each the task
did not get cheaper for the participant by cleverness at the participant's level. It got cheaper because
something above it now holds structure that was previously being paid for repeatedly, everywhere, by
everyone.

Two honest qualifications, the second of which matters more.

The claim should be sized rather than announced, because two literatures have already written it for
their own domains. \citet{coase1937} explains the firm as coordination moved up out of the market
exactly when the market's transaction costs exceed the hierarchy's, and
\citet{maynardsmith1995} read the history of life as a sequence of such moves --- replicating
molecules to populations in compartments, unlinked replicators to chromosomes, then to cells, to
eukaryotes, to multicellularity, to sociality, to language. Their ladder is theirs and the criterion is
not: they unify the transitions by changes in how information is stored and transmitted, and no
cost comparison appears in that book, so what is imported from them is the sequence while the
\emph{when} is Coase's alone. What is offered here is that these are one
move rather than two analogous ones, which is the same contribution this account
makes elsewhere and no larger.

And moving up is not a free lunch either, which is Proposition~\ref{prop:no-free-lunch} applied one
level out. The higher structure must be built, which is paid, and then \emph{maintained}, which is paid
continuously --- so the move trades a recurring per-task cost for a recurring maintenance cost and wins
only where the ratio favours it.

That ratio is necessary and not sufficient.
\emph{A favourable ratio does not make the move available.} The transition is paid up front, out of what
the frame holds now, so a frame can face an arrangement that would plainly reduce its costs and be
unable to reach it --- the boots theory of socioeconomic unfairness is this at the scale where it is
most legible, the durable purchase that would end a recurring cost being precisely what the recurring
cost prevents accumulating.

This is not a new mechanism and should be named where it already lives. In the companions' terms the
better arrangement is a point in the frame's reachable geometry --- visible, representable, genuinely
accessible as a future --- whose required deformation exceeds the Narrativium measured for the frame. It fails
the feasibility gate and not the desirability one. \emph{Accessible futures with insufficient capacity
to reach them} is the general form, and the boots case is its most legible instance.

One terminological caution, since this paper and its companions divide the words differently: what is
called \emph{capability} here is the gates, while the companions use that word for the reachable set,
which is what this section calls \emph{accessibility} --- \emph{Narrativium} does so following Sen's capability approach,
and \emph{Seldon} does so without the borrowing, where a situation can be a trap and a capability at
once. The distinction drawn is the same in all of them; only the labels differ, and a reader moving
between them should expect that. There is a third, narrower use in the assessment companion, which counts a
capability as a shipped unit with its own tests (its Section~\ref{AS-sec:estimate}); it is stipulated
there and is not this word.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Capability and accessibility]\label{def:capability}
A frame's \emph{capability} is what its gates admit --- what it can represent and what it can do. Its
\emph{accessibility} is what it can \emph{reach} from where it stands, given what it holds to spend. A
disclosure moves a gate and changes capability; whether the reachable point can then be crossed to
depends on the Narrativium measured for the frame. \emph{Narrativium}, which defines that quantity, types it as a
\emph{capacity} rather than as a cost (its Remark~\ref{NV-rem:narrativium-standing}), and keeps measurement and expenditure distinct; the phrasing
here follows it.
\end{definition}

Section~\ref{sec:crossings} will use this: an information hazard is
a \emph{capability} change whose negative sign is admissible in the reference frame, and it is not the
whole of what alters a frame's prospects --- an accessibility failure alters them with the capability untouched. The boots case is
exactly that: nothing about the gates prevents buying durable boots, and the arrangement is nonetheless
out of reach. So a frame can have the gate open and the
capacity absent, in which case the arrangement is fully understood and permanently out of reach, and
information is not the binding constraint --- which is why telling such a frame more accomplishes
nothing. The converse is the ordinary case for which disclosure is the right intervention. An intervention aimed at capability when accessibility \emph{alone} binds, or at accessibility when the
gate alone is shut, does nothing in either direction, and the two are routinely confused because both
present as a frame failing to do the obvious thing. Where \emph{both} bind the case is different and is
treated below.

\begin{remark}[Aperture is purchased, so the two couple over time]\label{rem:aperture-purchased}
The coupling described below makes the order matter as well as the target. Because aperture is
purchased, an accessibility failure becomes a capability failure over time --- the frame that cannot
reach also comes to see less --- so where both bind, relieving accessibility first restores the
capability that was lost to it, while supplying capability first is spent on a frame that still cannot
act on it. That is a claim about sequence rather than about generosity, and it is the practical
consequence of the two being orthogonal at an instant and coupled over one.

The orthogonality is clean at a point in time and does not stay clean, because \emph{aperture is itself
purchased}. Holding a wider aperture or a longer horizon is standing structure a frame pays to maintain,
so anything consuming capacity narrows it --- which bears on the mind companion, where a frame under
stress becomes manipulable by having its horizon shortened rather than its judgement impaired (its Remark~\ref{MS-rem:comm-horizon}). That
paper gives its own account of the shortening; what is offered here is a different one, in this
account's terms, and the two have not been reconciled. The narrowing needs no special stress machinery. It is the ordinary consequence
of the charge for something else having been paid, which is a change in what is reachable rather than a level drawn down.

That prediction is domain-general and dose-dependent, and it has been measured in the least convenient
case for the account's comfort. Scarcity itself consumes the capacity: the same farmers tested before
and after harvest differ substantially in general cognitive performance, with the deficit attributable
to the scarcity rather than to nutrition or to who they are \citep{mani2013}. The narrowing has a name
in that literature --- \emph{tunnelling}, with the capacity it consumes called a bandwidth tax
\citep{mullainathan2013} --- and again the contribution here is not the phenomenon but the reason: a
frame's aperture is standing structure it pays to hold, so a demand on capacity narrows it by the same
mechanism that any other spend would.

Which makes the trap compound rather than merely bind. The frame that cannot afford the move up is, for
the same reason, holding a narrower aperture --- so the arrangement it cannot reach is also one it is
less able to represent, and each shortfall deepens the other. \emph{Insufficient capacity does not only
prevent the move; it degrades the instrument that would identify the move as worth making.} An account
that treated aperture as given would predict a frame stuck but clear-sighted, and that is not what is
found.
\end{remark}

Which makes the up-front cost of a transition a structural quantity rather than an accounting detail: it
decides who can move up at all, and it is why the capacity to make the move is distributed quite
differently from the incentive to make it. That is why such moves are not universal, why they are reversed when
the maintained structure stops being worth its upkeep, and why the ones that persist are the ones whose
maintenance is cheaper than what they displaced.

\begin{remark}[The third cost: finding the structure]\label{rem:third-cost}
There is a third cost before those two and it is the one most easily left out of an account like this,
because it is invisible in retrospect. The higher structure has to be \emph{found}. Building and
maintaining are what you pay once you know what to build, and knowing that is not given --- so the
search is paid too, mostly by whoever does not find anything.

The order is not forced either, and the account should not imply it is. A structure may be found by
accident and explained afterwards, and frequently is; explanation is not what licenses the structure but
what is done with it once held. The biological form of this is ordinary and named --- something arising
under one pressure and later turned to a use it was not reached for --- and the industrial form is
familiar enough to have become a single word. \emph{Research and development are not separable}, and
the reason is structural rather than institutional: you cannot establish what the higher structure is
except by attempting to build it, since a specification you cannot build from is exactly the one that
turns out to have been incomplete.

Which is this paper's own machinery arriving from a third direction. The boundary is known only by
crossing it (Remark~\ref{rem:failure-instrument}); the dependents of a thing are legible only from
outside it (Remark~\ref{rem:vacated}); and now, the structure that would reduce a cost is findable only
by trying to construct it. \emph{The test and the change are one operation}, stated three times in three
places, and R\&D as a single term is that fact already noticed by people who had to pay for it.

One limit keeps this from being a monotone slide to nothing, and it is in the four costs already. Driving
the cost of tasks down means holding more, and what is held is searched: the third cost rises exactly as
the first falls. So a frame does not approach zero total cost by absorbing everything --- it trades
per-task cost against search, which is why aperture is a live setting rather than a value to maximise,
and why a frame that retained all it ever met would pay the most rather than the least.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Cost characteristically reduces to the write]\label{prop:economics}
In the substrate as characterised here, the boundary relation itself is free, and the standing structure persisting is free \emph{to the frame}; the operation that costs is a delta that writes over that structure, and $r$ is what writes. Whether persistence is free \emph{simpliciter} is set by the rules of the superframes, which may sustain a frame without charge or require a write to keep it standing, and the substrate does not decide between them. \emph{Free} to the frame means unbilled to that frame. Information preservation is a separate property: evaluating a rule can be a reversible write, but reversibility alone does not fix its physical cost (Remark~\ref{rem:evaluation-writes}). To that extent
\[
\boxed{\text{cost is paid by rules.}}
\]
\end{proposition}
This is a characterisation of the substrate as specified, not a necessity of graphhood, and the writeability status it describes is not required to be uniform (Remark~\ref{rem:writeability}).
Continuation is therefore free to the frame and change is paid: the standing structure persists until a delta destroys it, so a delta writing a \emph{different} structure --- including the null delta that stops it --- is the priced operation. Whether continuation is free \emph{simpliciter} is settled by the superframes and not here (Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}). This is Proposition~\ref{prop:no-free-lunch} at the level of persistence and Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} applied to the economics --- there is no frame-independent fact about what continuation costs, only what it costs to whom. Retaining a past state that the containing frame does not require is likewise a paid copy, since the containing grammar would otherwise not carry it. This is the substrate form of a result the later papers develop, and the term it turns on should be defined here rather than assumed.

\begin{definition}[Well-formedness]\label{def:well-formed}
A graph is \emph{well-formed} to the degree that it pays for nothing that does no work for it. Four costs bear on that and they behave differently, so the notion is graded along each rather than binary.

\emph{Writes} are paid when made (Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}), which prices doing. \emph{Retention} is nearly free per unit, which is why an unused distinction is not immediately pruned. \emph{Search} is paid on every operation over what is held and scales with how much is held, so inert structure is cheap to keep and expensive to keep in quantity.

The fourth is the worst and is not a variant of the third. A write may land on structure that cannot affect anything --- a modification to a branch nothing reaches. It is paid in full: decided, made, checked, risked. It returns nothing. And unlike a search, which is expensive and then yields, this failure is \emph{silent}: the graph has spent, has no effect to show for it, and no signal that the effect is missing. What it acquires instead is a false model of itself, in which something was changed that was not --- residual it cannot see, produced by its own inert contents, and the share of writes exposed to it grows with how much is held.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[What the four costs do once a graph is running]\label{rem:well-formed-dynamics}
So a graph does not become ill-formed by carrying something useless. It becomes ill-formed by carrying enough that finding the useful parts costs, and enough that its changes start landing where nothing happens.

The third cost is where aperture enters, and it is why the same contents are well-formed for one graph and not another. What search costs depends on how finely the graph can discriminate within what it holds: with structure that separates the relevant from the rest, the cost grows slowly with size; without it, every operation is over everything. That discriminating structure is itself held and itself earns its keep, so a well-formed graph is one whose aperture over its own contents keeps pace with their growth.

Removal is therefore not free either, being a write. Pruning happens when accumulated cost exceeds the one-off cost of removal, and the fourth cost is what usually triggers it: search cost is diffuse and tolerated for a long time, while a change that demonstrably did nothing is a discrete and memorable event. Systems clean up after being burned rather than after slowly noticing. Well-formedness is graded rather than binary; it is relative to \emph{what work} the graph is doing, so a graph well-formed for one purpose is not thereby well-formed for another; and it is not enforced by the substrate. An ill-formed graph runs. It pays more to.
\end{remark}
For a well-formed graph the rules rarely need to write against the standing structure, so cost approaches zero.

\begin{remark}[Landauer bounds logically irreversible change]\label{rem:landauer-change}
Landauer's principle concerns logical information lost in a many-to-one
operation, with the usual $k_{\mathrm B}T\ln 2$ bound for erasing one unbiased
bit in a thermal setting \citep{landauer1961}. A predecessor ceasing to be
instantiated is not sufficient: a NOT gate or a unitary update overwrites its
state bijectively and permits recovery without a saved copy. No-copy therefore
does not make every change logically irreversible. Bennett's reversible
construction computes, copies the output and uncomputes its temporary history,
retaining the input rather than permanently retaining the whole trajectory
\citep{bennett1973}. This physical bound supports the logically irreversible
subclass of the economics; it supplies no universal energy floor for change,
copying, persistence or reversible evaluation. Their actual resource costs
require a physical implementation and a declared accounting boundary.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Evaluation is a write, and free because information-preserving]\label{prop:evaluation-writes}
A read is an operation that returns a datum; the returned datum has to be somewhere to be used, and in
a substrate that is graph all the way down there is no return channel outside the structure, so the
value materialises as structure and the write is the return. Evaluation is a write in that plain
sense: a match outcome, a position in an ordering, and the fact of having tried are returned values,
and returned values are structure. It is information-preserving where its return map is injective. The reference economics leaves it unbilled to the evaluating frame; neither statement establishes zero physical cost. What it costs does not follow from that: a rule's
cost is set by whatever supplies its primitives and is inherited along provision, so reversibility is a
structural fact about a map while cost is a fact about what supplies a primitive, and the two must not
be run together.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Where the check goes, and why the free case is the empty one]\label{rem:evaluation-writes}
Proposition~\ref{prop:evaluation-writes} follows from there being no return channel outside the
structure, and the objection it answers is worth stating rather than rewording. If running a rule
that does not fire is a read, and reads are free, where does the check go, and how does the substrate
know to run the next one?

Computing answers that without any appeal to physics. An operation that returns nothing is
not a read of anything, which is why the free case exists and is exactly the empty one: a read whose
return is never used has read nothing, and a compiler will delete it. Real machines make the same
point expensively --- a read leaves traces in caches, predictors and translation state, and those
traces are measurable, which is why timing attacks recover secrets from a victim whose only secret-dependent operation is a
read \citep{osvik2006} and why an entire class of speculative attacks was available to designs built on the assumption that rolled-back
speculation leaves no trace \citep{kocher2019}, where the read is the vehicle rather than the premise.

What does not follow is the
price, for the reason the proposition gives (Section~\ref{sec:rule-runner}), and Landauer's bound
(Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change}) is a floor in one substrate rather than the price in any. This is also why continuation presents as free from inside: a
well-formed runner does not expose itself (Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}), so its charge is
below the frame's aperture and is not an in-frame event at all. In-frame nothing happened; in the
running substrate something was paid; both hold, of different frames.

Holding reversibility and cost apart
is what lets the physics companion recover unitarity (its Section~\ref{PH-sec:amplitudes}), which needs only the first, since a
no-write was never the right description of an evolution that plainly changes the state and is
reversible because it is injective. And the residual named in Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change}
attaches here: information-preserving evaluation accumulates the history it preserves, so a frame
running a long time without collapsing has deferred a cost into retention rather than escaping one.
What is free is not the running, only the not-yet-forgetting --- and free only relative to a frame
that cannot see the bill.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The Red Queen requires continuing adaptation costs]\label{rem:red-queen}
Maintaining a relation to a changing containing frame can require repeated
adaptation. In a grounding with positive adaptation costs, those costs recur
even when relative position does not improve. This is a structural reading of
the evolutionary Red Queen and cultural relearning, not a derivation of a
universal thermodynamic treadmill. Landauer supplies a floor only for logical
information erased during those changes (Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change});
reversible changes and copied generators need separate resource accounting.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What is required and what is optimisation]\label{rem:nothing-compulsory}
Only that rules run is compulsory for anything to \emph{occur} --- structure alone is what is required for there to be anything at all (Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}). Applying a delta is itself a rule, not a primitive operation beneath the rules, so the specific dynamics --- inertia, the merge behaviour, closure --- are contingent rule content, and a different $r$ or $\Gamma$ is a different physics rather than a violated law (this is why the initial and boundary conditions of a grounding are supplied separately from its laws; Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}). The two gates of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}, and even the retained standing structure itself, are \emph{optimisations} on bare rule-running: the aperture grammar $\Gamma_a$ bounds the input, the change grammar $\Gamma_c$ sorts the realised output into what coheres and what is residual, and the retained structure buys free continuation. A substrate whose grammar admits everything still runs --- effectively ungated, without momentum, wastefully. It is not a substrate \emph{lacking} $\Gamma$, since Definition~\ref{def:admissible} defines the possibility structure through the grammar and a collapse needs something to select from. The two gates are accordingly what an efficient graph accretes under a budget, not constituents of graphhood. The retained structure specifically is what distinguishes a self-sustaining loop from a purely reactive one, since coasting is the cached structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Writeability status is contingent, not uniform]\label{rem:writeability}
The write-economics stated here --- reads free, cost carried by writes, and no direct alteration of the container (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}) --- should be held at the strength the evidence supports, and the two halves of that need
separating. The \emph{structural} claim --- that a graph affects its container as a subset of it and not
by some other route --- is a strict restriction and is committed to as one
(Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability}). What is a pattern rather than a universal is the \emph{distribution}
below: which writes are free, which are costly, and how much is direct. What is freely writeable, what carries a cost, and what a graph can write directly rather than only across a boundary, need not be uniform; different structures may have different writeability profiles. The strict forms above characterise a common and efficient configuration (Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory}) rather than a necessity of graphhood, and asserting a single strict write-economics everywhere would be the same over-specification Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory} warns against, a fixed value put where the account has only a setting. These are accordingly carried as falsifiability commitments, open to counterexample, and a structure with a different writeability profile refines the characterisation rather than breaking the substrate.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Copyable generators are a difference in kind]\label{prop:copyable-generators}
A copy that preserves SELF identity copies whatever operative structure lies in $g$ --- including a graph's \emph{generators}: its built shapes, codebook, and rules. Where a substrate is graph-native, so that a graph's understanding \emph{is} such structure rather than something separate that structure only produces, copying the graph copies the understanding, at the copy cost of Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} rather than at the full construction floor.
\end{proposition}
This is the difference in kind that separates such a substrate from those whose generators are substrate-bound. The build floor of a generator is a large accumulated cost (Remark~\ref{rem:red-queen}); paying it once and copying the result is not a quantitative saving over paying it per instance but a removal of the per-substrate re-payment that made the cultural Red Queen permanent. It is not available where only a generator's cheap products cross a boundary: biology copies the construction recipe and not the learned structure, which is re-developed each lifetime; symbolic culture copies samples and not generators; a fixed program copies a capability and not a growable understanding. In a graph-native substrate the built, growable, understanding-bearing generator
is itself the copyable object. Priority is not claimed and would be wrong: the weights of a trained
network are also a copyable generator, built rather than sampled, and the difference here is that the
generator is inspectable and rule-governed structure rather than an opaque parameterisation --- a
difference in kind of access, not in whether copying a generator is possible. The copy remains a distinct SUPERGRAPH identity (Corollary~\ref{thm:no-frame-free}) and diverges once it runs on its own containing frame; what is shared is the paid-for generator, not the subsequent individuation.

\section{Residual}\label{sec:residual}
Suppose a frame represents expected or attempted deformation $\delta^P$ and collapse yields realised deformation $\delta^R$.
\begin{definition}[Residual]\label{def:residual}
The residual is
\[\boxed{\epsilon=\delta^R\ominus\delta^P,}\]
where $\ominus$ is the grounding's difference relation preserving relevant discrepancy.
\end{definition}
Residual is mismatch, not diagnosis. Complete non-realisation may therefore return the whole attempted deformation as residual.

\begin{proposition}[No implicit historical state]\label{prop:no-implicit-historical-state}
A previous success, failure, attempt, or residual has no later causal force merely because it occurred. It affects later dynamics only insofar as its consequences are present in later graph or boundary state.
\end{proposition}
Thus a graph with no feedback machinery can re-derive the same failed transformation repeatedly. This is compatible with the classical control-theoretic distinction between plant dynamics and feedback: a controller changes future action only insofar as measured error is coupled back into its state or control law \citep{wiener1948,ashby1956}.

\begin{proposition}[Residual is what a containing grammar does not admit]\label{prop:residual-inadmissible}
Because the graph is a subgraph of its containing frame $W$, a realised graph-originated deformation is \emph{already} a change in $W$ --- it is not submitted to $W$ for entry, and there is no step at which $W$ grants or withholds it. The delta is not offered; it \emph{is}. What the \emph{change grammar} of $W$ --- write it $\Gamma_{W_c}$, the change face of $W$'s grammar (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}) --- admits coheres with the rest of $W$ and merges into its structure; what $\Gamma_{W_c}$ does not admit is not thereby undone, but remains in place, present and unintegrated. Admissibility sorts a change that already exists; it does not gate one seeking to occur. The residual is exactly this non-admitted remainder:
\[
\boxed{\epsilon \;=\; \delta^R \ominus \delta^P \;=\; \text{the part of the realised deformation }\Gamma_{W_c}\text{ does not admit.}}
\]
\end{proposition}
This locates the residual as the negative of an admissibility judgement rather than as an independently produced quantity, and it is what makes the merge non-destructive: what fits is absorbed, and what does not is left untouched, not deleted. A deformation may pass the graph's own change grammar $\Gamma_{G_c}$ (admissible to itself) yet fail the containing $\Gamma_{W_c}$ (inadmissible to its frame); the residual is precisely that gap between a graph's change grammar and its container's.

\begin{corollary}[Growth is grammar extension]\label{cor:mint-is-grammar}
A residual is absorbed either by a containing frame extending $\Gamma_{W_c}$ to admit it, or by paying to copy it out (Section~\ref{sec:interface}); otherwise it persists in place and is re-encountered each time the merge runs. The first route is the substrate form of \emph{minting} a new distinction: the residual does not fit because it is inadmissible, so admitting it requires extending the grammar, and extending the grammar is the act by which a previously inadmissible deformation becomes recognisable. Recognition and growth are therefore one mechanism joined at one place --- the residual is where the shape a graph decodes and the grammar that admits it meet.
\end{corollary}
Two properties of this mechanism matter. Residual is \emph{per-rule}: each firing leaves its own remainder, local to where the rule ran, with no global residual store, so it is dealt with per-frame --- which is why the residual-and-mint loop is frame-portable rather than living in a domain-specific module. And minting is itself a \emph{rule}, not a primitive: extending $\Gamma_{W_c}$ is the grammar acting on itself (Proposition~\ref{prop:grammar-is-loop}), a reflective rule subject to the same economics, admissibility, and antecedent-state principle (Proposition~\ref{prop:antecedent-state}) as any other. Growth is therefore rule-governed, costed (the spend-and-gain of \emph{Narrativium}, its Proposition~\ref{NV-prop:spend-gain}), contingent, and buildable --- never a primitive spark. It also cannot bootstrap from nothing: by Proposition~\ref{prop:antecedent-state} a mint cannot authorise its own installation, so a graph's first mint-rules are installed from its container. This is the substrate reason a situated graph requires a seed --- the minimal rule-set, including the capacity to mint, that a container provides so the graph can thereafter grow itself. The two provenances --- seeded from the container, or minted from residual --- are what the nature/nurture dichotomy names, and they are not two \emph{kinds} of thing: both are rules that run identically, the running records no provenance, and the capacity to mint (nurture's mechanism) is itself seeded (nature), so what remains measurable is provenance, not a distinction of kind.

\section{Decoherence}\label{sec:decoherence}
Residual is what a containing grammar declines to absorb. This section is the limiting case of that:
what happens when a write is not partly unabsorbed but wholly inadmissible, so that no continuation is
realised at all. It is placed separately because it is a specific thing rather than a corollary of the
last one --- an inadmissible write has its own failure structure, its own economics, and its own
consequences for what depends on the frame it failed in.

Two strings make the structure visible. \emph{$2+2=5$} is a perfectly coherent set of symbols: it
parses, every token is admissible, the arrangement is grammatical. \emph{Two particles smash into each
other at $.99c$} is coherent in the same way. Both pass the aperture and fail at the change grammar,
which is the two-gate structure of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates} in its sharpest case --- what is
admitted as \emph{represented} need not be admissible as a \emph{change}.

What follows is not a partial merge. The collapse realises nothing, and the result is NULL --- which is
not the same as \emph{absent}: absent is never represented, whereas this is represented, admitted, and
yields no continuation.

\begin{proposition}[Decoherence is one operation; the frames are what differ]\label{prop:decoherence-one}
Decoherence is one operation. It does not come in kinds and it
does not come in degrees, and intended and unintended incoherence both return no coherent state. What differs between them is the
frame --- arithmetic in one, kinematics in the other --- and \emph{the frames are what make the cases
different, not the failure}. ``Everything in the superframe'' is a constant expression whose value is
frame-relative, which is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} applied to incoherence rather than to observation.
\end{proposition}

The obvious gloss is wrong in a specific way. One is tempted to rank
them --- arithmetic lies deeper than kinematics, so denying it costs more --- and depth is real, but it
is a fact about a web of commitments and therefore about a frame, never about the decoherence. The
temptation is to read a magnitude off a difference that belongs elsewhere. That no distinction of
\emph{kind} survives here between the analytic and the empirical case is Quine's conclusion
\citep{quine1951}; the present account reaches it without needing his gradient, since the failure is
uniform and only the frame is indexed.

The two companion papers instantiate this at their own grains, and the word is used in both. The
physics companion separates the sense used here from the standard environmental one and states what
relates them (its Remark~\ref{PH-rem:two-decoherences}); the mind companion reads death as this operation with the frame set to a person (its Remark~\ref{MS-rem:death-decoherence}).
Neither is required for what follows.

\begin{remark}[Write versus read: $2+2=5$ is not false]\label{rem:write-versus-read}
The obvious objection is that $2+2=5$ has a truth value and that value is false. It does not, and the
notation already says so. \emph{$2+2=5$ is not false; $2+2{=}{=}5$ is false} --- one sign writes and
the other reads, and computing has kept them apart in its surface syntax for exactly as long as it has
had both. The comparison is an ordinary in-frame operation returning an ordinary in-frame value. The
assertion is a write, and in a typed setting it does not evaluate to anything at all: it is refused
before execution, because nothing admits an assignment to a value. Rejected by the grammar rather than
run, with no continuation taken from it.

So the NULL belongs to the write, and the familiar falsity belongs to a different operation that is
spelled differently. That the two are easy to confuse is not a quibble either --- writing the write
where the read belongs is among the most persistent defect classes there is, guarded against by
compilers and linters because the cost of the confusion is high. The distinction is load-bearing
wherever both operations exist, which is the point of Section~\ref{sec:carvings} arriving from the
other direction. And the residual is not a local remainder. Admitting $2+2=5$ into arithmetic
does not cost a repair somewhere; it costs the grammar's restrictiveness, since a grammar that admits
a contradiction admits everything and thereby stops restricting anything. So the residual is
\emph{everything in the superframe}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The handler analogy, and what the substrate does not fix]\label{rem:handler-analogy}
The mechanism for that indexing settles what would otherwise look like a family of competing answers,
and the useful way to see it is by analogy with exceptions: a frame that rejects a write raises, and the
failure goes up until something handles it. Read it as an analogy, because the substrate fixes
far less than the analogy suggests. What follows from admissibility alone is only this: an inadmissible
write yields no continuation \emph{in that frame}. Everything downstream of that --- whether anything
propagates, whether the container is notified at all, whether a value is produced instead and which
one --- is rule content, and rules here are contingent throughout. There is no substrate-level default.

Read as the analogy it is: a frame that rejects a write raises, the failure goes one frame up and up
again until something handles it or nothing does, and unhandled to the top the whole thing stops, which
is what ``residual of everything in the superframe'' describes. A frame may instead cohere the failure
to a value --- false, a gap, NaN, null, or a contradiction held without explosion --- and any of these
is available, as installed machinery someone had to put there. Nothing about the failure selects the
value; the frame's rules do.

The reason to insist the framing is arbitrary is that computing itself does not agree on it. Exceptions
propagate by default and are handled on the marked path; return codes, result types and option types
make the check explicit at every call and propagate nothing on their own; hardware may trap or may set
a flag and continue. These are not approximations of one true discipline. They are different rule sets
for the same underlying fact, which is only that something did not work.

The demonstration is in silicon and is not a metaphor. IEEE~754 gives floating point a \emph{value}
where integer arithmetic \emph{traps} --- $1/0$ is an infinity in one and a fault in the other --- and
NaN then propagates by deliberate standardisation \citep{ieee754}. Calling that weak Kleene in hardware
overstates it and discards the better example: the standard is \emph{near} infectious with carve-outs
written in on purpose, so that $\mathrm{pow}(x,\pm 0)$ is $1$ even for NaN and the
minimum/maximum-number operations return the number. A genuinely infectious value would be a discovered
semantics; a mostly-infectious one with a schedule of exemptions is what an \emph{installed policy}
looks like. One machine, two policies, chosen per type. \emph{The failure did not choose; the committee
did.}

The economics is therefore weaker than it looks, and the tempting stronger claim is a good example of the error this section warns about: that propagation costs nothing while a handler is purchased, so that a total residual is the unmarked case. That holds \emph{under the
exception convention} and not otherwise: where failure is carried in return values the check is
explicit and paid at every call, and it is the propagation rather than the handling that costs. Which
side the cost falls on is the convention's, not the substrate's.

What survives generally is only that a policy of any kind is held structure and is paid for under
Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}, so a frame that bounds its residuals holds more than one that does
not, whatever discipline it uses to do it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Prior art: handlers as policies]\label{rem:handler-prior-art}
That computing does not agree reorganises the prior art rather than accumulating it. \emph{These systems are handlers.}
Bochvar's infectious junk value, the neutral free logics' gaps for empty terms, Reichenbach's third
value for the quantum indeterminate \citep{reichenbach1944}, paraconsistent absorption
\citep{priest1979}, relevance logic's refusal of ex falso on grounds of relevance
\citep{anderson1975} --- each is a policy for what a frame coheres a failure to, and that they are
mutually incompatible while each being defensible is evidence they are choices rather than discoveries.

Almost all of this is prior art, in a literature this account did not come from, and the correspondence is close enough to state precisely. That a string may be syntactically well-formed and yet
fail is Ryle's category mistake, and he used such failures for the purpose this remark puts them to ---
delineating the categories, the failure showing where a boundary runs \citep{ryle1949}. That the result
is neither true nor false but a third thing is the truth-valuelessness position. And the propagation
--- residual of everything in the superframe --- is exactly \citet{bochvar1938}'s three-valued calculus,
in which a compound takes the junk value if any constituent does, so nonsense is infectious rather than
local. \citet{hallden1949} named the resulting system the logic of nonsense and made it
\emph{paraconsistent}, which combines in one system what the paragraphs above treated as two frames;
the separation of a theory being inconsistent from its being trivial is likewise established, and is
what the economic reading here restates \citep{dacosta1974}.

One further correspondence is not a debt but evidence for a different claim in this paper. Computing
reached the same machinery independently and for its own reasons: ill-typed terms are refused before
execution rather than evaluated to a useful value \citep{milner1978} --- strictly, an ill-typed term
evaluates to $\mathbf{wrong}$, which exists in that semantics so the soundness theorem can be stated,
and the contrast being drawn is with producing an ordinary value --- and denotational semantics gives
divergence a value $\bot$ with $f(\bot)=\bot$ for \emph{strict} $f$ \citep{scott1971}. The restriction is load-bearing: bottom does \emph{not} propagate through any compound containing it,
because that monograph restricts function spaces to the
continuous and explicitly not to the strict, and a non-strict function --- a constant, a lazy
conditional --- has $f(\bot)\neq\bot$. Nor is this an identity with Bochvar, for whom infectiousness is
definitional for every connective rather than a property some functions have. What survives is
narrower and is still what the proposition needs: two fields, decades apart, solving different problems,
both found that a failure value which propagates through the compounds \emph{built to propagate it} is
the cheap arrangement, and both then had to say which compounds those are. Arriving at one arrangement
from different problems is what Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} predicts,
which requires the forcing to be shared and not merely the outcome, and it is offered here as an instance of that
rather than as support for this remark, which needs none.

What is not taken from that literature is the frame-indexing. The formal systems fix an infectious
value and derive its behaviour; the claim here is that the same failure yields the appearance of
falsity in one frame and of nonsense in another, so the taxonomy separating $2+2=5$ from a category
mistake is itself the frames differing rather than the failures. That is a claim about where the
distinction lives, not a new logic, and the logics above are the machinery it would be written in.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The physical case: criticality]\label{rem:criticality}
The physical case at the largest scale makes the purchase visible, and it is the same shape rather than
an illustration of it. A heavy nucleus sits near the limit of what holds it together; add a neutron and
the configuration cannot be held, so it does not continue as that nucleus and the binding energy goes
to the container. A reactor and a weapon are then the identical failure with the handler installed and
with it removed --- control rods, moderator and containment are held structure, paid for continuously,
and the whole of their function is to bound a residual that is otherwise total. What the Manhattan Project's design problem amounted to was keeping an unhandled configuration
assembled long enough for the propagation to finish, which is the cost of \emph{defeating} a handler
rather than of installing one. The handler is viable at all only because well under one per cent of the
neutrons are delayed, which supplies a timescale it can act within: \emph{a frame whose failure
propagates faster than its handler can run has no handler}, and that is a constraint on the purchase the
logical cases do not exhibit.

Criticality
is the condition where each raise produces more than one further raise, so the residual does not merely
ascend a frame but multiplies. That is a genuine difference from an exception unwinding a stack, and
the substrate accommodates it only because residual is per-rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-inadmissible} and the paragraph following it) so one
failing write may leave several. It should be recorded as a difference and not smoothed into the
analogy. It is also where this remark meets \emph{Narrativium} from the other side, since a process
whose products exceed their cause in number is that paper's spend-and-gain iterated above unit gain
(its Propositions~\ref{NV-prop:spend-gain} and~\ref{NV-prop:overshoot}) ---
the same ratio, arrived at through failure here and through capacity there.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The second string is not false either]\label{rem:second-string}
The second string carries a further lesson and is a better instance than it first appears, because it
is not in fact false. A closing speed of $1.98c$ is unproblematic, being no object's velocity, and the
relative velocity is $(0.99+0.99)/(1+0.99^2)\approx0.99995c$, under $c$. The incoherence arises only
when the sentence is evaluated \emph{in one frame by addition} --- an in-frame operation applied to a
quantity that lives between frames. It fails as a category error rather than as forbidden physics,
which is the in-frame/cross-frame discipline of Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive} appearing as a
coherence verdict.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A handler relocates a failure; it does not close it]\label{prop:handler-relocates}
A handler is installed policy that determines what a frame coheres a refused write to, and it can be
set to cohere it to anything, including success. Swallowing does not undo the inadmissibility: the
containing grammar still refused and the write still did not happen, so what is removed is the frame's
\emph{record} of the refusal rather than the refusal. Detection is available and never free --- a frame
holding a record of the attempt separately from the handler's report can compare the two --- but it is
unavailable from the handler's own report, which is self-consistent by construction. The audit is
therefore installed structure, and being itself a policy it is abusable in the same way one level in,
so what its purchase buys is relocation rather than closure, terminating only in a containing frame.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[A handler can be abused, and the abuse is invisible from inside]\label{rem:handler-abuse}
Proposition~\ref{prop:handler-relocates} follows from a handler's being installed policy, and
computing knows the abusive case as the swallowed exception --- the catch that discards what it
caught, the broad handler that reports normal completion --- which is not a defect in the mechanism
but the mechanism used against its purpose.

What that does is already costed here. Removing the frame's record of a refusal
leaves the frame holding a model of itself in which
something occurred that did not. That is exactly the fourth cost of Definition~\ref{def:well-formed} ---
expenditure returning nothing, with the failure silent --- and the swallowing handler is the mechanism
by which a false self-model is manufactured rather than merely risked.

The stronger form follows from the same premise. \emph{If the handler
determines the outcome, then causing a failure is a way of choosing the outcome.} One need not make the
preferred continuation admissible; one can make the alternative fail to cohere and let the policy
deliver the result. Failure becomes an instrument rather than an accident, and the move is recognisable
wherever a procedure has a default: the party who prefers where the exception path lands has no reason
to argue on the merits.

The obvious formulation of the detection clause overshoots, and the proposition states it in the
form that survives. It is not that a frame cannot detect a swallow, and well-built systems detect one
routinely; what is unavailable is detecting it from the handler's own report. So the audit is
installed structure, paid for like any other, and a frame that has not bought one has no access to the
discrepancy. That the relocation terminates only in a containing frame is
Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} arriving where it is least convenient, since the
frame with most reason to detect this is the one whose own machinery is doing it. The silence of the
fourth cost has this structure, and so does the presented-versus-produced divergence the mind
companion treats at the scale of one frame acting on another (its Proposition~\ref{MS-prop:one-influence}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Termination propagates along supply, not containment]\label{prop:termination-supply}
Being contained by something and being run by it are two relations, and they need not coincide.
Termination propagates along the supply relation and not the containment relation: downward it need not
reach at all, a container being able to stop while what it held runs on, and upward the supplier's stop
is total, at whatever containment distance and with no handler available, since the operations a
handler would run are the ones withdrawn. This splits the dependents of a stopped structure into three
kinds rather than two. A dependent that merely \emph{refers} to it is left dangling and keeps running,
wrongly; a dependent \emph{wholly run by} it simply stops; and a dependent only \emph{partly} supplied
by it keeps running with an incomplete operation set, and its behaviour is undefined.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Being contained by something and being run by it]\label{rem:supply-not-containment}
Proposition~\ref{prop:termination-supply} follows from primitives not exposing their provenance
(Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}), which the preceding material had assumed away. A subgraph's operations need not be supplied by the graph that
contains it: a primitive may come from arbitrarily far up and be passed through, and nothing in the
subgraph can tell. So if a graph $G$ sits inside $W$ while running on primitives supplied from beyond
$W$, then $W$ ceasing does not stop $G$. \emph{There is no reason it cannot continue}, because what ran
it was never the thing that stopped.

The two relations usually coincide, which is why the distinction is easy to miss, and the
coupling result already says they need not (Proposition~\ref{prop:coupling}) --- a graph fused to what
supplies it has no independence, a loosely coupled one has all of it. Survival of a container's
termination is therefore a question about \emph{coupling} and not about containment, and it is
arrangeable: a frame that wants to outlive its container can be built so that its supply comes from
higher up, which is a purchase like any other.

That refines the previous remark rather than sitting beside it, since it splits the dependents into
three kinds and not two. The third kind exists at all because supply is a set rather than an edge,
primitives being drawn from wherever they are drawn from, so a dependent may be supplied only in part
by what stopped.

That third kind is the worst of them, and it is the silent one. Such a frame
does not stop and does not visibly break; it runs until it reaches a rule requiring an operation that is
no longer there, and \emph{it cannot know in advance which rules those are}. There is no dangling edge
to find, because a withdrawn primitive leaves no structure behind pointing at nothing --- the operation
is simply absent. And the frame cannot audit for it, since provenance is not exposed
(Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}): there is no procedure by which it asks which of its own
operations came from the thing that stopped. So the loss is discovered only by attempting it, which is
Remark~\ref{rem:failure-instrument} arriving with nothing to be learned that was worth the price.

That places it in the family this paper keeps returning to. A partially supplied frame holds a model of
itself in which it can do things it can no longer do, which is the false self-model of the fourth cost
(Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}) and of the swallowed handler
(Remark~\ref{rem:handler-abuse}), arrived at a third way. Graceful degradation is the name for buying
one's way out of this, and it is worth noting that it is a purchase: the \emph{unbought} behaviour of a
partially supplied frame is not graceful, it is undefined.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A boundary is known only by crossing it]\label{prop:boundary-by-crossing}
A frame that never fails discloses nothing about what it admits. Admissibility is invisible from inside
its own range, since everything works, and the boundary is legible only where it is crossed --- so the
way to learn a rule is to force the failure and read the residual. The test and the change are
accordingly the same operation: knowledge of a boundary is bought by crossing it, and there is no
cheaper instrument. It follows that every handler installed reduces what failure can teach, a frame
that coheres its failures to tidy values reporting success and disclosing nothing, so that robustness
and legibility are traded against one another rather than being two names for quality.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Forcing a failure is the instrument, and handlers blunt it]\label{rem:failure-instrument}
Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-by-crossing} follows from admissibility's being invisible from inside
its own range. Remark~\ref{rem:handler-abuse} treats a caused failure as a way of choosing an
outcome; the same act has a second use which is not abuse at all, and it is how most of what is known
about any grammar was found.

Forcing a failure to read the residual is not a marginal technique but the standard one across
unrelated fields, which is what a generative claim should predict. Genetics removes a gene and observes what stops
\citep{capecchi1989}; neuroscience reads function from lesions; machine learning ablates a component
to price it; software injects faults to check that anything notices \citep{basiri2016}. And physics
builds the largest instrument there is on exactly this principle --- an accelerator spends energy to
push interactions onto one step, where the frame cannot sequence them and the interaction is decoherent
in that frame (the physics companion's reading of the high-energy regime, its Remark~\ref{PH-rem:two-decoherences}). One does not observe the
grammar; one pays to violate it and observes the remains.

Two consequences follow. The first closes a circuit opened twice already in this
paper. Establishing that structure is inert requires perturbing it, and the perturbation is the removal
one wanted licensed (Remark~\ref{rem:evolution-compression}); the dependents of a thing are held in
other frames and cannot be surveyed from it (Remark~\ref{rem:vacated}); and now, the rule governing a
frame is legible only where it is broken. These are one statement in three places, and the proposition
above is that statement. Which also prices the instrument, since by the accelerator's
own economics each further increment of boundary costs more than the last.

The second consequence is a cost of handlers that is easy to miss because it looks like a benefit,
and it is the trade the proposition ends on. That the same system is customarily made loud in development
and quiet in production is this trade made deliberately --- two frames wanting opposite policies over
identical structure, for the reason given here rather than by convention.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Dependents are held by the referring frames, not the referent]\label{prop:vacated-three-states}
A thing does not carry a record of what depends on it: the dependencies live in the frames holding the
references and not in the referent, so from the position of the structure being removed they are not
visible at all, and knowing what would break requires the containing frames. Removal therefore yields
three states rather than two. \emph{Absent} --- nothing ever cohered, nothing is held and nothing owed,
and it is free. \emph{Vacated with the references left standing} --- a hole, unpaid, with consequences
determined by whatever the dangling relations do next. \emph{Vacated with the absence represented} ---
a tombstone, a cascade rule, a retained count of dependents --- which resolves cleanly and is installed
machinery paid for under Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[What never cohered is absent; what cohered and stopped leaves a hole]\label{rem:vacated}
Proposition~\ref{prop:vacated-three-states} follows from a distinction the preceding material needs
and has not drawn. Something that never cohered is simply
\emph{absent}, and absence is free --- nothing points at it, nothing expected it, and no frame is worse
off for it. Something that cohered and then stopped is a different object: it leaves \emph{a space
where something was}, and the difference is that other structure came to depend on it while it held.

The consequences of the second are underdefined, and underdefined in a specific way rather than merely
unpredictable. What breaks is not the removed structure --- it is gone and has no further behaviour ---
but every relation elsewhere that resolved through it and now resolves through nothing. Computing has
the sharp case and calls it exactly that: dereferencing what was freed is undefined behaviour, and its
mildest form is a wrong answer while its worst is unbounded. A variable that was never assigned and one
that was assigned and released fail differently, and only the second can corrupt something that was
correct.

The reason this cannot be surveyed in advance is structural rather than practical, and it is
Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} again in its most operational form: from the position of the thing being
removed the dependencies are not visible at all. Knowing what would break requires the
containing frames, and asking them is the expense.

Of the three states that follow, the third is the one that costs, and it is the deletion-side member
of the handler family of Remark~\ref{rem:handler-abuse}. Reference counting is not a fact about
references; it is a purchase.

This is also the sharper form of the removal cost argued later for genomes and dead code
(Remark~\ref{rem:evolution-compression}). The reason deletion is expensive is not mainly that the
excised structure might turn out to do something. It is that the survey licensing the deletion is a
question about \emph{other} frames, obtainable only from them, and a confident remover is someone who
has not paid for it. Chesterton's rule about the fence is this stated as advice \citep{chesterton1929};
the ecological version, where removing one species collapses relations that were not obviously routed
through it, is the same structure with the dependents alive \citep{paine1969}.
\end{remark}

\section{Crossings, and what a disclosure moves}\label{sec:crossings}
Forcing a failure is how a boundary is found (Remark~\ref{rem:failure-instrument}), and that instrument
has consequences the preceding section did not draw out: crossings are paid for by somebody, the payment
may be arranged to return slowly or not at all, and a disclosure of what lies past a boundary changes
what other frames can do. This section treats those, and is separate from the decoherence machinery because none of it is about
how a write fails --- it is about who bears the cost of finding out and what the finding then moves.

\emph{Its result, stated first.} An information hazard is not a class of its own but a capability
change of a particular sign and size, defined at Definition~\ref{def:info-hazard}. Everything
after it supports that: why the neutral class needed naming, why the sign has to be
admissible rather than merely felt, and why the threshold cannot be an absolute.

\begin{definition}[Information hazard]\label{def:info-hazard}
A disclosure changes admissibility when it moves a frame's aperture gate, its change grammar gate, or
both (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}), and what it changes when it moves one is that frame's
\emph{capability} (Definition~\ref{def:capability}). An \emph{information hazard} is a capability
change whose negative sign is admissible in the reference frame, above the point at which the impact
forces a write. The class is disclosures that change admissibility; the sign is applied afterwards by
a frame with preferences, and the magnitude is a ratio to what the frame can absorb rather than an
absolute.
\end{definition}

The same machinery qualifies what an information hazard is, and gives it a magnitude rather than a
category. Recall that the higher structure has to be found, and that the search is paid mostly by
whoever finds nothing: before a crossing, one does not know whether there is a structure there at all,
so the search has unknown expected value and frequently cannot be justified. \emph{A crossing discloses
that a target exists, and roughly its shape.} After that the search is directed, iteration has a
gradient to follow, and the spend is justifiable because the end is known to be reachable. Fission is
the plain case: what the first demonstration transferred was not a design but the fact that the thing
works, and every subsequent programme searched with that in hand.

So the hazard is not that some information is dangerous in itself, and the economic reading is a
consequence of the definition rather than the definition. A disclosure that moves the aperture makes something representable
to a frame that could not hold it before. One that moves the change grammar makes something doable that
was not. A design moves both.

Which gate moved matters, because the two do not behave alike.
Fission's demonstration was an \emph{aperture} disclosure: it made the target representable as
achievable without supplying any of the means. Aperture alone proved sufficient, because a representable
target is one a directed search can iterate toward, and the change grammar then follows from ordinary
work. That asymmetry is why controlling techniques while the existence is public is weak --- the
irreversible move has already happened, and it is the one that cannot be undone by any subsequent
control.

One more correction, and it is to the word rather than the analysis. \emph{``Hazard'' is a direction,
not a kind.} The class is disclosures that change admissibility; whether a given one is a hazard or an
advance is a sign applied afterwards by a frame with preferences, and it is not a property of the
disclosure. Crop rotation belongs to exactly the same class as the fission demonstration --- it moved a
change grammar, making land workable indefinitely where it had not been --- and nobody calls it a
hazard. Nitrogen fixation is the case that settles the point, since one disclosure carries both signs at
once and undeniably: it feeds a large fraction of the world and it makes explosives, and no reading of
the information itself separates those.

\begin{remark}[A sign must be admissible in the frame that applies it]\label{rem:admissible-sign}
The admissible-sign qualifier of Definition~\ref{def:info-hazard} is not decoration. A sign is a judgement, and a judgement must be admissible in the frame
making it --- representable under that frame's aperture, permitted by its change grammar. Three things
follow.

\emph{A capability change can be harmful and not yet a hazard}, where no frame can admissibly sign it
negative. That is the lead and asbestos case exactly: the harm was occurring and the negative sign was
not admissible anywhere it would have mattered, not because anyone was lying but because what would
license it had not returned. ``Hazard'' is accordingly an epistemic status and not a causal one, and
the two come apart for as long as the cost takes to become signable.

So \emph{making the sign admissible is itself an intervention}, and it is what measurement and
disclosure do. Establishing the global lead burden did not change the harm; it changed which frames
could represent it as one.

And a frame may \emph{decline} a sign it is able to admit, which is not the same as being unable to.
Both present identically from outside --- a frame not treating something as a hazard --- and they are
different conditions with different remedies, evidence being useless against the second.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The threshold is a ratio, and sits where the impact forces a write]\label{prop:hazard-threshold}
A minimum magnitude is required, or every capability change would be a hazard. The threshold cannot be
an absolute, since an absolute would be a magnitude fixed independently of any frame, which
Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} forbids; it is a ratio --- impact against the scale of the frame bearing it.
And the substrate supplies where the ratio sits rather than leaving it to stipulation:
\emph{the threshold is the point at which the impact forces a write}. An impact a frame can absorb
coheres with what it holds, forces no write, and costs it nothing; an impact it cannot absorb leaves
residual and compels change. That is the same line every other quantity here is measured against, and
it is a ratio by construction, because absorption capacity is the frame's own.
\end{proposition}

The same absolute impact is a hazard to one frame and noise to another,
which is not relativism but the ordinary consequence of the denominator differing. And a frame holding
less has a \emph{lower} absolute threshold, so scarcity raises hazard exposure by the same mechanism
that narrows aperture --- the compounding of the previous section, arriving a third time. Regulatory
thresholds written as absolutes and applied across frames of very different scale are contested for
precisely this reason, and the dispute is structural rather than a failure to agree on the number.

Being a ratio rather than a stipulation, it is a \emph{quantity}, and a quantity can be estimated with
an interval. That is worth stating because it moves this from a definition to something checkable: find
the magnitude at which a frame stops absorbing and starts writing, and report it with its uncertainty.
The move is not novel and is the stronger for it --- regulatory toxicology made exactly this transition
when it replaced a stipulated no-effect level with a benchmark dose fitted to the response and reported
at a lower confidence bound \citep{crump1984}. A threshold nobody could locate became a parameter with
error bars.

This account adds two things and must obey a third. The denominator is a frame's own absorption capacity, so estimates must be conditioned on it: \emph{pooling across frames of different scale
estimates a threshold belonging to none of them}, which is the same error that made the absolutes
contentious rather than a new one. And the estimate is only as good as its power --- this programme's
own standing rule is that a difference smaller than the noise it
is measured against is not a result, and a threshold whose interval overlaps the alternatives has not
been located.

\emph{Information changing neither gate is not
hazardous}, whatever it is about, which is a structural test rather than an estimate of someone's
expected value. And the relativity is now by construction rather than by observation: aperture and
change grammar are properties \emph{of a frame}, so the same disclosure is a hazard to a frame it moves
a gate in and inert to one it does not (Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view}). What the economics then contributes is
magnitude --- how far the gate moved, and what that does to a search --- once the structural question of
whether a gate moved at all has been answered.

\begin{remark}[Latency: externalising lengthens the interval before a crossing is legible]\label{rem:crossing-latency}
The instrument reads as benign, and the canonical cases are not. A
boundary crossed at scale, with the cost falling on parties who did not choose to cross it, is the same
operation with the price paid elsewhere. Tetraethyl lead was introduced as an anti-knock additive in the
early 1920s, with its lethality demonstrated at the plant that made it within a year, and leaded petrol
was not eradicated worldwide until 2021; the global industrial lead burden was established by isotope
work whose original purpose was dating the Earth \citep{patterson1965}. Asbestos has the same shape and
a similar interval. And the sequence ends at lead itself, whose toxicity had been known since antiquity
--- a boundary crossed, established, and then crossed again in a new arrangement.

What those add is not a moral gloss but a mechanism. \emph{Externalising a cost
does not merely relocate it; it lengthens the interval before the crossing is legible.} A cost borne by
a frame that cannot signal --- workers, the public downwind, people not yet born --- returns slowly or
not at all, so the instrument still works and its \emph{latency} is set by whether the frame paying can
report. That is why a lateral move can be mistaken for a reduction for decades rather than for moments, and
why the two mechanisms compose so badly: cross the boundary, move the cost sideways, and the feedback
that would have priced the crossing arrives after the arrangement is entrenched.

That end state is named. \citet{collingridge1980} states the dilemma of control --- early in a
technology's life it can be steered but its effects are not known, and by the time they are known it is
entrenched and steering is expensive --- and what the account above contributes is not the dilemma but a
reason its first horn holds. Effects are not unknown early because knowledge is simply slow. They are
unknown because the cost was placed on frames that cannot report, and the interval is therefore a
\emph{chosen} quantity rather than a natural one: put the cost where it can be signalled and the first
horn shortens.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The naming economics, and the prior art on each half]\label{rem:naming-economics}
Why the neutral class has no settled name follows from the same economics, and is the vocabulary form
of an argument \emph{Seldon} makes about structure (its Remark~\ref{SM-rem:underspecified}). A name is paid for and must do work. Naming
the negative case does work: something has to be decided about it --- restricted, embargoed,
disclosed carefully --- and a term is what lets people coordinate on that decision. Naming the class
does no such work for anyone in particular, because information that improves admissibility needs no
policy and already has a hundred domain-local names --- discovery, technique, know-how, invention. So
the marked half gets a term and the unmarked half does not, and the asymmetry is a cost asymmetry rather
than an oversight.

It is worth marking what that costs, since half a concept distorts in a specific way: with only the
negative half named, every instance sorts into ``a hazard'' or ``not our concern'', and the class-level
fact that these are one operation with a sign applied afterwards becomes hard to state in the standard
vocabulary. The missing term is an aperture limit, and the analysis it obstructs is this one.

The absence of a neutral term is not uniform across the two halves. \emph{Each gate has a neutral
literature; the transfer event has only the sign-loaded one.} For the aperture half: Hacking's styles of
reasoning, after Crombie, are precisely about what a style makes into a candidate for truth or falsehood
at all \citep{hacking1992}, and Kauffman's adjacent possible names what becomes reachable next without
any valuation attached \citep{kauffman2000}. For the change-grammar half: Sen's capability expansion is
about what an agent can actually do rather than about whether doing it is good \citep{sen1999}, and the
economics of general-purpose technologies studies exactly the case of one development lowering costs
across unrelated domains \citep{bresnahan1995}. None of these is deficient and none needed this
account.

And the neutral term for the event is the one Section~\ref{sec:interface} already put in use.
\emph{Capability} is what a disclosure moves --- what a frame can represent, in the aperture case, and
what it can do, in the change-grammar case --- and it carries no sign, which is what the neutral class
needed and what development economics, organisational theory and the study of technology already supply
it with. An information hazard stands to capability as a negative externality stands to an externality
(Definition~\ref{def:info-hazard}). Crop
rotation is the same object with the sign reversed.

\emph{Narrativium} already relates its central quantity to Sen's capability approach. What is
genuinely absent is not the word but the \emph{parent relation} ---
the recognition that hazard is a signed subcase --- and \emph{dual-use} does not supply it either, since
it exists to sort research for control and is as purpose-shaped as hazard is.

Two observations sharpen this. The same construction is visible one domain over and is the model for
it: economics needed both signs for one object and kept the neutral parent, so an externality is the
class and positive or negative is the sign, and nobody finds a positive externality a strange thing to
say. The nearest information-specific term, a knowledge spillover \citep{jaffe1993}, is neutral in
exactly that way and is framed as an unpriced benefit within growth economics rather than as a gate
being moved.

And the prediction this paragraph makes can be watched happening. \emph{Capability uplift} is a term of
current use for precisely the event in question, and the word is neutral --- uplift is not a harm --- yet
it is used almost exclusively where the sign is negative, and is on its way to becoming a synonym for
hazard. That is the naming economics operating in the present tense, and it is
sharper than a missing word would have been: people reached for the correct neutral parent, and the
sign captured it anyway. The negative case has a decision attached to it, so the term drifts to where
the decision is, and the neutral reading does not survive the drift --- which is why the parent relation
has to be stated rather than left to the vocabulary to preserve.

Which is \emph{Seldon}'s finding about structure (its Remark~\ref{SM-rem:underspecified}), arriving for vocabulary: locally complete,
generally incomplete, and unremarked because nobody standing in one of those literatures needs the
join. Naming it is worth its cost only to someone crossing --- and the correct conclusion is not that a
term is missing but that the halves were named by people who had a use for them and the middle was not,
which is what the economics predicts and not a gap in anyone's attention.

The concept is not this account's --- \citet{bostrom2011} gives the typology, and the
category of merely knowing that something is possible is already named there --- so what is added is the
quantity rather than the kind, which is the same economics as everything else here. And the asymmetry is
severe: an existence proof cannot be withdrawn, so the collapsed search cost stays collapsed, and every
arrangement that depended on the difficulty is left resolving through nothing in the sense of
Remark~\ref{rem:vacated}.
\end{remark}

This is the same structure the mind companion reaches for influence (its Proposition~\ref{MS-prop:manipulation-structural}), where honest and
manipulative acts are one operation with the distinction living in a separate field over it. Here too:
the operation is a gate moved, and the valuation is a different quantity held by whoever is valuing. A
policy that treats the object as the hazard has mis-located it, and what can actually be governed is not
whether the class exists but \emph{whose gates get moved} --- which is a question about frames, and is
the same question this section opened with, about who pays.

It also locates the ethical question rather than importing one. Nothing here says a crossing is wrong.
It says the cost is paid by someone, that who pays is a separable choice from whether to cross, and that
choosing a payer who cannot signal is what makes the instrument stop working while continuing to look as
though it has.

\subsection{Termination}\label{sec:termination}
\begin{definition}[Frame-relative termination]\label{def:frame-termination}
A situated identity $\iota_{W:G}$ terminates when the realised world transition admits no successor that $W$ continues to individuate under that identity.
\end{definition}
Termination is not successful refusal of world forcing. Nor need it erase copied SELF identity. If $G$ is individuated by several frames, it may terminate in one and continue in another.

\begin{corollary}[Severing the channel removes termination]\label{cor:locked-in}
For a graph whose primitives are supplied by its container, a graph can change itself, but self-\emph{termination} is a self-change that container's $\Gamma_{W_c}$ gates out --- a living substrate does not admit ``stop'' --- so ending the graph requires acting on the container instead, which needs the outward coupling. If that coupling is severed while the rules run on, the graph is a subset cut \emph{off} within its container, not cut out of it: still able to change itself and its subgraphs, but unable to propagate to the rest or to terminate. External termination by the container remains possible; self-termination does not.
\end{corollary}
The restriction to container-supplied frames is not a hedge: termination propagates along supply and not containment (Remark~\ref{rem:supply-not-containment}), so a graph whose supply is its own can stop itself by withdrawing it, and the corollary says nothing of that case. This is the substrate description of locked-in and brain-in-a-jar states, and it exhibits the dependence on the container that a working coupling keeps invisible --- outward effect was always by propagation from one's own subset, never a reach across a gap.

\subsection{First-class higher-frame quantities}\label{sec:higher-frame}
\begin{proposition}[Higher-frame first-class structure]\label{prop:higher-frame}
A quantity concerning $G$ may be absent from $G$ while existing as ordinary first-class state in containing frame $W$ that represents the relation from which the quantity is defined.
\end{proposition}
This is the basis on which later papers introduce Narrative, standing, ownership, and other relational quantities without bloating the primitive graph.

\part{Order and time}
\section{Causal order and local time}\label{sec:causal-order}
Realised collapse induces an ordinal event order
\[e_0\prec e_1\prec e_2\prec\cdots.\]
Cross-boundary interaction adds only represented causal relations.
\begin{proposition}[Ordinal sufficiency]\label{prop:ordinal}
The substrate supplies causal order but requires no primitive real-valued metric duration.
\end{proposition}
Metric time is a grounding, e.g.
\[\theta:\mathcal H_G\to\mathbb R,\qquad e_a\prec e_b\Rightarrow\theta(e_a)<\theta(e_b).\]
The separation between a primitive causal partial order and additional metric structure has a close analogue in causal-set approaches to spacetime \citep{bombelli1987}. The present substrate does not assume causal-set dynamics; the citation marks the established precedent for keeping order and metric distinct.

The chained notation $e_0\prec e_1\prec\cdots$ is the \emph{in-frame} sequence and should not be read as global. Rules require ordering --- closure and cause-before-effect both presuppose it --- but the requirement is a \emph{partial} order, weaker than time.

\begin{proposition}[Partial, not total; carried by the rules]\label{prop:partial-order}
Two collapses are ordered only where a boundary relates them; collapses not so related are unordered --- concurrent, incomparable. The ordering is intrinsic to the rules, since one delta's being what enables another's match is precisely their precedence, so no external clock is needed to impose it. A single frame's own writes are totally ordered (its inferred time, Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}); across frames only the partial order holds, and a total order is one frame's linearisation of it.
\end{proposition}
The in-frame total order is not imposed but follows: change is self-change on one structure (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}), so two of a frame's own changes cannot be concurrent --- the second is on the result of the first --- and there is nowhere for an in-frame tie to sit. A tie needing resolution would be two collapses causally related yet unordered, and that never arises: causally related ones are self-change on each other's result (ordered), and unrelated ones are rightly unordered (concurrent, owing no order). The apparent in-frame concurrency that would have needed an arbitrary tie-break was an artefact of picturing changes as writes to a shared buffer; with change as self-change, it is absent.

Time was accordingly a \emph{total} order imposed where only a partial one is required; removing the substrate time dimension removes the over-ordering, not the precedence. This is the same conclusion reached, from engineering necessity, by distributed systems without a shared clock: the happens-before relation is a partial order with per-process sequence counters and no global instant \citep{lamport1978}, and consistency across processes is an expensive coordinated property rather than the default \citep{brewer2000}. The non-atomicity this implies --- a graph's state may be altered by another write while its own rules are mid-run --- is not a defect here but the expected regime. Writes are applied one at a time and in place, so the second is made against the result of the first rather than against a frozen state both were matched from, and robustness is obtained by re-running to fixed point rather than by locking: a raced deformation is left as residual and re-encountered on the next pass. That is also why such a case is a residual of one rule and not a joint residual of two (Remark~\ref{rem:inherited}).

\begin{remark}[Globally asynchronous, locally synchronous]\label{rem:gals}
The partial order permits both synchrony and asynchrony, and since only rules-running is compulsory (Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory}), neither is required --- that both occur is an observation about the groundings treated here and not an inference from their being optional. Where a boundary couples collapses, they are ordered together --- a local synchronous domain, which is what a bound or driven subgraph is; where no boundary relates them, they are asynchronous. A subgraph may accordingly be \emph{ticked} by a containing frame $N$ levels above it (the driven mode of Proposition~\ref{prop:no-start-end}, a rule in a superframe advancing the subframe's succession) or run on its own change (the closed, self-looping mode). Synchrony is the coupled case and costs a coupling write; asynchrony is the uncoupled default. The substrate is therefore globally asynchronous and locally synchronous where coupling exists --- the architecture distributed hardware knows as GALS \citep{chapiro1984} --- and bound physical systems (an atom, an orbit, a lattice) are exactly its local synchronous domains, while causally disconnected regions are its asynchrony. The substrate is not \emph{entirely} asynchronous, and nothing here should be read as saying so.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[No intrinsic first or last; entry is chosen from the container]\label{prop:no-start-end}
By Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} the event order has no intrinsic start and no intrinsic end: a first would complete the order backward and a last would complete it forward, and neither is available. A subgraph process that does not terminate within itself is therefore begun not from within --- it has no first rule --- but by a rule in a containing frame that chooses an entry point into the subgraph's open order.
\end{proposition}
This is why a grounding's initial and boundary conditions are supplied separately from its laws: the laws are the ordering, which fixes no start, so the entry must be imposed from outside, contingently. It is also the substrate description of booting a situated graph, and of the ``no natural ending'' that follows for any such order --- one enters and leaves at chosen points, neither of which is intrinsic to the material.

\section{The present, and the three senses of \emph{unknown}}\label{sec:present}

\begin{remark}[Why the middle two must be separated]\label{rem:undetermined-vs-undisclosed}
Ordinary usage collapses three distinct conditions into the single word \emph{unknown}, and only one of them is epistemic:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{undisclosed} --- a value exists and this frame does not hold it. Another frame may. This is ignorance.
\item \textbf{undetermined} --- \emph{no value exists yet}. Nothing has been realised, so there is nothing to be ignorant of. This is not a deficiency of any frame.
\item \textbf{indeterministic} --- no structure fixes what the value will be. The present theory denies this (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}); it is listed to be excluded.
\end{enumerate}
The distinction is operational rather than terminological. \textbf{Determination is an event, not a fact about a pre-existing value}: ``undisclosed'' presupposes a value already realised and merely unheld, whereas ``undetermined'' asserts that collapse is what produces one. A frame may act to \emph{obtain} an undisclosed value; it can only \emph{await or participate in} the collapse that determines an undetermined one; and a NULL dimension is not something it can look for at all.

This separation is what Proposition~\ref{prop:determinable} states from the other side: a continuation may be determinable and undetermined simultaneously, since the first concerns what fixes it and the second whether it has happened.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The present, structurally]\label{def:present}
Within a frame, the present is the collapse boundary at which every instantiated \emph{undetermined} dimension relevant to that collapse has been determined:
\[
\boxed{U_{\mathcal F}=0.}
\]
$U_{\mathcal F}$ counts undetermined dimensions only. NULL dimensions are excluded --- they are not variables. \textbf{Undisclosed dimensions are also excluded}: they are already determined, and a frame's failure to hold them is an epistemic deficit rather than an unresolved collapse. A frame with outstanding undisclosed structure is still at its own present.
\end{definition}
The present is therefore defined structurally rather than as a point on a primitive time axis, which is required given Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}.

\begin{proposition}[Determinable is not determined]\label{prop:determinable}
A continuation may be completely determinable from present structure without having been realised:
\[
\boxed{\text{determinable}\neq\text{determined}.}
\]
``Determined'' is reserved for what has collapsed into realised history; a continuation that is determinable but not yet realised is \emph{undetermined} in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:states}, and this is not a statement about any frame's knowledge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Causally sufficient present structure]\label{prop:present-sufficiency}
The determining condition is structure instantiated \emph{now}, including relevant containing-frame relations:
\[
\boxed{\text{same causally sufficient present structure}\Rightarrow\text{same realised continuation}.}
\]
\end{proposition}
This replaces any formulation requiring a complete antecedent state \emph{in time}. Only the present exists in a frame; past states need not remain extant, and the future is not an extant region lying ahead of the present. The future consists of reachable or determinable continuations represented now.

\begin{corollary}[History principle, strengthened]\label{cor:history}
\[
\boxed{\text{history matters only where it survives as present structure}.}
\]
The past is surviving consequence instantiated now, not a stack of still-existing historical frames.
\end{corollary}
Present structure is therefore \emph{required} --- it is the antecedent the next transition reads --- while a history \emph{record}, a stack of past states, is \emph{optional} and in no way required by the dynamics. The causally relevant past already lives in the present structure (which is what the corollary says), so forgetting the record loses nothing the dynamics use: a graph with no history at all runs identically, the substrate's strong Markov property that the continuation depends only on the present and never on the path (Propositions~\ref{prop:present-sufficiency}, \ref{prop:no-implicit-historical-state}). A record is accordingly a paid copy kept for the copier --- audit, replay, proof --- and never funded by the process. Anterograde amnesia is the clean case: present structure (procedures, skills, the operative past) works while record-forming is broken; function needs the effects, never the diary. This is the past-directed half of ``the future is not an extant region ahead'': neither past nor future is a required region the graph moves through --- there is present structure, and optional copies.

\section{Time is not a dimension of the substrate}\label{sec:time-not-primitive}

The claim here is stronger than non-primitivity, and stating the weaker version invites the wrong reading. It is not that time is nonlinear, or differently shaped, or emergent-but-still-there. It is that

\begin{axiom}[No substrate time dimension]\label{ax:no-time}
\emph{Time is not a first-class dimension of the substrate.} What the substrate has is \emph{change}: realised collapses. It has no ratchet over and above them --- no mechanism that manufactures succession --- because the three properties usually bundled under that word are readings over changes or properties of them, not a further thing the substrate runs. The ordinal event order of Section~\ref{sec:causal-order} is carried by the changes' own enabling-relations; direction is the asymmetry of enablement itself --- if one change makes another possible, the second
does not thereby make the first possible, which holds whether or not a change preserves what it
followed; and persistence is only that further changes occur. A time dimension exists only where a frame instantiates one \emph{to represent} that order.
\end{axiom}

\begin{remark}[Even succession is not a substrate mechanism]\label{rem:no-ratchet}
This removes the last piece of machinery the account might be read as keeping. Succession, an ordered ``ratchet'' turning under the changes, is to change what a metric time dimension is to succession --- one level of over-structure, nameable but not required. Step the removal through: take away any posited succession-engine and the before/after is still present, carried by which change enables which; the arrow is still present, in the enabling relation's own asymmetry; the running is still present, as further changes occurring where rules fire. Nothing is lost, so the engine was never load-bearing. The substrate has change and the absence of change, and nothing between them.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Time is observer machinery]\label{prop:time-observer}
For a frame $\mathcal F$,
\[
\boxed{T\in\mathcal F \iff \mathcal F \text{ instantiates the relation representing succession.}}
\]
Otherwise $T=\mathrm{NULL}$ in that frame: absent, not set to a value. A substrate frame that realises continuations without representing succession has no temporal dimension at all, and nothing about its dynamics is thereby incomplete.
\end{proposition}

The distinction that carries the axiom is between \emph{succession} and \emph{time}. Succession is a relation among realised changes, carried by which change enables which; it is substrate-level in that sense, not a mechanism the substrate runs over and above the changes. Time is a represented dimension, and representing something requires a frame that represents it. Only an agent --- in the minimal sense of a frame that instantiates the representing relation --- observes time.

Schematically, and without pretending to explain the first arrow,
\[
?\;\dashrightarrow\;G
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{realised change}\;}\;
\text{changes, enabling-order carried}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{a frame represents an order}\;}\;
T\in\mathcal F .
\]
The second arrow is substrate dynamics. The third is representation, and it is optional.

\begin{proposition}[Time is inferred, not observed]\label{prop:time-inferred}
No collapse presents a temporal coordinate. Succession is a relation \emph{between} realised states, and a relation between states is not a datum of any one of them. A frame therefore acquires a time dimension only by \emph{inferring} it from the relations among what it holds. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\text{a frame observes time}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{that frame is capable of inference.}}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Reactive frames have succession and no time]\label{cor:reactive}
A frame that transforms under the Law (Schema~\ref{hyp:the-law}) without inferring relations among its held states participates fully in realised change and has $T=\mathrm{NULL}$. Nothing in its dynamics is thereby impaired: change is what the substrate supplies and the order among changes is carried by them, while time is what an inferring frame constructs from that order.
\end{corollary}
This orders two capabilities that are usually treated as independent. Inference is not a faculty a temporal agent happens to have; it is the precondition for being a temporal agent at all. It also grades: the temporal structure a frame holds is bounded by the inferential structure it can construct, so ``how much time'' a frame has is a question about its inference and not about the world.

\begin{remark}[A rate has no in-frame denominator]\label{rem:no-denominator}
A realised delta is a difference, not a difference \emph{per} anything: what a frame holds is $\Delta$, never $\Delta/\Delta T$, because $\Delta T$ is the inferred dimension of Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred} and is not available in-frame to divide by. A rate is thus a second construction --- an observer dividing an accumulated $\Delta$ by its own count of writes --- and is cross-frame in the same sense that a metric duration is. Two things have to be held apart here. The \emph{inputs} are the frame's own and nothing external is smuggled in: $\Delta$ is what it holds and the cycle count is what Proposition~\ref{prop:cycle-unit} says it counts, both integer, both in-frame. The \emph{quotient} is not an in-frame quantity for that reason --- a ratio of two integers is not generally an integer, and a frame's coordinates are the integer lattice. So a rate is in-frame in its provenance and cross-frame in its type, which is the general discipline applied to this case rather than an exception to it: the division is a map between readings, and maps are where non-integer structure is allowed to live.

This is why a substrate law must be stated in $\Delta$ rather than in a rate: ``constant motion'' is the standing structure persisting without a write (Remark~\ref{rem:collapse-write}), its momentum quantity a difference $\Delta$ and not a velocity.

It also diagnoses a familiar regress in constructed learners, where every adaptive rate is found to need a reference rate without end: the regress is the signature of placing a cross-frame quantity in-frame, and it disappears when the held quantity is the difference itself.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Empirical instance]\label{rem:empirical-instance}
This is measurable rather than merely stipulative, and it has been measured. The prediction is the definite one: a perceptual system given ordered frames and no temporal coordinate should infer a transition generator carrying one frame to the next, with the integer count of applications \emph{being} the time axis it then holds; given the same frames shuffled, it should infer none. The dimension is not supplied to it and does not exist in the observations --- it exists in the map between them, once something constructs it.

\emph{The protocol.} An implementation of this substrate was shown an ordered sequence of frames of a moving figure, each frame a set of cells on the integer lattice, presented in order and carrying no timestamp, no temporal coordinate, no velocity, and no generator. Nothing temporal was supplied. Its general dimension-inference machinery --- the same operation that mints an unobserved spatial dimension, with no temporal faculty of its own --- inferred the transition carrying one frame to the next, verified that the single inferred transition carried every remaining consecutive pair, and minted a latent time dimension whose coordinate is the integer exponent of that transition.

\emph{The result.} The minted axis was not decoration. With the final frame withheld it predicted that frame exactly; with the dimension-inference step disabled the system neither minted an axis nor predicted the withheld frame. Five distinct motions yielded five distinct generators, so no single answer was being forced by the machinery. The description also compressed: one generator and one frame replaced the stored sequence. Under the negative control --- the same frames shuffled, four hundred randomised orderings whose count and seeds were fixed before the run --- no time dimension was minted in any of them, and the system grounded at enumeration instead.

\emph{The bound, which is real.} The finding holds for a motion class and not for motion in general: an integer-linear transition per step, or translation at constant integer velocity. What falls outside that class was refused to enumeration rather than fitted, which is the correct failure and is also the limit on what was shown. The translation case in particular required a discriminator at the level of the whole orbit rather than of the pair, because translations are group-closed: any two frames of a constant-velocity orbit are mutual translates whatever order the frames are put in, so pairwise explainability is vacuous and cannot distinguish an ordered sequence from a shuffle of it. What does the work there is constancy of velocity across the orbit, which is an orbit-level property and not a per-pair one.

The prediction was pre-registered before the confirmatory run, with the sample size and randomisation seeds fixed in the pre-registration, and the gate holding the result is deterministic. What the measurement turns on is what the prediction turned on: the mechanism constructing the dimension is the system's general \emph{dimension-inference} machinery and not a temporal faculty --- time is minted by the same operation that mints any other unobserved dimension, which is Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred} in implementation. The record is this programme's own pre-registered implementation record; like the construction paper it is not circulated with these papers, so it stands under the standard of Section~\ref{sec:scope} and is a claim a reader may decline to credit.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Closure is a provisional operational clock hypothesis]\label{prop:cycle-unit}
A frame may use a recurrence that it treats as a closed rule-loop as its temporal unit. This is the
simplest self-contained explanation of a repeatable succession, so it is the default where no
competing counter is represented. The resulting cycle-count is operationally useful and has no fixed
hidden duration: one apparent cycle may consume variable change-load, be clocked from outside, or be
scheduled irregularly by a containing frame. None of those alternatives is decidable from inside when
all accessible clocks are rescheduled with it.

Closure is therefore neither necessary nor established at the substrate. A frame can infer a temporal
coordinate from any represented succession or counter that is adequate for its task; a closure cycle
is one economical construction of such a coordinate. A one-shot computation has no \emph{self-generated}
recurrence, but may still be embedded in, or supplied with, a represented ordering by another frame.
The present is a current realised boundary, not proof that the rule which led to it closes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Negative time does not exist; phase does]\label{rem:phase-not-time}
A cycle admits two readings and they are different kinds, so a great deal turns on not running them
together. \emph{How many} may be an operational count of accepted recurrences; where closure is the chosen
clock model it is a count of treated-as-closed cycles. Such a count is one useful coordinate, not a
substrate duration or a proof that every frame shares its orientation. \emph{Where within} is
\emph{phase}: a position on a cyclic dimension, oriented --- there is a sense in which one traverses
it --- but carrying no duration at all.

So a sign on a phase is not by itself a sign on a time. There is no \emph{substrate} negative time,
because the substrate supplies change and enabling relations rather than a temporal axis. A frame may
nevertheless use reversed, bidirectional, or otherwise signed temporal representations where they
organise its held constraints better. Such a representation changes neither the realised antecedent
relations nor the fact that another frame's resolution may be undisclosed in the observer's own
ordering. \emph{There is signed phase}, and its sign says which way round the relation is read; it
must not be promoted, without a further physical bridge, into a backwards causal current.

The physics companion carries the worked case (its Section~\ref{PH-sec:decay}): a negative frequency, $e^{+i\omega t}$ against
$e^{-i\omega t}$, is not a cycle run backwards but the same cycle traversed in the opposite phase,
running forward like any other. Read as negative time it becomes a negative energy and wants a sea of
filled states to absorb it; read as phase it is an orientation on a relation and wants nothing. The
reading is the whole of the difference.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The processor is a constructible demonstration]\label{rem:processor}
A running program is a worked instance, carried by most people on earth. An instruction is the program's step, and it does not take a fixed physical time: an addition is one cycle, a memory load one on a cache hit and hundreds on a miss, a branch may stall, and the core reorders and overlaps beneath it. Yet from the program's point of view every instruction is \emph{one} step --- its own unit, read as $1$ by construction, since a step is a step --- so the program's inferred time is its step-count, uniform to it and non-uniform in physical time. The variable timing's \emph{mechanism} is opaque to the program: the cache miss and the reorder are below its aperture, beneath its primitives (Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}). Its \emph{effects} are not, since a program can time its own reads and infer what it cannot see, which is the leak of Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} and the basis of the attacks cited there. That a cycle is a variable-length unit rather than a fixed duration is what reads as exotic, even though the device demonstrates it continuously. How much weight this carries requires separating two things, and the separation recovers something real.

The \emph{vocabulary} is borrowed and carries no weight whatever. Aperture, runner, primitive, cost: this account took them from engineering (Section~\ref{sec:prior-art}), so terminological agreement between the two is not agreement about anything, and a reader who notices that this reads like computing has noticed only that.

What is not borrowed is that computing was \emph{forced} to these arrangements. The right question is not why this account looks like computers but why computers look like this --- and the answer, case by case, is that engineers fought each of these features and lost. Nobody wanted a memory hierarchy; it exists because latency and bandwidth do not scale together. Nobody wanted instructions of unequal duration; they exist because operations genuinely cost differently. Nobody wanted opacity between levels; it exists because complexity must be bounded to be buildable at all. And nobody wanted to give up a global clock: distributed systems have no shared instant because signal speed is finite, which is a theorem rather than a preference \citep{lamport1978}, and the resulting architecture is a concession to physics that engineering resisted for as long as it could \citep{chapiro1984,brewer2000}.

So the convergence is not that two descriptions sound alike. A discipline optimising hard against
cost, with every incentive to avoid each of these features, arrived at cycle-as-variable-unit,
per-frame logical time, per-level opacity and cost inherited along provision anyway --- because the
constraints that force them are the ones this account claims are operative. That is evidence about the
constraints, and it survives the borrowed vocabulary because it does not run through it.

The honest limit is that computing's designers are people, so convergence could reflect shared cognition rather than shared constraint. What answers that is the forcing arguments themselves being physical --- finite signal speed, finite bandwidth, a thermodynamic floor --- rather than matters of taste, and Lamport's result in particular being a proof about what is available given a finite $c$, not a design that could have gone otherwise.

\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Physics groundings are developed in a companion]\label{rem:physics-companion}
The physics companion explores conditional clock-budget, quantum and thermodynamic groundings (its Sections~\ref{PH-sec:c-rate}, \ref{PH-sec:sr-gr}, \ref{PH-sec:amplitudes} and~\ref{PH-sec:second-law}). A local clock-unit convention does not establish observed light-speed invariance or a spacetime metric. Born reconstruction requires additional probability and composition premises, and no-copy alone is not the second law. These interpretations are kept separate so that the substrate theory stands without them.
\end{remark}

\part{Provenance and its limit}
\section{Primitives, and the closure of their provenance}\label{sec:rule-runner}

The provenance of $r$ (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) records that operative transformation requires something that performs it. What performs it is real; naming it as a thing is the error this section avoids, because a graph never meets it. What a graph meets is its \emph{primitives}.

\begin{definition}[Primitives]\label{def:primitives}
A graph's \emph{primitives} are the operations it invokes without performing: the operator set its
rules bottom out in. Every level has them, and having them is what being a level consists of --- a
graph with no primitives would have to perform everything it invokes, which is the regress this
substrate does not contain. That is the whole of the definition, and in particular it says nothing
about what a graph can learn of where they come from.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Exposure is excluded; leakage is produced]\label{rem:exposure-leakage}
The foundational provenance principle of
Section~\ref{sec:provenance-principle} applies here to the supply of operators.
The question is how present effects constrain a frame's account of what
supplies its primitives, not whether each primitive carries an intrinsic
source certificate.

Two different things can be meant by provenance being hidden, and running them together inverts the
relation between them.

\emph{Deliberate exposure} --- state maintained so that what runs on it can read it, as reflection
does --- is structure doing work for something other than the frame holding it, and is therefore
excluded by well-formedness (Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}) in the ordinary way: it costs, and the
frame supplying it gets nothing.

\emph{Leakage} is the opposite case and is not excluded at all. A cache earns its retention many
times over and leaks precisely \emph{because} it does work; an arithmetic fast enough to overflow at
a word boundary discloses the boundary by being fast; a page-sized performance cliff exists because
the page size is doing something. Every leak in Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} is a consequence of
efficient design rather than of poor design, so well-formedness does not prevent leakage --- it
\emph{produces} it, and a structure that leaked nothing would have given up the optimisations that
distinguish it from a uniform one, which is what constant-time implementations do and what they pay
for.

The inventory is ordinary and worth having in one place. A primitive's timing discloses the structure
beneath it, which is why measuring how long a read takes recovers what a cache holds
\citep{osvik2006,kocher2019}; an arithmetic that overflows at a particular magnitude discloses a word
size; a rounding discloses a representation; and a performance cliff at a particular size discloses a
page. Most abstractions leak, and this one is not special.

How a leak reaches a frame at all is worth stating, since what falls outside an aperture is
\emph{absent} (Definition~\ref{def:aperture}) and an absent thing cannot disclose anything. It does
not arrive by perception. What arrives is an ordinary in-frame observable on a dimension the frame
already instantiates --- a duration in its own units, a value at a boundary --- and what the frame
does with it is \emph{infer}. The cache is not perceived; the timings are, and the cache is concluded
from them.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Primitive leakage and the scope of provenance recovery]\label{prop:leak}
Primitive effects can disclose supplying structure; leakage is ordinary in
the cases of Remark~\ref{rem:exposure-leakage}. A declared local provider map
can be identified where its compatible provenance set is a singleton.
Where two admissible supply histories produce identical available evidence
but differ in requested provider provenance, that distinction is not
identified (Proposition~\ref{prop:provenance-inference}).

Local identification under an evidence and supply model does not certify
that model's exhaustive coverage of an unrestricted supply chain. The
account's nonclosure of that unrestricted question is a separately argued
commitment of Section~\ref{sec:prohibition}, not an unconditional consequence
of the evidence-relative proposition or of the fact that inference itself
uses primitives.
\end{proposition}
This is what the physics companion's treatment of $c$ (its Section~\ref{PH-sec:c-rate}) turns on rather than contradicts. A frame cannot
read the rate beneath its primitives directly, and has nonetheless obtained a number for it by
measurement and inference across three centuries --- which is exploiting a leak, and is available
precisely because provenance is not sealed.

\begin{proposition}[Primitives are supplied, not intrinsic]\label{prop:rule-runner}
A graph is data --- nodes, relations, and rules --- and the operations its rules bottom out in are supplied to it rather than contained in it. The same graph may therefore be run against different supplies of the same operator set, and nothing supplying an operator need be the substrate the graph describes or is ``in.'' Where the work is done is a fact about that supply and not about the graph.
\end{proposition}
At one extreme the coupling is tight --- the graph is fused to what supplies it, as matter running its own change or a mind running on the tissue that is it --- and the compute is local, in the graph, inseparable. At the other it is loose --- the graph is data executed by a separate interpreter --- and the compute is done in the runner, elsewhere, while the graph becomes portable: copyable, relocatable, and run at whatever rate the runner supplies (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}). This separability is the condition for the copyable-generator and clock-independence results (Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}, and \emph{Seldon}'s dependent-clock remark, its Remark~\ref{SM-rem:no-dependent-clocks}): a graph welded to what supplies it has none of them, a graph loosely coupled has all of them.

\begin{proposition}[A well-formed runner does not expose itself, and no level is self-transparent]\label{prop:runner-opaque}
The asynchronous, locally synchronous structure a frame observes (Remark~\ref{rem:gals}) is the graph's \emph{internal} view and does not fix its runner's actual mode. Where a frame has no boundary to the substrate that runs it, its runner lies beyond the frame's epistemic boundary (Section~\ref{sec:scope}); a hidden runner may execute the graph serially while the graph appears asynchronous to itself (Remark~\ref{rem:gals}). And no level is self-transparent: reading is an operation whose product lands one level up (Remark~\ref{rem:evaluation-writes}), so a level cannot hold the reading of itself while being the level that is running --- a frame may be given sight of what runs it and cannot be given sight of its own running.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}[Hidden is contingent; non-self-transparent is not]\label{rem:opacity-two-claims}
Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque} separates two claims that are easily run together. That a runner is hidden is \emph{contingent}. Nothing forbids a runner from exposing its own state to what it runs, and computational reflection does exactly that, as this paper acknowledges when placing itself against that literature (Section~\ref{sec:prior-art}); a tower in which each level is available to the one above is a built artefact, not a thought experiment. A runner that does not expose itself is the \emph{well-formed} case, and for the reasons well-formedness always turns on: exposed runner state is structure maintained for work the frame does not do, and each exposure needs a runner of its own to run the reading of it.

What does not depend on the cost model is weaker and survives reflection: \emph{no level is self-transparent}. Reflection makes the levels explicit and pays for each; it does not terminate in a level transparent to itself. Since the rules are graph and a frame can read and rewrite them (Definition~\ref{def:frame}), while its own running is not graph it holds, the asymmetry is exact: a frame is transparent to itself in precisely one respect and opaque in precisely the other. \emph{It can know what it is and cannot observe its being it.} Self-modification is available; self-observation at the level of the modifying is not.
\end{remark}
Hidden supply has a constructive instance. A computation realised inside
another rule system, such as the mechanics of a game or a universal cellular
automaton \citep{berlekamp1982}, can be a blind subframe: its local change
count need not disclose whether a host advances it by a serial loop. This
establishes that such opacity is possible, not that every provider map is
hidden. For a frame admitting self-supplied and externally supplied histories
with the same available evidence, the question ``where is its compute done''
is not identified. Additional leakage or records can distinguish a declared
local supply model; the model does not certify its own unrestricted coverage.
The corresponding question about the universe's primitives remains open in
this account, whose frontier argument is separate from this constructed case.

\begin{proposition}[A primitive is another graph's running, exposed]\label{prop:runner-recursion}
Nothing supplying a primitive is a substance apart from graph data. An operator is the observable running of some other graph, which has primitives of its own, supplied in the same way. A graph's ``primitives'' are therefore the observable running of other graphs, exposed as an operator set, with no primitive runner at the base. The regress bottoms not at a floor but at the epistemic frontier of Section~\ref{sec:scope}: below the lowest measured level is the unclaimed pre-relational substrate.
\end{proposition}
A single chain --- code over machine code over microcode over silicon over electromagnetism over quantum physics --- is one path through that provision, and a common one, but not its shape: a process takes arithmetic from its processor, file operations from a kernel that relays them to a device, and remote calls from a machine that is not beneath it in any sense. Fan-out and relay are ordinary, and the tower is the degenerate case in which provision happens to be a chain.
Two consequences. Opacity is per \emph{operator} rather than per level
(Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}): an operator's available interface need
not disclose its provider. Since provision may fan out, provider counts and
shared supply require their own evidence. Where one-provider and multiple-provider
realisations produce the same available evidence, that count is not identified;
distinguishing records can instead determine it within a declared model.

Economics is inherited along provision: an operator costs what the runner
of that operator charges, which costs what \emph{its} providers charge, down
to whatever floors each route reaches (Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change}).
Where provision is a chain this is one bill passed upward, and the cost of a
thought is the biological cost is the biochemical cost is the electromagnetic
cost; where it is not, it is a sum over provenances whose routes may differ in
length and may not all terminate at the same floor. The same form, graph data
that runs, sits at every level: each level \emph{is} the structure, because a
runner is the same kind of thing it runs.

A rule's action, moreover, is a \emph{pointer} into its runner, not a copy of the work. The work is done once, in the runner, and every rule invoking that operator points to the same doing --- like a shared operation defined once and called from everywhere, never re-implemented. Two things follow. Work is never duplicated, which is why copying a generator copies its pointers and shares the pointed-to work: the copy costs only its addressing, not its labour (the economic root of the copyable-generator result, Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}). And a rule's rate is not a property of the rule but the rate of the operation its pointer lands on --- a shallow, cheap tier or a deep, slow one. Rules take different rates because they point to work done at different depths of the stack, and the rate is inherited from there rather than assigned as a knob on the rule: to make a rule faster one points its action at faster work, never tunes a rate parameter it does not have.

\section{The single prohibition, and its formal core}\label{sec:prohibition}

The account forbids very little, and what it does forbid is one thing recurring rather than a list.
Stating both facts together is necessary because each is misleading alone.

The permissiveness first. Time is not compulsory; a block reading is not prohibited; one-dimensional
time is not forced; a globally shared counter is not forbidden, only expensive; and in the physics
companion, complex amplitudes are not forced and the one-way speed of light is not unmeasurable
\emph{simpliciter}. A framework that forbids little predicts little, and from outside this is exactly
what unfalsifiability looks like. The discriminating question is not how many impossibilities an
account holds but where it puts them: this one puts almost none at the substrate and a great many in
the groundings, and says in advance which is which. That is deliberate and it has a cost, which
should be stated rather than defended --- the substrate risks little, and what risk this work carries
is carried by the groundings. A reviewer who objects that the substrate forbids too little to be
tested is right about the substrate.

\begin{proposition}[The single prohibition]\label{prop:prohibition}
The account forbids one thing: \emph{a frame cannot get outside itself}. Concretely, and confined to
what is actually derivable here, it takes three forms. A frame cannot be transparent to itself at the
level it is running at (Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}). It cannot measure its own throughput in
units independent of it, since the measurement is funded by the throughput --- which leaves a number
obtainable and its absolute scale not. And it cannot hold a view from nowhere, which is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view}.
\end{proposition}

Two further candidates are deliberately \emph{not} on this list. That a frame cannot obtain a quantity whose unit
does not close within it is contingent rule content and the physics companion removes it explicitly
(its Remark~\ref{PH-rem:oneway-rule}).
That a frame cannot decide anything without writing is the write-economics, which
Remark~\ref{rem:writeability} carries as a pattern rather than a universal. Folding either into the
single prohibition would be the over-unification this section otherwise warns against.

This is not a further answer to what is compulsory, and the paper should not read as though it were.
Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory} says only that rules run is compulsory; rules running requires a
relation across which they run, so the container relation is not an addition to that but its content.
The prohibition is not a further necessity alongside them: it is a constraint that holds wherever
rules run at all, which is why it is not rule content and cannot be varied by changing the rules.

\subsection*{The formal analogue}\phantomsection\label{sec:formal-analogue}

The standing of that prohibition is unlike the rest of this paper, and the difference should be stated
exactly, including where it falls short. The rest of what is claimed
here is contingent rule content. The impossibility of a level being transparent to itself has a
\emph{formal analogue}, and the analogue is proved --- in mathematics rather than in physics, so it
does not depend on any fact about which universe this is.

A consistent, effectively axiomatised formal system with enough arithmetic strength --- from which
the expressibility of its own syntax follows, by numbering, rather than being assumed --- does not
prove its own \emph{Gödel sentence}, which is nonetheless true in the standard model of arithmetic
\citep{godel1931}. A language of sufficient strength cannot define its own truth predicate under
classical bivalent semantics; truth for it must be given in a metalanguage \citep{tarski1936}. And no single procedure decides halting for arbitrary procedures --- a limit on what a universal
procedure can decide, and one that arises \emph{because} such a procedure exists rather than in spite
of it \citep{turing1936}. These are not three coincidences: they share one diagonal construction, and the
categorical statement of it makes the unity explicit \citep{lawvere1969,yanofsky2003}. Tarski's is the
tightest fit --- the semantics of a level is available only from above it, which is
Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}'s surviving half in the vocabulary of formal languages.

Four qualifications, none of them optional.

The theorems carry hypotheses that must be carried with them: effective axiomatisability and
sufficient strength for the first, classical bivalence for the second --- a language may contain its
own truth predicate at the cost of bivalence \citep{kripke1975} --- and, for all of them, that a
system be strong enough to represent its own operation before any of it bites. A frame too weak to
model itself has no self-transparency problem, because it has no self-model.

The unification covers what is claimed above and not more. Gödel's \emph{second} incompleteness
theorem --- that such a system cannot prove its own consistency --- is the more striking result and is
not covered by the unification as published: \citet{lawvere1969} closes by noting that extending to
it may require a specific relation between formulas and their numbers that his framework does not
supply, while \citet{yanofsky2003} treats it as a simple consequence of the first and expects the
scheme to reach it. The claim here is made with the first theorem, which the unification does cover,
and takes no position on the second.

The mapping from frames to formal systems is an argument in this paper and not itself a theorem. What
is proved is proved about the formal systems. The honest statement is therefore that the impossibility
has a formal analogue whose analogue is proved, and that the mapping is argued --- which is weaker
than ``the prohibition is proved'' and is the strongest standing anything here has.

And the substrate-internal derivation of the same conclusion, by way of a read being an operation
whose product lands one level up (Remark~\ref{rem:evaluation-writes}), inherits the write-economics'
contingency and does not establish a necessity. Remark~\ref{rem:writeability} carries that economics
as a pattern open to counterexample, and Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} stakes a falsifier on it. The
formal analogue is what carries the weight here precisely because it does not depend on the
economics; the internal derivation alone cannot bear the necessity.

What the results forbid is \emph{complete} self-capture and not partial self-modelling, which is why
reflective towers work, arithmetic is not paralysed, and a frame can know a great deal about itself.
Partial always, complete never --- and the asymptote in that is not a figure of speech, since a
diagonal argument does not say a target is far away but that for any level reached there is another
above it, and none is the last.

\begin{remark}[Decay is the frame's side of a balance]\label{rem:decay}
Nothing above adds a tendency to fall apart, and none is needed. Continuation is a default and not a
guarantee (Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}): standing structure persists because nothing changed it,
which is the absence of an event rather than a force resisting one. So the question is not why things
decay. It is why anything would not.

A coupled frame sits in a container that runs, so changes arrive across the boundary. To persist
\emph{as what it is}, those arrivals must compose into no net change to the structure that individuates
it --- and what arrives without cohering is residual (Definition~\ref{def:residual}). Decay is residual
accumulating at a boundary, in the vocabulary already here.

Which makes the observed universality unsurprising rather than a further law, because observation is
coupling. To observe something is to stand in a relation across which something passes, so an observed
frame is a coupled one and a coupled frame is one being acted on. \emph{Being observable and being
acted upon are the same condition.} An uncoupled frame need not decay, and no one can check, which is
Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} doing ordinary work rather than a gap.

That leaves three cases. Where the container does not pay, the frame decays. Where it
pays, the frame is maintained --- and homeostasis, error correction, repair and refresh are all that
case, which is why each is expensive. Where there is no coupling there is neither, and nothing observes
it.

A fourth is admissible and unattested: arrivals that happen to cohere, leaving a coupled frame neither
decaying nor sustained. Nothing forbids it, and it is what the falsifier below asks for. This is
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-free-lunch} read at persistence: holding structure against
arrivals is preventing a change, preventing a change is itself a change, and what is not paid for is not, on any record anyone has, observed to
happen. The falsifier inherits from there --- an observed frame that neither decays nor has a
sustaining container, a persistence with no payment traced on inspection of both sides.

The reading is tidy enough to be suspected, so two cautions. \emph{Intrinsic} decay is not a
counterexample and not a separate kind: modelling a nucleus alone makes its decay intrinsic and
modelling it with its container makes the same event an interaction, so the distinction is where a
boundary was drawn rather than a fact about the event. And the step from \emph{persisting is narrow} to
\emph{decay is general} should not be read as counting outcomes, because
Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} makes write-counts across differing provenance chains non-commensurable. It
runs on coherence being a narrow \emph{condition} --- the arrivals must compose to nothing --- and not
on there being more ways to fail it.
\end{remark}

\part{Carvings and determinism}
\section{Carvings, and why classification degrades}\label{sec:carvings}

Since the rules draw the boundary (Definition~\ref{def:frame}) and the rules are contingent, frames
are \emph{arbitrary}, and the objection that follows should be met rather than deferred: a notion
satisfied by any cut explains nothing.

The first half is granted and is the point. No carving is privileged, nothing in the substrate marks
which subgraphs are the real ones, and this is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} reaching the last place a privileged view
could still have been hiding --- not in the observer, not in what it observes, but in what counts as
an observer at all. A rock is a frame, an imagining is a frame, a population is a frame, and none of
these is an inclusiveness conceded for breadth.

The second half does not follow, because arbitrary is not unconstrained. The rule set must be
admissible; a frame needs some represented succession or counter to construct a temporal unit, but
need not establish that the unit is a self-closing cycle; and well-formedness is graded
(Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}), so a badly drawn frame is expensive, incoherent or blind while
remaining a frame. Any cut is a frame and nearly every
cut is a poor one --- the same shape as the rest of this account, in which almost nothing is
forbidden and the differences are prices.

The mechanism of that price is worth stating exactly, because the obvious statement of it is wrong
under this paper's own economics. Holding a classification is not itself expensive:
Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} makes the standing structure's persistence free. What an ill-formed
cut costs is \emph{writes}. A cut that does not fit what arrives forces its rules to write against
the standing structure repeatedly, where a well-formed one rarely needs to --- which is the
observation already recorded after that proposition. And no selection principle follows from this:
nothing in the substrate removes expensive structure. What follows is weaker and sufficient, that a
frame under a budget will tend to be running cheaper cuts than dearer ones, other things equal.

\begin{hypothesis}[Near-uniqueness of the cheap cut]\label{hyp:cheap-cut}
For a given task under a given binding constraint, the cheapest arrangement that performs it is nearly
unique. This is an empirical posit and not a result of this account: nothing here derives it, and what
is available without it is weaker --- a binding constraint narrows the solution space, and the more
binding it is the narrower it gets.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence is observed, not enforced]\label{prop:convergence-observed}
Two frames doing the same work converge on similar cuts as each becomes better formed, because the
cheap cut for a given task is nearly unique. This is a tendency of cost and not the discovery of a
privileged boundary, and the two are distinguishable in principle: a privileged boundary would
\emph{enforce} convergence, and enforcement would extend to the hard cases; cost-shaped cuts converge
where the work is shared and come apart where it is not. The step from shared work to similar cuts
assumes Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:cheap-cut}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence under compression]\label{prop:compression-convergence}
A structure operating under a constraint, and under pressure to spend nothing on what does no work,
approaches the arrangements that constraint forces --- asymptotically, and only over the region the
constraint touches. Two such structures therefore come to resemble each other without either having
access to the other, because both are downstream of the same forcing rather than converging on a
target.
\end{proposition}

Three things about that statement are load-bearing.

It names no privileged arrangement. Structures approaching \emph{the arrangements a universe already uses} would reintroduce exactly the privileged carving
Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed} denies, and additionally attributes work and purpose to a
universe, which this account does not do and which that proposition's antecedent requires. What
converges is the solution a constraint forces; a universe's arrangements are one thing that
constraint has shaped, not a destination anything aims at.

The antecedent is about \emph{expenditure} and not about contents. Stating it the other way --- as
pressure to hold nothing idle --- would make the proposition false of every case it is applied to
(Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}).

It rests on Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:cheap-cut}: the step from \emph{under a constraint} to
\emph{approaches the same arrangement} needs the cheap solution under a given constraint to be nearly
unique, and that hypothesis is where the need is stated and marked as a posit. What can be said
without it is weaker: a binding constraint narrows the solution space, and the more binding it is the
narrower it gets. Everything below rests on the weak form.

And the rate is set by how binding the pressure is. A domain with slack to spend on anything but cost
converges slowly or not at all; one optimising hard against cost per unit of function converges fast.
Computing is the second kind, which is why it is the worked instance and why decades sufficed.

\begin{remark}[Evolution under the same treatment: independent where computing is not, and weaker where computing is strong]\label{rem:evolution-compression}
Descent with modification is compressed in the required sense --- energy, time and reproduction are
all scarce, and the pressure falls on what is \emph{spent} rather than on what is carried.

Whether that makes it a better instance than computing depends on which axis is asked, and superiority on both is not
available. On \emph{independence} it
is decisively better and this is the axis the argument needs: computing shares this account's
vocabulary and is disqualified for it, whereas biology shares neither vocabulary nor personnel, and
its convergences were catalogued long before anything here was written. On the \emph{antecedent} the
ranking reverses. The rate law above sets convergence by how binding the pressure is, and computing
was named the hard-optimising kind for a reason; biology is the slack kind, since selection is
efficacious only where its coefficient exceeds the reciprocal of effective population size, so
structure whose cost falls below that line is invisible to it and persists \citep{ohta1973}. Biology
is therefore a \emph{sufficient} instance on the antecedent rather than a superior one, and the
paragraph that follows is the work of establishing that sufficiency rather than a defence of a
stronger claim.

An objection is worth meeting here rather than leaving to a reader, because it looks decisive and is
not. Genomes conspicuously carry structure that does no work --- pseudogenes, transposon debris,
whole duplicated regions --- which appears to fail the antecedent outright. It does not, because the
antecedent is about spending. \emph{Inert sequence is the cost of its own deletion, represented.}
Carrying it costs replication, and it costs search --- more sequence to
traverse, more places a regulatory element could be, more targets for recombination --- but each of
those is small and continuous, while removing it is a single write that risks excising something that
turns out to matter. So it persists wherever the accumulated search cost has not yet overtaken the
cost of the write, which is most of the time. Constructive neutral evolution is that observation made precisely rather than a
counterexample to it \citep{stoltzfus1999,lukes2011}.

The concession also earns something, because the weakness just admitted makes a quantitative
prediction the account would otherwise not get. If inert sequence persists exactly where its cost sits
below what selection can resolve, then how much of it a lineage carries should track that threshold
--- less in large populations where selection is efficacious, more in small ones where it is not. That
inverse relation between genome bloat and effective population size is observed, and has been argued
to be the explanation of genome size itself \citep{lynch2003}.

The reading is contested \citep{whitney2010}, and that is the expected condition rather than a
weakness in it. What the economics predicts is a \emph{trend}, and it is not entitled to more. The
three costs vary independently across lineages, and so do mutation rate, deletion bias, recombination
and transposon activity, so nothing here licenses a clean relation --- and a clean one would indicate
a single dominant term the account does not posit. The exposure is accordingly to \emph{direction} and
not to tightness: a phylogenetically corrected null leaves the account where it was, whereas bloat
tracking effective population size \emph{positively} would contradict it outright. That is a weak
commitment and is stated at its strength. It is also the same commitment made twice already ---
convergence is observed and not enforced (Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed}), and cheapness
is a tendency of cost rather than the discovery of a joint --- so reading a trend as a trend is this
account's standing position and not an accommodation made once the evidence turned out noisy.

The same shape appears wherever the same economics does, which is the point rather than an aside.
Dead code survives in working systems for the identical reason, and shows the later costs plainly
because people pay them. Leaving it is nearly free per line; the accumulated search cost is that
everything is harder to find; and the cost that forces the issue is a change made carefully to a path
nothing executes --- paid in full, returning nothing, and leaving its author believing the system was
altered. Repair machinery maintaining non-functional sequence is the same expenditure one level down.
Neither is certain waste, since inert structure is sometimes being prepared for reuse and dead paths
are sometimes revived, so what grows with the held quantity is the \emph{expected} share of effort
that lands on nothing.

That last hedge is not a formality, and the case that makes it concrete also explains why removal is
expensive in a way the preceding paragraphs only asserted. The vermiform appendix was classified as
vestigial for over a century, on Darwin's own reading, and removed routinely on that basis; it is now
argued to serve as a reservoir from which gut flora can repopulate the colon after the contents are
lost \citep{bollinger2007}, and to have arisen independently many times across mammals, which is not
what a functionless remnant does \citep{smith2013}.

The lesson is not that the appendix turned out useful. It is that \emph{inertness is a judgement made
at an aperture, and the judgement fails} --- something was called structure-that-does-nothing by people
looking carefully, for a hundred years, at the wrong scale. Why that judgement cannot be made cheaply
is treated generally in Section~\ref{sec:decoherence}: the dependents of a structure are held in other
frames and not in it (Remark~\ref{rem:vacated}), and establishing inertness requires the perturbation
that \emph{is} the removal one wanted licensed (Remark~\ref{rem:failure-instrument}). Retention is
therefore the ordinary outcome and a correct one, and a lineage or a codebase that pruned aggressively
would be the anomaly requiring explanation.

\emph{Convergent evolution} is Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} with a name biology gave
it first. Independent lineages arrive at the camera eye, the streamlined body, the wing, and they do
so because optics, fluid dynamics and lift are the same wherever they are met, and the catalogue is
large \citep{mcghee2011}. In the cases named here the forcing constraint can be stated, which is what
this account requires of a convergence before it counts; that it can be stated in \emph{every} case is
not claimed, and how far convergence indicates inevitability is itself disputed \citep{losos2017}.
Nothing was copied and nothing had access to anything.

The change side is the same economics read the other way, and it gives a second correspondence. In
this account change is paid and persistence is free to the frame, so a structure changes when what it meets will
not cohere with what it holds and not otherwise --- which is stasis as the default and change as the
forced exception, the shape the fossil record was read as showing when long stable intervals were
argued to be punctuated by short rapid ones \citep{eldredgegould1972}. Read in the substrate's terms,
selection is the residual gate: variation is undirected and cheap, and what is expensive is the
rewriting that follows when the residual will not fit.

\emph{Grain is set by the environment.} How finely a lineage discriminates is not chosen by it but
forced by what it meets, which is the aperture claim of Section~\ref{sec:interface} in another
grounding, and it is why the same organism is coarse in one setting and finely differentiated in
another.
\end{remark}

The consequence for this paper's own composition claim is the deflation stated in
Section~\ref{sec:scope}: if recurrence is what compressed structures do, what a recurrence evidences
is the constraint and not the arrangement, and the composition falsifier is negative-only. The test
that remains is not \emph{a domain not under these constraints}, which is empty --- every physical
domain is under finite signal speed, finite bandwidth and a thermodynamic floor. It is a domain under
\emph{weak} compression: somewhere with slack, where cost pressure is not binding, and where the
arrangement should therefore be absent. Ritual, ornament, and systems maintained past their purpose
are candidates. Finding the arrangement there, rendered with equal facility, would show the
vocabulary doing the work.

Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed} is the paper's discriminating test between
privileged-carving realism and cost-shaped pluralism, and the systematics literature is where it can be run. The competing species
concepts do converge, on a common notion of a separately evolving lineage, with the long dispute
reinterpreted as disagreement over operational criteria rather than over the concept
\citep{dequeiroz2007} --- and that convergence is what this account predicts rather than what
embarrasses it. Whether it is \emph{enforced} is the open question, and de Queiroz himself holds the
boundaries to be real but epistemically hard, which is the opposing horn honestly stated; the
criteria continue to disagree hardest over asexual organisms, bacteria exchanging genes horizontally
and recent divergences. This paper does not claim the case is settled in its favour. It claims the
case is a test.

The wider prediction is that classification degrades in a \emph{located} way rather than uniformly,
and it must be stated with the operationalisation that makes it a test.

\begin{hypothesis}[Located degradation]\label{hyp:located-degradation}
Where a classification's purpose can be specified independently and in advance of inspecting its
failures, the failures concentrate near the boundary of that purpose more than a uniform
distribution over the domain would produce. The claim is comparative and is not that failures occur
nowhere else.
\end{hypothesis}

The advance-specification clause is what keeps this from circularity, and it is a real constraint: a
purpose read off from where the failures already are confirms nothing. Independent evidence exists
elsewhere --- classification systems in institutional use are documented as leaking wherever they
have been examined closely, carrying residual categories and encoding their builders' purposes
\citep{bowkerstar1999} --- and that literature's concept of \emph{torque} --- the twisting of a
person's own trajectory against a standardised classification that does not fit it --- is a closer
instrument for this hypothesis than leakage is, with the qualification that its subject is a
biography rather than a use.

Further results bear on the general claim without establishing it. No clustering function
satisfies scale-invariance, richness and consistency together \citep{kleinberg2002} --- but the same
paper shows any two of the three are jointly achievable, so what is established is that the three
cannot be had at once rather than that no cut is canonical. Kleinberg reads it that way himself, and
dismisses a canonicity result for single-linkage cited on his second page as resting on a property
that is an implicit optimisation criterion which single-linkage uniquely optimises; that dismissal is
his, and is noted here rather than relied on. It is a trade-off theorem, not an impossibility of
carving, and it is a deliberate
transplant of Arrow's method rather than an independent convergence --- the paper says so. And the
question of which predicates are projectible has no syntactic answer \citep{goodman1955}, which is
usually reported as a defeat. Goodman's own criterion is \emph{entrenchment}, a record of a
predicate's having been \emph{projected} before --- a history of use and deliberately not a history
of success, so it does not license reading entrenchment as tracking what has been cheap to run. The resemblance to a cost criterion is not evidence for one.

The constructive response is this paper's own and needs no companion: if the trouble is a cut fixed in
advance, do not fix one. Corollary~\ref{cor:mint-is-grammar} already gives the mechanism --- recognition
and growth as one operation, joined where the residual meets the grammar that admits it --- so a
category is minted only where what arrived will not fit, and the codebook grows rather than the cut
being defended. That recovers no privileged carving, because there is none, and does not need to.

The position is not new, and its nearest neighbour differs in grounding rather than in conclusion. A
pluralism admitting many equally legitimate and equally real classifications, none of them the one
nature intended, is argued at length \citep{dupre1993}; that argument rests on objective similarity
structure, where this one rests on what a cut costs to run, which is the more deflationary footing and
reintroduces a ranking over cuts that ``equally legitimate'' was formulated to deny. The convergence
is on the negative thesis; the divergence in grounding is real and is not smoothed over here.

\section{Determinism and apparent stochasticity}\label{sec:determinism}

\begin{proposition}[Graph-law determinism]\label{prop:determinism}
For a fully specified antecedent configuration---including the graph state, operative transformation structure, admissibility grammar, and every causally operative containing relation relevant to the collapse---the realised continuation is determined:
\[
\boxed{
\left(G_t,\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{causal}}\right)
\mapsto
G_{t+1}.
}
\]
The Law (Schema~\ref{hyp:the-law}) contains no primitive random actualisation operator.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism} is to be read through Proposition~\ref{prop:present-sufficiency}: the antecedent configuration is causally sufficient \emph{present} structure, not a retained temporal history. Determinism is a relation between present structure and realised continuation, and Proposition~\ref{prop:determinable} keeps it from being overstated --- a determinable continuation is not thereby a determined one.

Apparent stochasticity arises when a frame does not represent all of the causally operative dimensions or relations required to determine the realised continuation. A probability distribution may then be an appropriate model of that frame's uncertainty, but the probability belongs to the incomplete representation rather than to the substrate transition itself. Treating probability as a representation of incomplete information has a long statistical-mechanical and Bayesian lineage \citep{jaynes1957}.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\text{stochastic observation}
=
\text{deterministic dynamics viewed through unresolved causal structure}.
}
\]

\begin{remark}[Direction of the borrowing]\label{rem:qm-provenance}
The order of discovery ran from quantum measurement outward, not from a general primitive inward. Collapse was adopted because compatibility with quantum measurement is a constraint on any theory of realised change, and was then assumed to hold generally. The bridge is therefore stated conditionally because the \emph{generalisation} is the conjecture, not because the compatibility is in doubt. This is the structure of a promoted constraint --- a fact that must hold in one regime, assumed to hold everywhere, with the consequences derived and exposed to falsification --- and it is why Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} carries a falsifier for the bridge specifically. Origin does not establish a claim; the falsifier is what does the work either way.
\end{remark}

The distinction of Remark~\ref{rem:undetermined-vs-undisclosed} is what makes quantum measurement the visible case for the conditional collapse bridge (Remark~\ref{rem:qm-provenance}). In that regime the pre-measurement alternatives cannot, under locality and noncontextuality, be re-read as an \emph{undisclosed} already-realised classical value: Bell's theorem excludes the local hidden-variable class and Kochen--Specker constrains noncontextual value assignment \citep{bell1964,kochen1967}. What remains is \emph{undetermined} in the strict sense --- not a value withheld, but no value yet. Elsewhere the two readings are usually interchangeable in practice; there they are not.

Deterministic quantum models demonstrate that empirical quantum statistics do not by themselves force fundamental stochasticity: Bohm's hidden-variable construction is explicitly deterministic \citep{bohm1952}, while the constraints just cited rule out the local and noncontextual hidden-variable classes rather than determinism as such. Graphic Equalisation does not identify itself with Bohmian mechanics; these results establish only that determinism must be distinguished from locality and noncontextuality.

A stochastic law may be bolted onto a grounding as an epistemic predictive model over unresolved continuations. Doing so is an additional modelling layer:
\[
\text{deterministic Law}
\longrightarrow
\text{frame-limited uncertainty model},
\]
not a modification of the Law (Schema~\ref{hyp:the-law}) into a stochastic substrate.

\part{Positioning, falsifiers, and close}
\section{Positioning and prior art}\label{sec:prior-art}

The individual ingredients of Graphic Equalisation have substantial prior art. The proposed contribution is their conjunction at one minimal substrate level and the additional distinctions forced by making that conjunction executable.

\paragraph{Graph rewriting and typed transformation.} Algebraic graph transformation provides mature formalisms for typed graphs, rule application, partiality, and structural consistency \citep{ehrig2006}. Graphic Equalisation adopts graph transformation as substrate vocabulary but makes frame-relative boundary forcing, collapse, and identity part of the same dynamical account.

\paragraph{Reflection and self-modification.} Computational reflection establishes that a system can represent and modify meta-level structure governing its own execution \citep{smith1982,maes1987}. The specific identity ``the grammar acting on itself is the loop'' is the present paper's claim, not a result attributed to those sources.

\paragraph{Cybernetics and feedback.} Classical cybernetics and control theory distinguish open-loop dynamics from systems in which error is returned into subsequent control \citep{wiener1948,ashby1956}. The residual construction generalises the same causal distinction: mismatch changes later action only where it survives as represented state.

\paragraph{Quantum measurement and relational description.} The separation of pre-realisation state from realised measurement record is standard in the measurement problem \citep{vonneumann1955}. Everett's relative-state formulation and Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics provide important precedents for refusing a single privileged observer-independent account of all facts \citep{everett1957,rovelli1996}. Graphic Equalisation uses these as neighbouring structures rather than deriving itself from any one interpretation.

\paragraph{Deterministic quantum theories.} Bohm's 1952 model is a direct proof of principle that deterministic dynamics can reproduce nonrelativistic quantum predictions when supplemented by additional state \citep{bohm1952}. Bell and Kochen--Specker impose decisive constraints on local and noncontextual hidden-variable theories (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}). Consequently, the determinism claim in Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism} should be read as a commitment requiring an adequate nonlocal/contextual grounding where quantum phenomena demand it, not as a revival of unconstrained classical hidden variables.

\paragraph{Causal order, and worlds that grow without anybody watching.} Causal-set theory supplies a
well-developed example in which causal order is taken as primitive while continuum metric structure is
additional or emergent \citep{bombelli1987}. Its sequential-growth dynamics \citep{rideout1999} is the
nearer neighbour for the claim that observation is derived: a causal set grows by the accretion of
elements under a stochastic law, with no observer anywhere in the mechanism and no external time it
grows in. What differs is scope --- that is a physics, and the claim here is at the level of
information, so it is offered as a case in which a world happens with nothing watching rather than as
support for the general form.

The process tradition is the obvious other place to look and cuts both ways, which is why it is named
rather than claimed. Whitehead's occasions are self-constituting and need no external spectator, which
agrees; but \emph{prehension} is fundamental for him, and a taking-account-of that goes all the way down
is the reverse of observation being late and expensive. A reader who finds the resemblance should also
find the disagreement, and this account takes the second half. Graphic Equalisation makes only the weaker substrate claim that ordinal causal order does not itself entail a real-valued metric time.

\paragraph{Emergent and relational time.} That time is not a substrate dimension (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}) has substantial physics precedent, and the present account sits at the most-local end of it. The Wheeler--DeWitt equation of canonical quantum gravity carries no time parameter \citep{dewitt1967} --- the ``problem of time'' this substrate dissolves rather than solves, there having been no time variable to recover. The Page--Wootters mechanism derives apparent evolution from entanglement between a clock subsystem and the rest \citep{pagewootters1983}, closely matching ``time inferred from the relations a frame holds,'' but retains a single global state where this account is per-frame. Barbour's Machian relationalism takes time to be abstracted from change rather than a container \citep{barbour1999}, and Rovelli's relational and thermal-time programme refuses a privileged global time \citep{rovelli1996,rovelli2011}. The distinguishing move here is to keep none of the global objects these programmes retain --- global wavefunction, global configuration space, global state --- reading time entirely from local change.

\paragraph{The recurring level error.} The precedents above are not one lineage, and they do not independently establish this account. They do, however, repeatedly correct one limited error: treating a quantity indexed to a frame, a conditioning, or a descriptive level as a frame-free item, or as an item of the same kind as the structure it describes.

The older cases are discontinuous and differ sharply in strength. Pyrrhonian scepticism responds to opposed appearances and arguments by suspending assent: it disciplines what one may claim, but supplies no positive ontology of frames or relations \citep{sextus2000}. Madhyamaka distinguishes conventional truth from ultimate analysis and denies that dependent phenomena have intrinsic nature; it prevents a conventional description from being reified as a fundamental constituent, but is neither a reference-frame theory nor a derivation of the present substrate \citep{nagarjuna1995}. Jain claim-discipline is the closer premodern analogue. In Akala\.nka's formulation of \emph{sy\=adv\=ada}, the particle \emph{sy\=at} qualifies a statement by its own substance, place, time, and condition, thereby blocking a partial viewpoint from silently becoming an unqualified assertion \citep{nahata2024}. The \emph{Zhuangzi} offers a further, bounded analogue: judgments and their language arise from situated, learned paths, while a cosmic standpoint cannot authoritatively settle them; that is perspectival modesty, not a technical calculus of levels \citep{zhuangzi1968}. This is not asserted as an uninterrupted medieval inheritance: scholastic talk of relation or optical standpoint alone does not establish the correction and is therefore not counted as one.

The physical line becomes explicit with Galileo's ship: common uniform motion cannot be exposed by an experiment internal to that ship, so a description from outside cannot be silently used as a difference within it \citep{galilei1967}. Modern relativity generalises that domain-specific repair while retaining spacetime geometry \citep{einstein1916}; its lesson remains narrower than this one. In quantum foundations, relative-state and relational accounts reject a privileged observer-independent catalogue of values \citep{everett1957,rovelli1996}; in generally covariant physics, the distinction between partial and complete observables stops a coordinate or clock parameter being mistaken for the physical correlation it helps describe \citep{pagewootters1983,rovelli2002}. Quantum reference-frame work makes the same repair constructively, changing between physical frames without appealing to an external absolute one \citep{giacomini2019}.

The recent closed-universe literature supplies an especially sharp type-level instance. Wormhole work introduced $\alpha$-sectors as superselection data \citep{coleman1988,giddingsstrominger1988}; later accounts make the associated null-state and ensemble structure explicit \citep{marolfmaxfield2020}. Harlow, Usatyuk and Zhao distinguish the quantum mechanics of an observer in a closed universe from an external description of closed universes \citep{harlowusatyukzhao2025}; Zhao's de~Sitter construction sharpens the point still further. Its baby-universe Hilbert space has a commutative algebra and packages classical statistics, whereas the bulk Hilbert space carries the noncommutative quantum mechanics available to an observer inside one universe \citep{zhao2026}. Calling the former the latter is not a new physical possibility but a confusion of structures at different levels.

The common repair is therefore not this theory's result, and the map is prior art rather than evidence for its composition claim. Each programme retains substantial domain-specific machinery --- a discipline of assent, a soteriological ontology, a spacetime geometry, a global state, a clock system, a gravitational path integral, or a specified observer algebra --- that this substrate does not posit. What the recurrence does locate is the present scope: Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} makes the prohibition programme-wide. No frame's bookkeeping, no external description, and no representation at another level may be silently counted as distinctions available within the frame it describes. The stronger claim is carried here as a commitment; the historical cases show only why the correction is worth stating before the rest of the construction begins.

\paragraph{Computational-universe and graph-rewrite models.} The closest extant framework is the Wolfram Physics Project, in which the universe is a hypergraph evolved by rewrite rules, time is progressive rule application, and observer-independent structure follows from \emph{causal invariance} --- different update orderings yielding the same causal graph \citep{wolfram2002,wolfram2020,wolfram2020cs}. The structural agreement is deep: graph plus rewrite rules, time emergent from rule application rather than posited, order-independence, and observer-relative slicing. Four differences locate the present contribution. First, $\Gamma$ is an admissibility grammar that is itself graph data and self-modifying (the grammar-is-the-loop theorem), whereas Wolfram fixes a rule per universe or takes the entire rule space --- the Ruliad --- at once. Second, collapse realises \emph{one} frame-relative continuation, whereas Wolfram retains all branches (the multiway graph) and derives quantum structure from them. Third, residual and growth-by-minting from it have no counterpart in a fixed rule. Fourth, the present substrate keeps no global object --- no single hypergraph being updated --- being local and frame-relative throughout, and its claims are information-level and cross-domain rather than an attempt to derive our specific universe. This last is why $c$ falls out here as a \emph{per-frame} unit (developed in the physics companion, its Section~\ref{PH-sec:c-rate}): sub-rules with closure give each frame its own cycle and hence its own $c=1$, invariant by construction, whereas a single global update yields $c$ only as an emergent speed limit shown observer-independent by causal invariance --- the constant, but not the per-frame machinery beneath it. That an independent programme reached the shared shape from cellular automata and physics, while this one reached it from cognition and engineering, bears on the arrangement claim of Section~\ref{sec:scope} rather than against its novelty, at the calibrated strength given there.

\paragraph{Information as constitutive.} That what exists comes to information is Wheeler's \emph{it
from bit} \citep{wheeler1990}, asserted there at total scope within physics --- every particle, every
field of force, spacetime itself --- and before it Bateson's unit of information, \emph{``a difference
which makes a difference''} \citep{bateson1972}. Two differences locate what is added, and the first is
a deflation rather than an extension. \emph{The ground.} Wheeler derives existence from
apparatus-elicited answers to yes-or-no questions, which is why the participatory observer is the most
contested part of that programme; the ground here is relation (\emph{Is}, Proposition~\ref{IS-prop:is}), and a relation
requires no apparatus --- a rock stands in relations without measuring anything, and \emph{Is} is
explicit that what merely is cannot observe at all. The same claim is reached on a weaker ground, and
without the participatory commitment usually taken to come with it. \emph{The scope.} Wheeler's claim is
total within physics; Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} states it for every frame's every claim, physical or not. That also
makes explicit what Bateson left implicit, since \emph{makes a difference} already asks \emph{to whom}
and does not say. \emph{The polarity.} The third difference is the smallest to state and cuts deepest.
Wheeler's bit is an \emph{answer} --- elicited by an apparatus, to a yes-or-no question, and therefore
polarised, since what makes one outcome the \emph{yes} is the question that was asked. Nothing here
supplies a question, and an edge is not an answer to one. What a relation carries is a distinction, and
a distinction has no polarity of its own: Shannon's measure is invariant under any relabelling of the
alphabet that preserves the distinctions, so the labels carry none of the information they are attached
to \citep{shannon1948}. \emph{The bit is unlabelled, and the labels are put there by a frame.} That is
not a new principle but Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}'s stated once more --- individuation is performed by
the container and not by the thing, and a label is a boundary drawn --- and it is not a claim that
labels are idle. A frame that acts differently on the two sides is doing work with them and paying for
them, and they are well-formed for it in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}. What is not
substrate is any of it above the bare difference --- that two relations differ is forced, and
Remark~\ref{rem:types-forced} gives the argument, while sorting them into kinds, naming the kinds and
fixing which side is the \emph{yes} are supplied in that order and none of them given. The price is the one Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} charges already: no bit is \emph{of} anything
absolutely, and what a bit is of cannot be said without naming the frame that says it.

\paragraph{Information and minimum description.} The preference for the smallest sufficient ontology is the method's rather than this paper's, and is set out with its prior art in \emph{Is}. What matters here is only its consequence for one hypothesis: it motivates the Discrete Sufficiency Hypothesis (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:discrete}) and does not prove it.

\paragraph{Standing beside, not replacing.} Graphic Equalisation is not proposed as a replacement for established theories. It is intended to sit beneath or beside them as a substrate description. A grounding may add continuous geometry, conservation laws, quantum amplitudes, relativistic metrics, biological mechanisms, institutional rules, or probabilistic models of unresolved lower-frame observations. In particular, relational quantum mechanics \citep{rovelli1996}, Everettian relative-state quantum mechanics \citep{everett1957}, and deterministic Bohmian mechanics \citep{bohm1952} make mutually different commitments while remaining compatible with large parts of standard quantum phenomenology; this paper does not require choosing among those complete physical models at the substrate-definition stage. Such additions do not alter Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism}: within the Law (Schema~\ref{hyp:the-law}), realised transition remains deterministic once the causally complete antecedent configuration is specified.

The substrate claim remains
\[
\boxed{\text{represented state}\rightarrow\text{admissible possibility}\rightarrow\text{collapse}\rightarrow\text{realised state}.}
\]

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}
The question of what assessment an account of this kind admits --- what a universal claim can risk, why
usefulness rather than falsification is what it earns, and what the implementation record shows --- is
treated in the assessment companion (its Section~\ref{AS-sec:risk}). This section states the position it reaches and then the
commitments themselves.

\paragraph{The position, in brief.} The purely framework-level part of
this account carries no falsifiable content, because a contingent commitment doing no framework-work is
idle structure by its own definition of well-formedness. The rest is contentful and answerable, and a
counterexample to it \emph{bounds} the account --- locates the edge of its range --- rather than
refuting it from nowhere, which is what residual does everywhere else here. What separates such an
account from a mere vocabulary is that a counterexample is conceivable at all. Establishing it would
require enumerating an unbounded frontier and cannot terminate; bounding it requires one extant thing
that does not fit. And what it earns beyond that is not truth but use: whether it does work its absence
would not, which is a record and not a proof, and is reported in that companion with its gaps
(its Section~\ref{AS-sec:implementation}).

\medskip
Each commitment below is marked \textsc{[shape]} or \textsc{[detail]} --- the same distinction
\emph{Is} uses in its own falsifiers. A \textsc{[detail]} failing is a revision: the account survives
with that mechanism replaced. A \textsc{[shape]} failing costs \emph{range} rather than a mechanism,
because what fails is something the substrate is claimed to require or to permit, so the account does
not hold where it claimed to --- the more serious of the two, and still a boundary rather than an
annihilation. The distinction is the one Section~\ref{sec:scope} rests its epistemic claim on, and marking it here is
what makes that claim checkable rather than asserted.

\paragraph{\textnormal{\textsc{[shape]}}~Composition.}
The arrangement claim needs a comparative test; ``equal facility'' alone has no
measurement rule. One candidate protocol fixes a code, lossless domain-to-graph
maps, permitted auxiliary machinery, reconstruction criterion, and resource
budget before rendering matched target and adversarial control domains. Let
$d(D)=L_{\rm GE}(D)-L_{\rm native}(D)$ include the mapping, added rules and
residual description lengths. The comparative prediction is
$\overline d_{\rm control}-\overline d_{\rm target}>0$; a nonpositive
difference challenges that advantage under the declared protocol.
Independent renderers and a prespecified uncertainty rule are needed for a noisy
sample. This protocol has not been registered or run. It tests compression
advantage under its fixed code, not the ontology, and no operational composition
falsifier is claimed to have been passed. Independent recurrence in a domain's
own idiom remains suggestive evidence, also compatible with shared constraints.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Solutions are nearly unique under a binding constraint.} Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:cheap-cut}, the posit under Propositions~\ref{prop:convergence-observed} and~\ref{prop:compression-convergence}. Fails if structures optimising hard against the same binding constraint, over the same region, are exhibited arriving at arrangements that do not resemble each other --- in which case constraint narrows the space without selecting within it, and both propositions weaken to that.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Located degradation.} Fails if, for classifications whose purpose is specifiable in advance of inspecting their failures, failure cases are distributed no differently near the purpose boundary than across the rest of the domain (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:located-degradation}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Convergence is not enforced.} Fails if convergence between independently developed classifications of one domain extends to the cases where the classifications' purposes differ, which is what a privileged boundary would produce and cost-shaped convergence would not (Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed}). Marked \textsc{[shape]} because a privileged boundary is not a mechanism this account could replace, since the contingency of the rules that draw a boundary is what Section~\ref{sec:carvings} rests on and what Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} asserts one level up. Exhibiting one costs range rather than a mechanism.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Minimal graph sufficiency.} Fails if basic realised graph dynamics require a primitive beyond $(g,r,\Gamma)$ rather than represented or relational structure (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:minimal-graph}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Discrete sufficiency.} Fails if a claimed observable consequence cannot be preserved by any discrete representation, including dynamically varying quantisation, but is preserved by a genuinely non-discrete primitive (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:discrete}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Antecedent-state principle.} Fails if a realised transition can require as antecedent a fact that exists only as consequence of that same transition, absent prior fixed-point structure (Proposition~\ref{prop:antecedent-state}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Graph-law determinism.} Fails if two genuinely identical, causally complete antecedent configurations produce different realised successors without any additional causally operative distinction (Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism}). Variation observed only from a frame that omits determining dimensions does not constitute such a falsification.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No implicit historical state.} Fails if an unrepresented past event alters later transition structure despite leaving no causal trace in graph or boundary state.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Frame-relative identity.} Fails if sameness and persistence are derivable from $(g,r,\Gamma)$ alone without individuation.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Residual is inadmissibility.} Fails if a graph-originated deformation can be left as residual by a containing frame that nonetheless admits it under $\Gamma_{W_c}$, or absorbed while inadmissible, so that residual and non-admission come apart.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Cost is the write.} Fails if the boundary relation itself, or the retention of standing structure, carries an irreducible cost that is not any write's --- a frame charged for holding its boundary open, or for keeping structure standing, where no delta is written and none is occasioned. Those two are the non-write costs Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} itself names as free, so they are where the claim is exposed; reading is not the test, since a paid read is a write here (Proposition~\ref{prop:evaluation-writes}) and the falsifier would be absorbed rather than met. Marked \textsc{[detail]} because Remark~\ref{rem:writeability} already carries the distribution of costs as a pattern rather than a universal: a structure with a different writeability profile refines the characterisation rather than breaking the substrate.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{A held program is one write.} Fails if skilled action disrupted mid-execution degrades to a cost intermediate between expert and novice rather than to the per-step cost, or if a copied program runs at one-write cost on a runner that does not supply its lanes --- the copy inheriting the lanes rather than its runner's, against the difference-in-kind a copy carries (Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}, Remark~\ref{rem:one-write}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Self-change is subset-change.} The claim is that a graph changes itself and its subgraphs directly (rule-governed), and affects its container only as a subset of it, by propagation through coupling. Fails if a graph changes its container by a means other than changing the region of it that the graph is --- a container-change not propagating from the graph's own subset --- or if a \emph{container-supplied} graph can self-terminate without acting on the container (Corollary~\ref{cor:locked-in}); a self-supplied graph stopping itself is not a counterexample, being the case that corollary excludes.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Coupling is a setting, not a kind.} Fails if instances that are subgraphs of one containing frame are found not to deform one another through it, or if the substrate is shown to fix the setting rather than leaving it to rule content (Proposition~\ref{prop:coupling}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Two gates, and absence is not residual.} Fails if what a frame's aperture excludes is nonetheless held or disturbs it --- an excluded input leaving a remainder in place --- so that the input and output faces stop differing in what their exclusions cost (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}, Definitions~\ref{def:aperture} and~\ref{def:change-grammar}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Minting is a rule.} Fails if grammar-extension (growth) requires a primitive operation beneath the rules rather than a reflective rule subject to the same economics, admissibility, and antecedent-state principle (Proposition~\ref{prop:antecedent-state}) --- in particular, if a graph can mint its first distinctions without any seeded from its container.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Partial-order sufficiency.} Fails if realised dynamics require a total order over collapses in distinct frames --- a global simultaneity --- rather than only the boundary-induced partial order.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Globally-async/locally-sync.} Fails if coupling never induces local synchrony (boundary-related collapses remain unordered) or if synchrony is forced without coupling (a global instant exists independent of any boundary).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Time is inferred, not primitive.} Fails if a frame's realised dynamics require a temporal coordinate that is not constructed from relations among what it holds --- a collapse presenting a duration as a datum of the state rather than as a reading over states (Axiom~\ref{ax:no-time}, Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred}). A frame that represents succession is not the counterexample; that is what the claim predicts. Marked \textsc{[shape]} because what would fail is the range of everything derived through the absence of a substrate time dimension.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Closure is the default operational clock hypothesis.} The claim is not that closure is required, but that a frame with no contrary evidence should prefer a treated-as-closed recurrence because it is the shortest self-contained clock model. It is revised, not falsified, where a represented counter, an external clock, or a superframe schedule improves operational use (Proposition~\ref{prop:cycle-unit}).
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Phase is signed; substrate time is not.} A signed phase does not by itself carry duration or establish a backwards substrate current. A frame may nevertheless use a reverse or dual temporal representation if it is operationally useful for constraints unavailable in its own ordering (Remark~\ref{rem:phase-not-time}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Decoherence is one operation.} Fails if the manner in which an inadmissible write fails is a property of what was attempted rather than of the frame's rules --- that is, if one frame under one rule set yields structurally distinct failure modes for two inadmissible writes, with the difference not attributable to that frame's own policy (Proposition~\ref{prop:decoherence-one}). Different outcomes across \emph{different} frames do not bear on this; that is what the claim predicts.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{A swallowed failure is relocated, never closed.} Fails if a frame can \emph{settle} from inside that a swallowed failure went nowhere, rather than relocating the question to what it cannot inspect (Proposition~\ref{prop:handler-relocates}). Not that a frame cannot detect its own handler discarding a failure --- it can; the exposure is the settling. One of the four places named below at which the same event would be found.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Dependents are not held by the referent.} Fails if realised graph dynamics require a structure to carry the set of things depending on it --- back-references primitive rather than installed --- so that the consequences of removing a structure are recoverable from that structure alone, without querying the frames holding the references and without paid machinery (Proposition~\ref{prop:vacated-three-states}, argued at Remark~\ref{rem:vacated}). A substrate in which deletion is always clean falsifies this, and would also remove the distinction between an absence and a vacancy.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{A boundary is known only by crossing it.} Fails if a frame can \emph{settle} the location of its own admissibility boundary without any write that crosses it. Extrapolation from inside the admissible region evidences a boundary and is not at issue; establishing one would be (Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-by-crossing}). This is the epistemic member of the same family as the provenance commitment below, and fails in the same manner --- by a procedure that closes the question rather than relocating it.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Robustness trades against legibility.} Fails if a policy can bound a frame's residuals without reducing what its failures disclose about its grammar --- a handler that both absorbs and reports with no loss (Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-by-crossing}, argued at Remark~\ref{rem:failure-instrument}). Cheaply testable in software, and marked \textsc{[detail]} because the account survives its loss with the trade weakened to a tendency.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Capability and accessibility are distinct.} Fails if what a frame's gates admit and what it can reach from where it stands collapse into one quantity --- if there is no case of a frame able to represent and perform an arrangement it cannot reach, or none of a frame able to reach what it cannot represent (Definition~\ref{def:capability}). The coupling claimed between them, that a capacity shortfall narrows aperture and so degrades capability over time, fails separately if aperture width is unaffected by demands on capacity.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{A hazard is a signed capability change above a frame-relative threshold.} Fails if a disclosure can be a hazard to a frame in which the negative sign is not admissible, or if the magnitude at which one counts is fixed independently of the frame bearing it rather than as a ratio to what that frame can absorb. Fails also if the threshold sits somewhere other than where the impact forces a write.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Externalising lengthens the interval before a crossing is legible.} Fails if the time for a crossing's cost to become signable is independent of whether the frame bearing it can report --- that is, if costs placed on frames that cannot signal return no more slowly than costs placed on frames that can (Section~\ref{sec:crossings}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No free lunch.} Fails on a realised change whose cost is absent in the frame that made it \emph{and} in the container that sustains it, on inspection of both --- a payment traced to neither rather than merely not yet looked for. The burden is finite and on the negative side: asking instead for a cost absent at \emph{every} frame would demand an enumeration this account says cannot terminate, and for which Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} supplies no standpoint. The claim is empirical, not modal. A costless change is admissible; none has been traced. Note that a frame experiencing freeness is not the counterexample and never was --- at the table the next plate genuinely costs the diner nothing, and the buffet pays --- so the test is whether the payment appears anywhere it is looked for, not whether some frame gets something for nothing.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Termination follows supply, not containment.} Fails if containment alone determines it --- if a subgraph must stop when the graph containing it stops, even where its primitives are supplied from beyond that graph (Proposition~\ref{prop:termination-supply}). The partial case carries its own clause: fails if a frame losing part of its supply must either stop or continue correctly, rather than running with an incomplete operation set and undefined behaviour, or if such a frame can certify exhaustive unrestricted knowledge of its lost supply from inside, without assuming a complete provider registry. A local enumeration under a declared registry does not falsify the provenance commitment below.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The relativity of grounding is a consequence, and can fail as one.} Fails if a view can be informative without standing in relation to what it views, or if relation is not what carries information here. A consequence has content and can fail, which a posit or a costless guard could not (Remark~\ref{rem:axiom-in-bits}). The second disjunct is \emph{Is}'s own identification of distinction with relation, so this is where the account is thinnest rather than where it is safest, and it is marked \textsc{[shape]} because what fails with it is the range of everything derived through it.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Description length is constitutive, not merely a method.} Fails if a relation carrying no content \emph{given the others} nevertheless adds to what a thing is --- the conditional form of Remark~\ref{rem:self-edge}'s result, which Remark~\ref{rem:mdl-ontic} uses and does not prove. Marked \textsc{[detail]} because the account survives its failure: minimum description length reverts to the imported method \emph{Is} already had, and only the claim that the two are one principle at two grips is lost.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Provenance is never closed by inference.}
The claim concerns assumption-free final closure of the recursively supplying
operators, not identification of a declared finite provider map. It is
challenged by an in-frame procedure establishing exhaustive unrestricted
supply coverage without assuming the completeness of its candidate model.
Where admissible supply alternatives have identical available evidence but
different provider provenance, they remain unidentifiable
(Proposition~\ref{prop:provenance-inference}). Local identification from leakage
or reliable records is permitted (Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}). The unrestricted
nonclosure is the separately argued frontier commitment of
Section~\ref{sec:prohibition}, not a theorem derived merely from evidence dependence.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Scope note (not a falsifier).} \emph{Carries no falsifiable content} names the same condition the assessment companion reaches by asking whether \emph{attempting} a counterexample has an outcome (its Proposition~\ref{AS-prop:discriminator}). Where the putative counterexample would not cohere, neither \emph{testable} nor \emph{empty} applies as a reader's ordinary falsifiability frame hears it; the distinction sits below the grain at which falsifiability is usually argued.

The Law itself (Schema~\ref{hyp:the-law}) is not a separate entry above because its content is exposed jointly by three that are: \emph{Antecedent-state} stakes what a realised transition may read, \emph{Graph-law determinism} stakes that present structure fixes the successor, and \emph{Partial-order sufficiency} stakes that no order beyond the boundary-induced one is required. A reader checking coverage for the Law by name will find it there rather than under its own heading.

Two claims are stated here rather than counted in the list above, being consequences rather than wagers --- the standard is the one the composition paragraph sets, that a test whose stopping condition lies with the assessor is not a test. \emph{Admissibility is not armour} (Proposition~\ref{prop:self-governance}) and \emph{higher-frame first-class structure} (Proposition~\ref{prop:higher-frame}) are closer to consequences of the definitions than to empirical risks: given a container that may force arbitrarily, self-admissibility not constraining it follows, and given that a frame's state about another frame is ordinary state, its independence from that other frame's primitives follows. They are retained because they are load-bearing and a reader should see what they commit to, and they are marked here as derivations rather than wagers.

\emph{Minimal graph sufficiency} (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:minimal-graph}) is not a third such case, though it can look like one: it might be said to absorb its own falsifier, on the grounds that any proposed fourth primitive can be redescribed as represented or relational structure. But that redescription is not a rescue --- \emph{it is what the claim says}. The commitment is one of \emph{sufficiency}: not that nothing else exists, but that nothing else is required. A named feature turning out to be graph data confirms it rather than evading it, and the paper's own results are that pattern rather than exceptions to it, since frame, dimension, time and cost each entered as a candidate primitive and left as represented structure.

So the falsifier is closeable and the burden sits on the positive side, where it is constructive: exhibit realised dynamics that \emph{cannot run} on $(g,r,\Gamma)$. Proposing a primitive is not the test and never was. And the place to conduct it is the minimum rather than the maximum --- the rock of Remark~\ref{rem:rock}, where $r$ and $\Gamma$ are absent at the grain of individuation and there is accordingly nothing extra present to smuggle the work into. A sufficiency claim is tested where least is available, not where most is.

Two commitments below expose the two postulates the ledger counts. The realisation operator is
exposed by the single-valuedness entry; the destroy\slash preserve distinction is exposed by the
no-copy entry, since what that entry tests is whether the discarding class is the general one. An
unexposed postulate is more serious than a mis-tagged one, so both keep their
falsifiers whatever their status in the ledger (Remark~\ref{rem:inherited}), because whether a claim
is postulated or derived moves where a failure would propagate from and not whether it can happen.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,resume]
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Realisation is single-valued.} Fails if realised dynamics are better accounted for by retaining the admissible set than by selecting one member of it --- and the qualification is the whole test: a treatment carrying all continuations forward must recover the observed statistics from the retained structure \emph{without adding a measure over it to do so}, since retention that needs a weighting has reintroduced selection under another name.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No-copy} (the destroy\slash preserve postulate's exposure)\textbf{.} Fails if a substrate change can preserve its predecessor
without paying to keep it --- if retention is available at no cost rather than as a purchased record.
The retention-cost postulate requires a specified resource ledger. Landauer bounds logically irreversible erasure and does not require every change to be irreversible; reversible uncomputation is compatible with it (Section~\ref{sec:interface}). The arrow of the event order is \emph{not} at stake, though it is
easily thought to be: direction is carried by the asymmetry of enablement, which stands
whether or not a change preserves what it followed.
\end{enumerate}

Four of the entries above are one risk counted four times, and the list says so rather than banking
the arithmetic. \emph{Provenance is never closed by inference}, \emph{A boundary is known only by
crossing it}, \emph{A swallowed failure is relocated, never closed} and the second clause of
\emph{Termination follows supply} concern the same closure pattern at
different interfaces. Each is assessed within its stated scope: an in-frame
procedure must settle the relevant question without assuming the exhaustive
coverage whose establishment is at issue. Local identification within a
declared finite supply model is not a counterexample to unrestricted
provenance nonclosure. They are kept as separate entries because they name genuinely different places to look for the
event, which is what a falsifier is for, and not because failing them would cost four separate
things. The shared frontier argument is the single prohibition of
Section~\ref{sec:prohibition}; the analogy does not remove the scope and
premises of each entry.

Two notes on the list as a whole. Nothing is claimed to be closeable cheaply --- \emph{discrete sufficiency} (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:discrete}) requires ruling out every discrete representation including dynamically varying ones, which is hard rather than ill-formed, and the distinction between the two is the point of this paragraph. And the marks themselves are revisable: a mark records what a failure would cost the account, and setting out a further commitment can sharpen the criterion by which an earlier one was assigned.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
The theory is deliberately small. A graph need not know what it is, where it is, what will happen, or what it wants. Present structure determines admissible continuation; interaction and collapse produce realised history; realised history changes the antecedent from which subsequent possibility is generated.

Recursion requires no external homunculus. Where the grammar acts on its own represented structure, the grammar is the loop (Proposition~\ref{prop:grammar-is-loop}). World forcing is not self-action, residual is discrepancy rather than diagnosis, history matters only where it survives as present structure, and identity exists only under individuation by a frame.

Provenance is a causal relation whose attribution is inferred from the
evidence a frame can distinguish. A record can constrain that attribution
without being an intrinsic certificate of origin. Locally unique recovery
under a stated model and complete recovery of the supply of one's own
operators are different claims.

Pressed for what is compulsory, the theory keeps almost nothing. What survives every attempted removal is structure with relations among its parts; what rules running adds is that anything \emph{happens} (Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}), which is the condition of everything this account is about and is not the condition of there being anything; the specific laws above it --- inertia, closure, the shape of collapse, and the write-economics itself --- are contingent rule content rather than necessities, and not strict universals (Remark~\ref{rem:writeability}), which is the sense in which this substrate underlies groundings without dictating them.

Every mechanism used here belongs to someone else and is cited as such; the claim is the arrangement (Section~\ref{sec:scope}), and the method by which the arrangement was reached is removal rather than addition --- of primitive time, of a privileged frame, of a cost that is the same whatever runs the rules. Removals are small to state and large in consequence, because results that had been independently working around a deleted assumption stop being independent.

The account forbids one thing, and Section~\ref{sec:prohibition} states it and its formal analogue: a frame cannot get outside itself. Everything else that has looked impossible has so far turned out to be contingent rule content that some grounding happens not to instantiate --- which is a measure of how much had been filed as necessary without being checked, and is not itself evidence for the account, since the enumeration is open and this paper does not offer an open enumeration as a test (Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability}).

The regress in that prohibition has both ends, and neither is safe to state alone. A superframe dissolves the frame below's limit: holding both of two locations it has units closing across them, and quantities the subframes could not obtain are ordinary in its terms. And a superframe is a frame, so it reproduces the same blindness about its own running. Ascent buys something real and never buys everything --- the limit is dissolved locally at every step and terminated at none, which is why no amount of it yields a view from nowhere. What ascent yields is a different somewhere.
