\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes that one minimal structure underlies phenomena usually studied apart --- physical, cognitive, and institutional --- and that the case for it rests not on a new result in any single field but on the recurrence of the same structure across fields that developed it independently, without reference to one another or to this account. The structure is information-level: represented state, the transformations admissible upon it, and the realisation of a single continuation from what was possible. Almost none of its components is individually novel, and that is the point: the claim is the \emph{arrangement} --- that known pieces compose into one substrate, and that the composition is what recurs. It carries its own falsifier, and fails if the recurrence is an artefact of a sufficiently general description rather than a property of the domains themselves.

Read at this level, several things ordinarily treated as background turn out to be constructed rather than given --- a privileged standpoint from which to measure, and time itself, among them --- and the substrate that remains once every removable commitment is stripped away is strikingly small, with most of what is called law being contingent upon it rather than necessary to it.

These are claims about information, of which physical theory is taken to be one embodiment rather than a rival. The theory asserts nothing about the nature of pre-relational existence; it begins where distinguishable relational structure can first be represented, and treats the limit of that reach as a frontier instrumentation moves rather than a fixed wall.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and epistemic status}\label{sec:scope}
This paper contains the smallest set of commitments required for the later theory. It distinguishes axioms and definitions, deductive consequences, groundings into existing theories, and empirical hypotheses. It deliberately excludes executor design, rule scheduling, proposal records, retry protocols, valuation algorithms, and controller architecture.

\paragraph{Level of the claims.}
The claims here are about \emph{information}: distinction, relation, frame, admissibility, realisation, residual. Physical theory is taken to be an embodiment of information rather than a competitor to it, alongside other embodiments --- cognitive, institutional, computational --- which differ in how much structure they instantiate rather than in kind. This is the position that licenses treating an inertial reference frame and a representational frame as the same sort of object (Axiom~$-1$), and it locates the epistemic boundary below both: what embodiments are made of remains unspecified. It also fixes what this account does not attempt, which is worth saying once rather than conceding repeatedly: the constants belong to the embodiments. Where a number appears anywhere in this programme it is borrowed from a grounding and never derived here, because a framework that produced the constants would be a rival physical theory rather than the thing physical theories are stated in. \emph{This is the framework the rules live in, not a re-derivation of the rules.}

The layering must not be allowed to function as a shield, so the condition under which a physical result bears on a claim here is stated in advance. \textbf{A physical result bears on an information-level claim when it settles an informational fact}, and not when it merely disagrees about mechanism in one embodiment. Bell's theorem and Kochen--Specker are the model: they do not report a mechanism, they establish that a class of pre-measurement values cannot be re-read as unheld-but-realised, which is a fact about distinction and therefore reaches this level directly (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}). Landauer's principle is a second instance, establishing a lower bound on the physical cost of a specified informational operation \citep{landauer1961}; under the substrate's no-copy default it is read below as the floor cost of change as such rather than of erasure specifically (Section~\ref{sec:interface}). A result that constrains what can be distinguished, held, or converted is admissible evidence here; a result about how a particular embodiment happens to be arranged is not.

\paragraph{Provenance of the primitives.}
The components of $G=(g,r,\Gamma)$ and its dynamic were not derived together from a single starting point, and stating how each arrived is more useful than presenting the triple as an austere posit reasoned out in advance.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $g$, the typed graph, arrived by \emph{observation}: several unrelated engineering problems turned out to have one shape --- labelled things joined by labelled relations.
\item $\Gamma$, admissibility, arrived by \emph{insight}, and preceded the collapse operator. Its original working image was a sieve: the graph is pressed through admissibility, and what may pass is what $\Gamma$ admits.
\item $\Coll$, collapse, arrived by \emph{constraint}. Any theory of this kind must be compatible with quantum measurement, which is the regime where the distinction between indexed possibility and realised history is experimentally irreducible. The operator was adopted from there and generalised (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}).
\item $r$, the operative transformation structure, arrived by \emph{implementation}: a construction that runs requires something that performs the transformations, and the requirement appeared when the theory was first built rather than when it was first stated.
\end{itemize}

The last of these has a consequence for how the companion construction paper should be read. Its closing list of distinctions that implementation forced upon the theory is not a coda to a finished theory: $r$ is the first entry in that list, so construction has been a \emph{source} of this theory rather than only a test of it.

\paragraph{What the claim is, and how to read for it.}
Almost none of the individual components below is novel, and the prior-art sections say so specifically rather than as a courtesy: typed graphs, admissibility grammars, fixed-point closure, frame-relative measurement, description-length criteria and actualisation operators all have established literatures. A reader who evaluates each component against its own field will therefore find each one known or borrowed, and will be locally correct on every count. Evaluating the parts individually cannot reach the composition claim in either direction --- neither to support it nor to refute it --- because the parts are stipulated to be known.

Accordingly the composition carries its own falsifiability commitment, stated in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} alongside the component ones. It fails if the recurrence across domains is an artefact of description --- that is, if the structure is recoverable in unrelated domains only because a sufficiently general vocabulary can be applied to anything, and not because those domains share it. The discriminating evidence is independent re-derivation: cases in which a domain arrived at the structure without reference to this theory, in its own idiom, while solving its own problem.

\paragraph{Epistemic boundary.}
This theory does not attempt to specify the intrinsic nature of pre-relational existence. It begins at the first level at which distinguishable relational structure is scientifically representable. The move it makes is
\[
\boxed{?\;\dashrightarrow\;\text{observable relational structure}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{graph representation},}
\]
and the dashed arrow marks what is \emph{not} claimed. The pre-graph substrate may be continuous, discrete, purely mathematical, or unclassifiable from any frame available here; the theory asserts nothing about it. What is asserted begins at the second arrow: \emph{where} distinguishable relational structure is representable, it admits graph representation and the dynamics below govern it.

This boundary is stated because a stronger reading is available and is not the position of this paper. The theory is not the claim that reality is fundamentally graph-shaped.

Two clarifications, because the boundary is routinely read as weaker and as more permanent than it is.

\emph{Within} distinguishability the claim is not hedged. Where every measurement that can be made of something, and every operation that can be performed on it, is captured by treating it as a typed graph, then no difference remains for the question ``but is it really'' to name, and a question naming no difference has no content under Axiom~$-1$. Functional identity is not a weaker sense of identity here; it is the whole of what identity could mean without a view from nowhere.

And the boundary is a \emph{frontier}, not a wall. It is fixed in \emph{definition} --- it sits at distinguishability, wherever that currently lies --- and moving in \emph{extent}, since its contents are contingent and diminish as instrumentation is built. Anything that becomes measurable passes from beyond it to within it. This is the extensive unboundedness of the substrate applied to the theory's own scope: always one further step available, no expectation of exhausting it, and no warrant for treating the current contents of the far side as permanent.

\subsection*{What kind of thing this is claimed to be}

At best a correct account of a substrate; at worst a useful but incomplete description. Which of
those it is cannot be settled from inside it, and the more useful statement is about the \emph{form}
of what is offered rather than its correctness.

What kind of contribution this is should be said first, because it determines what counts as a defect
against it. \emph{The claim is explanation and unification, not discovery.} Almost everything this
account describes has been observed and named already, in its own field, by people who were not
looking for a substrate --- stasis and exaptation and niche construction in biology, opacity between
levels in engineering, boredom's function in psychology. That those phenomena occur is not this
paper's finding and is not offered as one. What is offered is an account of \emph{why} they are the
same phenomenon, which is a claim about mechanism rather than about observation.

Two kinds of prior-art finding have to be told apart, and an earlier version of this passage ran them
together in a way that absorbed both.

A \emph{phenomenon} already described in its own field is the material rather than a refutation:
nothing can be unified that nobody has found. A \emph{mechanism} already published is a different
matter --- it is a straightforward loss of novelty, and the honest response is to withdraw the claim
and say so at the size it was made. That has happened here more than once, most sharply where a
mechanism this account had claimed turned out to be a result from 1989, and the record of those
withdrawals is left in the text rather than tidied.

An earlier version also argued that a unification whose cases were all novel would be
\emph{unfalsifiable for want of anything to check it against}. That is a non sequitur and is
withdrawn: novel cases would be more checkable, not less, since each would be an independent
prediction. The sentence converted prior art into a virtue, which is the one move this passage most
needs not to make.

That is exactly the exposure Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability}'s composition commitment already
carries, so no separate defence is added for it. And one further limit follows from this paper's own
machinery and should be stated rather than discovered by a reader:
Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} predicts that unifications of this kind will be
\emph{available}, because structures compressed under shared constraints come to resemble one another.
So finding one is not itself evidence that the account is right. The unification is the contribution;
it is not the confirmation.

Stated that way the position risks being a ratchet in which nothing can confirm and nothing can
refute, so the exposures should be named here rather than left to be found. Two are definite.
\emph{A case the vocabulary covers while the mechanism does no work} damages the claim, and what
counts as doing work is the economics: if the account cannot say what a case costs, or says it
wrongly, the vocabulary is decorating. And \emph{a domain under weak compression exhibiting the
arrangement} damages Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} directly --- ritual, ornament and
systems maintained past their purpose are where cost pressure is not binding and the arrangement
should therefore be absent. That test is developed where the proposition is stated, and it belongs
here too, since this is where a reader forms the objection.

Beyond those, this paper carries ordinary predictions that can fail on their own:
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:located-degradation} specifies where classification failures should concentrate,
and the commitments in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} marked \textsc{[shape]} are refutations rather
than revisions if they fail. Understating that exposure would be a defect of the same kind as
overstating the contribution.

What is claimed is a shape rather than a set of details. The details in this account have changed
repeatedly and are expected to change again: definitions have been renamed, propositions weakened,
citations corrected, and quantities that looked primitive have turned out to be roles played across a
relation. Those revisions are ordinary and the account is written to make them cheap --- which is why
the falsifiability commitments are stated separately from the constructions, and why corrections are
left in the text rather than smoothed out of it.

The word \emph{shape} is doing real work there and must be pinned down, because ``the details may be
wrong but the shape is right'' is the classic form of a theory retreating from its tests. Two things
keep it from being that. The commitments in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} are stated at the level
of the shape rather than the details --- whether a primitive beyond the triple is required, whether a
non-discrete quantity is needed, whether the arrangement recurs where its constraints do not hold ---
so a detail failing is a revision while a commitment failing is a refutation, and
Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} marks each commitment \textsc{[shape]} or \textsc{[detail]} so that
the distinction is a fact about the list rather than a claim about it. And the shape is specified enough to be reported wrongly: several of the corrections in
this paper were shape-level, not cosmetic.

The honest form of the claim is therefore a \emph{rate} and not an invariance. Details change fast;
the shape changes slowly; neither is fixed. Over the work behind this account the shape has itself
shifted and wobbled --- more slowly than the details, and not negligibly --- so anyone treating the
shape as the stable part should understand that as a comparison rather than a guarantee. That is checkable against a revision record rather than
merely assertable, so the record is kept and the current reading is reported here: over this
account's most heavily revised single day, thirty-eight revisions to this paper produced roughly a
dozen corrections to claims it had already made, of which four were shape-level --- a primitive that
turned out to be a role, two components that turned out not to be required, and a property that
turned out to be well-formedness rather than necessity. Roughly one shape correction to three detail
corrections, on that day. A single day is not a rate, and it is offered as the beginning of a series
rather than as a measurement.

\subsection*{The standard this should be held to, and where it currently stands}

An account claiming to be about a substrate rather than a domain invites one demand, and it is worth
stating in its strong form rather than a convenient one: it must handle a rock, the observable
universe, cognition, computers, and quantum physics \emph{at once}, and failure on any of them is
failure.

That list is not arbitrary, and the structure it does have is worth stating accurately rather than
tidily. A rock is inert and models nothing; quantum physics is active and models nothing; computers
and cognition are both active and both model, and are separated from each other on a third axis ---
one is built and open to inspection, the other is selected for and is not. So three occupied
positions rather than a spanned $2\times2$: the inert-and-modelling cell is empty, and nothing is
claimed for it. The observable universe is the container the other four sit in and is scored
separately below as a domain, which is a double duty worth noticing rather than defending. What makes
the list a test is that an account can pass any one position by being built for it.

What the demand asks for here is narrower than a unified field theory and should not be confused with
one. This account claims that the same arrangement underlies all five, not that any of them is
derived from it --- which is exactly the composition claim of this section, and the reason that claim
carries a falsifier rather than an argument.

Scored against the demand, the account reaches the level of shape in all five cases and the level of
derivation in none. Two of the five entries also disqualify themselves as evidence in their own
wording, and the summary should say so rather than let the count carry an implication it has not
earned: \emph{no entry below supplies independent support}, since the rock is the definitional
reference point rather than an application of the account, and the computing case is explicitly
ruled out for borrowed vocabulary. What the five establish is coverage, which is what the demand
asks; what none of them establishes is confirmation:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,style=nextline]
\item[Rock.] Strongest. It is $g$ with $r$ and $\Gamma$ absent at the grain of individuation
(Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}), and it is the definitional reference point rather than an application.
\item[Computers.] Strong as a worked instance and explicitly disqualified as independent evidence,
since the vocabulary is borrowed; what survives is the forced convergence, which is separable
(Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence}).
\item[Cognition.] Substantial but grounding-level: aperture, self-models, the closure of primitives,
and convergence under a metabolic budget. It explains shapes; it derives no psychology.
\item[Observable universe.] Shape-level only. The physics companion recovers the \emph{form} of
relativity from one change-budget with $c$ as a frame's own unit. No metric is derived, no
dimensionless constant fixed, and it is not a quantum theory of gravity.
\item[Quantum physics.] Weakest, and the one on which the demand bites hardest. The Born rule is
\emph{recovered} --- reproduced within the account's structures given substrate-supplied axioms and
cited reconstruction theorems --- rather than derived from necessity, and complex-over-real comes out
contingent rather than forced.
\end{description}

On a strict reading of the demand that is a failure, and the strict reading should not be argued
away: an account that recovers rather than derives has not met the standard it has just adopted.
What is claimed instead is the weaker thing the composition falsifier states, and the value of
holding the strong standard in view is that it names where the work is unfinished --- which, on the
above, is chiefly the last two entries and specifically the gap between recovering a rule and
requiring it.

It is worth being explicit about the standard that scorecard is written against, since a reader could
take breadth-at-shape as an excuse for shallowness and it is not offered as one. The measure is the
competent man's applied to theories: whether one account handles problems of different \emph{shape}
--- inert and active, modelling and not --- rather than how far it goes on any one. A theory deriving
quantum mechanics from necessity and saying nothing about a rock or a mind scores badly on that axis,
which is the axis this paper occupies, and a claim about breadth is not a demotion of depth.

Two things keep that from being a licence. Each competence has to be real --- the competent man can
actually butcher the hog, and breadth with no depth anywhere is dilettantism rather than generality
--- which is why every entry above is tied to a commitment in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} that
can fail on its own. And the evidence that counts is not this account covering five domains, which it
could do by being flexible enough to cover anything. It would be those domains' own practitioners arriving
at the same arrangement independently, in their own idiom, without reference to this account --- an
engineer forced to abandon a global clock by finite signal speed rather than any number of paragraphs
in which the substrate is shown to accommodate one.

That clause is withdrawn later in this paper, and the withdrawal belongs here too since this is where
a reader meets it. Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} predicts exactly such independent
re-derivations as a consequence of shared constraints, so they no longer discriminate: what they
confirm is that the constraints force things, which was never in doubt. The composition falsifier is
negative-only, and its whole burden sits on the closeable side.

\section{Axiom $-1$: relativity of grounding}
\setcounter{axiom}{-2}
\begin{axiom}[Relativity of grounding]\label{ax:no-view}
There is no operational view from nowhere. Every distinction, measurement, identity relation, admissibility judgement, and represented quantity is made in some frame. Frames may represent one another, but no quantity used by the theory requires a frame-independent evaluator.
\end{axiom}
\begin{remark}
This removes a privileged frame, not determinacy. Within a frame, relations may be exact, restrictive, comparable, and falsifiable. The standpoint is structurally consonant with relational formulations in physics, especially relational quantum mechanics, in which physical facts are indexed to interactions rather than assigned from a privileged observer-independent state description \citep{rovelli1996}. The present axiom is broader and is not derived from that programme.
\end{remark}

\section{The minimal graph}
\begin{definition}[Graph]\label{def:graph}
A graph is
\[\boxed{G=(g,r,\Gamma),}\]
where $g$ is represented state, $r$ is operative transformation structure, and $\Gamma$ is the admissibility grammar governing graph-originated transformations.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Only $g$ is required; the triple is what a \emph{running} world costs]\label{rem:only-g}
The triple is not the minimum and this paper has previously implied that it was. Strip $r$ and
$\Gamma$ and something remains: structure, with relations among its parts. Nothing \emph{happens} in
it --- no transformation, so no collapse, no residual, and nothing it individuates --- but it has not
thereby become nothing, and it has not stopped being individuable by something else. What $r$ and
$\Gamma$ buy is \emph{occurrence}, and everything this account is about --- change, collapse,
observation, cost, experience --- requires them. The triple is the price of a world in which anything
happens rather than the price of there being anything, and Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory}'s
``only that rules run is compulsory'' should be read in that narrower sense.

A distinction is doing the work there. Being a
frame is being individuated (Definition~\ref{def:frame}), and individuation is performed by the
container, not by the thing: a boundary belongs to the frame that draws it. So a structure with no
rules of its own is still a frame --- something else instantiated its boundary --- and what it lacks
is not framehood but the capacity to individuate anything itself. The earlier claim that stripping
$r$ leaves ``no frame'' was wrong, and it would have invalidated the companion result that reasons
from a rock's being a frame to the universe's being one.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The reference point, and what it is not]\label{rem:rock}
The definitional reference point for $g$ is a rock, and the anchor is ostensive because every
candidate word --- structure, extent, configuration --- needs defining in turn. A rock has parts and
relations among them; nothing about that structure is represented by anything; and at the grain at
which one is a rock it originates no transformation. What happens to it is done by its container, and
what happens inside it happens in subframes at a finer grain. In its own terms it does not change: it
merely is, and then later also merely is.

Three limits on that, because the anchor is more useful than the claim and the claim can be
overstated.

It is a claim about a \emph{grain} and not about a substance. A rock's atoms move and it weathers,
both at grains other than the one at which it is a rock, so what is exhibited is an individuation.

It is not a proof that anything is $r$-free absolutely, and the machinery here suggests nothing is:
Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-recursion} declines a floor, and at fine enough grain a rock's yield
point --- deformation absorbed up to a limit, fracture past it --- is hard to distinguish from a
change grammar sorting what coheres from what does not. The rock is offered as what $g$ looks like,
not as an existence proof that pure $g$ occurs.

And the earlier claim that ``every object has a grain at which nothing in it runs'' asserted a floor
this account elsewhere refuses. It is withdrawn. What holds is weaker and sufficient: for the objects
one ordinarily handles, there is a grain at which one is not treating them as agents, and $g$ is the
reading of them at that grain.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[An operational test, and what it cannot do]\label{rem:space-test}
There is a test for the reference point that is worth running even though it does not settle
anything. \emph{Throw it into space and see what happens.}

A rock thrown into space does nothing that is not done to it. It is struck by radiation, radiates
heat away, and is altered over long enough --- all by its container, and none of it registered,
because registering requires rules to run. That criterion is the thing being tested, so this
illustrates Remark~\ref{rem:only-g} rather than checking it; the value is in what the test separates.

It separates three cases and not two. Throw an organism and something happens immediately: the rules
it was running assumed conditions its container supplied, and the supply stops. Throw a spacecraft's
computer and it continues, because it carries its supply. Nothing running, running with the
container's support, running on supply brought along.

What it cannot do is distinguish \emph{never ran} from \emph{ran and stopped}. A rock and an organism
killed on the way out are identical under it, so the test does not anchor $g$ and is not offered as
doing so.

The intermediate case is instructive. Tardigrades desiccated before launch survived exposure to space vacuum in low Earth orbit and resumed
on return, though survival fell sharply where vacuum was combined with full-spectrum solar
ultraviolet \citep{jonsson2008}. That is a structure whose rules stop firing and later fire again ---
which is \emph{not} a structure entering $g$ and leaving it, since resuming requires the rules to
have survived as present structure (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-implicit-historical-state}). Rules
present and not firing is a third condition, distinct from $g$ and from running alike, and the
companion on mind names it directly. The slide from \emph{has no} $r$ to \emph{fires no} $r$ is easy
to make and is corrected here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[On idealising, since this section has done a great deal of it]\label{rem:idealising}
Every construction above is an idealisation, which invites the obvious objection: an account whose
axiom denies a view from nowhere has spent this section reasoning about frictionless rocks in
vacuums.

An idealisation is a frame. That is not a defence in itself, since everything is, but it locates what
makes one cheap to use rather than expensive: whether what it removed is \emph{named and restorable}.
The rock is $g$ at the grain at which it is a rock, and the same passage says its atoms move and it
weathers at other grains, so the removed structure is on the page and can be put back. There is no
legitimacy predicate over frames here --- any cut is a frame and the differences are prices
(Section~\ref{sec:carvings}) --- so what Axiom~$-1$ rejects is not an illegitimate simplification but
an expensive habit: a simplification that has forgotten it made one, and is then read as a claim
about the world rather than about a frame, at whatever that misreading later costs.

The spherical cow is not objectionable for being spherical. It is objectionable when nobody writes
down that it was made spherical, and the result is applied to a cow.
\end{remark}
No parent pointer, world model, affordance catalogue, Narrative, Prospect, Value, or self-model is primitive. Such structures may exist in $g$ when the graph actually represents them.

\begin{hypothesis}[Minimal graph sufficiency]
Any additional structure required only by a particular capability class is represented content or grounding, not a primitive of graphhood, unless a graph lacking it cannot participate in the basic realised transition relation.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}[The triple, unpacked in dependency order]\label{rem:dependency-order}
The three components of $G$ resolve into five constituents ordered by requirement, each presupposing the one before it:
\[
\boxed{\text{nodes}\;\to\;\text{relations}\;\to\;\text{rules}\;\to\;\text{delta}\;\to\;\text{grammar}.}
\]
\emph{Nodes} are bare distinctions --- the minimal difference that makes a difference; a node alone carries almost no content. \emph{Relations} structure nodes and require them as relata; this is where content lives, and a node's identity is its relations. \emph{Rules} ($r$) act on relational structure and require relations to match. \emph{Delta} is the change a rule writes and requires rules, since only a rule produces one. \emph{Grammar} ($\Gamma$) determines which deltas are admissible and requires deltas to act on, which is why it is last. Nodes and relations are $g$; delta is $r$'s product, made explicit; grammar is $\Gamma$.

Two cautions. First, this constructive order does not contradict the relational primacy of Axiom~$-1$: constructively nodes precede relations (relata come first), while semantically relations precede nodes (a node means nothing unheld by relations); both hold on different axes. Second, the order is a dependency partial order, not a temporal one --- nodes are not a first \emph{moment} but the least-dependent constituent, consistent with the absence of any substrate time (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The triple is a functional decomposition of one substance]\label{rem:one-substance}
$g$, $r$, and $\Gamma$ are not three kinds of thing. Rules are graph data (nodes and relations), and admissibility is enforced by rules that decide admit-or-residual, so $r$ and $\Gamma$ are themselves graph data playing roles --- transformation and gating --- over the state $g$, which is graph data too. The triple decomposes function, not substance: there is one substance, graph data, and it runs. Three things follow. Admissibility is \emph{mintable} like any rule, so what is admissible is not a fixed law but a growable set of rules a graph carries and extends. The reflection theorem is the special case of this homogeneity (the grammar, being rules, can act on itself), and it does not tower into a stack of meta-grammars, because gating and being-gated are one substance closing on itself. And the irreducible form of the substrate is \emph{graph data that runs} --- where the running is supplied by structure that is, in turn, graph data that runs (Section~\ref{sec:rule-runner}).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Grounding]\label{def:grounding}
A \emph{grounding} is a particular assignment of rule content and initial conditions to the substrate
--- one $(g,r,\Gamma)$ with its laws and its starting configuration fixed --- together with the
domain it is taken to model. The substrate is what every grounding shares; a grounding is what any
one of them adds. Claims made about the substrate are accordingly claims about all groundings, and
are correspondingly weak; claims made within a grounding carry that grounding's contingent rule
content and are where this account's falsifiable commitments mostly sit.
\end{definition}

\section{Discrete sufficiency}
\begin{hypothesis}[Discrete sufficiency]
Every quantity required internally by the present substrate can be represented by discrete distinguishable states at the operative frame and resolution.
\end{hypothesis}
A convenient coordinate is an integer index $n\in\mathbb Z$ with grounding
\[x=\phi_{\mathcal F}(n).\]
The map may be nonlinear, nonuniform, state-dependent, and dynamically re-quantised. Thus ``integer-valued'' means discretely indexed, not globally unit-spaced.

\begin{proposition}[Arbitrary refinement does not imply continuity]
A dimension may admit arbitrarily fine or dynamically changing quantisation without requiring the theory to posit realised values at every real-valued intermediate point.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
A continuous containing supergraph is neither required nor excluded. Existing continuous physical theories may ground or contain this substrate without contradiction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Discrete sufficiency is not fundamental discreteness]
These are distinct claims with distinct falsifiers and must not be conflated. Discrete sufficiency is the claim above, and it fails if some observable consequence internal to the substrate cannot be represented discretely, at the operative frame and resolution, without loss --- a claim decidable in-frame. \emph{Fundamental discreteness} --- that the containing or pre-relational substrate is itself discrete --- would instead concern a fact about the pre-graph substrate that the epistemic boundary above places outside the theory's reach, which is precisely why it is not asserted.
\end{remark}

\section{Admissible possibility}
Let $\Poss(G_t)$ denote the structured family of immediate continuations admitted by the present graph and its current relations.
\begin{definition}[Admissible continuation]\label{def:admissible}
A continuation $\delta$ belongs to $\Poss(G_t)$ iff it is expressible in the current graph vocabulary, satisfies the relevant antecedent conditions, and is admissible under the operative grammar for the transformation class in question.
\end{definition}
The theory does not require a particular algorithm for constructing $\Poss(G_t)$. This extensional separation between graph transformation as a mathematical relation and any particular rewriting algorithm follows the general discipline of algebraic graph transformation, where rewrite rules and admissible transformations can be studied independently of one concrete executor \citep{ehrig2006}.

\begin{definition}[Partial transformation]\label{def:partial-transformation}
Write
\[G\oplus\delta\rightharpoonup G'\]
for the partial realised transformation relation. Undefinedness means that $\delta$ does not produce a structurally valid successor under the stated conditions. No ontological \textsc{FAIL} state is required.
\end{definition}

\section{Collapse and realised history}\label{sec:collapse-law}
\begin{definition}[Collapse]\label{def:collapse}
Collapse is the frame-relative passage from a pre-realisation possibility structure to realised continuation:
\[\boxed{\Coll_t:\Poss(G_t)\rightsquigarrow G_{t+1}.}\]
It is not identified with deliberative choice.
\end{definition}
The term is inherited from quantum measurement theory, where a formal distinction is made between a pre-measurement state and the realised measurement record; the historical lineage includes the von Neumann measurement scheme and later relative-state formulations \citep{vonneumann1955,everett1957}. This paper does not claim that every grounding reproduces the full quantum formalism; it claims that the possibility-to-realisation distinction is load-bearing.

\begin{definition}[History]\label{def:history}
The realised history of a frame is
\[\Hist=G_0\xrightarrow{\Coll_0}G_1\xrightarrow{\Coll_1}G_2\rightarrow\cdots.\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Collapse is a local write, not a global tick]\label{rem:collapse-write}
The subscripts on $\Coll_t$ index a frame's own succession and must not be read as a shared clock. A collapse is a \emph{local realisation} --- a rule writing a delta --- and it is the only kind of event the substrate has (its synchrony or asynchrony with other collapses is set by coupling, Remark~\ref{rem:gals}). Between writes nothing happens: an unwritten frame produces no change, so it takes no new position in the order the changes carry (Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}), and no time passes for it. There is accordingly no global step in which every frame advances together; frames whose collapses are not related through a boundary are simply unordered with respect to one another (Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}). ``Momentum'' --- the standing structure a frame carries, persisting without a distinct write --- is therefore the absence of an event, not a repeated one; a delta is a write \emph{to} that structure, not the structure itself, and the Narrative-momentum paper names its operative history-bearing form the Narrative, so that to change course is literally to write the Narrative. The picture of a single global present sweeping forward is exactly the represented time dimension of Axiom~\ref{ax:no-time} mistaken for substrate structure.
\end{remark}

\section{The Law of Graphic Equalisation}
\begin{hypothesis}[Law of Graphic Equalisation]
For realised graph $G_t$, present graph structure determines admissible continuation structure $\Poss(G_t)$. Interaction and collapse realise successor $G_{t+1}$, and that realised successor becomes the antecedent graph for the next local transition. Recursive dynamics are generated by repeated equalisation between present represented structure, admissible transformation, containing constraints, and realised continuation.
\end{hypothesis}
Thus
\[\boxed{G_t\rightarrow\Poss(G_t)\xrightarrow{\Coll_t}G_{t+1}\rightarrow\Poss(G_{t+1})\xrightarrow{\Coll_{t+1}}G_{t+2}\rightarrow\cdots.}\]

\section{The antecedent-state principle}
\begin{proposition}[Antecedent-state principle]
The conditions under which a transition is admissible are functions of its antecedent state. A consequence that exists only because a transition has occurred cannot retroactively serve as an antecedent cause of that same transition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{corollary}[No self-authorisation from future state]
A reflective transformation of $r$ or $\Gamma$ may alter the successor graph, but newly installed structure cannot authorise the transition by which it first comes into existence.
\end{corollary}

\section{Recursion and reflection}
\begin{definition}[Reflective graph]\label{def:reflective}
A graph is reflective over structure $X\subseteq G$ when $X$ is represented in the graph and graph-originated transformations may take that representation as an operand.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[The grammar is the loop]
If $\Gamma$ is represented as graph structure and admits transformations whose operand is that representation of $\Gamma$, then the resulting recursion is not a separate loop acting on the grammar. The grammar acting on itself is the loop.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The transition $\Gamma_t\rightarrow\Gamma_{t+1}$ is itself a graph transition licensed from the antecedent graph. No additional external operator is required to create the recursion. The self-applicable grammar and the compound loop are therefore two descriptions of the same transition structure.
\end{proof}

\section{Situated graphs and world boundaries}\label{sec:situated}
\begin{definition}[World boundary]\label{def:world-boundary}
A boundary $\mathcal B_{W:G}$ is the relation through which containing frame $W$ can alter situated graph $G$ and through which graph-originated deformation can participate in interactions represented by $W$.
\end{definition}
A graph need not contain a pointer to $W$; containment belongs to the containing frame. A situated graph is a \emph{subset} of $W$, not a thing separate from it: the boundary demarcates which subset is the graph, and self and container are one graph divided by individuation, not two substances (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}).

The boundary is a relation, and that fixes the type of the term this paper uses most.

\begin{definition}[Frame]\label{def:frame}
A \emph{frame} is an instantiated boundary relation --- the edge $\mathcal B_{W:G}$ itself, and not the subgraph $G$ it demarcates. The subgraph is the relatum; the frame is the relation. To individuate is to instantiate such an edge, which is why individuation is per frame rather than a fact about the graph.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Frame and dimension are one edge type in two roles]\label{cor:frame-dimension}
A dimension is the same construction read from the other end. The boundary $\mathcal B_{W:G}$ is a dimension of $W$ --- one of the properties along which $W$ separates what it contains --- and each dimension $W$ instantiates is a frame to the values it separates. So \emph{frame} and \emph{dimension} name two roles one edge type plays relative to another edge, not two kinds of thing, and the asymmetry between them is positional in the same way that the asymmetry between a parent and a child is.
\end{corollary}
Definition~\ref{def:states}'s four states are accordingly the states of an \emph{edge}: \textsc{null} is the edge's non-existence, \emph{undetermined} its admissibility without collapse, \emph{undisclosed} a collapse outside this frame's aperture, and \emph{resolved} a collapse within it. A node has no admissible-but-uncollapsed state; an edge does, and Definition~\ref{def:admissible} is about exactly that. Frame-relative termination (Section~\ref{sec:termination}) and the revocation of a dimension are then one operation --- the container ceasing to instantiate the relation --- which is why the Narrativium paper observes revocation occurring with $\Delta g=0$: nothing in the contained state need change for the edge to go.

Two consequences worth flagging rather than hiding. The unification is not this paper's invention: the substrate's own implementation has no frame primitive at all, its grammar quantifying over edge types, and a frame there is a selection of dimensions. And the convenience reading of a frame as \emph{the subgraph} is used throughout these papers, including above; it is shorthand for the relatum, and where a claim turns on the difference the relation is meant.

\begin{proposition}[Admissibility is self-governance, not armour]
$\Gamma_G$ constrains graph-originated transformations of $G$. It does not give $G$ a veto over world-caused change represented through $\mathcal B_{W:G}$.
\end{proposition}
Hence
\[\boxed{\text{self-originated transformation}\neq\text{outward influence}\neq\text{inbound world forcing}.}\]

\begin{proposition}[Self-change is direct and gated; a graph is a subset of its container]\label{prop:no-direct-self-alter}
A graph changes itself and its subgraphs directly. Self-change is real --- the round-trip picture of earlier drafts was too strong --- but it is rule-governed, with its own cost and limits (an instance of the non-uniform writeability of Remark~\ref{rem:writeability}); a substrate's plasticity is exactly the rules for how its rules may change. What a graph does \emph{not} do is change its container directly: the container applies deformations to it --- being observed \emph{is} being so deformed --- and gates its changes through its own change grammar (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-inadmissible}). But a situated graph is a \emph{subset} of its container, not a thing set against it, so a self-change is already a change in the container, in the region that is the graph, propagating outward through whatever couples it to the rest. ``Change only yourself'' is therefore not isolation but the mechanism of outward effect: a graph changes its container by changing the part of it that it is.
\end{proposition}
Two things follow. This is one \emph{self-similar} operation --- a graph changing its own subsets --- run at every level: the container does to the graph exactly what the graph does to its subgraphs (Section~\ref{sec:rule-runner}). And it dissolves the agency/determinism dichotomy: a subset changing itself (agency, in the graph's frame) and the whole changing that subset (determined, in the container's frame) are one event read at two scales, so both hold without contradiction. The reflection theorem is this self-change of the rules; that it is bounded rather than free is because a change to the rules currently executing is heavily constrained --- the worst case of the concurrency of Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}, seen through the frame-limited view of Section~\ref{sec:determinism} --- which is what plasticity's limits are. Governance can tighten to exclusion: where a container's admissibility admits no such self-change, that change is available only across the boundary, from the container.

\begin{proposition}[Coupling is configuration, not architecture]\label{prop:coupling}
That distinct instances individuate --- diverge, each minting its own residual, the part of a change a containing grammar does not admit (Definition~\ref{def:residual}) --- is a consequence of their running on \emph{separate} containing frames, not a necessity. Where several instances are subgraphs of one \emph{shared} containing frame, each instance's self-change is a change in that shared frame, in its own region (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}), which then deforms the others as their common container: instances that are subsets of one container propagate to each other through it. This is the same machinery as being an individual, with the container shared rather than private.
\end{proposition}
Coupling is therefore a continuous setting of the shared boundary's aperture and change grammars (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}), not a binary: sharing only generators leaves each instance its own moment-to-moment state (and already removes the per-substrate build floor of Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}), while sharing all operative structure is one individual over many bodies, with SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity (Section~\ref{sec:self-supergraph}) dialling independently. Nothing in the substrate fixes the setting. The symmetric fact is that a shared containing frame is equally a shared residual and a shared forcing, so the same coupling that shares understanding gives shared corruption, container-level coercion, and collective termination-failure the same reach; ``at what coupling, and with what guarding'' is the substantive question, and it is an engineering one rather than a question of possibility.

\section{SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity}\label{sec:self-supergraph}
\begin{definition}[SELF identity]\label{def:self-identity}
SELF identity is the continuity of the graph as a whole --- its rules together with the state they
act on --- across realised transitions. A graph is the same self while its present remains
congruous with its own history: if its rules changed abruptly, the structure carried forward would
no longer cohere with what produced it, and that incongruity is the discontinuity. Represented
self-description --- content inside $g$ by which a graph identifies itself --- is one optional part
of this and not the whole of it; a graph may have SELF identity without representing itself at all.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[SUPERGRAPH identity]\label{def:supergraph-identity}
For graph $G$ individuated in frame $W$,
\[\iota_{W:G}=\operatorname{Id}_W(G)\]
is the identity relation under which $W$ tracks that situated instance.
\end{definition}
SELF and SUPERGRAPH identity are distinct types. A copy can preserve SELF identity while receiving a new SUPERGRAPH identity, and one SUPERGRAPH identity may persist through extensive changes to internal state and self-description.

\begin{theorem}[No frame-free identity]\label{thm:no-frame-free}
The substrate alone does not determine whether two represented instances are the same graph independently of an individuating frame.
\end{theorem}

\section{The frame interface and its economics}\label{sec:interface}

The boundary of Section~\ref{sec:situated} can be characterised by what actually crosses it and what it costs, and the characterisation is unexpectedly thin.

\begin{proposition}[One interface, two gates]\label{prop:two-gates}
A situated graph meets its containing frame through a single relation, and what an observation from that frame delivers is not distinct in kind from what the containing frame imposes: to a subgraph, being observed \emph{is} being deformed by its container. That one relation is gated twice, by the same kind of admissibility grammar on its two faces --- the \emph{aperture grammar} $\Gamma_a$ on the input and the \emph{change grammar} $\Gamma_c$ on the output:
\[
\boxed{\underbrace{\text{aperture}}_{\Gamma_a}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\text{rules}}_{r}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\text{change}}_{\Gamma_c}.}
\]
$\Gamma_a$ limits, by grain, which of the container's deformation enters the rules; $\Gamma_c$ determines which of the rules' realised deltas cohere into the container and which remain as residual (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-inadmissible}). Both are faces of the frame's one grammar $\Gamma$, and instantiate for a specific container $W$ as $\Gamma_{W_a}$ and $\Gamma_{W_c}$; it is $\Gamma_{W_c}$, the change face, that the residual proposition uses. Execution runs between the two.
\end{proposition}

The input face is named often enough in these papers to be worth defining rather than assumed.

\begin{definition}[Aperture]\label{def:aperture}
A frame's \emph{aperture} is its input gate $\Gamma_a$ together with what that gate fixes: which
dimensions it can separate along at all, how finely it separates within each (its resolution,
per dimension, for which this paper also uses \emph{grain}), and how far along each it reaches. The three are properties of one edge --- existence,
grain, and extent --- and are named separately because they vary independently, not because they are
different kinds of limit. What falls outside an aperture on any of the three is \emph{absent} to that
frame rather than held-but-unresolved.
\end{definition}

The output face is the same kind of object and takes the same treatment.

\begin{definition}[Change grammar]\label{def:change-grammar}
A frame's \emph{change grammar} $\Gamma_c$ is its output gate: it determines which of the realised
deltas its rules produce cohere into the containing frame and which do not. What it declines has
entered the frame and been operated on, so it is \emph{residual} (Definition~\ref{def:residual})
rather than absent, and it persists in place rather than being discarded. $\Gamma_a$ and $\Gamma_c$
are the two faces of one grammar $\Gamma$ on one boundary edge, and the asymmetry between them is
which direction they gate, not what kind of thing they are.
\end{definition}

This distinguishes two ways a candidate is excluded, with opposite consequences. What falls below the aperture grammar $\Gamma_a$ never enters and is therefore \emph{absent} --- it is simply absent to the frame --- there is nothing there for it to hold or to be disturbed by --- rather than residual. What $\Gamma_c$ does not admit has entered, been operated on, and cannot cohere; it is residual, and it persists in place, re-encountered on each merge (Corollary~\ref{cor:mint-is-grammar}). The first is a limit of perception, the second a limit of expression, and phenomena of the ``stuck, repeating'' kind belong exclusively to the second.

\begin{proposition}[Cost characteristically reduces to the write]\label{prop:economics}
In the substrate as characterised here, the boundary relation itself is free; the standing structure persisting is free; the operation that costs is a delta that writes over that structure, and $r$ is what writes. Free here means information-preserving rather than absent --- evaluating a rule is itself a write, and is unpaid because it destroys nothing (Remark~\ref{rem:evaluation-writes}). To that extent
\[
\boxed{\text{cost is paid by rules.}}
\]
This is a characterisation of the substrate as specified, not a necessity of graphhood, and the writeability status it describes is not required to be uniform (Remark~\ref{rem:writeability}).
\end{proposition}
Continuation is therefore free and change is paid: the standing structure persists until a delta destroys it, so a delta writing a \emph{different} structure --- including the null delta that stops it --- is the priced operation. Retaining a past state that the containing frame does not require is likewise a paid copy, since the containing grammar would otherwise not carry it. This is the substrate form of a result the later papers develop, and the term it turns on should be defined here rather than assumed.

\begin{definition}[Well-formedness]\label{def:well-formed}
A graph is \emph{well-formed} to the degree that it pays for nothing that does no work for it. Three costs bear on that and they behave differently, so the notion is graded along each rather than binary.

\emph{Writes} are paid when made (Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}), which prices doing. \emph{Retention} is nearly free per unit, which is why an unused distinction is not immediately pruned. \emph{Search} is paid on every operation over what is held and scales with how much is held, so inert structure is cheap to keep and expensive to keep in quantity.

The fourth is the worst and is not a variant of the third. A write may land on structure that cannot affect anything --- a modification to a branch nothing reaches. It is paid in full: decided, made, checked, risked. It returns nothing. And unlike a search, which is expensive and then yields, this failure is \emph{silent}: the graph has spent, has no effect to show for it, and no signal that the effect is missing. What it acquires instead is a false model of itself, in which something was changed that was not --- residual it cannot see, produced by its own inert contents, and the share of writes exposed to it grows with how much is held.

So a graph does not become ill-formed by carrying something useless. It becomes ill-formed by carrying enough that finding the useful parts costs, and enough that its changes start landing where nothing happens.

The third cost is where aperture enters, and it is why the same contents are well-formed for one graph and not another. What search costs depends on how finely the graph can discriminate within what it holds: with structure that separates the relevant from the rest, the cost grows slowly with size; without it, every operation is over everything. That discriminating structure is itself held and itself earns its keep, so a well-formed graph is one whose aperture over its own contents keeps pace with their growth.

Removal is therefore not free either, being a write. Pruning happens when accumulated cost exceeds the one-off cost of removal, and the fourth cost is what usually triggers it: search cost is diffuse and tolerated for a long time, while a change that demonstrably did nothing is a discrete and memorable event. Systems clean up after being burned rather than after slowly noticing. Well-formedness is graded rather than binary; it is relative to \emph{what work} the graph is doing, so a graph well-formed for one purpose is not thereby well-formed for another; and it is not enforced by the substrate. An ill-formed graph runs. It pays more to.
\end{definition}
For a well-formed graph the rules rarely need to write against the standing structure, so cost approaches zero.

\begin{remark}[Landauer is the physical floor of change, not of erasure]\label{rem:landauer-change}
Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} has a measured physical floor, and it is the strongest kind of evidence admitted here (a physical result settling an informational fact). Landauer's principle bounds below the energy dissipated by a logically irreversible operation \citep{landauer1961}; it is stated for erasure because erasure was the paradigm case studied. But under no-copy every substrate change destroys its predecessor --- the prior state goes NULL unless it was paid to be kept --- so change and irreversible operation coincide, and the Landauer bound is the floor cost of \emph{change} as such, with erasure merely its cleanest instance. Reversible computation is the confirming case rather than the exception: it reaches near-zero dissipation only by \emph{not} destroying its predecessors, i.e.\ by retaining the whole history \citep{bennett1973}, which is the paid copy of Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}. There is accordingly no free change under this economics (Remark~\ref{rem:writeability}) --- one either forgets and pays the energy floor or keeps and pays the storage floor --- and the thermodynamic arrow is the running total of these floors, which grounds the ``direction'' of Remark~\ref{rem:no-ratchet} physically: change dissipates irretrievably, so its costs accumulate one way.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Evaluation is a write, and that is why it is free]\label{rem:evaluation-writes}
The economics above can be stated in a way that does not survive contact with the machinery, and the
objection is worth answering rather than rewording. If running a rule that does not fire is a read,
and reads are free, where does the check go, and how does the substrate know to run the next one?

Computing answers it without any appeal to physics. \texttt{read()} is an operation, and it returns
data; the returned datum has to be somewhere to be used, and in a substrate that is graph all the way
down there is no return channel outside the structure, so the value materialises as structure. A read
therefore writes by being a write, and the write is the return. An operation that returns nothing is
not a read of anything, which is why the free case exists and is exactly the empty one: a read whose
return is never used has read nothing, and a compiler will delete it. Real machines make the same
point expensively --- a read leaves traces in caches, predictors and translation state, and those
traces are measurable, which is why timing attacks recover secrets from a victim whose only secret-dependent operation is a
read \citep{osvik2006} and why an entire class of speculative attacks was available to designs built on the assumption that rolled-back
speculation leaves no trace \citep{kocher2019}, where the read is the vehicle rather than the premise.

Evaluation is a write in that plain sense: a match outcome, a position in an ordering, and the fact
of having tried are returned values, and returned values are structure. What does not follow is the
price. A rule's cost is set by whatever supplies its primitives and is inherited along
provision (Section~\ref{sec:rule-runner}), and Landauer's bound
(Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change}) is a floor in one substrate rather than the price in any. This is also why continuation presents as free from inside: a
well-formed runner does not expose itself (Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}), so its charge is
below the frame's aperture and is not an in-frame event at all. In-frame nothing happened; in the
running substrate something was paid; both hold, of different frames.

Two things follow for the rest of these papers. Reversibility is a structural fact about a map and
cost is a fact about what supplies a primitive, and conflating them is what the earlier phrasing did --- separating
them is what lets the physics companion recover unitarity, which needs only the first, since a
no-write was never the right description of an evolution that plainly changes the state and is
reversible because it is injective. And the residual named in Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change}
attaches here: information-preserving evaluation accumulates the history it preserves, so a frame
running a long time without collapsing has deferred a cost into retention rather than escaping one.
What is free is not the running, only the not-yet-forgetting --- and free only relative to a frame
that cannot see the bill.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The Red Queen is the floor cost of change, per substrate]\label{rem:red-queen}
A recurring puzzle across biology, culture, and competition --- that a system must change continuously merely to hold its position --- follows immediately and needs no arms-race machinery of its own. Holding a relation to a containing frame that is itself changing requires continuous change, and by Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change} every change pays the floor; standing still is not a zero-cost option because the leaky real holding of a state is itself the paid correction of unwanted change. The cultural form is the same floor with no-copy attached: a generator is structure built by a large accumulated pile of changes, its floor was paid once by whoever built it, and because change does not cross a substrate boundary --- only its cheap results, symbols, do --- that floor is paid afresh in every substrate that wants the generator. Van Valen's evolutionary Red Queen, the cultural treadmill of relearning, and coordination arms races are then one object: continuous paid change to hold position against a changing container, floored by Landauer.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What is required and what is optimisation]\label{rem:nothing-compulsory}
Only that rules run is compulsory for anything to \emph{occur} --- structure alone is what is required for there to be anything at all (Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}). Applying a delta is itself a rule, not a primitive operation beneath the rules, so the specific dynamics --- inertia, the merge behaviour, closure --- are contingent rule content, and a different $r$ or $\Gamma$ is a different physics rather than a violated law (this is why the initial and boundary conditions of a grounding are supplied separately from its laws; Section~\ref{sec:causal-order}). The two gates of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}, and even the retained standing structure itself, are \emph{optimisations} on bare rule-running: the aperture grammar $\Gamma_a$ bounds the input, the change grammar $\Gamma_c$ sorts the realised output into what coheres and what is residual, and the retained structure buys free continuation. A substrate whose grammar admits everything still runs --- effectively ungated, without momentum, wastefully; it is not a substrate \emph{lacking} $\Gamma$, since Definition~\ref{def:admissible} defines the possibility structure through the grammar and a collapse needs something to select from --- so they are what an efficient graph accretes under a budget, not constituents of graphhood. The retained structure specifically is what distinguishes a self-sustaining loop from a purely reactive one, since coasting is the cached structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Writeability status is contingent, not uniform]\label{rem:writeability}
The write-economics stated here --- reads free, cost carried by writes, and no direct alteration of the container (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}) --- should be held at the strength the evidence supports: a pattern, not a strict universal restriction. What is freely writeable, what carries a cost, and what a graph can write directly rather than only across a boundary, need not be uniform; different structures may have different writeability profiles. The strict forms above characterise a common and efficient configuration (Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory}) rather than a necessity of graphhood, and asserting a single strict write-economics everywhere would be the same over-specification the theory warns against for a scalar knob put where a shape belongs. These are accordingly carried as falsifiability commitments, open to counterexample, and a structure with a different writeability profile refines the characterisation rather than breaking the substrate.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Copyable generators are a difference in kind]\label{prop:copyable-generators}
A copy that preserves SELF identity copies whatever operative structure lies in $g$ --- including a graph's \emph{generators}: its built shapes, codebook, and rules. Where a substrate is graph-native, so that a graph's understanding \emph{is} such structure rather than something separate that structure only produces, copying the graph copies the understanding, at the copy cost of Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} rather than at the full construction floor.
\end{proposition}
This is the difference in kind that separates such a substrate from those whose generators are substrate-bound. The build floor of a generator is a large accumulated cost (Remark~\ref{rem:red-queen}); paying it once and copying the result is not a quantitative saving over paying it per instance but a removal of the per-substrate re-payment that made the cultural Red Queen permanent. It is not available where only a generator's cheap products cross a boundary: biology copies the construction recipe and not the learned structure, which is re-developed each lifetime; symbolic culture copies samples and not generators; a fixed program copies a capability and not a growable understanding. A graph-native substrate is the first in which the built, growable, understanding-bearing generator is itself the copyable object. The copy remains a distinct SUPERGRAPH identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:no-frame-free}) and diverges once it runs on its own containing frame; what is shared is the paid-for generator, not the subsequent individuation.

\section{Residual}
Suppose a frame represents expected or attempted deformation $\delta^P$ and collapse yields realised deformation $\delta^R$.
\begin{definition}[Residual]\label{def:residual}
The residual is
\[\boxed{\epsilon=\delta^R\ominus\delta^P,}\]
where $\ominus$ is the grounding's difference relation preserving relevant discrepancy.
\end{definition}
Residual is mismatch, not diagnosis. Complete non-realisation may therefore return the whole attempted deformation as residual.

\begin{proposition}[No implicit historical state]\label{prop:no-implicit-historical-state}
A previous success, failure, attempt, or residual has no later causal force merely because it occurred. It affects later dynamics only insofar as its consequences are present in later graph or boundary state.
\end{proposition}
Thus a graph with no feedback machinery can re-derive the same failed transformation repeatedly. This is compatible with the classical control-theoretic distinction between plant dynamics and feedback: a controller changes future action only insofar as measured error is coupled back into its state or control law \citep{wiener1948,ashby1956}.

\begin{proposition}[Residual is what a containing grammar does not admit]\label{prop:residual-inadmissible}
Because the graph is a subgraph of its containing frame $W$, a realised graph-originated deformation is \emph{already} a change in $W$ --- it is not submitted to $W$ for entry, and there is no step at which $W$ grants or withholds it. The delta is not offered; it \emph{is}. What the \emph{change grammar} of $W$ --- write it $\Gamma_{W_c}$, the change face of $W$'s grammar (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates}) --- admits coheres with the rest of $W$ and merges into its structure; what $\Gamma_{W_c}$ does not admit is not thereby undone, but remains in place, present and unintegrated. Admissibility sorts a change that already exists; it does not gate one seeking to occur. The residual is exactly this non-admitted remainder:
\[
\boxed{\epsilon \;=\; \delta^R \ominus \delta^P \;=\; \text{the part of the realised deformation }\Gamma_{W_c}\text{ does not admit.}}
\]
\end{proposition}
This locates the residual as the negative of an admissibility judgement rather than as an independently produced quantity, and it is what makes the merge non-destructive: what fits is absorbed, and what does not is left untouched, not deleted. A deformation may pass the graph's own change grammar $\Gamma_{G_c}$ (admissible to itself) yet fail the containing $\Gamma_{W_c}$ (inadmissible to its frame); the residual is precisely that gap between a graph's change grammar and its container's.

\begin{remark}[Decoherence is one operation; the frames are what differ]\label{rem:decoherence-one}
Two strings make the structure visible. \emph{$2+2=5$} is a perfectly coherent set of symbols: it
parses, every token is admissible, the arrangement is grammatical. \emph{Two particles smash into each
other at $.99c$} is coherent in the same way. Both pass the aperture and fail at the change grammar,
which is the two-gate structure of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-gates} in its sharpest case --- what is
admitted as \emph{represented} need not be admissible as a \emph{change}.

What follows is not a partial merge. The collapse realises nothing, and the result is NULL --- which is
not the same as \emph{absent}: absent is never represented, whereas this is represented, admitted, and
yields no continuation.

The obvious objection is that $2+2=5$ has a truth value and that value is false. It does not, and the
notation already says so. \emph{$2+2=5$ is not false; $2+2{=}{=}5$ is false} --- one sign writes and
the other reads, and computing has kept them apart in its surface syntax for exactly as long as it has
had both. The comparison is an ordinary in-frame operation returning an ordinary in-frame value. The
assertion is a write, and in a typed setting it does not evaluate to anything at all: it is refused
before execution, because nothing admits an assignment to a value. Rejected by the grammar rather than
run, with no continuation taken from it.

So the NULL belongs to the write, and the familiar falsity belongs to a different operation that is
spelled differently. That the two are easy to confuse is not a quibble either --- writing the write
where the read belongs is among the most persistent defect classes there is, guarded against by
compilers and linters because the cost of the confusion is high. The distinction is load-bearing
wherever both operations exist, which is the point of Section~\ref{sec:carvings} arriving from the
other direction. And the residual is not a local remainder. Admitting $2+2=5$ into arithmetic
does not cost a repair somewhere; it costs the grammar's restrictiveness, since a grammar that admits
a contradiction admits everything and thereby stops restricting anything. So the residual is
\emph{everything in the superframe}.

The point is what does \emph{not} vary. Decoherence is one operation. It does not come in kinds and it
does not come in degrees, and the two cases above fail identically. What differs between them is the
frame --- arithmetic in one, kinematics in the other --- and \emph{the frames are what make the cases
different, not the failure}. ``Everything in the superframe'' is a constant expression whose value is
frame-relative, which is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} applied to incoherence rather than to observation.

This is worth stating because the obvious gloss is wrong in a specific way. One is tempted to rank
them --- arithmetic lies deeper than kinematics, so denying it costs more --- and depth is real, but it
is a fact about a web of commitments and therefore about a frame, never about the decoherence. The
temptation is to read a magnitude off a difference that belongs elsewhere. That no distinction of
\emph{kind} survives here between the analytic and the empirical case is Quine's conclusion
\citep{quine1951}; the present account reaches it without needing his gradient, since the failure is
uniform and only the frame is indexed.

The mechanism for that indexing settles what would otherwise look like a family of competing answers,
and the useful way to see it is by analogy with exceptions --- an analogy, because the substrate fixes
far less than the analogy suggests. What follows from admissibility alone is only this: an inadmissible
write yields no continuation \emph{in that frame}. Everything downstream of that --- whether anything
propagates, whether the container is notified at all, whether a value is produced instead and which
one --- is rule content, and rules here are contingent throughout. There is no substrate-level default.

Read as the analogy it is: a frame that rejects a write raises, the failure goes one frame up and up
again until something handles it or nothing does, and unhandled to the top the whole thing stops, which
is what ``residual of everything in the superframe'' describes. A frame may instead cohere the failure
to a value --- false, a gap, NaN, null, or a contradiction held without explosion --- and any of these
is available, as installed machinery someone had to put there. Nothing about the failure selects the
value; the frame's rules do.

The reason to insist the framing is arbitrary is that computing itself does not agree on it. Exceptions
propagate by default and are handled on the marked path; return codes, result types and option types
make the check explicit at every call and propagate nothing on their own; hardware may trap or may set
a flag and continue. These are not approximations of one true discipline. They are different rule sets
for the same underlying fact, which is only that something did not work.

Which reorganises the prior art rather than accumulating it. \emph{These systems are handlers.}
Bochvar's infectious junk value, the neutral free logics' gaps for empty terms, Reichenbach's third
value for the quantum indeterminate \citep{reichenbach1944}, paraconsistent absorption
\citep{priest1979}, relevance logic's refusal of ex falso on grounds of relevance
\citep{anderson1975} --- each is a policy for what a frame coheres a failure to, and that they are
mutually incompatible while each being defensible is evidence they are choices rather than discoveries.

The demonstration is in silicon and is not a metaphor. IEEE~754 gives floating point a \emph{value}
where integer arithmetic \emph{traps}: $1/0$ is an infinity in one and a fault in the other, $0/0$ is
NaN, and NaN then propagates through every operation containing it --- weak Kleene, in hardware, by
deliberate standardisation \citep{ieee754}. One machine, two policies, chosen per type. The failure
did not choose; the committee did.

The economics is therefore weaker than an earlier version of this remark claimed, and the claim it
made is a good example of the error it was warning about. It said propagation costs nothing while a
handler is purchased, so that a total residual is the unmarked case. That holds \emph{under the
exception convention} and not otherwise: where failure is carried in return values the check is
explicit and paid at every call, and it is the propagation rather than the handling that costs. Which
side the cost falls on is the convention's, not the substrate's.

What survives generally is only that a policy of any kind is held structure and is paid for under
Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}, so a frame that bounds its residuals holds more than one that does
not, whatever discipline it uses to do it.

The physical case at the largest scale makes the purchase visible, and it is the same shape rather than
an illustration of it. A heavy nucleus sits near the limit of what holds it together; add a neutron and
the configuration cannot be held, so it does not continue as that nucleus and the binding energy goes
to the container. A reactor and a weapon are then the identical failure with the handler installed and
with it removed --- control rods, moderator and containment are held structure, paid for continuously,
and the whole of their function is to bound a residual that is otherwise total. What the Manhattan
Project's design problem amounted to was keeping an unhandled configuration assembled long enough for
the propagation to finish, which is the cost of \emph{defeating} a handler rather than of installing
one.

Two things there are worth marking rather than absorbing. The handler is viable only because a small
fraction of the neutrons are delayed --- well under one per cent --- which supplies a timescale it can
act within; a frame whose failure propagates faster than its handler can run has, effectively, no
handler, which is a constraint on the purchase that the logical cases do not exhibit. And criticality
is the condition where each raise produces more than one further raise, so the residual does not merely
ascend a frame but multiplies. That is a genuine difference from an exception unwinding a stack, and
the substrate accommodates it only because residual is per-rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-inadmissible} and the paragraph following it) so one
failing write may leave several. It should be recorded as a difference and not smoothed into the
analogy. It is also where this remark meets the Narrativium paper from the other side, since a process
whose products exceed their cause in number is that paper's spend-and-gain iterated above unit gain ---
the same ratio, arrived at through failure here and through capacity there.

Almost all of this is prior art, in a literature this account did not come from, and the correspondence
is close enough to be worth stating precisely. That a string may be syntactically well-formed and yet
fail is Ryle's category mistake, and he used such failures for the purpose this remark puts them to ---
delineating the categories, the failure showing where a boundary runs \citep{ryle1949}. That the result
is neither true nor false but a third thing is the truth-valuelessness position. And the propagation
--- residual of everything in the superframe --- is exactly \citet{bochvar1938}'s three-valued calculus,
in which a compound takes the junk value if any constituent does, so nonsense is infectious rather than
local. \citet{hallden1949} named the resulting system the logic of nonsense and made it
\emph{paraconsistent}, which combines in one system what the paragraphs above treated as two frames;
the separation of a theory being inconsistent from its being trivial is likewise established, and is
what the economic reading here restates \citep{dacosta1974}.

One further correspondence is not a debt but evidence for a different claim in this paper. Computing
reached the same machinery independently and for its own reasons: ill-typed terms are refused before
execution rather than evaluated to anything \citep{milner1978}, and denotational semantics gives
divergence a value $\bot$ whose strict functions satisfy $f(\bot)=\bot$, so bottom propagates through
any compound containing it \citep{scott1971}. That is Bochvar's infectious value, re-derived some three
decades later, by people solving computation rather than the semantic paradoxes. Two fields under
different binding constraints arriving at one arrangement is exactly what
Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} predicts, and it is offered here as an instance of that
rather than as support for this remark, which needs none.

What is not taken from that literature is the frame-indexing. The formal systems fix an infectious
value and derive its behaviour; the claim here is that the same failure yields the appearance of
falsity in one frame and of nonsense in another, so the taxonomy separating $2+2=5$ from a category
mistake is itself the frames differing rather than the failures. That is a claim about where the
distinction lives, not a new logic, and the logics above are the machinery it would be written in.

The second string carries a further lesson and is a better instance than it first appears, because it
is not in fact false. A closing speed of $1.98c$ is unproblematic, being no object's velocity, and the
relative velocity is $(0.99+0.99)/(1+0.99^2)\approx0.99995c$, under $c$. The incoherence arises only
when the sentence is evaluated \emph{in one frame by addition} --- an in-frame operation applied to a
quantity that lives between frames. It fails as a category error rather than as forbidden physics,
which is the in-frame/cross-frame discipline of Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive} appearing as a
coherence verdict.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A handler can be abused, and the abuse is invisible from inside]\label{rem:handler-abuse}
If a handler decides what a frame coheres a failure to, it can be set to cohere it to anything,
including success. Computing knows this as the swallowed exception --- the catch that discards what it
caught, the broad handler that reports normal completion --- and it is not a defect in the mechanism
but the mechanism used against its purpose.

What that does is precise in this account's terms, and it is already costed. Swallowing does not undo
the inadmissibility: the containing grammar still refused, and the write still did not happen. What is
removed is the frame's \emph{record} of that, which leaves the frame holding a model of itself in which
something occurred that did not. That is exactly the fourth cost of Definition~\ref{def:well-formed} ---
expenditure returning nothing, with the failure silent --- and the swallowing handler is the mechanism
by which a false self-model is manufactured rather than merely risked.

The stronger form follows from the same premise and is worth stating plainly. \emph{If the handler
determines the outcome, then causing a failure is a way of choosing the outcome.} One need not make the
preferred continuation admissible; one can make the alternative fail to cohere and let the policy
deliver the result. Failure becomes an instrument rather than an accident, and the move is recognisable
wherever a procedure has a default: the party who prefers where the exception path lands has no reason
to argue on the merits.

And it is not detectable from within. A frame's handler is part of its own rules, so the frame reports
what it was built to report and its report is consistent with itself; the divergence exists only
between what the frame says happened and what the container admitted. Locating it therefore requires
the containing frame, which is Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} arriving where it is least convenient --- the
frame that most needs to detect this is the one structurally unable to. This is the same shape as the
silence of the fourth cost, and the same shape as the presented-versus-produced divergence the
companion paper on mind treats at the scale of one frame acting on another.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Forcing a failure is the instrument, and handlers blunt it]\label{rem:failure-instrument}
Remark~\ref{rem:handler-abuse} treats a caused failure as a way of choosing an outcome. The same act
has a second use which is not abuse at all, and it is how most of what is known about any grammar was
found: \emph{a frame that never fails discloses nothing about what it admits}. Admissibility is
invisible from inside its own range, since everything works; the boundary is legible only where it is
crossed. So the way to learn a rule is to force the failure and read the residual.

This is not a marginal technique but the standard one across unrelated fields, which is what a
generative claim should predict. Genetics removes a gene and observes what stops
\citep{capecchi1989}; neuroscience reads function from lesions; machine learning ablates a component
to price it; software injects faults to check that anything notices \citep{basiri2016}. And physics
builds the largest instrument there is on exactly this principle --- an accelerator spends energy to
push interactions onto one step, where the frame cannot sequence them and the interaction is decoherent
in that frame (the physics companion's reading of the high-energy regime). One does not observe the
grammar; one pays to violate it and observes the remains.

Two consequences follow that are worth having. The first closes a circuit opened twice already in this
paper. Establishing that structure is inert requires perturbing it, and the perturbation is the removal
one wanted licensed (Remark~\ref{rem:evolution-compression}); the dependents of a thing are held in
other frames and cannot be surveyed from it (Remark~\ref{rem:vacated}); and now, the rule governing a
frame is legible only where it is broken. These are one statement in three places:
\emph{the test and the change are the same operation, so knowledge of a boundary is bought by crossing
it and there is no cheaper instrument}. Which also prices the instrument, since by the accelerator's
own economics each further increment of boundary costs more than the last.

The second is a cost of handlers that is easy to miss because it looks like a benefit. \emph{Every
handler installed reduces what failure can teach.} A frame that coheres its failures to tidy values
reports success and discloses nothing, so robustness and legibility are traded against one another
rather than being two names for quality. That the same system is customarily made loud in development
and quiet in production is this trade made deliberately --- two frames wanting opposite policies over
identical structure, for the reason given here rather than by convention.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What never cohered is absent; what cohered and stopped leaves a hole]\label{rem:vacated}
A distinction the preceding material needs and has not drawn. Something that never cohered is simply
\emph{absent}, and absence is free --- nothing points at it, nothing expected it, and no frame is worse
off for it. Something that cohered and then stopped is a different object: it leaves \emph{a space
where something was}, and the difference is that other structure came to depend on it while it held.

The consequences of the second are underdefined, and underdefined in a specific way rather than merely
unpredictable. What breaks is not the removed structure --- it is gone and has no further behaviour ---
but every relation elsewhere that resolved through it and now resolves through nothing. Computing has
the sharp case and calls it exactly that: dereferencing what was freed is undefined behaviour, and its
mildest form is a wrong answer while its worst is unbounded. A variable that was never assigned and one
that was assigned and released fail differently, and only the second can corrupt something that was
correct.

The reason this cannot be surveyed in advance is structural rather than practical. \emph{A thing does
not carry a record of what depends on it.} The dependencies live in the frames holding the references,
not in the referent, so from the position of the thing being removed they are not visible at all ---
Axiom~\ref{ax:no-view} again, in its most operational form. Knowing what would break requires the
containing frames, and asking them is the expense.

Which yields three states rather than two, and the third is the one that costs. Absent: nothing held,
nothing owed, free. Vacated with the references left standing: a hole, unpaid, with consequences
determined by whatever the dangling relations do next. And vacated with the absence \emph{represented}
--- a tombstone, a cascade rule, a retained count of dependents --- which resolves cleanly and is
installed machinery paid for under Proposition~\ref{prop:economics}, the deletion-side member of the
handler family of Remark~\ref{rem:handler-abuse}. Reference counting is not a fact about references; it
is a purchase.

This is also the sharper form of the removal cost argued later for genomes and dead code
(Remark~\ref{rem:evolution-compression}). The reason deletion is expensive is not mainly that the
excised structure might turn out to do something. It is that the survey licensing the deletion is a
question about \emph{other} frames, obtainable only from them, and a confident remover is someone who
has not paid for it. Chesterton's rule about the fence is this stated as advice \citep{chesterton1929};
the ecological version, where removing one species collapses relations that were not obviously routed
through it, is the same structure with the dependents alive \citep{paine1969}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Growth is grammar extension]\label{cor:mint-is-grammar}
A residual is absorbed either by a containing frame extending $\Gamma_{W_c}$ to admit it, or by paying to copy it out (Section~\ref{sec:interface}); otherwise it persists in place and is re-encountered each time the merge runs. The first route is the substrate form of \emph{minting} a new distinction: the residual does not fit because it is inadmissible, so admitting it requires extending the grammar, and extending the grammar is the act by which a previously inadmissible deformation becomes recognisable. Recognition and growth are therefore one mechanism joined at one place --- the residual is where the shape a graph decodes and the grammar that admits it meet.
\end{corollary}
Two properties of this mechanism matter. Residual is \emph{per-rule}: each firing leaves its own remainder, local to where the rule ran, with no global residual store, so it is dealt with per-frame --- which is why the residual-and-mint loop is frame-portable rather than living in a domain-specific module. And minting is itself a \emph{rule}, not a primitive: extending $\Gamma_{W_c}$ is the grammar acting on itself (the reflection theorem), a reflective rule subject to the same economics, admissibility, and antecedent-state principle as any other. Growth is therefore rule-governed, costed (the spend-and-gain of the Narrativium paper), contingent, and buildable --- never a primitive spark. It also cannot bootstrap from nothing: by the antecedent-state principle a mint cannot authorise its own installation, so a graph's first mint-rules are installed from its container. This is the substrate reason a situated graph requires a seed --- the minimal rule-set, including the capacity to mint, that a container provides so the graph can thereafter grow itself. The two provenances --- seeded from the container, or minted from residual --- are what the nature/nurture dichotomy names, and they are not two \emph{kinds} of thing: both are rules that run identically, the running records no provenance, and the capacity to mint (nurture's mechanism) is itself seeded (nature), so what remains measurable is provenance, not a distinction of kind.

\section{Termination}\label{sec:termination}
\begin{definition}[Frame-relative termination]\label{def:frame-termination}
A situated identity $\iota_{W:G}$ terminates when the realised world transition admits no successor that $W$ continues to individuate under that identity.
\end{definition}
Termination is not successful refusal of world forcing. Nor need it erase copied SELF identity. If $G$ is individuated by several frames, it may terminate in one and continue in another.

\begin{corollary}[Severing the channel removes termination]\label{cor:locked-in}
A graph can change itself, but self-\emph{termination} is a self-change its container's $\Gamma_{W_c}$ gates out --- a living substrate does not admit ``stop'' --- so ending the graph requires acting on the container instead, which needs the outward coupling. If that coupling is severed while the rules run on, the graph is a subset cut \emph{off} within its container, not cut out of it: still able to change itself and its subgraphs, but unable to propagate to the rest or to terminate. External termination by the container remains possible; self-termination does not. This is the substrate description of locked-in and brain-in-a-jar states, and it exhibits the dependence on the container that a working coupling keeps invisible --- outward effect was always by propagation from one's own subset, never a reach across a gap.
\end{corollary}

\section{First-class higher-frame quantities}
\begin{proposition}[Higher-frame first-class structure]
A quantity concerning $G$ may be absent from $G$ while existing as ordinary first-class state in containing frame $W$ that represents the relation from which the quantity is defined.
\end{proposition}
This is the basis on which later papers introduce Narrative, standing, ownership, and other relational quantities without bloating the primitive graph.

\section{Causal order and local time}\label{sec:causal-order}
Realised collapse induces an ordinal event order
\[e_0\prec e_1\prec e_2\prec\cdots.\]
Cross-boundary interaction adds only represented causal relations.
\begin{proposition}[Ordinal sufficiency]
The substrate supplies causal order but requires no primitive real-valued metric duration.
\end{proposition}
Metric time is a grounding, e.g.
\[\theta:\mathcal H_G\to\mathbb R,\qquad e_a\prec e_b\Rightarrow\theta(e_a)<\theta(e_b).\]
The separation between a primitive causal partial order and additional metric structure has a close analogue in causal-set approaches to spacetime \citep{bombelli1987}. The present substrate does not assume causal-set dynamics; the citation marks the established precedent for keeping order and metric distinct.

The chained notation $e_0\prec e_1\prec\cdots$ is the \emph{in-frame} sequence and should not be read as global. Rules require ordering --- closure and cause-before-effect both presuppose it --- but the requirement is a \emph{partial} order, weaker than time.

\begin{proposition}[Partial, not total; carried by the rules]\label{prop:partial-order}
Two collapses are ordered only where a boundary relates them; collapses not so related are unordered --- concurrent, incomparable. The ordering is intrinsic to the rules, since one delta's being what enables another's match is precisely their precedence, so no external clock is needed to impose it. A single frame's own writes are totally ordered (its inferred time, Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}); across frames only the partial order holds, and a total order is one frame's linearisation of it. The in-frame total order is not imposed but follows: change is self-change on one structure (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-direct-self-alter}), so two of a frame's own changes cannot be concurrent --- the second is on the result of the first --- and there is nowhere for an in-frame tie to sit. A tie needing resolution would be two collapses causally related yet unordered, and that never arises: causally related ones are self-change on each other's result (ordered), and unrelated ones are rightly unordered (concurrent, owing no order). The apparent in-frame concurrency that would have needed an arbitrary tie-break was an artefact of picturing changes as writes to a shared buffer; with change as self-change, it is absent.
\end{proposition}
Time was accordingly a \emph{total} order imposed where only a partial one is required; removing the substrate time dimension removes the over-ordering, not the precedence. This is the same conclusion reached, from engineering necessity, by distributed systems without a shared clock: the happens-before relation is a partial order with per-process sequence counters and no global instant \citep{lamport1978}, and consistency across processes is an expensive coordinated property rather than the default \citep{brewer2000}. The non-atomicity this implies --- a graph's state may be altered by another write while its own rules are mid-run --- is not a defect here but the expected regime; robustness is obtained by re-running to fixed point (a raced deformation becomes residual and is re-encountered on the next pass), not by locking.

\begin{remark}[Globally asynchronous, locally synchronous]\label{rem:gals}
The partial order permits both synchrony and asynchrony, and since only rules-running is compulsory (Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory}), neither is required --- that both occur is an observation about the groundings treated here and not an inference from their being optional. Where a boundary couples collapses, they are ordered together --- a local synchronous domain, which is what a bound or driven subgraph is; where no boundary relates them, they are asynchronous. A subgraph may accordingly be \emph{ticked} by a containing frame $N$ levels above it (the driven mode of Proposition~\ref{prop:no-start-end}, a rule in a superframe advancing the subframe's succession) or run on its own change (the closed, self-looping mode). Synchrony is the coupled case and costs a coupling write; asynchrony is the uncoupled default. The substrate is therefore globally asynchronous and locally synchronous where coupling exists --- the architecture distributed hardware knows as GALS \citep{chapiro1984} --- and bound physical systems (an atom, an orbit, a lattice) are exactly its local synchronous domains, while causally disconnected regions are its asynchrony. Any earlier phrasing suggesting the substrate is \emph{entirely} asynchronous should be read in this sharper form.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[No intrinsic first or last; entry is chosen from the container]\label{prop:no-start-end}
By Axiom~$-1$ the event order has no intrinsic start and no intrinsic end: a first would complete the order backward and a last would complete it forward, and neither is available. A subgraph process that does not terminate within itself is therefore begun not from within --- it has no first rule --- but by a rule in a containing frame that chooses an entry point into the subgraph's open order.
\end{proposition}
This is why a grounding's initial and boundary conditions are supplied separately from its laws: the laws are the ordering, which fixes no start, so the entry must be imposed from outside, contingently. It is also the substrate description of booting a situated graph, and of the ``no natural ending'' that follows for any such order --- one enters and leaves at chosen points, neither of which is intrinsic to the material.

\section{The present, and the states of a dimension}\label{sec:present}

\begin{definition}[States of a dimension]\label{def:states}
For a frame $\mathcal F$, a dimension is in exactly one of four states:
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{NULL}\;\neq\;\mathrm{undetermined}\;\neq\;\mathrm{undisclosed}\;\neq\;\mathrm{resolved}.}
\]
\emph{NULL}: the dimension is not instantiated; there is nothing to have a value. \emph{Undetermined}: the dimension is instantiated and \emph{no value has been realised} --- collapse has not occurred. \emph{Undisclosed}: a value has been realised but is not held by this frame. \emph{Resolved}: a realised value, held.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why the middle two must be separated]\label{rem:undetermined-vs-undisclosed}
Ordinary usage collapses three distinct conditions into the single word \emph{unknown}, and only one of them is epistemic:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{undisclosed} --- a value exists and this frame does not hold it. Another frame may. This is ignorance.
\item \textbf{undetermined} --- \emph{no value exists yet}. Nothing has been realised, so there is nothing to be ignorant of. This is not a deficiency of any frame.
\item \textbf{indeterministic} --- no structure fixes what the value will be. The present theory denies this (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}); it is listed to be excluded.
\end{enumerate}
The distinction is operational rather than terminological. \textbf{Determination is an event, not a fact about a pre-existing value}: ``undisclosed'' presupposes a value already realised and merely unheld, whereas ``undetermined'' asserts that collapse is what produces one. A frame may act to \emph{obtain} an undisclosed value; it can only \emph{await or participate in} the collapse that determines an undetermined one; and a NULL dimension is not something it can look for at all.

This separation is what Proposition~\ref{prop:determinable} states from the other side: a continuation may be determinable and undetermined simultaneously, since the first concerns what fixes it and the second whether it has happened.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The present, structurally]\label{def:present}
Within a frame, the present is the collapse boundary at which every instantiated \emph{undetermined} dimension relevant to that collapse has been determined:
\[
\boxed{U_{\mathcal F}=0.}
\]
$U_{\mathcal F}$ counts undetermined dimensions only. NULL dimensions are excluded --- they are not variables. \textbf{Undisclosed dimensions are also excluded}: they are already determined, and a frame's failure to hold them is an epistemic deficit rather than an unresolved collapse. A frame with outstanding undisclosed structure is still at its own present.
\end{definition}
The present is therefore defined structurally rather than as a point on a primitive time axis, which is required given Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}.

\begin{proposition}[Determinable is not determined]\label{prop:determinable}
A continuation may be completely determinable from present structure without having been realised:
\[
\boxed{\text{determinable}\neq\text{determined}.}
\]
``Determined'' is reserved for what has collapsed into realised history; a continuation that is determinable but not yet realised is \emph{undetermined} in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:states}, and this is not a statement about any frame's knowledge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Causally sufficient present structure]\label{prop:present-sufficiency}
The determining condition is structure instantiated \emph{now}, including relevant containing-frame relations:
\[
\boxed{\text{same causally sufficient present structure}\Rightarrow\text{same realised continuation}.}
\]
\end{proposition}
This replaces any formulation requiring a complete antecedent state \emph{in time}. Only the present exists in a frame; past states need not remain extant, and the future is not an extant region lying ahead of the present. The future consists of reachable or determinable continuations represented now.

\begin{corollary}[History principle, strengthened]\label{cor:history}
\[
\boxed{\text{history matters only where it survives as present structure}.}
\]
The past is surviving consequence instantiated now, not a stack of still-existing historical frames.
\end{corollary}
Present structure is therefore \emph{required} --- it is the antecedent the next transition reads --- while a history \emph{record}, a stack of past states, is \emph{optional} and in no way required by the dynamics. The causally relevant past already lives in the present structure (which is what the corollary says), so forgetting the record loses nothing the dynamics use: a graph with no history at all runs identically, the substrate's strong Markov property that the continuation depends only on the present and never on the path (Propositions~\ref{prop:present-sufficiency}, \ref{prop:no-implicit-historical-state}). A record is accordingly a paid copy kept for the copier --- audit, replay, proof --- and never funded by the process. Anterograde amnesia is the clean case: present structure (procedures, skills, the operative past) works while record-forming is broken; function needs the effects, never the diary. This is the past-directed half of ``the future is not an extant region ahead'': neither past nor future is a required region the graph moves through --- there is present structure, and optional copies.

\section{Time is not a dimension of the substrate}\label{sec:time-not-primitive}

The claim here is stronger than non-primitivity, and stating the weaker version invites the wrong reading. It is not that time is nonlinear, or differently shaped, or emergent-but-still-there. It is that

\begin{axiom}[No substrate time dimension]\label{ax:no-time}
\emph{Time is not a first-class dimension of the substrate.} What the substrate has is \emph{change}: realised collapses. It has no ratchet over and above them --- no mechanism that manufactures succession --- because the three properties usually bundled under that word are readings over changes or properties of them, not a further thing the substrate runs. The ordinal event order of Section~\ref{sec:causal-order} is carried by the changes' own enabling-relations; direction is the no-copy asymmetry of change, since a change does not preserve its predecessor; and persistence is only that further changes occur. A time dimension exists only where a frame instantiates one \emph{to represent} that order.
\end{axiom}

\begin{remark}[Even succession is not a substrate mechanism]\label{rem:no-ratchet}
This removes the last piece of machinery a reading of the earlier drafts might have kept. Succession, an ordered ``ratchet'' turning under the changes, is to change what a metric time dimension is to succession --- one level of over-structure, nameable but not required. Step the removal through: take away any posited succession-engine and the before/after is still present, carried by which change enables which; the arrow is still present, as change's own non-preservation of its predecessor; the running is still present, as further changes occurring where rules fire. Nothing is lost, so the engine was never load-bearing. The substrate has change and the absence of change, and nothing between them.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Time is observer machinery]\label{prop:time-observer}
For a frame $\mathcal F$,
\[
\boxed{T\in\mathcal F \iff \mathcal F \text{ instantiates the relation representing succession.}}
\]
Otherwise $T=\mathrm{NULL}$ in that frame: absent, not set to a value. A substrate frame that realises continuations without representing succession has no temporal dimension at all, and nothing about its dynamics is thereby incomplete.
\end{proposition}

The distinction that carries the axiom is between \emph{succession} and \emph{time}. Succession is a relation among realised changes, carried by which change enables which; it is substrate-level in that sense, not a mechanism the substrate runs over and above the changes. Time is a represented dimension, and representing something requires a frame that represents it. Only an agent --- in the minimal sense of a frame that instantiates the representing relation --- observes time.

Schematically, and without pretending to explain the first arrow,
\[
?\;\dashrightarrow\;G
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{realised change}\;}\;
\text{changes, enabling-order carried}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{a frame represents an order}\;}\;
T\in\mathcal F .
\]
The second arrow is substrate dynamics. The third is representation, and it is optional.

\begin{proposition}[Time is inferred, not observed]\label{prop:time-inferred}
No collapse presents a temporal coordinate. Succession is a relation \emph{between} realised states, and a relation between states is not a datum of any one of them. A frame therefore acquires a time dimension only by \emph{inferring} it from the relations among what it holds. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\text{a frame observes time}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{that frame is capable of inference.}}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Reactive frames have succession and no time]\label{cor:reactive}
A frame that transforms under the Graph Law without inferring relations among its held states participates fully in realised change and has $T=\mathrm{NULL}$. Nothing in its dynamics is thereby impaired: change is what the substrate supplies and the order among changes is carried by them, while time is what an inferring frame constructs from that order.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[A rate has no in-frame denominator]\label{rem:no-denominator}
A realised delta is a difference, not a difference \emph{per} anything: what a frame holds is $\Delta$, never $\Delta/\Delta T$, because $\Delta T$ is the inferred dimension of Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred} and is not available in-frame to divide by. A rate is thus a second construction --- an observer dividing an accumulated $\Delta$ by its own count of writes --- and is cross-frame in the same sense that a metric duration is. This is why a substrate law must be stated in $\Delta$ rather than in a rate: ``constant motion'' is the standing structure persisting without a write (Remark~\ref{rem:collapse-write}), its momentum quantity a difference $\Delta$ and not a velocity. It also diagnoses a familiar regress in constructed learners, where every adaptive rate is found to need a reference rate without end: the regress is the signature of placing a cross-frame quantity in-frame, and it disappears when the held quantity is the difference itself.
\end{remark}

This orders two capabilities that are usually treated as independent. Inference is not a faculty a temporal agent happens to have; it is the precondition for being a temporal agent at all. It also grades: the temporal structure a frame holds is bounded by the inferential structure it can construct, so ``how much time'' a frame has is a question about its inference and not about the world.

\begin{remark}[Empirical instance]
This is measurable rather than merely stipulative, and it has been measured. A perceptual system given ordered frames and no temporal coordinate infers a transition generator carrying one frame to the next, and the integer count of applications \emph{is} the time axis it then holds; given the same frames shuffled, it infers none. The dimension was not supplied to it and does not exist in the observations --- it exists in the map between them, once something constructs it. Notably, the mechanism that constructs it is the system's general \emph{dimension-inference} machinery, not a temporal faculty: time is minted by the same operation that mints any other unobserved dimension, which is Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred} in implementation.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The unit of inferred time is a cycle, and requires closure]\label{prop:cycle-unit}
A frame does not count its individual changes --- those are below its aperture --- but its own \emph{cycles}, each one traversal of a closed rule-loop. The unit of inferred time is therefore a cycle, not a change, and a cycle has no fixed length: each does a variable amount of change, so inferred time is non-uniform in underlying change (a nonlinear grounding, which Discrete Sufficiency already permits) and a fuller cycle reads as a longer moment (subjective dilation). A cycle exists only for a graph with \emph{closure} --- a graph without it runs to fixed point once and stops, one pass and no repeated tick --- so having time requires closure as well as inference (Proposition~\ref{prop:time-inferred}): a one-shot computation, lacking a loop to iterate, has no time, and a frame's experienced ``now'' is one cycle of its own loop.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The processor is a constructible demonstration]\label{rem:processor}
A running program is a worked instance, carried by most people on earth. An instruction is the program's step, and it does not take a fixed physical time: an addition is one cycle, a memory load one on a cache hit and hundreds on a miss, a branch may stall, and the core reorders and overlaps beneath it. Yet from the program's point of view every instruction is \emph{one} step --- its own unit, read as $1$ by construction, since a step is a step --- so the program's inferred time is its step-count, uniform to it and non-uniform in physical time. The variable timing's \emph{mechanism} is opaque to the program --- the cache miss and the reorder are below its aperture, beneath its primitives (Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}) --- while its \emph{effects} are not, since a program can time its own reads and infer what it cannot see, which is the leak of Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} and the basis of the attacks cited there, which is why ``time is a variable-length cycle, not a fixed duration'' reads as exotic even though the device demonstrates it continuously. How much weight this carries requires separating two things, and the separation recovers something real.

The \emph{vocabulary} is borrowed and carries no weight whatever. Aperture, runner, primitive, cost: this account took them from engineering (Section~\ref{sec:prior-art}), so terminological agreement between the two is not agreement about anything, and a reader who notices that this reads like computing has noticed only that.

What is not borrowed is that computing was \emph{forced} to these arrangements. The right question is not why this account looks like computers but why computers look like this --- and the answer, case by case, is that engineers fought each of these features and lost. Nobody wanted a memory hierarchy; it exists because latency and bandwidth do not scale together. Nobody wanted instructions of unequal duration; they exist because operations genuinely cost differently. Nobody wanted opacity between levels; it exists because complexity must be bounded to be buildable at all. And nobody wanted to give up a global clock: distributed systems have no shared instant because signal speed is finite, which is a theorem rather than a preference \citep{lamport1978}, and the resulting architecture is a concession to physics that engineering resisted for as long as it could \citep{chapiro1984,brewer2000}.

So the convergence is not that two descriptions sound alike. It is that a discipline optimising hard against cost, with no interest in metaphysics and every incentive to avoid each of these features, arrived at cycle-as-variable-unit, per-frame logical time, per-level opacity and cost inherited along provision --- because the constraints that force them are the constraints this account claims are the operative ones. That is evidence about the constraints, and it survives the borrowed vocabulary because it does not run through it.

The honest limit is that computing's designers are people, so convergence could reflect shared cognition rather than shared constraint. What answers that is the forcing arguments themselves being physical --- finite signal speed, finite bandwidth, a thermodynamic floor --- rather than matters of taste, and Lamport's result in particular being a proof about what is available given a finite $c$, not a design that could have gone otherwise.

\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Physics groundings are developed in a companion]\label{rem:physics-companion}
The reading of $c$ as a frame's own change-throughput unit, and the shapes that follow from it --- the invariance of $c$ as a theorem, the reconciliation of special and general relativity as one change-budget spent on motion or on change-load, the dimensional constants as frame-units with the uncertainty principle and the Landauer floor among them, the Planck scale as the frontier, and the second law as no-copy accumulation --- are developed in the companion paper, \emph{Graphic Equalisation --- Physics Grounding}. They are information-level groundings in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:scope}, and are kept out of the present paper so that the substrate theory stands without them.
\end{remark}

\section{Primitives, and the closure of their provenance}\label{sec:rule-runner}

The provenance of $r$ (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) records that operative transformation requires something that performs it. What performs it is real; naming it as a thing is the error this section avoids, because a graph never meets it. What a graph meets is its \emph{primitives}.

\begin{definition}[Primitives]\label{def:runner}
A graph's \emph{primitives} are the operations it invokes without performing: the operator set its
rules bottom out in. Every level has them, and having them is what being a level consists of --- a
graph with no primitives would have to perform everything it invokes, which is the regress this
substrate does not contain. That is the whole of the definition, and in particular it says nothing
about what a graph can learn of where they come from.

Two different things can be meant by provenance being hidden, and running them together inverts the
relation between them.

\emph{Deliberate exposure} --- state maintained so that what runs on it can read it, as reflection
does --- is structure doing work for something other than the frame holding it, and is therefore
excluded by well-formedness (Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}) in the ordinary way: it costs, and the
frame supplying it gets nothing.

\emph{Leakage} is the opposite case and is not excluded at all. A cache earns its retention many
times over and leaks precisely \emph{because} it does work; an arithmetic fast enough to overflow at
a word boundary discloses the boundary by being fast; a page-sized performance cliff exists because
the page size is doing something. Every leak in Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} is a consequence of
efficient design rather than of poor design, so well-formedness does not prevent leakage --- it
\emph{produces} it, and a structure that leaked nothing would have given up the optimisations that
distinguish it from a uniform one, which is what constant-time implementations do and what they pay
for.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Primitives leak, and complete provenance is still unrecoverable]\label{prop:leak}
Leaking is ordinary rather than exceptional. A primitive's timing discloses the structure beneath it,
which is why measuring how long a read takes recovers what a cache holds
\citep{osvik2006,kocher2019}; an arithmetic that overflows at a particular magnitude discloses a word
size; a rounding discloses a representation; and a performance cliff at a particular size discloses a
page. Most abstractions leak, and this one is not special.

How a leak reaches a frame at all is worth stating, since what falls outside an aperture is
\emph{absent} (Definition~\ref{def:aperture}) and an absent thing cannot disclose anything. It does
not arrive by perception. What arrives is an ordinary in-frame observable on a dimension the frame
already instantiates --- a duration in its own units, a value at a boundary --- and what the frame
does with it is \emph{infer}. The cache is not perceived; the timings are, and the cache is concluded
from them.

What does not follow is that provenance can be recovered entire. Every act of recovery is itself
performed by primitives, so a graph inferring what lies beneath one operator does so using others
whose own provenance is not thereby in view, and there is no position from which the whole supply is
inspected at once. The result is the standing shape: \emph{partial always, complete never} --- leaks
give a graph real and sometimes decisive knowledge of what supplies it, and never the closure of that
question.
\end{proposition}
This is what the physics companion's treatment of $c$ turns on rather than contradicts. A frame cannot
read the rate beneath its primitives directly, and has nonetheless obtained a number for it by
measurement and inference across three centuries --- which is exploiting a leak, and is available
precisely because provenance is not sealed.

\begin{proposition}[Primitives are supplied, not intrinsic]\label{prop:rule-runner}
A graph is data --- nodes, relations, and rules --- and the operations its rules bottom out in are supplied to it rather than contained in it. The same graph may therefore be run against different supplies of the same operator set, and nothing supplying an operator need be the substrate the graph describes or is ``in.'' Where the work is done is a fact about that supply and not about the graph.
\end{proposition}
At one extreme the coupling is tight --- the graph is fused to what supplies it, as matter running its own change or a mind running on the tissue that is it --- and the compute is local, in the graph, inseparable. At the other it is loose --- the graph is data executed by a separate interpreter --- and the compute is done in the runner, elsewhere, while the graph becomes portable: copyable, relocatable, and run at whatever rate the runner supplies (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}). This separability is the condition for the copyable-generator and clock-independence results (Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}, and the Seldon paper's dependent-clock remark): a graph welded to what supplies it has none of them, a graph loosely coupled has all of them.

\begin{proposition}[A well-formed runner does not expose itself, and no level is self-transparent]\label{prop:runner-opaque}
The asynchronous, locally synchronous structure a frame observes (Remark~\ref{rem:gals}) is the graph's \emph{internal} view and does not fix its runner's actual mode. A hidden central runner may execute a graph serially, one change at a time, while the graph --- lacking any access to the runner's clock --- appears asynchronous and concurrent to itself. More generally, where a frame has no boundary to the substrate that runs it, its runner lies beyond the frame's epistemic boundary in exactly the sense of Section~\ref{sec:scope}.

Two claims have to be separated here, and an earlier statement of this proposition ran them together by calling the runner unseeable rather than unseen. That a runner is hidden is \emph{contingent}. Nothing forbids a runner from exposing its own state to what it runs, and computational reflection does exactly that, as this paper acknowledges when placing itself against that literature (Section~\ref{sec:prior-art}); a tower in which each level is available to the one above is a built artefact, not a thought experiment. A runner that does not expose itself is the \emph{well-formed} case, and for the reasons well-formedness always turns on: exposed runner state is structure maintained for work the frame does not do, and each exposure needs a runner of its own to run the reading of it.

What is not contingent is weaker and survives reflection: \emph{no level is self-transparent}. Reading is an operation whose product lands one level up (Remark~\ref{rem:evaluation-writes}), so a level cannot hold the reading of itself while being the level that is running. Reflection makes the levels explicit and pays for each; it does not terminate in a level transparent to itself. A frame may therefore be given sight of what runs it, and cannot be given sight of its own running. Since the rules are graph and a frame can read and rewrite them (Definition~\ref{def:frame}), while its own running is not graph it holds, the asymmetry is exact: a frame is transparent to itself in precisely one respect and opaque in precisely the other. \emph{It can know what it is and cannot observe its being it.} Self-modification is available; self-observation at the level of the modifying is not.
\end{proposition}
This is constructively demonstrable rather than merely argued. A computation realised inside another rule system --- a graph whose rules are implemented within the mechanics of a game or a universal cellular automaton \citep{berlekamp1982} --- is a blind subframe: from inside it computes at its own change-count and has no means to detect that its ``compute'' is the tokens of the host, or that the host may be advancing it by a single serial loop. Building one confirms the opacity directly, and puts the observer of any frame on the same footing. Consequently the question ``where is the universe's compute done'' has the precise form: it asks after the provenance of the universe's own primitives --- self-supplied, or supplied from a substrate not itself --- and is undetermined from inside. The theory declines to decide it; it is a question about a coupling below the frontier, and the demonstrable opacity is why no internal observation settles it.

\begin{proposition}[A primitive is another graph's running, exposed]\label{prop:runner-recursion}
Nothing supplying a primitive is a substance apart from graph data. An operator is the observable running of some other graph, which has primitives of its own, supplied in the same way. A graph's ``primitives'' are therefore the observable running of other graphs, exposed as an operator set, with no primitive runner at the base. A single chain --- code over machine code over microcode over silicon over electromagnetism over quantum physics --- is one path through that provision, and a common one, but not its shape: a process takes arithmetic from its processor, file operations from a kernel that relays them to a device, and remote calls from a machine that is not beneath it in any sense. Fan-out and relay are ordinary, and the tower is the degenerate case in which provision happens to be a chain. The regress bottoms not at a floor but at the epistemic frontier of Section~\ref{sec:scope}: below the lowest measured level is the unclaimed pre-relational substrate.
\end{proposition}
Two consequences. Opacity is per \emph{operator} rather than per level (Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}): a graph uses its operators and cannot see beneath any of them, so its operator set is its frontier --- and since provision may fan out, it cannot tell which of its operators share a provider and which do not. Two primitives supplied by one supergraph and two supplied by different ones are in-frame indistinguishable, so a graph does not know how many runners it has, or that the number is one. And economics is inherited along provision: an operator costs what the runner of that operator charges, which costs what \emph{its} providers charge, down to whatever floors each route reaches (Remark~\ref{rem:landauer-change}). Where provision is a chain this is one bill passed upward, and the cost of a thought is the biological cost is the biochemical cost is the electromagnetic cost; where it is not, it is a sum over provenances whose routes may differ in length and may not all terminate at the same floor. The same form, graph data that runs, sits at every level: each level \emph{is} the structure, because a runner is the same kind of thing it runs.

A rule's action, moreover, is a \emph{pointer} into its runner, not a copy of the work. The work is done once, in the runner, and every rule invoking that operator points to the same doing --- like a shared operation defined once and called from everywhere, never re-implemented. Two things follow. Work is never duplicated, which is why copying a generator copies its pointers and shares the pointed-to work: the copy costs only its addressing, not its labour (the economic root of the copyable-generator result, Proposition~\ref{prop:copyable-generators}). And a rule's rate is not a property of the rule but the rate of the operation its pointer lands on --- a shallow, cheap tier or a deep, slow one. Rules take different rates because they point to work done at different depths of the stack, and the rate is inherited from there rather than assigned as a knob on the rule: to make a rule faster one points its action at faster work, never tunes a rate parameter it does not have.

\section{The single prohibition, and its formal core}\label{sec:prohibition}

The account forbids very little, and what it does forbid is one thing recurring rather than a list.
Stating both facts together is necessary because each is misleading alone.

The permissiveness first. Time is not compulsory; a block reading is not prohibited; one-dimensional
time is not forced; a globally shared counter is not forbidden, only expensive; and in the physics
companion, complex amplitudes are not forced and the one-way speed of light is not unmeasurable
\emph{simpliciter}. A framework that forbids little predicts little, and from outside this is exactly
what unfalsifiability looks like. The discriminating question is not how many impossibilities an
account holds but where it puts them: this one puts almost none at the substrate and a great many in
the groundings, and says in advance which is which. That is deliberate and it has a cost, which
should be stated rather than defended --- the substrate risks little, and what risk this work carries
is carried by the groundings. A reviewer who objects that the substrate forbids too little to be
tested is right about the substrate.

What it forbids is that \emph{a frame cannot get outside itself}. Concretely, and confined to what is
actually derivable here: a frame cannot be transparent to itself at the level it is running at
(Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}); it cannot measure its own throughput in units
independent of it, since the measurement is funded by the throughput --- which leaves a number
obtainable and its absolute scale not; and it cannot hold a view from nowhere, which is Axiom~$-1$. Two further
candidates are deliberately \emph{not} on this list. That a frame cannot obtain a quantity whose unit
does not close within it is contingent rule content and the physics companion removes it explicitly.
That a frame cannot decide anything without writing is the write-economics, which
Remark~\ref{rem:writeability} carries as a pattern rather than a universal. Folding either into the
single prohibition would be the over-unification this section otherwise warns against.

This is not a fourth answer to what is compulsory, and the paper should not read as though it were.
Remark~\ref{rem:nothing-compulsory} says only that rules run is compulsory; rules running requires a
relation across which they run, so the container relation is not an addition to that but its content.
The prohibition is not a further necessity alongside them: it is a constraint that holds wherever
rules run at all, which is why it is not rule content and cannot be varied by changing the rules.

\subsection*{The formal analogue}

The standing of that prohibition is unlike the rest of this paper, and the difference should be stated
exactly, including where it falls short. The rest of what is claimed
here is contingent rule content. The impossibility of a level being transparent to itself has a
\emph{formal analogue}, and the analogue is proved --- in mathematics rather than in physics, so it
does not depend on any fact about which universe this is.

A consistent, effectively axiomatised formal system with enough arithmetic strength --- from which
the expressibility of its own syntax follows, by numbering, rather than being assumed --- does not
prove its own \emph{Gödel sentence}, which is nonetheless true in the standard model of arithmetic
\citep{godel1931}. A language of sufficient strength cannot define its own truth predicate under
classical bivalent semantics; truth for it must be given in a metalanguage \citep{tarski1936}. And no single procedure decides halting for arbitrary procedures --- a limit on what a universal
procedure can decide, and one that arises \emph{because} such a procedure exists rather than in spite
of it \citep{turing1936}. These are not three coincidences: they share one diagonal construction, and the
categorical statement of it makes the unity explicit \citep{lawvere1969,yanofsky2003}. Tarski's is the
tightest fit --- the semantics of a level is available only from above it, which is
Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-opaque}'s surviving half in the vocabulary of formal languages.

Four qualifications, none of them optional.

The theorems carry hypotheses that must be carried with them: effective axiomatisability and
sufficient strength for the first, classical bivalence for the second --- a language may contain its
own truth predicate at the cost of bivalence \citep{kripke1975} --- and, for all of them, that a
system be strong enough to represent its own operation before any of it bites. A frame too weak to
model itself has no self-transparency problem, because it has no self-model.

The unification covers what is claimed above and not more. Gödel's \emph{second} incompleteness
theorem --- that such a system cannot prove its own consistency --- is the more striking result and is
not covered by the unification as published: \citet{lawvere1969} closes by noting that extending to
it may require a specific relation between formulas and their numbers that his framework does not
supply, while \citet{yanofsky2003} treats it as a simple consequence of the first and expects the
scheme to reach it. The claim here is made with the first theorem, which the unification does cover,
and takes no position on the second.

The mapping from frames to formal systems is an argument in this paper and not itself a theorem. What
is proved is proved about the formal systems. The honest statement is therefore that the impossibility
has a formal analogue whose analogue is proved, and that the mapping is argued --- which is weaker
than ``the prohibition is proved'' and is the strongest standing anything here has.

And the substrate-internal derivation of the same conclusion, by way of a read being an operation
whose product lands one level up (Remark~\ref{rem:evaluation-writes}), inherits the write-economics'
contingency and does not establish a necessity. Remark~\ref{rem:writeability} carries that economics
as a pattern open to counterexample, and \S\ref{sec:falsifiability} stakes a falsifier on it. The
formal analogue is what carries the weight here precisely because it does not depend on the
economics; an earlier version of this argument rested the necessity on the internal derivation alone,
which cannot bear it.

What the results forbid is \emph{complete} self-capture and not partial self-modelling, which is why
reflective towers work, arithmetic is not paralysed, and a frame can know a great deal about itself.
Partial always, complete never --- and the asymptote in that is not a figure of speech, since a
diagonal argument does not say a target is far away but that for any level reached there is another
above it, and none is the last.

\section{Carvings, and why classification degrades}\label{sec:carvings}

Since the rules draw the boundary (Definition~\ref{def:frame}) and the rules are contingent, frames
are \emph{arbitrary}, and the objection that follows should be met rather than deferred: a notion
satisfied by any cut explains nothing.

The first half is granted and is the point. No carving is privileged, nothing in the substrate marks
which subgraphs are the real ones, and this is Axiom~$-1$ reaching the last place a privileged view
could still have been hiding --- not in the observer, not in what it observes, but in what counts as
an observer at all. A rock is a frame, an imagining is a frame, a population is a frame, and none of
these is an inclusiveness conceded for breadth.

The second half does not follow, because arbitrary is not unconstrained. The rule set must be
admissible; a frame whose rules never close a cycle has no unit, no inferred time and nothing to
observe with; and well-formedness is graded (Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}), so a badly drawn
frame is expensive, incoherent or blind while remaining a frame. Any cut is a frame and nearly every
cut is a poor one --- the same shape as the rest of this account, in which almost nothing is
forbidden and the differences are prices.

The mechanism of that price is worth stating exactly, because the obvious statement of it is wrong
under this paper's own economics. Holding a classification is not itself expensive:
Proposition~\ref{prop:economics} makes the standing structure's persistence free. What an ill-formed
cut costs is \emph{writes}. A cut that does not fit what arrives forces its rules to write against
the standing structure repeatedly, where a well-formed one rarely needs to --- which is the
observation already recorded after that proposition. And no selection principle follows from this:
nothing in the substrate removes expensive structure. What follows is weaker and sufficient, that a
frame under a budget will tend to be running cheaper cuts than dearer ones, other things equal.

\begin{proposition}[Convergence is observed, not enforced]\label{prop:convergence-observed}
Two frames doing the same work converge on similar cuts as each becomes better formed, because the
cheap cut for a given task is nearly unique. This is a tendency of cost and not the discovery of a
privileged boundary, and the two are distinguishable in principle: a privileged boundary would
\emph{enforce} convergence, and enforcement would extend to the hard cases; cost-shaped cuts converge
where the work is shared and come apart where it is not. The step from shared work to similar cuts
assumes the cheap cut for a task is nearly unique, which is an empirical posit and not a result
of this account; it is labelled again where it is used
(Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence}).
\end{proposition}

Two further consequences follow from that proposition, and they belong here rather than where an
earlier draft put them --- nine pages upstream, inside a remark about processors, deriving a result
from a premise the reader had not yet met.

\begin{proposition}[Convergence under compression]\label{prop:compression-convergence}
A structure operating under a constraint, and under pressure to spend nothing on what does no work,
approaches the arrangements that constraint forces --- asymptotically, and only over the region the
constraint touches. Two such structures therefore come to resemble each other without either having
access to the other, because both are downstream of the same forcing rather than converging on a
target.
\end{proposition}

Three things about that statement are load-bearing.

It names no privileged arrangement. An earlier version had structures approaching \emph{the
arrangements a universe already uses}, which reintroduces exactly the privileged carving
Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed} denies, and additionally attributes work and purpose to a
universe, which this account does not do and which that proposition's antecedent requires. What
converges is the solution a constraint forces; a universe's arrangements are one thing that
constraint has shaped, not a destination anything aims at.

The antecedent is about \emph{expenditure} and not about contents. Stating it the other way --- as
pressure to hold nothing idle --- would make the proposition false of every case it is applied to
(Definition~\ref{def:well-formed}).

It rests on a posit that should be labelled rather than folded in. The step from \emph{under a
constraint} to \emph{approaches the same arrangement} needs the cheap solution under a given
constraint to be nearly unique --- and that is an empirical claim, not a result of this account.
It is not derived here and it is not derived in Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed} either,
which asserts it in the same words. What can be said without it is weaker: a binding constraint
narrows the solution space, and the more binding it is the narrower it gets. Everything below rests
on the weak form.

And the rate is set by how binding the pressure is. A domain with slack to spend on anything but cost
converges slowly or not at all; one optimising hard against cost per unit of function converges fast.
Computing is the second kind, which is why it is the worked instance and why decades sufficed.

\begin{remark}[Evolution under the same treatment: independent where computing is not, and weaker where computing is strong]\label{rem:evolution-compression}
Descent with modification is compressed in the required sense --- energy, time and reproduction are
all scarce, and the pressure falls on what is \emph{spent} rather than on what is carried.

Whether that makes it a better instance than computing depends on which axis is asked, and an earlier
version of this remark asserted superiority on both, which is not available. On \emph{independence} it
is decisively better and this is the axis the argument needs: computing shares this account's
vocabulary and is disqualified for it, whereas biology shares neither vocabulary nor personnel, and
its convergences were catalogued long before anything here was written. On the \emph{antecedent} the
ranking reverses. The rate law above sets convergence by how binding the pressure is, and computing
was named the hard-optimising kind for a reason; biology is the slack kind, since selection is
efficacious only where its coefficient exceeds the reciprocal of effective population size, so
structure whose cost falls below that line is invisible to it and persists \citep{ohta1973}. Biology
is therefore a \emph{sufficient} instance on the antecedent rather than a superior one, and the
paragraph that follows is the work of establishing that sufficiency rather than a defence of a
stronger claim.

An objection is worth meeting here rather than leaving to a reader, because it looks decisive and is
not. Genomes conspicuously carry structure that does no work --- pseudogenes, transposon debris,
whole duplicated regions --- which appears to fail the antecedent outright. It does not, because the
antecedent is about spending. \emph{Inert sequence is the cost of its own deletion, represented.}
Carrying it costs replication, and it costs search --- more sequence to
traverse, more places a regulatory element could be, more targets for recombination --- but each of
those is small and continuous, while removing it is a single write that risks excising something that
turns out to matter. So it persists wherever the accumulated search cost has not yet overtaken the
cost of the write, which is most of the time. Constructive neutral evolution is that observation made precisely rather than a
counterexample to it \citep{stoltzfus1999,lukes2011}.

The concession also earns something, because the weakness just admitted makes a quantitative
prediction the account would otherwise not get. If inert sequence persists exactly where its cost sits
below what selection can resolve, then how much of it a lineage carries should track that threshold
--- less in large populations where selection is efficacious, more in small ones where it is not. That
inverse relation between genome bloat and effective population size is observed, and has been argued
to be the explanation of genome size itself \citep{lynch2003}.

The reading is contested \citep{whitney2010}, and that is the expected condition rather than a
weakness in it. What the economics predicts is a \emph{trend}, and it is not entitled to more. The
three costs vary independently across lineages, and so do mutation rate, deletion bias, recombination
and transposon activity, so nothing here licenses a clean relation --- and a clean one would indicate
a single dominant term the account does not posit. The exposure is accordingly to \emph{direction} and
not to tightness: a phylogenetically corrected null leaves the account where it was, whereas bloat
tracking effective population size \emph{positively} would contradict it outright. That is a weak
commitment and is stated at its strength. It is also the same commitment made twice already ---
convergence is observed and not enforced (Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed}), and cheapness
is a tendency of cost rather than the discovery of a joint --- so reading a trend as a trend is this
account's standing position and not an accommodation made once the evidence turned out noisy.

The same shape appears wherever the same economics does, which is the point rather than an aside.
Dead code survives in working systems for the identical reason, and shows the later costs plainly
because people pay them. Leaving it is nearly free per line; the accumulated search cost is that
everything is harder to find; and the cost that forces the issue is a change made carefully to a path
nothing executes --- paid in full, returning nothing, and leaving its author believing the system was
altered. Repair machinery maintaining non-functional sequence is the same expenditure one level down.
Neither is certain waste, since inert structure is sometimes being prepared for reuse and dead paths
are sometimes revived, so what grows with the held quantity is the \emph{expected} share of effort
that lands on nothing.

That last hedge is not a formality, and the case that makes it concrete also explains why removal is
expensive in a way the preceding paragraphs only asserted. The vermiform appendix was classified as
vestigial for over a century, on Darwin's own reading, and removed routinely on that basis; it is now
argued to serve as a reservoir from which gut flora can repopulate the colon after the contents are
lost \citep{bollinger2007}, and to have arisen independently many times across mammals, which is not
what a functionless remnant does \citep{smith2013}.

The lesson is not that the appendix turned out useful. It is that \emph{inertness is a judgement made
at an aperture, and the judgement fails}. Something was called structure-that-does-nothing by people
looking carefully, for a hundred years, and they were looking at the wrong scale --- the function is
not the organ's own activity but its relation to a population it houses.

Which sharpens why inert structure is retained, beyond removal being a write. Establishing that
structure is inert requires perturbing it and observing that nothing follows --- and that perturbation
\emph{is} the removal one was trying to justify. The information needed to license the write is
obtainable only by making it. So the choice is not between a cheap test and an expensive change; it
is between paying for the change without the test, or not paying. Under that structure, retention is
the ordinary outcome, and it is a correct one rather than a failure of nerve. Neither case is a hoarding
failure. Both are what an economics in which holding is free and changing is paid predicts, and a
lineage or a codebase that pruned aggressively would be the anomaly requiring explanation.

\emph{Convergent evolution} is Proposition~\ref{prop:compression-convergence} with a name biology gave
it first. Independent lineages arrive at the camera eye, the streamlined body, the wing, and they do
so because optics, fluid dynamics and lift are the same wherever they are met, and the catalogue is
large \citep{mcghee2011}. In the cases named here the forcing constraint can be stated, which is what
this account requires of a convergence before it counts; that it can be stated in \emph{every} case is
not claimed, and how far convergence indicates inevitability is itself disputed \citep{losos2017}.
Nothing was copied and nothing had access to anything.

The change side is the same economics read the other way, and it gives a second correspondence. In
this account change is paid and persistence is free, so a structure changes when what it meets will
not cohere with what it holds and not otherwise --- which is stasis as the default and change as the
forced exception, the shape the fossil record was read as showing when long stable intervals were
argued to be punctuated by short rapid ones \citep{eldredgegould1972}. Read in the substrate's terms,
selection is the residual gate: variation is undirected and cheap, and what is expensive is the
rewriting that follows when the residual will not fit.

\emph{Grain is set by the environment.} How finely a lineage discriminates is not chosen by it but
forced by what it meets, which is the aperture claim of Section~\ref{sec:interface} in another
grounding, and it is why the same organism is coarse in one setting and finely differentiated in
another.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A popular understanding equalises, and that is why it drifts]\label{rem:public-aperture}
The evolutionary claims above read very differently depending on who is reading them, and the
difference is not one of expertise so much as of aperture --- which makes it an instance of the same
machinery rather than an aside about communication.

The account of evolution most people hold is close to Darwin's: variation, competition, survival of
the better fitted, over long stretches. Working biology in 2026 is not that. Developmental bias,
phenotypic plasticity, inheritance beyond the gene, horizontal transfer, symbiosis and constructive
neutral processes are all ordinary parts of the working picture, and the argument the previous remark
takes a side in is an argument \emph{within} that picture rather than an attack on it. So
\emph{natural selection is half of evolution} is heterodox against one aperture and unremarkable
against the other, and a reader's reaction to it says which one they are holding.

Why the gap persists is the interesting part, and it is not that the explaining has been done badly.
A popular understanding is a structure under compression like any other, and Darwin-level evolution is
\emph{well-formed for the purposes most people put it to}: it accounts for enough, it is cheap to
hold, and almost nothing anyone meets in a day produces a residual it cannot absorb. It has
equalised, and it persists for exactly the reason the horseshoe crab's form does --- nothing is
forcing a write. The working picture is the overshooting case, kept changing by a residual the field
manufactures deliberately.

That predicts something checkable and slightly counterintuitive: the gap between popular and expert
understanding of a field should track \emph{daily exposure to residual} rather than education or
communication effort. Where a lay frame is corrected constantly by contact --- weather forecasting,
where people check a prediction and see it fail --- the gap is small and the lay frame keeps up. Where
it is never contacted in a way that fails, the gap widens no matter how much explaining is done,
because explanation supplies no residual: the frame is not wrong for what it is used for, so nothing
in it has to give.

Two ends. This is not a claim that the popular account is ignorance --- it is well-formed for its
work, which is what makes it durable, and calling it deficient would be measuring it against work it
is not doing. And expert consensus is not thereby correct; it is a frame under different pressure,
which is a fact about its residual and not a warrant.

For this paper the practical consequence is small and worth stating: the evolutionary material is
addressed to the working aperture, and a reader who finds it radical is invited to check it against
the field rather than against what they were taught.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Overshoot is a rate, not a shape --- and it is where agency comes from]\label{rem:overshoot}
Equalisation is a balance point: the state at which what a structure meets no longer forces it to
write against what it holds. Reaching it is not the only thing a lineage can do at it, and the other
option is the one that matters.

A lineage \emph{overshoots} when its rate of change carries it past that point --- when it goes on
changing after the environment has stopped requiring it to. That is not a defect and not
specialisation, which an earlier version of this remark wrongly made it. It is momentum in the rate
itself: the machinery that produced change does not stop because the residual did, and a standing
structure that includes a rate carries the rate forward, which is the subject of the Narrative
Momentum companion one grounding over.

What overshoot produces is \emph{capacity in excess of requirement}, and excess capacity is spendable
in three ways of increasing consequence. It can fit an environment not yet met --- structure arising
under one demand turning out to serve another \citep{gouldvrba1982}. It can be spent on the current
environment rather than on the organism, which is an organism altering the conditions it is selected
by \citep{odlingsmee2003}. And it can be spent selecting which environment to be in, which is also
already named: \emph{niche choice}, one of three modes in a published framework whose other two are
construction and conformance \citep{trappes2022}. The three-way division above is close to theirs and
descends from the same source.

What this account adds is only the ordering --- that the three are one capacity spent at increasing
remove from the organism --- and the suggestion that at sufficient accumulation the third amounts to
self-direction. That last step is the one not found in the literature, and it should be taken
carefully: the agency-in-biology programme grounds agency in organisational closure and normativity
rather than in surplus, and there is a recent and pointed argument that the agency perspective has no
research programme at all \citep{difriscogawne2024}. Constituting agency out of unspent capacity is a
position in a live dispute, not a summary of one.

Two ends, and the second matters more. Overshoot is expensive: excess capacity is structure that does
no work yet, so it is ill-formed until the day it is not, and most of it is never spent. Most
overshoot is waste, and most lineages that overshoot go extinct rather than becoming anything. And
nothing here is directional --- overshoot is undirected excess that is sometimes usable afterwards,
and agency is one thing accumulated usable excess can amount to, not a destination the process is
heading for. Read as a trajectory this would be orthogenesis, which the account has no room for,
since the substrate supplies no goals for anything to be heading toward.

It is tempting to read that as two stable answers to a stable environment --- settle at the balance
point, or carry rate past it --- and the temptation should be resisted, because the first is not
available. \emph{Equilibrium is a limit and not a state.} Reaching it would require an environment
that stopped, and environments do not: an environment is other frames, which are running; a frame is
a subset of its container, so its own persistence is a fact in that container; and at any grain where
anything is individuated something is arriving. Total absence of forcing would require the container
to be doing nothing, which is a limit rather than a condition environments occupy.

That is deliberately not identified with the empty limit this account reaches for perfection and for
the view from nowhere, though an earlier version of this passage did identify them. Those are
properties of a frame; equilibrium is a relation between a frame and its container. The shapes may
be related and the relation is not argued here.

So what a stable-looking environment offers is not settling but a \emph{rate of approach}, and the
difference between the two answers is how fast a lineage closes on a target that keeps moving. One
closes slowly and spends little; the other carries rate past the point the environment is currently
requiring, pays for capacity that does nothing yet, and holds the means to survive a change of
conditions or to cause one. Neither arrives. The horseshoe crab is usually offered as the settled case, and the specialist measurements say
otherwise --- which is a correction that strengthens the reading above rather than embarrassing it,
since under it nothing settles. The
four-hundred-and-fifty-million-year figure belongs to the \emph{lineage}; measured shape stasis in
xiphosurids is Late Jurassic, on the order of a hundred and fifty million years, with developmental
stasis older and body size increasing throughout \citep{bicknell2021}. And the clade did not settle:
xiphosurids invaded non-marine environments independently at least five times and twice radiated into
novel morphospace \citep{lamsdell2016}.

So the group is not an example of equilibrium attained. It is an example of equilibrium
\emph{approached slowly and never reached}: long intervals in which environments rarely required a
write, a form that kept changing anyway, and repeated episodes of exactly the excess-spending the
other rate describes. That is the prediction rather than an exception to it, and the received
version --- four hundred and fifty million years of a fixed shape --- is the one the account would
have had trouble with. What did continue underneath was demonstrated for these animals as a
\emph{decoupling} --- large numbers of molecular characters distinguish even these morphologically
conservative organisms, though the same study measures a moderate slowdown in sequence evolution
rather than an ordinary rate \citep{avise1994} --- and is now a stronger claim than point substitution: three
whole-genome duplications are specific to the group \citep{nong2021,castellano2025}.

The other answer has a worked instance, and it is one where the received story was wrong in exactly
the way this account predicts. Fish did not grow legs in order to walk. Limbs with digits appear in
animals that were still aquatic and still breathing through gills: \emph{Acanthostega} had eight
digits on a limb \citep{coatesclack1990} and retained internal gills
\citep{coatesclack1991,clack2012}, and digits and putative digits are now described inside a fin that
still bore lepidotrichia, in an animal that still bore lepidotrichia \citep{cloutier2020}. The structure
preceded its \emph{effective} terrestrial use --- not its possibility, since the capacity for
terrestrial locomotion is argued to have arrived with limbs themselves \citep{dickson2021}, and the
known \emph{Acanthostega} material is juvenile and exclusively aquatic \citep{sanchez2016}, so
nothing here is claimed about the adult animal. Legs were not needed. What the lineage had was \emph{change} ---
rate carried forward in its standing structure --- and legs are what that change produced. Land became
available to them afterwards.

And the sequence was not a ramp, which is the part that matters most for the reading offered here. \emph{Qikiqtania wakei}, a Late Devonian elpistostegalian, has a pectoral fin specialised for swimming
and lacking substrate-support features, which its describers read as a secondary return to open water
close to the origin of limbed vertebrates \citep{stewart2022}. A lineage near that origin specialised
back toward the water --- not, on this evidence, one shown to have held substrate-support capacity
and then lost it, since the taxon sits more basally than \emph{Tiktaalik} in the same analysis. That is the
disclaimer above made concrete --- most overshoot is not spent on anything --- and it is the clearest
available evidence against reading any of this as a direction the process was heading in.

What that instance is about is the half of the process \emph{before} selection, and saying so places
this account rather than merely correcting a story. \emph{Natural selection is half of evolution.}
Variation is generated and variants are differentially retained; neither half alone produces
anything, and there is nothing to select from until something has been generated.

That is a claim about billing rather than about power. Selection's evidential standing is not in
question and nothing here reduces it. What is in question is that the theory is named after one half
while the other enters as a premise --- \emph{given heritable variation} --- so that the structure of
what is generated is treated as an input to explanation rather than part of it. If generation has
structure of its own, the shape of what evolves depends on it as much as on what is retained, which
is the substance of a live dispute rather than a neutral observation: an extended synthesis arguing
that developmental bias, plasticity, niche construction and inheritance beyond the gene deserve equal
standing, against a position that they are already accommodated \citep{laland2015}. This account
takes the first side, and takes it as a position rather than as settled.

The gap is old and named. Natural selection explains the survival of the fittest and not the
\emph{arrival} of the fittest, which is de~Vries's formulation and has stayed in use for over a
century \citep{devries1905,wagner2014}. It is not neglected --- developmental constraint, evolvability
and the biased structure of available variation are all active, and variation is known not to be
isotropic: lineages move more readily along some directions than others, and the direction is a fact
about the organism rather than about the pressure \citep{schluter1996}. This account belongs on that
side of the line and has nothing to add to the selection side.

The mechanism this account would offer there is named already, and the name is the same one: a
lineage that has been changing does not stop when selection does. Maternal inheritance makes a
population continue to evolve for many generations after selection ceases, sometimes in the direction
selection opposes, and the effect is described as a form of
\emph{evolutionary momentum} in the follow-on treatment \citep{kirkpatricklande1989,landekirkpatrick1990}.
A related effect has been measured in the field, where responses in red squirrels were influenced by
the previous generation's selection as well as the current one --- under selection that was ongoing
rather than ceased \citep{mcadamboutin2004}. Rate itself is likewise treated as an inherited, autocorrelated quantity in
molecular evolution \citep{thorne1998}, and whether evolutionary patterns arise from variational
properties of traits or lineages, or instead from ecology, is stated as the open question by the people working on it
\citep{love2021}.

So this is not a gap and nothing here fills one, and an earlier version of this passage claimed a
remaining novelty that the momentum paper has since withdrawn: the published account's carrier is the
maternal phenotype, which is present structure doing the work, so it holds the position this account
holds rather than a rival to it. What the correspondence supplies is a grounding, not a contribution.

\emph{Need does not generate structure, and cannot}: a need for legs is a relation to legs, and there
are none yet to be related to. Need filters what has been produced, which is exactly selection's
work. The producing is the part above, and if change were proportional to what is required nothing
new would ever appear, since every change would be a closer fit to a demand already present.
\emph{Novelty requires overshoot} --- structure has to exist before anything can find a use for it, so
the capacity that looks wasteful is the only thing that has ever produced anything not already
implied.

That inference is not new either, and one version of it holds in simulation. Search directed at an
objective can be actively prevented from reaching it by that objective, and on two benchmark tasks
--- maze navigation and biped walking --- search for novelty alone outperformed it
\citep{lehmanstanley2011}. Those are evolved controllers rather than organisms, so this transfers a
result across domains and does not supply biological evidence. Redundant capacity released from
immediate requirement is the textbook biological route to novel function \citep{ohno1970}, and
complexity arising with no adaptive cause is an established position in molecular evolution
\citep{stoltzfus1999,lukes2011}. What is thin rather than absent is the tie to an overshooting
\emph{rate} specifically.

Three qualifications. Morphological stasis is not the absence of evolution and only the former is
claimed. The mechanism of stasis \emph{in general} is contested --- stabilising selection
\citep{estesarnold2007} and ephemeral divergence with gene flow \citep{futuyma2010}, whose title names
constraint as the other live account, are the developed positions --- but there is no
horseshoe-crab-specific three-way contest, and an earlier version of this remark implied one, and
misdescribed the study that measured the group: it argues habitat and developmental constraint and
\emph{also} endorses stabilising selection explicitly, including in its conclusion. It fits no
evolutionary-mode models at all, which is worth knowing beside \citet{hunt2025} below. And settling is not safety: \emph{Limulus} is presently threatened by harvesting and
habitat loss, which is the environment moving faster than any trade was struck for.

A caution about stasis generally, since the remark leans on it. The punctuated reading is not the
settled state of the evidence: a recent assessment finds stasis favoured in fewer than half of
examined cases, with meandering random walks or more complex models accounting for most of the rest, which is why the
citation above is worded as what the record \emph{was read as} showing \citep{hunt2025}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cognition under the same treatment]\label{rem:cognition-compression}
Cognition is compressed by any measure --- a brain takes on the order of a fifth of a body's
resting energy for a fiftieth of its mass, and an energy budget for signalling has been computed in
detail \citep{attwell2001} --- for rodent grey matter, with the whole-body figures quoted there from
earlier work rather than established by it. The stronger claim, that neural arrangements are forced
by that budget rather than chosen, is \citet{sterling2015}'s thesis and not the budget paper's. Two
constraints can then be named in the way the discipline above requires, and only two; neither is
supplied by either citation, and both are ordinary neuroscience. Conduction takes time over distance, so there is no central
synchroniser and coordination is local and coupled --- the same concession distributed engineering
made, for the same reason. And bandwidth is scarce, so what is not predicted is what gets sent.

Two further candidates are excluded. That cost is inherited along provision is this account's own
thesis (Proposition~\ref{prop:runner-recursion}), so offering it as an independent constraint on
brains would be circular; and that introspection does not reach the machinery is not a constraint but
the conclusion relabelled.

What this licenses is narrower than \emph{cognition is the same}. Two structures under the same
binding constraints land on the same arrangements over the region those constraints touch, and no
further: cognition is not thereby computation, all cognitions are not thereby alike, and nothing here
says a brain is a computer.
\end{remark}

The consequence for this paper's own composition claim is the deflation stated in
Section~\ref{sec:scope}: if recurrence is what compressed structures do, what a recurrence evidences
is the constraint and not the arrangement, and the composition falsifier is negative-only. The test
that remains is not \emph{a domain not under these constraints}, which is empty --- every physical
domain is under finite signal speed, finite bandwidth and a thermodynamic floor. It is a domain under
\emph{weak} compression: somewhere with slack, where cost pressure is not binding, and where the
arrangement should therefore be absent. Ritual, ornament, and systems maintained past their purpose
are candidates. Finding the arrangement there, rendered with equal facility, would show the
vocabulary doing the work.

That proposition is the paper's discriminating test between privileged-carving realism and
cost-shaped pluralism, and the systematics literature is where it can be run. The competing species
concepts do converge, on a common notion of a separately evolving lineage, with the long dispute
reinterpreted as disagreement over operational criteria rather than over the concept
\citep{dequeiroz2007} --- and that convergence is what this account predicts rather than what
embarrasses it. Whether it is \emph{enforced} is the open question, and de Queiroz himself holds the
boundaries to be real but epistemically hard, which is the opposing horn honestly stated; the
criteria continue to disagree hardest over asexual organisms, bacteria exchanging genes horizontally
and recent divergences. This paper does not claim the case is settled in its favour. It claims the
case is a test.

The wider prediction is that classification degrades in a \emph{located} way rather than uniformly,
and it must be stated with the operationalisation that makes it a test.

\begin{hypothesis}[Located degradation]\label{hyp:located-degradation}
Where a classification's purpose can be specified independently and in advance of inspecting its
failures, the failures concentrate near the boundary of that purpose more than a uniform
distribution over the domain would produce. The claim is comparative and is not that failures occur
nowhere else.
\end{hypothesis}

The advance-specification clause is what keeps this from circularity, and it is a real constraint: a
purpose read off from where the failures already are confirms nothing. Independent evidence exists
elsewhere --- classification systems in institutional use are documented as leaking wherever they
have been examined closely, carrying residual categories and encoding their builders' purposes
\citep{bowkerstar1999} --- and that literature's concept of \emph{torque} --- the twisting of a
person's own trajectory against a standardised classification that does not fit it --- is a closer
instrument for this hypothesis than leakage is, with the qualification that its subject is a
biography rather than a use.

Two further results bear on the general claim without establishing it. No clustering function
satisfies scale-invariance, richness and consistency together \citep{kleinberg2002} --- but the same
paper shows any two of the three are jointly achievable, so what is established is that the three
cannot be had at once rather than that no cut is canonical. Kleinberg reads it that way himself, and
dismisses a canonicity result for single-linkage cited on his second page as resting on a property
that is an implicit optimisation criterion which single-linkage uniquely optimises; that dismissal is
his, and is noted here rather than relied on. It is a trade-off theorem, not an impossibility of
carving, and it is a deliberate
transplant of Arrow's method rather than an independent convergence --- the paper says so. And the
question of which predicates are projectible has no syntactic answer \citep{goodman1955}, which is
usually reported as a defeat. Goodman's own criterion is \emph{entrenchment}, a record of a
predicate's having been \emph{projected} before --- a history of use and deliberately not a history
of success, so it does not license reading entrenchment as tracking what has been cheap to run. The
resemblance to a cost criterion is worth noticing and is not evidence for one.

The constructive response is this paper's own and needs no companion: if the trouble is a cut fixed in
advance, do not fix one. Corollary~\ref{cor:mint-is-grammar} already gives the mechanism --- recognition
and growth as one operation, joined where the residual meets the grammar that admits it --- so a
category is minted only where what arrived will not fit, and the codebook grows rather than the cut
being defended. That recovers no privileged carving, because there is none, and does not need to.

The position is not new, and its nearest neighbour differs in grounding rather than in conclusion. A
pluralism admitting many equally legitimate and equally real classifications, none of them the one
nature intended, is argued at length \citep{dupre1993}; that argument rests on objective similarity
structure, where this one rests on what a cut costs to run, which is the more deflationary footing and
reintroduces a ranking over cuts that ``equally legitimate'' was formulated to deny. The convergence
is on the negative thesis; the divergence in grounding is real and is not smoothed over here.

\section{Determinism and apparent stochasticity}\label{sec:determinism}

\begin{proposition}[Graph-law determinism]\label{prop:determinism}
For a fully specified antecedent configuration---including the graph state, operative transformation structure, admissibility grammar, and every causally operative containing relation relevant to the collapse---the realised continuation is determined:
\[
\boxed{
\left(G_t,\mathcal R_t^{\mathrm{causal}}\right)
\mapsto
G_{t+1}.
}
\]
The Graph Law contains no primitive random actualisation operator.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism} is to be read through Proposition~\ref{prop:present-sufficiency}: the antecedent configuration is causally sufficient \emph{present} structure, not a retained temporal history. Determinism is a relation between present structure and realised continuation, and Proposition~\ref{prop:determinable} keeps it from being overstated --- a determinable continuation is not thereby a determined one.

Apparent stochasticity arises when a frame does not represent all of the causally operative dimensions or relations required to determine the realised continuation. A probability distribution may then be an appropriate model of that frame's uncertainty, but the probability belongs to the incomplete representation rather than to the substrate transition itself. Treating probability as a representation of incomplete information has a long statistical-mechanical and Bayesian lineage \citep{jaynes1957}.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\text{stochastic observation}
=
\text{deterministic dynamics viewed through unresolved causal structure}.
}
\]

\begin{remark}[Direction of the borrowing]\label{rem:qm-provenance}
The order of discovery ran from quantum measurement outward, not from a general primitive inward. Collapse was adopted because compatibility with quantum measurement is a constraint on any theory of realised change, and was then assumed to hold generally. The bridge is therefore stated conditionally because the \emph{generalisation} is the conjecture, not because the compatibility is in doubt. This is the structure of a promoted constraint --- a fact that must hold in one regime, assumed to hold everywhere, with the consequences derived and exposed to falsification --- and it is why Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} carries a falsifier for the bridge specifically. Origin does not establish a claim; the falsifier is what does the work either way.
\end{remark}

The distinction of Remark~\ref{rem:undetermined-vs-undisclosed} is what makes quantum measurement the visible case for the conditional collapse bridge (Remark~\ref{rem:qm-provenance}). In that regime the pre-measurement alternatives provably cannot be re-read as an \emph{undisclosed} already-realised classical value: Bell's theorem excludes the local hidden-variable class and Kochen--Specker constrains noncontextual value assignment \citep{bell1964,kochen1967}. What remains is \emph{undetermined} in the strict sense --- not a value withheld, but no value yet. Elsewhere the two readings are usually interchangeable in practice; there they are not.

Deterministic quantum models demonstrate that empirical quantum statistics do not by themselves force fundamental stochasticity: Bohm's hidden-variable construction is explicitly deterministic \citep{bohm1952}, while the constraints just cited rule out the local and noncontextual hidden-variable classes rather than determinism as such. Graphic Equalisation does not identify itself with Bohmian mechanics; these results establish only that determinism must be distinguished from locality and noncontextuality.

A stochastic law may be bolted onto a grounding as an epistemic predictive model over unresolved continuations. Doing so is an additional modelling layer:
\[
\text{deterministic Graph Law}
\longrightarrow
\text{frame-limited uncertainty model},
\]
not a modification of the Graph Law into a stochastic substrate.

\section{Positioning and prior art}\label{sec:prior-art}

The individual ingredients of Graphic Equalisation have substantial prior art. The proposed contribution is their conjunction at one minimal substrate level and the additional distinctions forced by making that conjunction executable.

\paragraph{Graph rewriting and typed transformation.} Algebraic graph transformation provides mature formalisms for typed graphs, rule application, partiality, and structural consistency \citep{ehrig2006}. Graphic Equalisation adopts graph transformation as substrate vocabulary but makes frame-relative boundary forcing, collapse, and identity part of the same dynamical account.

\paragraph{Reflection and self-modification.} Computational reflection establishes that a system can represent and modify meta-level structure governing its own execution \citep{smith1982,maes1987}. The specific identity ``the grammar acting on itself is the loop'' is the present paper's claim, not a result attributed to those sources.

\paragraph{Cybernetics and feedback.} Classical cybernetics and control theory distinguish open-loop dynamics from systems in which error is returned into subsequent control \citep{wiener1948,ashby1956}. The residual construction generalises the same causal distinction: mismatch changes later action only where it survives as represented state.

\paragraph{Quantum measurement and relational description.} The separation of pre-realisation state from realised measurement record is standard in the measurement problem \citep{vonneumann1955}. Everett's relative-state formulation and Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics provide important precedents for refusing a single privileged observer-independent account of all facts \citep{everett1957,rovelli1996}. Graphic Equalisation uses these as neighbouring structures rather than deriving itself from any one interpretation.

\paragraph{Deterministic quantum theories.} Bohm's 1952 model is a direct proof of principle that deterministic dynamics can reproduce nonrelativistic quantum predictions when supplemented by additional state \citep{bohm1952}. Bell and Kochen--Specker impose decisive constraints on local and noncontextual hidden-variable theories (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}). Consequently, the determinism claim in Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism} should be read as a commitment requiring an adequate nonlocal/contextual grounding where quantum phenomena demand it, not as a revival of unconstrained classical hidden variables.

\paragraph{Causal order.} Causal-set theory supplies a well-developed example in which causal order is taken as primitive while continuum metric structure is additional or emergent \citep{bombelli1987}. Graphic Equalisation makes only the weaker substrate claim that ordinal causal order does not itself entail a real-valued metric time.

\paragraph{Emergent and relational time.} That time is not a substrate dimension (Section~\ref{sec:time-not-primitive}) has substantial physics precedent, and the present account sits at the most-local end of it. The Wheeler--DeWitt equation of canonical quantum gravity carries no time parameter \citep{dewitt1967} --- the ``problem of time'' this substrate dissolves rather than solves, there having been no time variable to recover. The Page--Wootters mechanism derives apparent evolution from entanglement between a clock subsystem and the rest \citep{pagewootters1983}, closely matching ``time inferred from the relations a frame holds,'' but retains a single global state where this account is per-frame. Barbour's Machian relationalism takes time to be abstracted from change rather than a container \citep{barbour1999}, and Rovelli's relational and thermal-time programme refuses a privileged global time \citep{rovelli1996,rovelli2011}. The distinguishing move here is to keep none of the global objects these programmes retain --- global wavefunction, global configuration space, global state --- reading time entirely from local change.

\paragraph{Computational-universe and graph-rewrite models.} The closest extant framework is the Wolfram Physics Project, in which the universe is a hypergraph evolved by rewrite rules, time is progressive rule application, and observer-independent structure follows from \emph{causal invariance} --- different update orderings yielding the same causal graph \citep{wolfram2002,wolfram2020}. The structural agreement is deep: graph plus rewrite rules, time emergent from rule application rather than posited, order-independence, and observer-relative slicing. Four differences locate the present contribution. First, $\Gamma$ is an admissibility grammar that is itself graph data and self-modifying (the grammar-is-the-loop theorem), whereas Wolfram fixes a rule per universe or takes the entire rule space --- the Ruliad --- at once. Second, collapse realises \emph{one} frame-relative continuation, whereas Wolfram retains all branches (the multiway graph) and derives quantum structure from them. Third, residual and growth-by-minting from it have no counterpart in a fixed rule. Fourth, the present substrate keeps no global object --- no single hypergraph being updated --- being local and frame-relative throughout, and its claims are information-level and cross-domain rather than an attempt to derive our specific universe. This last is why $c$ falls out here as a \emph{per-frame} unit (developed in the companion physics-grounding paper): sub-rules with closure give each frame its own cycle and hence its own $c=1$, invariant by construction, whereas a single global update yields $c$ only as an emergent speed limit shown observer-independent by causal invariance --- the constant, but not the per-frame machinery beneath it. That an independent programme reached the shared shape from cellular automata and physics, while this one reached it from cognition and engineering, is evidence for the arrangement claim of Section~\ref{sec:scope} rather than against its novelty.

\paragraph{Information and minimum description.} The preference for the smallest sufficient ontology follows the same methodological direction as information-theoretic and minimum-description-length approaches: do not introduce representational structure that buys no additional explanatory or predictive distinction \citep{shannon1948,rissanen1978}. This motivates but does not prove the Discrete Sufficiency Hypothesis.

\paragraph{Standing beside, not replacing.} Graphic Equalisation is not proposed as a replacement for established theories. It is intended to sit beneath or beside them as a substrate description. A grounding may add continuous geometry, conservation laws, quantum amplitudes, relativistic metrics, biological mechanisms, institutional rules, or probabilistic models of unresolved lower-frame observations. In particular, relational quantum mechanics \citep{rovelli1996}, Everettian relative-state quantum mechanics \citep{everett1957}, and deterministic Bohmian mechanics \citep{bohm1952} make mutually different commitments while remaining compatible with large parts of standard quantum phenomenology; this paper does not require choosing among those complete physical models at the substrate-definition stage. Such additions do not alter Proposition~\ref{prop:determinism}: within the Graph Law, realised transition remains deterministic once the causally complete antecedent configuration is specified.

The substrate claim remains
\[
\boxed{\text{represented state}\rightarrow\text{admissible possibility}\rightarrow\text{collapse}\rightarrow\text{realised state}.}
\]

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}
Each commitment below is marked \textsc{[shape]} or \textsc{[detail]}. A \textsc{[detail]} failing is a revision: the account survives with that mechanism replaced. A \textsc{[shape]} failing is a refutation, because what fails is something the substrate is claimed to require or to permit. The distinction is the one Section~\ref{sec:scope} rests its epistemic claim on, and marking it here is what makes that claim checkable rather than asserted.
\paragraph{\textsc{[shape]}~Composition.} Prior to the component commitments below: the arrangement claim of Section~\ref{sec:scope} fails if the structure's recurrence across unrelated domains is an artefact of description rather than a property of those domains. Evidence against it would be a demonstration that any sufficiently rich domain can be rendered in this vocabulary with equal facility, including domains chosen adversarially to lack the structure. Evidence for it is independent re-derivation, in a domain's own idiom, without reference to this theory. The burden here is deliberately on the negative side, because that side is closeable: a single adversarial domain rendered with equal facility settles it against the claim. No requirement is placed on the positive side to exhibit domains that share \emph{nothing} --- every pair of domains shares something, so that standard is unmeetable in principle, and discharging it would mean enumerating domains until an assessor chose to stop. A test whose stopping condition lies with the assessor rather than with the claim is not a test, and this paper does not offer one.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Solutions are nearly unique under a binding constraint.} The posit under Propositions~\ref{prop:convergence-observed} and~\ref{prop:compression-convergence}. Fails if structures optimising hard against the same binding constraint, over the same region, are exhibited arriving at arrangements that do not resemble each other --- in which case constraint narrows the space without selecting within it, and both propositions weaken to that.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Located degradation.} Fails if, for classifications whose purpose is specifiable in advance of inspecting their failures, failure cases are distributed no differently near the purpose boundary than across the rest of the domain (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:located-degradation}).
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Convergence is not enforced.} Fails if convergence between independently developed classifications of one domain extends to the cases where the classifications' purposes differ, which is what a privileged boundary would produce and cost-shaped convergence would not (Proposition~\ref{prop:convergence-observed}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Minimal graph sufficiency.} Fails if basic realised graph dynamics require a primitive beyond $(g,r,\Gamma)$ rather than represented or relational structure.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Discrete sufficiency.} Fails if a claimed observable consequence cannot be preserved by any discrete representation, including dynamically varying quantisation, but is preserved by a genuinely non-discrete primitive.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Antecedent-state principle.} Fails if a realised transition can require as antecedent a fact that exists only as consequence of that same transition, absent prior fixed-point structure.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Graph-law determinism.} Fails if two genuinely identical, causally complete antecedent configurations produce different realised successors without any additional causally operative distinction. Variation observed only from a frame that omits determining dimensions does not constitute such a falsification.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Admissibility is not armour.} Fails if self-admissibility must constrain arbitrary containing-world forcing.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No implicit historical state.} Fails if an unrepresented past event alters later transition structure despite leaving no causal trace in graph or boundary state.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Frame-relative identity.} Fails if sameness and persistence are derivable from $(g,r,\Gamma)$ alone without individuation.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Higher-frame first-class structure.} Fails if a containing graph cannot possess ordinary relational state about a lower graph unless the same quantity is primitive inside the lower graph.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Residual is inadmissibility.} Fails if a graph-originated deformation can be left as residual by a containing frame that nonetheless admits it under $\Gamma_{W_c}$, or absorbed while inadmissible, so that residual and non-admission come apart.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Cost is the write.} Fails if a substrate operation other than writing a delta carries an irreducible cost --- if reading, holding a standing structure, or the boundary relation itself must be paid for with a cost that is not the cost of the write they occasion.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Self-change is subset-change.} The claim is that a graph changes itself and its subgraphs directly (rule-governed), and affects its container only as a subset of it, by propagation through coupling. Fails if a graph changes its container by a means other than changing the region of it that the graph is --- a container-change not propagating from the graph's own subset --- or if a graph can self-terminate without acting on the container.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Minting is a rule.} Fails if grammar-extension (growth) requires a primitive operation beneath the rules rather than a reflective rule subject to the same economics, admissibility, and antecedent-state principle --- in particular, if a graph can mint its first distinctions without any seeded from its container.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Partial-order sufficiency.} Fails if realised dynamics require a total order over collapses in distinct frames --- a global simultaneity --- rather than only the boundary-induced partial order.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Globally-async/locally-sync.} Fails if coupling never induces local synchrony (boundary-related collapses remain unordered) or if synchrony is forced without coupling (a global instant exists independent of any boundary).
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Provenance is never closed by inference.} Not that primitives fail to leak --- they do leak, and Proposition~\ref{prop:leak} says so. This fails if a frame can \emph{settle} the provenance of its own operator set from inside: if some in-frame procedure establishes, rather than merely evidences, whether its operators are self-supplied, how many distinct structures supply them, or whether a serial hidden supply underlies an apparently asynchronous frame The escape is not that further primitives were used, which is unavoidable, but that their provenance is open in the same way, so no procedure closes the question rather than relocating it.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The theory is deliberately small. A graph need not know what it is, where it is, what will happen, or what it wants. Present structure determines admissible continuation; interaction and collapse produce realised history; realised history changes the antecedent from which subsequent possibility is generated.

Recursion requires no external homunculus. Where the grammar acts on its own represented structure, the grammar is the loop. World forcing is not self-action, residual is discrepancy rather than diagnosis, history matters only where it survives as present structure, and identity exists only under individuation by a frame.

Pressed for what is compulsory, the theory keeps almost nothing. What survives every attempted removal is structure with relations among its parts; what rules running adds is that anything \emph{happens} (Remark~\ref{rem:only-g}), which is the condition of everything this account is about and is not the condition of there being anything; the specific laws above it --- inertia, closure, the shape of collapse, and the write-economics itself --- are contingent rule content rather than necessities, and not strict universals (Remark~\ref{rem:writeability}), which is the sense in which this substrate underlies groundings without dictating them.

Every mechanism used here belongs to someone else and is cited as such; the claim is the arrangement (Section~\ref{sec:scope}), and the method by which the arrangement was reached is removal rather than addition --- of primitive time, of a privileged frame, of a cost that is the same whatever runs the rules. Removals are small to state and large in consequence, because results that had been independently working around a deleted assumption stop being independent.

The account forbids one thing, and Section~\ref{sec:prohibition} states it and its formal analogue: a frame cannot get outside itself. Everything else that has looked impossible has so far turned out to be contingent rule content that some grounding happens not to instantiate --- which is a measure of how much had been filed as necessary without being checked, and is not itself evidence for the account, since the enumeration is open and this paper does not offer an open enumeration as a test (Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability}).

The regress in that prohibition has both ends, and neither is safe to state alone. A superframe dissolves the frame below's limit: holding both of two locations it has units closing across them, and quantities the subframes could not obtain are ordinary in its terms. And a superframe is a frame, so it reproduces the same blindness about its own running. Ascent buys something real and never buys everything --- the limit is dissolved locally at every step and terminated at none, which is why no amount of it yields a view from nowhere. What ascent yields is a different somewhere.
