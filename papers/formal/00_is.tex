\begin{abstract}
One claim, made without machinery. To be is to stand in relation, and nothing further is required for
there to be anything at all --- no sequence, no cause, no rule about what may follow what. The
companion accounts begin after this point and say so; this one supplies what they begin after. It is
short because there is very little to say once the claim is stated.
The work is not in the claim but in making a case for it.
\end{abstract}

\section{The claim}
\emph{What is, is.} That is the whole of it, and everything below is an attempt to say what the sentence
commits to and what it does not.

To be \emph{something} rather than nothing at all is to be distinguishable, and to be distinguishable is
to stand apart from something. \emph{Distinction is relation.} There is no third option in which a thing
is determinate and stands in no relation whatever: such a thing would differ from nothing, and a thing
that differs from nothing is not a thing but the absence of one.

\begin{proposition}[To be is to be related]\label{prop:is}
Anything that exists as a determinate something stands in at least one relation. Contrapositively:
where no relation holds, there is no distinction, and where there is no distinction there is no
determinate something --- not one that cannot be told apart from nothing, but no second thing there to
be told apart.
\end{proposition}

The second sentence was formerly given as \emph{equivalent} to the first and phrased as a distinction
\emph{not available to be drawn}, which was a mistake worth correcting in the open, because it made an
ontological claim sound like an epistemic one. Availability implies somebody to whom something is
available, and there is nobody here: the base of this account has no observers and no views at all, so a criterion resting on what can be told apart would have nothing to rest on. The claim is
not that an unrelated existent is one we could never detect. It is that the description picks out
nothing --- there is no fact it could be a fact about, because a fact requires two things and a
relation between them. That reading owes nothing to verificationism, which the account could not use
anyway: what exists here does not wait on observation, and a tree falling unobserved is exactly as
related to its forest as one that is watched.

What follows in this paper is therefore not an elaboration of the claim but a case for it, and the
distinction is worth keeping because it sets what would count as a rebuttal. There is no complexity in the
\emph{claim} to disagree with: the sentence is short enough to hold entire, it says less than any
account of what there is has any business saying, and a reader can see all of it at once. That is the
intended difficulty, and it is a claim about the proposition rather than about this document --- the
method, the reading of how formalisms move, and the accounting of what derivation costs are all
separable and all rejectable on their own, as the next section says. What the shortness buys is
narrower: the proposition cannot be rescued by adjusting an apparatus, because it has none, so the
burden on it falls entirely on whether the case is compelling.

The companion accounts begin from this claim and add to it what a world in which things \emph{happen}
costs, which this paper is careful to say is not required for there to be anything. An earlier version
of this paragraph said they add \emph{exactly one thing}, and that was wrong, and the paragraph's whole purpose was to state something checkable.
The companion's own ledger, corrected after audit and moved
in both directions since, now carries the admission and nothing else: what it once counted as further
commitments have each turned out to be the admission unpacked, and its Axiom~$-1$ is a discipline
against importing unpaid assumptions rather than a claim that has to be granted. Transformation is not among them: something
has to do the happening, whatever does it is a graph because everything is, and the least graph that
can do anything is a pattern together with a change it makes --- a rule. So a rule is a graph that does
something, and rules running is what this paper's own claim looks like where something occurs rather
than a second kind of thing put beside it. That is this paper's method used one level along: it asks
what the least structure that can \emph{be} is and answers relation; the companion asks what the least
structure that can \emph{do} is and answers rule. The economics does not cost extra either, and the reason is worth stating because two attempts to
derive it failed before this one. What a thing does, when it does
anything, is \emph{change}; and \emph{change is the cost of change} --- the cost is not a quantity in
another currency that the change is priced in, it is the change. So a happening that cost nothing would
be a change in which nothing changed. Cost can be unboundedly small and is never zero, and what a
particular floor is belongs to the groundings. Nor is realisation a further
commitment. Nothing selects among candidate continuations: what matches, writes, two rules matching one
state make a bigger change rather than a contest, and writes that do not compose leave residual --- so
a successor is single because the change is. Realisation is then a rule about rules, as a frame is a
graph about graphs, and the companion already holds that an operator is some other graph's running
exposed. It is turtles all the way down, and the recursion is the architecture rather than a regress.

So what the companions add to this paper is the admission that things
happen, and past that, the bets they
state as hypotheses rather than as commitments. What those commit to
together is then unpacked at length, and the unpacking is not further claims about what exists.

The strong form of the statement is therefore false and the useful form survives: the base is small,
what is added to it is enumerable, and anything in the companions that is an independent posit
uncounted by that enumeration is a defect in their economy. The count has moved repeatedly under
that standard --- five posits found where two were claimed, folds made and two of them found unsound
and reversed, claims narrowed where they had been stated too strongly --- and the movement is the
procedure running rather than a caveat on its result. A ledger that had never moved would be evidence
of nothing except that nobody had checked it. What should be read from the destination is an increase
in exposure and not a reduction: an account resting on fewer things has fewer places where a failure
would be contained.

The relation between this paper's length and the companions' runs in the direction one might not
expect. This paper's shortness is not a contrast with
the length of the companions but the thing that licenses it. A reader meeting a fifty-page theory
without this one in front of them has no way to read its length except as the size of what it assumes,
and an account that assumed fifty pages' worth would be an extravagant thing to be asked to grant. Put
the base first and the same fifty pages change character: they are the length of a \emph{derivation},
not of a commitment, and the two are not costs of the same kind. This is the ordinary accounting of
minimum description length applied to an account rather than to a string --- what is charged for is the
generating structure and not what unrolling it produces, so a short generator with a long output is
cheap and a long generator is not, however short its output. The companions are meant to be output.

Meant to be, and not wholly: where
a companion introduces a \emph{definition} --- an aperture, a change grammar, a residual, a frame --- it
is adding vocabulary, and vocabulary sits on the generator's side of that ledger however faithfully it
was motivated. Only the reasoning \emph{over} those terms is output. So the licence covers less than
all of the length, the fraction it covers is not measured anywhere, and the accurate statement is a
comparison rather than an exemption: fifty pages of derivation over a declared vocabulary is a smaller
commitment than fifty pages of assumption, which is not the same as costing nothing.

Which is also the standard this paper puts itself under, since the reasoning is comfortable enough to be
dangerous. \emph{It is derivation} is available to be said about any quantity of text whatever, and
saying it costs nothing; what stops it being an excuse for length is that each part must be traceable to
the base and the tracing must be something a reader can attempt and fail at. Where that is not
available, the licence is not either, and the length reverts to being what it appeared to be.

\section{Which is to say: a graph}
The claim has a name, and refusing to use it would be coy. Relata standing in relations are nodes
standing in edges. \emph{Everything is a graph} --- and that sentence is the whole thesis of the
companion accounts, stated here in the only form that requires nothing to state it.

The question this paper asks is the dumbest one available: \emph{what is there?} A child can ask it and
nothing prevents anyone answering. It is also the largest, because an answer to it constrains every
answer to anything else, and because there is nowhere further back to stand.

Two things follow about the form of this paper. It builds \emph{no machinery}, deliberately: no
transformation, no grammar, no economics --- none of the apparatus a reader could dispute
\emph{instead of} disputing the claim. That is not the same as offering nothing else to disagree with,
and an earlier version of this paragraph said it was. What follows below is a method, a reading of how
formalisms move under compression, and an accounting of what derivation costs; each is separable from
the claim and each can be rejected on its own. The honest form is narrower: no \emph{structure is
posited} here that could absorb an objection meant for the claim, so a reader who rejects the claim
cannot be answered by adjusting a mechanism, because there is none to adjust.

And the objection that arrives immediately can be answered in a line. \emph{Everything can be
represented as a graph, so the claim is empty} --- yes to the first clause, and the answer is that this
is not a claim about representability. Nodes and edges are cheap; what is asserted is that there is
\emph{nothing else}, that relata and relations exhaust what there is, and that time and cause and rule
in particular are not among the furniture. And the assertion is about \emph{this} instance rather than about
what is possible: a world with a global clock in its floor, or with production as a primitive, is not
prohibited by anything here --- such a world could be built and would be perfectly coherent --- the
claim is that \emph{this one is not that}. What would bear on it is therefore a demonstration that this
world has such a floor, which is an empirical matter and not a modal one, and imagining a world that
does costs nothing and settles nothing.

That is also why finding one would bound the account rather than refute it, in the way a later theory
bounds an earlier: the claim would hold of worlds without such a floor and this would not be one of
them. The companions argue all of this at length; here it is a paragraph, because here there is nothing
else to argue about.

\section{How the claim is reached}
A claim of this shape is worth nothing if it arrives by assertion, and the difference between an account
and a pronouncement is whether there is a procedure behind it that someone else can run. There is one here, and it is short.

\emph{Ask at maximum scope.} The question is put to everything at once rather than to a domain, because
a shortest sufficient account of one domain tells you about that domain and a shortest sufficient
account of all of it tells you what is common to all of it. The scope is what does the work: the more
that must be accounted for, the more the structure peculiar to any part washes out and the more only
what is shared survives.

\emph{Prefer the shortest account that suffices.} Between two accounts covering the same ground, the one
carrying less structure is preferred, and this is the ordinary discipline of minimum description length
rather than anything invented here. Applied at maximum scope it has a direction: keep removing until
removing costs coverage.

\emph{Remove, and see what breaks.} This is the operation and it is the only one. Take a candidate ---
time, cause, rule, substance, an observer, a global clock --- and put the account without it. If what
was accounted for is still accounted for, the candidate was not required and it goes. If coverage is
lost, it stays, and the loss shows what it was doing. Each removal is a test whose outcome is determinate in one
direction and not in the other, and the asymmetry is this: a
\emph{successful} removal settles the matter, since an account that stands without the candidate is
exhibited and can be inspected, while a \emph{failed} one records only that nobody has yet seen how,
and stays open to a reader who does. That is what makes the procedure repeatable rather than a matter
of taste, and it is also why the paper expects to be shortened.

Run that far enough and it stops in one place. Time can go: nothing in the claim requires sequence.
Cause can go: relation is not production. Rule can go: nothing needs to be permitted or forbidden for
things to stand as they stand. Relation cannot go, because removing it removes distinction, and removing
distinction removes the possibility of there being determinate somethings for the account to be about at
all. \emph{The claim is not asserted here. It is what survived.}

\emph{A removal must be named and restorable.} This is the discipline that separates the method from
mere simplification, and it is the one place the procedure can be abused. Every account reached this way
is an idealisation, and an idealisation is just a frame --- which is no defence on its own, since
everything is one. What makes a frame cheap to hold rather than expensive is whether what it removed is
written down and can be put back. The rock is what this claim looks like at the grain at which it is a
rock; that its atoms move and it weathers at other grains belongs on the same page, so the removed
structure is recoverable and the idealisation costs little. \emph{The spherical cow is not objectionable
for being spherical.} It is objectionable when nobody writes down that it was made spherical, and the
result is then applied to a cow.

The preference for the shortest sufficient account is not this programme's invention. It is
\citet{rissanen1978}'s: do not introduce structure that buys no additional distinction. The
information-theoretic setting it is stated against is \citet{shannon1948}, which supplies the measure
and the coding bound and does \emph{not} supply the parsimony norm --- it contains no model selection
and treats redundancy as useful where a channel is noisy, so citing the two together for the norm, as
an earlier version of this sentence did, borrowed a name that did not make the claim. What is added
here is only the scope at which the norm is applied.

Two honest limits on the method, and the second matters more.

Removal establishes that nothing \emph{tried} was required, which is not the same as nothing being
required. The result is only as good as the candidate list, so that list should be stated rather than
implied: time, causation, substance or intrinsic nature, an observer, a privileged frame or global
clock, admissibility, and --- less obviously --- any axiom restricting where a distinction may be made
from. That last is worth naming because it is easy to keep by habit: a rule that every distinction is
made in some frame governs frames, and there are none here to govern. What removing it does \emph{not}
do is remove every commitment about distinction, and the difference is worth being exact about since
the two are easily run together. Removed: any rule about where a distinction may be made \emph{from}.
Retained, and load-bearing: that a distinction is a relation, which is the identification the whole
claim turns on and which is a commitment rather than a discovery. A reader who holds that a thing can
be determinate without standing apart from anything rejects this paper at that sentence and needs to go
no further --- which is where the disagreement should be, rather than several pages downstream. It
arrives with the machinery it
constrains, in the companion account, and is not required for anything this paper says. Anyone who thinks something else belongs at the base is invited to add it and
run the same procedure, and that is the form disagreement with this paper should take.

And the procedure does not deliver certainty, only survival. A candidate that has not yet been removed
successfully may still be removable by someone who sees how, and the account would then be shorter than
it is here.

\subsection*{One operation, applied to what came bundled}
It should be said plainly that there is only one move here and that it is used repeatedly, because a
reader who expects a method proportioned to the size of the claim will go looking for machinery that
does not exist. The operation above is stated as removing posited structure --- time, cause, rule ---
but its more productive target is different, and is the same operation pointed the other way.

Almost every claim worth taking at maximum scope is one that some field already accepts, and accepts
with a restriction attached. Relations are basic \emph{in this formalism}. There is no privileged
observer \emph{in mechanics}. The shortest description is preferred \emph{for this class of model}.
The restriction is generally not a discovered property of the subject; it is the restriction that
constitutes the field, and it travelled with the claim because within a field there was never an
occasion to separate them. So the move is to take the claim seriously and decline the restriction ---
which is not a new procedure but \emph{remove, and see what breaks} applied to an inherited limit
rather than to a posited entity, with the same asymmetric outcome and the same requirement that the
removal be named and restorable.

Two things follow. The
first is that results reached this way look simultaneously trivial and consequential, and are both:
trivial because nothing was added and the claim was already granted, consequential because the
discarded restriction was doing work that nobody had audited, having never been separable from the
subject inside the field that held it.

The second is a limit, and it is severe. Most restrictions are load-bearing --- they are attached
because removing them breaks something, and taking a claim past its restriction usually produces
nonsense rather than generality. The operation is cheap to run and returns nothing on almost every
attempt --- a rate this paper commits to in Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability} and cannot yet support,
since no ledger of the failures was kept and the base rate is therefore reconstructed rather than
recorded --- and the ones that return something are worth reporting for that reason and not because
the operation is powerful. Nothing here is a recipe for producing results by taking things literally.
It is a cheap test, run often, with a low yield, and a signal on firing that is clear about \emph{that}
something did not break while saying nothing about whether what survived is worth having.

\section{A consequence with a track record}
The claim fixes what compression is aiming at, and that turns out to be checkable in a way the claim
itself is not.

If what there is, is relata standing in relations, then the shortest sufficient description of anything
is stated \emph{in terms of} relata and relations. The qualification is doing real work and an earlier
version of this passage omitted it, giving instead that a description shorter than its subject's
structure has lost something --- which is a picture theory, and worse, contradicts the accounting two
sections above. Minimum description length charges for a generator, not for what unrolling it produces:
the shortest sufficient description of the complete graph on a million vertices is a phrase naming the
relation and the count, not five hundred billion enumerated pairs, and a description isomorphic to its
subject is very often the expensive one. So the claim cannot be that the shortest description
\emph{enumerates} relata and relations.

What it can be, and is, is a claim about the vocabulary the generator has to be written in. ``The
complete graph on a million vertices'' is a short generator whose terms are a relation type, an arity
and a count; it is about relata and relations without listing any. The prediction is therefore that
formalisms under compression converge on \emph{primitives} that are relata and relations, not on
descriptions the size of their subjects. \emph{The ideal information shape is a graph} in that sense
--- what the terms denote is fixed by what is being described, while how briefly they can be combined
is not. So a field pushing its own formalism toward minimum description should \emph{move toward}
graph form, and it should do so without anyone intending it.

That is a prediction about the history of formalisms rather than about the world, and it appears to
hold: physics reaching for spin networks, tensor networks and causal sets; chemistry settling on the
molecular graph; linguistics on dependency structure; computing on data structures that are graphs and
on a categorical foundation that is arrows before objects; neuroscience on the connectome; biology on
phylogenies and interaction networks. None of these was adopted because anybody held the thesis of this
paper. Each was adopted because it was the cheapest thing that still accounted for the subject.

One case is worth setting out rather than listing, because it is the oldest, it has been kept longest,
and it is usually described as an invention. \emph{Double-entry bookkeeping is not an invention. It is
a compression.} A transfer has two sides, so the shortest record of one that loses nothing has two
entries --- an account, an account, and the movement between them, which is relata and a relation and
nothing else. Nobody derived it from a thesis about what exists.

The history is what makes it a case rather than an analogy. \citet{pacioli1494} is usually credited
with the system, and what he wrote was a description: the \emph{Particularis de Computis et Scripturis},
a treatise inside a mathematics textbook, setting out a method already in use among Venetian merchants.
The practice is older than the account of it by roughly two centuries --- \citet{lee1977} reads the
Farolfi ledger of 1299--1300 as full double entry --- so the system was found in use and then written
down, which is the order a compression arrives in and not the order an invention does. And it has been
kept for five centuries, in the formalism the field teaches and computes with, which is what the
commitment below actually asks for.

The honest counterweight belongs here too, because the history is contested in a way that bears on the
claim. \citet{yamey1949} argued against the then-standard view that double entry was adopted for
rational profit calculation, holding that early merchants did not use it that way. That cuts against
reading the technique as designed for a purpose --- which, for the reading here, is help rather than
harm: a method adopted without a theory of what it was for, and kept anyway, is better evidence that it
fitted its subject than one adopted because somebody argued it should be.

And it earns the case rather than illustrating it, because of what the practice detects. A ledger that
does not balance is not a rule violation but a \emph{residual} --- something recorded that does not
cohere with the rest --- and the trial balance is a coherence check, which is why the technique catches
errors at all. A convention that did not match the two-sidedness of what it records would have no such
property, so the survival of the technique is evidence about the subject and not only about the
bookkeepers.

That reading has a mathematical statement and it is not this paper's.
\citet{ellerman1985,ellerman2014} shows that double entry implicitly uses the \emph{group of
differences} over pairs of unsigned numbers --- the T-account is the pair --- so a transaction is a
difference and the two entries are its two components rather than one record written twice. Balance is
then the group identity, and an unbalanced ledger is a residual in the exact sense, which is where the
error-detecting property comes from. The dating is the part that bears on the claim here: the
construction was only formulated abstractly in mathematics in the nineteenth century, after the
practice had used it for over five centuries. So the structure was in use long before anyone had the
mathematics to say what it was --- found, then written down as a method, then formalised four hundred
years after that. \citet{arya2000} take the same structure the other way and read the ledger as a
network whose accounts are nodes and transactions are edges, recovering transactions from statements by
its connectivity, which is the relata-and-relations reading arrived at without the thesis.

The discriminator matters and is easy to lose. \emph{The prediction is about movement, not about
drawability.} That a field's subject matter can be drawn as a graph shows nothing, since graphs are
expressive and almost anything can be. What would count is a field that compressed its formalism and
moved \emph{away} from graph form --- toward a primitive that is not a relatum or a relation and cannot
be reduced to them --- and that is what the second commitment below asks for.

\section{One is not enough}
A single relatum with nothing to relate to is not a minimal case of existence but a failed one. There is
nothing for it to be distinguished from, so there is no fact about what it is; it does not sit at the
bottom of the hierarchy of existing things, it fails to enter. \emph{One relatum is decoherent.}

The empty case is the other end and is cleaner. Nothing at all stands in no relation, has no parts to be
in tension, and admits no failure of fit --- it is complete in the only sense available to it. That is
why the empty set is the only perfect thing and why perfection is not something an existent can have:
\emph{to exist is to be imperfect}, because to exist is to be related, and to be related is to be
partial with respect to what one is related to.

\section{One direction only}
A consequence of the restraint deserves its own statement, because it is what makes the claim's subject
matter peculiar. \emph{What merely is cannot observe; it can only be observed.}

To observe is to make a distinction, and making one is an act --- something is done, and afterwards the
observer holds what it did not hold before. That requires transformation and some grammar of what may
follow what, and this paper has neither. So what is described here does nothing at all, and observing is
among the things it does not do.

It can be the \emph{object} of observation without difficulty, and in practice it is nothing else. A
rock is what this looks like from outside: met, related to, distinguished by something that does have
the machinery. But there is no corresponding inside. \emph{There is no view from anywhere} --- not a narrow
view, not a limited one, none.

The reason is not that framehood needs rules --- it does not, and the companion is explicit that a
structure with no rules of its own is still a frame, because \emph{individuation is performed by the
container and not by the thing}. A boundary belongs to the frame that draws it, so what a rule-less
structure lacks is not framehood but the capacity to individuate anything itself. It is also why an
\emph{empty} frame is impossible rather than merely unattested: an edge needs relata, so where there is
nothing there is nothing to demarcate.

What the base lacks is the drawing. A frame is a boundary relation \emph{instantiated}, and
instantiating is something a container does; a level at which nothing runs has nothing doing it, so it
has no frames --- not because a frame would need rules of its own, but because it needs someone
else's.

What is missing is \emph{making}. A view is not had by standing in a relation; it is had by a frame
that \emph{makes} a distinction, and making is doing, and doing is what this level does not have ---
that is precisely what the companions add when they admit that things happen. So the familiar claim,
that every view is had from somewhere by someone positioned, presupposes there are views and asks where
they are from. Here there are none to ask about: \emph{no view from nowhere, and no view from anywhere
either}, not for want of a place to view from but for want of anything doing the viewing.

An axiom restricting where a distinction may be \emph{made} from therefore cannot be stated at this
level, let alone assumed --- because it governs an act, and there are no acts here ---
which is also why there are no frames at this level to govern, since instantiating a boundary is one. It arrives with the machinery
that performs them.

\begin{proposition}[Observation is one-directional at the base]\label{prop:one-way}
What stands in relation without transformation or grammar can be observed and cannot observe. It is
therefore always the object of a distinction and never the maker of one.
\end{proposition}

\section{What is not claimed}\label{sec:not-claimed}
The claim above is smaller than it may appear, and the restraint is the point.

\emph{No time.} Nothing here requires sequence. Relations hold; they do not happen. There is no earlier
and no later in this paper, and no order in which anything is arrived at.

\emph{No cause.} Nothing here makes anything else be. Relation is not production, and the account has no
resources with which to say that one relatum brings about another.

\emph{No rule.} Nothing here forbids anything or licenses anything. There is no admissibility, nothing
that may or may not follow, and therefore nothing that could be violated.

Those three are not oversights and they are not deferred out of modesty. \emph{They cost extra.} A world
in which things also \emph{happen} requires transformation and a grammar of what may follow what, and
that is what the companion accounts introduce and pay for. This paper is what remains when they are
removed, and it is offered as evidence that they are separable rather than constitutive.

\section{Prior positions}
None of this is new and it should not be dressed as though it were. The neighbours are easy to name.
That relations are prior to, or on a level with, their relata is the ontic structural realist position
\citep{ladymanross2007,esfeld2004}; that a thing is nothing over and above what it stands in is older
and starker \citep{nagarjuna1995}; and the bundle tradition reaches a neighbouring conclusion by a
different route \citep{dcwilliams1953}.

The relation to them is not the usual one, and the difference can be stated exactly rather than as a
matter of emphasis. \emph{Each of those accounts supposes a particular \textbf{type} of relation where
the argument only requires that there be one.} Ontic structural realism is a thesis about physics ---
about what our best theories say the world is made of --- so the relation carrying its weight is
measurement: an observer edge, running from something that theorises to something theorised about. The
perspective literature does the same with an edge of viewing. Neither is mistaken about the structure;
both are over-specified about the edge.

The removal is immediate and is the method's own. Take the observer edge out and put any other edge in
its place --- a gravitational one, a chemical bond, mere adjacency, one part of a rock against
another --- and everything the structural claim needs is still there. Two relata and a relation between
them do not care which relation it is. So what those accounts treat as constitutive is a \emph{choice of
edge type}, salient in the domains they were written for and doing no work in the claim itself; and what
survives removing it is the claim this paper makes.

The same over-specification is visible in the two fields that own this vocabulary, which is worth saying
because they are the ones a reader will expect to have settled the matter. Information theory defines
its quantities over a source and a channel: there is something sending, something receiving, and a
distribution over what might have been sent. That is an observer edge again, wearing different clothes,
and it is why entropy is a property of a source-and-receiver arrangement rather than of a thing. And
graph theory, in its standard presentation, takes a vertex set first and defines edges as pairs drawn
from it --- which builds in the priority of relata over relations before any question about that
priority has been asked. Neither field is careless. Each fixed an edge type or an order of construction
because its own problems required one.

Which yields the deflationary version of this whole paper. \emph{If everything is a graph, then the
minimum structure required to be a graph is the minimum structure required, full stop.} That structure
is not something this paper discovers: it has been written down in every textbook of the subject for a
century, as relata and relations and nothing else. What is being done here is not deriving it but
\emph{taking it seriously} --- reading a definition that was built as a formalism as though it were an
answer to the largest question, and then checking that nothing else has to be added. The removals above
are that check. That is the entire contribution, and it is smaller than it sounds while being, if the
claim holds, sufficient.

One qualification even there, and it is not a criticism of the formalism. The textbook definition takes
a vertex set as given and edges as pairs over it, which makes relata prior --- and it does so for a good
reason. \emph{Graph theory's objects do not need to exist.} They are built, and building takes an order:
you must have the vertices in hand before you can say which pairs of them are edges. The priority is a
feature of \emph{construction}, and construction is what mathematics does.

Nothing here is constructed. What exists was not assembled in an order, and there is no stage at which
the relata were available and the relations not yet drawn. So the priority does not merely go unneeded
in this paper --- it is \emph{unavailable}, and for a reason already established: an order of
construction is a sequence, and there is no time here for one to run in. A definition that builds cannot
be read as a description of something that was never built without carrying its scaffolding across.

Which sharpens the earlier point rather than softening it. Graph theory is not over-specified for its
own purposes; it is exactly specified for constructing objects. What does not transfer is the
scaffolding, and noticing that it is scaffolding is most of the work of taking the definition
seriously.

That is also why the closest neighbour is the one furthest from physics. \citet{nagarjuna1995} makes the
claim in a form that does not require an observer edge --- nothing needs to be looking, measuring or
theorising for a thing to be nothing over and above what it stands in --- which is the property the
others lack, and which only became the discriminating one once the edge type was seen to be removable.
That reading is contested and the stronger form given here previously, that there is no observer edge
anywhere in the account, is not one the secondary literature licenses: conceptual or nominal dependence
is standardly the third and strongest of the dependence relations at issue, and on that reading the
carving is ours. What the comparison needs is only the weaker claim, that the position is
\emph{statable} without the edge, which both readings allow.

There remains the fact that this is simple enough to state in a sentence and appears not to have been
stated, which invites the reaction that it must therefore be either trivial or wrong. The account has its own explanation
for that pairing and it is in the companion on assessment, so a short one will do here, and the short one
is structural rather than a remark about incentives. \emph{People do not study everything. They study
something.} A field is constituted by its restriction --- what it attends to and what it sets aside ---
and a field with no restriction is not a field. So the question this paper asks, which has to be put at
maximum scope or it is not the same question, belongs to no discipline: there is no subject matter of
which it is the subject matter, and no method calibrated for it, because every method is calibrated to
a something.

That is why the general form goes unwritten while every local version gets written well.

And the mechanism has a worked case in this paper already, which is worth pointing at because it makes
the explanation checkable instead of merely available. The gap between double entry being in daily
use and the group of differences being written down is roughly four hundred years, on top of the two
centuries between the practice and Pacioli's description of it. That is not a hard construction ---
it is how the integers are built from the naturals --- and it was not being hidden from anyone. It went
unwritten because it belonged to nobody: merchants had no reason to state abstractly what they could
already do, and mathematicians were not reading ledgers, so a trivial structure in continuous use by
thousands of people sat unformalised in the gap between two restrictions.

The parallel is in the sociology and not in the content, and the difference matters. What double entry
says is not what this paper says. What the case establishes is that \emph{a thing can be simple, in
constant use, and unwritten for centuries, for no reason except that no field owned it} --- which is the
mechanism this section claims, evidenced somewhere it can be dated. Whether this paper's claim is
another instance of it is not something the case decides, and a reader who thinks the sentence is
trivial and wrong rather than trivial and unwritten is disagreeing with the claim, which is the right
place to disagree with it. It also
explains, without needing a separate reason, why such statements tend to arrive from someone crossing
between domains: crossing is what asking at maximum scope looks like from inside an arrangement where
every position is a restricted one.

The claim of absence should be held loosely, though, and more loosely than the rest of the paper. It is
the kind of claim this programme has repeatedly got wrong, always in the same direction --- the prior
art nearer than expected --- and the sentence above is one nobody could be confident of having searched
exhaustively. It is offered as an invitation: if this has been written down as a claim about what
exists, rather than as a formalism or as a thesis within one field, the right response to this paper is
the reference.

What is contributed is accordingly not the thesis but its \emph{position}: that it is enough on its own;
that time, cause, rule, an observer, and any axiom about where distinctions may be made from are not
required for it; and that an account of how things change can be built on top without folding any of
them back in. The companion papers are that account, and their first move is to assume this one.

\section{Falsifiability}\label{sec:falsifiability}
Three commitments. The first is what the paper claims; the second is what the claim predicts and is the easiest to test; the third is a claim the paper
makes about its own method.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{To be is to be related.} A claim about this instance and not about every
possible one. Fails on the exhibition of an extant something standing in no relation whatever --- not merely one whose relations are unknown or unmeasured, which is
the ordinary condition of most things, but one for which the claim that it stands in any relation at all
is false. The burden is entirely on that side: no enumeration establishes the claim, since the domain
does not terminate, and one instance would bound it.

An objection to this commitment has to be met, and meeting it says what kind of claim the proposition
is. The objection runs: the paragraph above holds that the description of an unrelated existent
\emph{picks out nothing}, so the falsifying case is one in which there is no fact to report, and a
falsifier nothing could answer to is not a falsifier. The first half is granted. The conclusion is not,
because it assumes that a falsifier which cannot be \emph{coherently} met is thereby not a falsifier,
and the distinction those two run together is the one this whole account is about.

The commitment is falsifiable and it is not coherently falsifiable, and the second is the content of
the first rather than a defect in it. A claim with no content has no attemptable counterexample at all:
put one to a vocabulary and you get another expression in the vocabulary, which is not a failure of
anything. Put one to this claim and the attempt has a determinate outcome --- it decoheres, in the
companion's exact sense, returning nothing with the whole of the frame as residual. That outcome is
checkable, and it is distinguishable by inspection from the vocabulary case: \emph{I tried and got
nothing back} is a different result from \emph{there was nothing to try}. What the objection has found
is not that the claim risks nothing. It is that the claim is at maximum scope, so the only thing that
could refute it is the one thing the claim says cannot be assembled --- and a question whose refutation
would have to be incoherent is the most a question of this shape can be, not the least.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Compression moves toward graph form.} A consequence of the first and
easier to test. Fails if a field, pushing its own formalism toward the shortest account that still
suffices, moves \emph{away} from relata-and-relations --- adopting under compression pressure a
primitive that is neither, and \emph{keeping} it --- carrying it in the formalism the field teaches and
computes with rather than in a reduction nobody uses. What is asked is retention and not
irreducibility, because graphs are expressive enough that almost any candidate can be argued to
reduce, and a test whose confirming door is shut by that fact must not have its refuting door bolted
by it too.

Read strictly, that is already failed, and the failure is visible in this paper's own worked example.
\emph{The complete graph on a million vertices} keeps an arity and a count, and neither is a relatum or
a relation; every confirming case above keeps something similar --- amplitudes, irrep labels, branch
lengths, edge weights --- and each is taught and computed with. So the commitment cannot be about
whether a formalism carries \emph{only} relata and relations, since none does and none is likely to.
What it can be about, and is here restricted to, is the \emph{bearer of the field's distinctions}:
whether what a field individuates and quantifies over under compression pressure is relata and
relations, with the rest carried as labels on them, or whether the individuated thing is something
else. A field whose primitive bearer of distinction stopped being relata-and-relations would fail this.
That is narrower than the sentence it replaces and it is what the evidence offered actually supports.
It also still lacks an operationalisation of \emph{compression pressure}, which is the remaining hole.
Expressiveness is not at issue in the other direction either: that a subject \emph{can} be drawn as a
graph is no evidence, and only the direction of movement under compression counts.

\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The method's yield is low.} The claim that the operation of declining an
inherited restriction returns nothing on almost every attempt, which this paper leans on --- it is what
makes a rare result evidence of a discipline rather than of a machine for producing results. It is also
a base rate over attempts nobody recorded, which is a defect and is stated as one. It fails if a
record of such attempts, kept prospectively rather than reconstructed, shows the operation succeeding
often; and this paper owes that record before the claim should be granted.
\end{enumerate}

One objection to the first of these has to be met, because an earlier version of this section raised it
and was wrong. It runs: exhibiting a counterexample means observing it, observing relates it to the
observer, so anything offered as unrelated is related by the act of being offered, and the falsifier
cannot be met.

That slides from \emph{relation} to \emph{observation}, and the two are not the same. \emph{Observation
is not required for existence.} The tree falls in the empty forest and stands in relation throughout ---
to the ground it strikes, to the air it moves, to its own fibres as they part --- and not one of those
relations waits on anybody looking. Observation is a \emph{kind} of relation, and a late and expensive
one, since it requires a frame with the machinery to make a distinction; relation is the general case
and requires nothing.

So the claim here is not that to be is to be perceived, which is a different thesis with a different
name and is not held. What is claimed is that to be is to be \emph{related}, and the falsifier asks for
something standing in no relation whatever --- not for something unobserved, of which there is a great
deal. That makes the commitment harder to meet rather than easier, since a candidate cannot be rescued
by pointing out that it is now related to whoever produced it: the question is whether it stood in any
relation at all, and the fallen tree settles that without an observer anywhere.

Proposition~\ref{prop:one-way} survives this unchanged. What merely is still cannot observe. It simply
does not follow that it needs to be observed in order to be.

A second thing would count against the paper without falsifying the proposition, and is worth stating
separately: if time, cause, or rule turned out not to be separable --- if the account of change built on
this one could not be constructed without folding one of them back into the base --- then the restraint
claimed in Section~\ref{sec:not-claimed} would be a fiction and this paper would be an incomplete statement of the base
rather than a minimal one.
