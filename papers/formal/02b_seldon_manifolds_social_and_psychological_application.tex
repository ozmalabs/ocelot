\begin{abstract}
Four classical experiments were put to a substrate built for other reasons, and one of the four does
not reproduce. Contact comfort \citep{harlow1958} does, with an ablation control establishing which
structure carries it, and so does learned helplessness \citep{seligman1967}, with the contingency
manipulation that makes it a result about coupling rather than about aversiveness.
The delay-of-gratification task does something more useful than reproduce: the substrate exhibits,
constructively, why the measure is confounded --- identical dispositions produce different wait
times under different beliefs about reliability, and dispositions differing fourfold produce
identical wait times under compensating beliefs. That agrees with what the empirical literature
already found \citep{kidd2013,watts2018}, which is the point rather than a discovery: an account
that had to be told this would be worth less than one in which it falls out. The fourth is a negative and is reported as the most informative of them: face inversion
\citep{yin1969} and the Thatcher illusion \citep{thompson1980} are \emph{not} reproduced, because the
substrate has no privileged upright frame to be costly to leave --- a diagnosed boundary rather than a
failure, and one that carries a commitment about what adding the missing frame should do.
\emph{Nothing here is evidence about children or monkeys.} It is evidence about what this substrate
does, and the paper is careful throughout about which of the two is being claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{What this paper is, and what it is not}\label{sec:scope}
\emph{Seldon} says what a Seldon manifold is; the Seldon grounding asks whether a biological
nervous system implements anything of that shape and answers in pieces. This paper runs the third
question, which is the one the substrate can settle on its own: put the substrate in the position of
a classical experimental subject and report what it does.

\emph{The results below are about a substrate and not about people.} A simulation that reproduces an
experimental finding has established something about the simulation. It becomes evidence about the
world only through an argument this paper does not make and the Seldon grounding makes only in
pieces. What a reproduction does establish is narrower and still worth having: that the finding is
\emph{available} from the substrate's own machinery without being put there, which is a fact about
what that machinery entails.

Two disciplines are enforced by the runs themselves rather than promised here. Each records the
commit it ran against and the seed it ran with, so a claim below is either reproducible from the
record or withdrawn. And each carries a \emph{source audit} --- an automated check that the
decisions were taken by graph rules rather than by the host language, reported in the record with
the run.

One standard governs the whole of that, and this paper is \emph{entirely} such a report. What is
offered here is a pre-registered implementation record which is not circulated with these papers,
and it is a claim a reader may decline to credit on exactly the terms Graphic Equalisation sets for
the same case. The commits, the seeds and the audits make each claim below reproducible by the
programme and not by a reader holding only this paper; a reader who declines to credit them is
declining on terms this paper accepts rather than disputes. Such a report states what was compared,
what was found, and the scope the run holds under; it names no implementation identifiers, and it
is not re-disclaimed at each section below.

\section{Contact comfort}\label{sec:harlow}
\citet{harlow1958} found infant macaques preferring a cloth surrogate offering no food to a wire
surrogate offering it, and fleeing to the cloth under stress. The substrate was given a contact
drive and the same two-surrogate arrangement.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
$n=200$ per arm & \textbf{contact drive present} & \textbf{ablated}\\
\midrule
cloth-directed fraction & $0.797$ \ [$0.792$, $0.803$] & $0.000$ \ [$0.000$, $0.000$]\\
flee-to-cloth under stress & $1.000$ \ [$1.000$, $1.000$] & $0.000$ \ [$0.000$, $0.000$]\\
infants preferring cloth & $200/200$ & $0/200$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\emph{What the interval is over needs saying, because two different estimands sit in that table.}
Each of the $200$ subjects contributes a \emph{fraction} of its own directed behaviour, and the
interval is the normal-approximation interval on the mean of those fractions --- between-subject
$\mathrm{sd}=0.039$, so $\pm 1.96\,\mathrm{sd}/\sqrt{n}$. It is \emph{not} a binomial interval on a
proportion of subjects, which at this $n$ would be about ten times wider ($[0.742, 0.853]$). The
across-subject count is the third row and is reported separately for that reason.

Several cells in this table and in the one in Section~\ref{sec:helplessness} are degenerate
intervals ($[1.000, 1.000]$ or $[0.000, 0.000]$). \emph{A degenerate interval records zero
between-subject variance at this $n$} --- every subject returned the same value, the mechanism
being deterministic given its inputs --- and is not a claim of certainty about the quantity.

The intervals are separated and the preference is above half, which is the finding. The second
column is what makes it worth reporting: \emph{ablating the contact drive removes the preference
entirely rather than reducing it}, so the effect is carried by that structure and not by the
arrangement. A glass-box readout taken from the graph rather than from
behaviour agrees with the behavioural measure: over the sixty-one ticks of one subject's run, the
serialised graph records the cloth surrogate as the chosen approach target on $53$ of them and the
wire surrogate on $8$. The proximity figure is that tally of ticks and not a distance.

\begin{remark}[The ethics gate, and why a simulation needs one]\label{rem:ethics}
Harlow's procedure is among the most criticised in the discipline's history, and a substrate that
reproduces it is running a deprivation protocol on something. The run therefore carries a gate: the
stressed state must be restored \emph{byte-identically} afterwards, verified by comparison of the
serialised graph. It is: arousal moves $63 \rightarrow 347 \rightarrow 63$ across deprivation and
restoration with the restoration non-oriented, and the graph returns byte-identical.

This is offered as a discipline rather than as a settlement. \emph{Byte-identical restoration
establishes that nothing persisted, not that nothing happened.} Whether a state that is entered and
perfectly reversed is thereby of no account is a question this paper raises and does not answer, and
a reader who thinks the gate insufficient is disagreeing with something the paper also does not
claim to have settled.
\end{remark}

\section{Delay of gratification}\label{sec:marshmallow}
The delay task is the more interesting case, because the substrate does not merely reproduce it.

The arrangement is a factorial grid: three dispositions (a drive weight of $2$, $4$, $8$) crossed
with three beliefs about how reliable the promised second reward is ($60$, $80$, $100$ on a scale of
$100$), $n=60$ per cell, grid fixed in advance of the run.

\begin{remark}[Both factors are real, and each has a null control]\label{rem:dissolution}
Holding the belief distribution fixed and varying disposition alone gives mean waits of $12.05$
[$10.40$, $13.67$] against $4.13$ [$3.68$, $4.58$], intervals separated. Holding disposition fixed
and varying belief alone gives $2.92$ [$2.60$, $3.27$] against $13.23$ [$11.63$, $14.97$], separated.

Each has a matched null control in which the factor is held \emph{constant} and the intervals are
required to overlap, which they do in both cases. A run in which the machinery manufactured an
effect would fail there, and the controls are reported because a positive result without them says
less than it appears to.

Two quantities are separately operative here, and \emph{Seldon}'s two-field construction
(Definition~\ref{SM-def:trap}) is the theoretical form of the same situation: where two fields both
bear on a continuation, the comparison between them cannot be stated within either. The measure
below is a single number asked to carry both.
\end{remark}

\emph{The measure is not identifiable for disposition}, and the grid shows it in both directions.
One disposition ($w=4$) under three beliefs ($60$, $80$, $100$) waits $3$, $8$, $13$ ticks: a naive
score reads one subject as three levels of self-control. Three dispositions differing fourfold
($w=2,4,8$) under compensating beliefs ($60$, $68$, $85$) wait $5$, $5$, $5$: the same score reads
three subjects as identical.

\begin{remark}[Impatience as the rational output of a correct belief]\label{rem:rational-impatience}
The sharpest form holds disposition fixed at its \emph{most patient} setting and varies only the
belief. Under an unreliable prior it waits $5.12$ [$4.90$, $5.32$]; under a reliable one, $23.82$
[$23.60$, $24.03$]. The intervals are separated. The \emph{deterministic anchors} at those two cells are $5$ and
$25$: the mechanism carries no noise, so one subject's wait at a given cell of the grid is exact,
and the $n=60$ spread quoted above comes from the jitter in the disposition and belief drawn for
each subject rather than from the mechanism.

So a patient subject waits briefly, and does so because waiting briefly is what its belief
recommends. \emph{The short wait is attributable to the prior and not to a deficit}, and no
disposition needs to be posited to explain it. What a naive reading scores as poor self-control is
here a correct inference from an environment that does not keep its promises.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[This agrees with the literature, which is the point]\label{rem:priority}
\citet{kidd2013} showed children's wait times moving with experimentally manipulated reliability of
the environment, and \citet{watts2018} found the long-run predictive power of the original measure
much reduced once background is controlled. The finding above is theirs and is not claimed here.

What is claimed is smaller and different in kind. \emph{A substrate assembled for unrelated reasons,
carrying no term for self-control and no term for the task, produces the confound as a consequence
of its ordinary machinery.} An account that had to be told about the confound would be worth less
than one in which it falls out, and this is the second. That is a fact about the substrate's
entailments and not additional evidence for the psychology, which already has better.
\end{remark}

\section{Learned helplessness, and what the contingency manipulation shows}\label{sec:helplessness}
\citet{seligman1967} established that the deficit follows from \emph{non-contingency} between action
and outcome rather than from aversive experience as such, which is what the contingent control arm is
for. Both arms were run at $n=200$ over $60$ ticks.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
$n=200$ per arm & \textbf{contingent} & \textbf{non-contingent}\\
\midrule
contingency belief & $1.000$ \ [$1.000$, $1.000$] & $0.256$ \ [$0.253$, $0.259$]\\
escape-attempt rate & $1.000$ \ [$1.000$, $1.000$] & $0.031$ \ [$0.031$, $0.032$]\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Intervals over per-subject means as above, separated on both measures. \emph{The arms differ only in
whether the action is coupled to the outcome}, and the aversive experience is matched --- which is
the point of the original design and the reason the result is about contingency and not about
suffering. The same ethics gate applies: belief and arousal move under induction
($0.500 \rightarrow 0.245$; $0.0 \rightarrow -9.25$ --- the deterministic trace of the induction
episode, the $0.256$ in the table being the $n=200$ mean over the reported arms) and restore
byte-identically.

\section{A negative: no privileged upright frame}\label{sec:inversion}
Recognition of inverted faces is disproportionately impaired relative to other inverted objects
\citep{yin1969}, and local feature inversion within an upright face is far more visible than within an
inverted one \citep{thompson1980}. \emph{The substrate does not reproduce either, and the reason is
diagnosed rather than guessed.}

Four pre-registered arms. An inversion-cost arm returns a differential of exactly zero --- the
differential being the configural channel's inversion cost minus the part-based channel's, each
cost being the fraction of inverted patterns no longer recognised as their own identity: configural
and part-based recognition are identical upright and inverted. A Thatcher arm finds no frame gating in
$12$ of $12$ configural cases. A reference-frame arm collapses all $4$ inversion pairs --- the
substrate treats a configural pattern and its $180^{\circ}$ twin as \emph{one identity} --- while a
part-based control remains discriminable, so the collapse is a property of the configural channel and
not of the measure. A floor arm confirms that both verdicts the measure can return are reached --- upright and
inverted exemplars are recognised as their own identity, and the locally-inverted probe is not
recognised --- so the zero differential is a measurement and not a dead instrument; $100$ noise
seeds force no spurious maps.

\begin{remark}[Why this negative is the useful kind]\label{rem:negative}
The mechanism is identified: uniform inversion folds to a rotation the composition already treats as
free --- a transform in the basis the composition holds, in Graphic Equalisation's sense, rather
than a new thing to be recognised --- so \emph{there is no privileged upright frame to be costly to
leave}. The substrate is
reference-frame invariant where a human visual system is not, and the invariance is the same property
that makes the composition work elsewhere.

That is a boundary on this programme and is reported as one. A paper reporting only reproductions
would be the less informative document, and the correct reading of this arm is not that the substrate
is deficient but that \emph{holistic upright-biased processing is an additional commitment a
psychology has and this substrate does not} --- available to be added, and not present by accident.
\end{remark}

\section{What the runs enforce}\label{sec:discipline}
The runs carry automated gates whose results are reported with them, because a claim that
the decisions were taken in the substrate rather than in the host language is not one a reader
should take on trust.

The delay run records zero occurrences of the host rule-engine entry points, and zero occurrences of
reinforcement vocabulary --- no reward signal, no punishment, no reinforcement, no backpropagation,
no gradient --- so the waiting is not shaped. The decisions are named: valuation by two graph rules,
choice by a third taking an argmax over candidate values, with the host confined to setting the
disposition, supplying the belief, sweeping elapsed time and reading the chosen action. World
constants are integers and are listed. The canonical seed set is checked untouched by digest.

\emph{None of this establishes that the model is right.} It establishes that the model is the thing
being reported, which is a different and lower bar, and one that a great deal of published
simulation work does not clear.

\section{The wider record}\label{sec:record}
Four experiments are reported here in full, and they come from three different lines of the same
substrate's record rather than from one. Contact comfort and delay of gratification come from the
affect line, which runs on drives, appraisal and the regulation of affect; learned helplessness
comes from the social-simulation line, which runs on conduct, institutional decision, bond
formation and the dynamics of coercive relationships; face inversion comes from the vision line.
Each of the three holds a larger body of pre-registered runs than the four reported here. No count
of them is given: a count is a thing that can be held while the list beneath it changes, so what is
claimed here is the provenance of the four and not the size of what they were drawn from.

That record is not summarised here and should not be read as supporting anything above. \emph{A
result is reported in this programme when it has been written up against a pre-registration, not
when it exists}, and each of the four above stands on that footing. The remainder is named so that
a reader knows the selection was made from something, and so that a later paper reporting more of
it is understood as continuing this one rather than announcing a new result.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}

\noindent\emph{On the tags.} Each commitment below is marked \textsc{[shape]} or \textsc{[detail]}.
A \textsc{[detail]} failing is a revision: the account survives with that mechanism replaced. A
\textsc{[shape]} failing costs \emph{range} rather than a mechanism, because what fails is something
the substrate is claimed to require or to permit --- so the account does not hold where it claimed to,
which is the more serious of the two and is still a boundary rather than an annihilation.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Contact preference is carried by the contact drive.} Fails if the
preference survives ablation of that drive at comparable magnitude, in which case the arrangement
rather than the drive is producing it.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The delay measure is not identifiable for disposition.} Fails if some
assignment of beliefs recovers disposition uniquely from wait time in this substrate --- that is, if
the two directions exhibited above can both be blocked without adding structure for the purpose.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Impatience does not require a deficit.} Fails if a short wait cannot
be produced from a patient disposition by belief alone, which is the arm reported in
Remark~\ref{rem:rational-impatience} and would be settled against by its non-replication.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Helplessness follows non-contingency, not aversive experience.} Fails
if the contingent arm develops the deficit at comparable magnitude, the aversive experience being
matched across arms by construction --- in which case the manipulation is not doing the work the
design attributes to it.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The inversion cost is absent because a privileged upright frame is
absent.} \emph{Fails if adding a privileged upright frame does not restore the inversion cost.} This
is the negative of Section~\ref{sec:inversion} stated as a claim about what the missing commitment
\emph{is}, rather than as a report that something did not happen: the diagnosis says uniform inversion
folds to a rotation the composition treats as free, so a composition that does not treat it as free
should show the cost. If it does not, the diagnosis is wrong and the absence has some other cause.
The arm adding that frame has not been run, so this entry states what \emph{would} be risked rather
than what currently is.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The pair collapse belongs to the configural channel.} Fails if the
part-based control also collapses under the same manipulation, which would make the result a property
of the measure rather than of what is being measured.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The waiting is not shaped.} Fails on any occurrence of reinforcement
machinery in the path that produces the choice; the audit is reported with each run and can be
re-run.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Scope note (not a falsifier).} Reproduction is not evidence about organisms. No claim
below the level of the substrate is made anywhere above, and a reader who finds one has found an
error rather than a result --- which is a fact about what this paper says rather than a commitment
that could be settled against it.
