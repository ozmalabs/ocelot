\begin{abstract}
A system that answers a question only when it can certify the answer, and otherwise returns an honest
miss, is not a competitor on a benchmark. It is an \emph{auditor} of one. Run across the text-only
portion of Humanity's Last Exam \citep{phan2025}, deriving every answer before the key is read, it
certified $43$ answers: $36$ confirm their keys and $5$ convict them, with no accusation withdrawn and
one of its own earlier flags retracted after re-derivation found a sign error in the system rather
than in the key. The five convictions are set out with their diagnoses. \emph{Coverage is $1.8\%$ and
that is the honest headline}: this is not a result about how much the system can answer, it is a
result about what certified answering makes visible when it is pointed at the key rather than at the
score.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why a frontier benchmark must contain errors}\label{sec:why}
This is arithmetic and not an accusation, and stating it first prevents the rest reading as one.

A benchmark of some $2{,}370$ research-grade questions is authored at the pointy end of every field it
covers, which is exactly where its reviewers stand at the edge of their own knowledge. At any
plausible post-review error rate the expected number of wrong keys exceeds one. \emph{The instrument
built to test reasoning is bound by the same arithmetic of error rates as everything else}, and a
benchmark with no wrong keys would be the surprising object, not this.

What follows is therefore not a complaint about the benchmark, which is a serious piece of work whose
difficulty is the reason it is useful here. It is an argument that a certain kind of answering system
is the right instrument for finding the errors any such benchmark must have.

\section{Certification turns answering into auditing}\label{sec:audit}
The system answers only where it can derive the answer under an independent certificate --- a second
derivation by a structurally different route, a held-exact computation, or a conservation or limiting
case forced by the stem. Where it cannot, it returns a miss. \emph{The key is read only afterwards,
and only to compare.}

That ordering is what makes the arrangement an audit. Every certified answer puts one key on trial and
returns one of two verdicts: it confirms the key, or it convicts it. A conviction is banked only when
the derivation is certified sound, the key's error is demonstrable, and no reading of the stem rescues
the key --- the last checked adversarially, by trying to rescue it.

\begin{remark}[Zero false accusations is the whole of the value]\label{rem:zero}
An audit claiming three convictions with airtight proofs of each is worth more than one claiming
thirty. The discipline is therefore asymmetric on purpose: a disagreement is not a conviction, and a
sound-but-rescuable disagreement is recorded and \emph{not} banked (Section~\ref{sec:tierb}).

The system also retracts against itself. One earlier physics flag was withdrawn when re-derivation
located a sign error in the system rather than in the key. \emph{An auditor that has never withdrawn
anything has not been audited}, and the withdrawal is reported here for that reason rather than
omitted as untidy.
\end{remark}

\section{The five convictions}\label{sec:convictions}
Each is stated as: what the key says, what was certified, and where the key's reasoning goes wrong.
The diagnosis matters more than the disagreement --- a conviction that cannot say \emph{why} the key
is wrong is only a second opinion.

\paragraph{Mathematics --- rank of a Hurewicz kernel (key $28$, certified $95$).}
For $Y = X_1 \# X_2 \# X_3$ with $\pi_1(Y) = \mathbb{Z}/5 * \mathbb{Z}/8 * \mathbb{Z}/2$, the kernel of
abelianisation is free by Kurosh, and its rank is $95$ by three independent routes: an orbifold Euler
characteristic ($\chi(K) = 80\cdot(-47/40) = -94$, rank $= 1 - \chi(K)$), a Schreier deficiency count,
and an explicit degree-$80$ cover ($V=80$, $E=240$, $F=66$). \emph{The key computed
$\prod(n_i - 1) = 28$, which is the correct kernel rank only for \textbf{two} free factors} --- verified
against $[5,8] \rightarrow 28$, and against a divergent control $[2,2,2]$ where the construction gives
the correct $5$ and the key's formula gives $1$.

\paragraph{Physics --- sudden expansion of an infinite well (key $4.03\%$, certified $0\%$).}
The ground state of a well of width $L$ with the right wall moved \emph{instantaneously} to $3L$. Under
a sudden change the state vector does not have time to evolve, so the expectation of the energy is
unchanged. \emph{A decrease of $4.03\%$ requires $\langle E \rangle$ to change, which the word
``instantaneous'' in the stem forbids.}

\paragraph{Chemistry --- contracted basis functions for toluene in 6-31G (key $54$, certified $79$).}
Counting per atom under 6-31G gives $9$ for carbon and $2$ for hydrogen, so
$7\cdot 9 + 8\cdot 2 = 79$. \emph{The key's $54$ corresponds to no coherent count} --- it matches none
of the standard countings of the same basis.

\paragraph{Mathematics --- domino tilings of an Aztec diamond (key $6$, certified $8$).}
The tiling count of an Aztec diamond of order $n$ is $2^{n(n+1)/2}$ \citep{elkies1992}, so it is a
power of two for every $n$: $2$, $8$, $64$, $1024$, and so on. \emph{Six is not a power of two.} The
certified answer of $8$ is the order-two count, obtained by two independent routes. This is the
cleanest of the five, because refuting the key requires only the parity of a theorem and no
recomputation of the question at all.

\paragraph{Computer science --- true boolean expressions of length five (key $44$, certified $47$).}
Two structurally independent routes --- filtering all $7^5$ strings through a recursive-descent parser,
and generating bottom-up from the grammar --- both yield $90$ valid expressions of which $47$ are true,
on the identical string set. \emph{The key is self-refuting}: the setter's own enumeration includes two
expressions that evaluate to false while filed as true, and omits five genuinely true expressions of
equal standing. It retains both redundant parentheses and repeated negation, so no consistent grammar
excludes what it excludes, and no precedence convention yields $44$ --- standard precedence gives $47$,
the opposite convention gives $43$.

\section{What was not convicted, and why that section matters more}\label{sec:tierb}
Six further questions produced a sound internal value disagreeing with the key, and none was banked,
because in each case a reading of the stem rescues the key. \emph{These are the cases that make the
five above worth anything.}

They include an underdetermined projectile problem where the explosion energy is unspecified, a
liquid-mirror exponent that is regime-dependent on an unstated rest depth, an $\alpha$-range question
reproducible under one range law but not the textbook one, and two convention- or rounding-dependent
values.

The sharpest is a graph-theoretic fixed-point count where an exhaustive search over the standard atlas
finds \emph{seven} against the key's six, and the sole difference is the single-vertex graph $K_1$ ---
which is connected, and is a fixed point, and which the setter's own code excludes by filtering
degree-zero vertices. \emph{That exclusion is defensible, so no conviction was recorded}, even though
the literal reading of the stem gives seven. An audit willing to accuse here would be worth less
everywhere.

\section{What this is not}\label{sec:not}
\emph{Coverage is $43$ questions of $2{,}370$, which is $1.8\%$.} The system declines the overwhelming
majority of the benchmark, and nothing here should be read as a claim about how much of it can be
answered. A benchmark score and a certified audit are different measurements and the second is not a
weak version of the first.

Nor is any of it a claim about the reasoning of systems in general. The faculties are general rather
than per-question --- no hand-coding, no answer-baking, no reverse-engineering from keys --- but the
sample that reaches certification is selected precisely by being certifiable, which is a strong and
non-random condition. \emph{The right reading is that certified coverage doubles as audit at no
additional cost}, and the audit is what is reported.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Zero false accusations.} Fails on the exhibition of a rescuing reading
for any one of the five, in which case that conviction is withdrawn and the count is reported lower.
Each is stated with its diagnosis specifically so that this is attemptable.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The certificate is independent of the key.} Fails if any certified
value can be shown to depend on the key having been read, which the ordering is designed to prevent
and which the record is kept to make checkable.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The diagnoses locate the error.} Fails where a key is wrong for a
different reason than the one given --- the conviction survives and the explanation does not, which is
a distinct and weaker failure and is reported as such.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Tier-B is not a holding pen.} Fails if any Tier-B item is later
convicted on evidence available at the time it was set aside, which would mean the bar was applied
inconsistently rather than conservatively.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Coverage is not the claim.} This paper commits to the negative: no
statement about benchmark performance is made or implied, and a reader who extracts one has found an
error rather than a result.
\end{enumerate}
