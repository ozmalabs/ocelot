\begin{abstract}
This companion develops the cosmological groundings of the substrate, and its work is mostly negative in
a specific and useful way: it identifies which cosmological questions are ill-posed rather than merely
hard, and says why on the account's own results. The spine is the measure problem of eternal inflation,
which is the no-prior-free proposition in another domain --- a probability over pocket universes requires
a frame-free denominator, and the companion proves no such thing is a member of the space, so the
difficulty is not a missing regularisation but a request that cannot be met. Around that: the universe
\emph{as a whole} is one of the three degenerate readings the base paper already prices at zero, so the
observable universe is the object rather than a sample of one; a particle horizon is an instantiated
boundary relation, and what lies beyond it is neither absent nor concealed but \textsc{null} in this
frame --- a superframe dimension, real and related through the container we are a subframe of, and
unreadable from here in the way an entangled partner's purity is unreadable, so that what is refused is
the value and not the existence; and the cosmological principle
divides cleanly into an observation that survives and an inference that does not, the second leg being
modal reasoning of exactly the kind the base paper declines. \emph{This paper derives no cosmological
parameter, predicts no observation, and settles nothing empirical.} It says which questions have answers
and which have none, which is a smaller thing, and it agrees with the field's own instincts more often
than it corrects them --- homogeneity is called an assumption and the measure problem is called
unsolved, and both are correct in kind for reasons given here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, and what this adds}\label{sec:scope}
\emph{The physics companion already treats the initial state.} It gives the Big Bang as a chosen entry
rather than an intrinsic edge, the Past Hypothesis as a seed and therefore rightly an assumption, and
fine-tuning inverted by self-location. \emph{None of that is repeated here.} What follows takes up the
questions that are cosmological rather than initial: what the subject of cosmology is, what a horizon
is, what may be said about what lies past one, and what happens to probability when the sample space has
no outside.

\emph{Two disciplines carry over and are stated once.} The account prices operations and does not
license them, and it distinguishes what is \emph{observed} from what is \emph{modelled} --- a
distinction the base paper makes for its own object and which does most of the work below.

\section{The universe is not an object}\label{sec:not-an-object}
\emph{Cosmology's ordinary subject does not exist, and the base paper already says so.} That paper gives
three readings which each yield nothing: a single relatum with nothing to relate to, the empty case, and
\emph{everything at once read from outside, which has nothing left over to stand in relation to it}. The
universe as a whole is the third.

\begin{proposition}[The totality is a degenerate reading]\label{prop:totality}
There is no frame from which everything is the relatum, since a frame is an instantiated boundary
relation and a boundary requires something on the other side. \emph{And the containment does not
terminate}: whatever frame is offered as the totality is individuated by something, which is then
outside it. So \emph{the universe as a whole} names no object --- not because the outside is empty but
because there is always one more step outward, which is this programme's unbounded frontier read in the
extensive direction.
\end{proposition}

\emph{The consequence is a correction and not a limitation.} It is usual to say that we observe only
part of the universe and must infer the rest. On this account the observable universe is not a sample of
the object --- \emph{it is the object}, being what stands in relation to the frame doing the
cosmology. The horizon does not truncate a larger thing; it individuates the thing there is.

\emph{This is less revisionary than it sounds}, and the practice already runs this way. Cosmological
parameters are fitted to what is inside the horizon, and the fits do not degrade for want of what is
outside. A quantity that made no difference to any fit was doing no work in the account, which is the
base paper's own test.

\section{A horizon is a boundary relation}\label{sec:horizon}
\emph{A particle horizon is the boundary relation itself, not a fence inside a larger space.} The
companion's frame is an instantiated boundary $\mathcal{B}_{W:G}$, and individuation is performed by the
container rather than by the thing. A horizon individuates a cosmological frame in exactly that sense,
and its dependence on the observer's worldline is then expected rather than awkward: different
worldlines instantiate different boundaries, so they have different frames, and there is no frame
containing all of them from which to say which is right.

\emph{It does not follow that there is nothing outside, and the account says the opposite.} A frame is
individuated by its container, so a frame that is individuated \emph{has} one --- \emph{the boundary
relation is itself a relation to the outside}, and a cosmological frame could not be a frame without it.
\emph{There is an outside. We are not in it.}

\begin{proposition}[The relation beyond a horizon runs up, not across]\label{prop:beyond}
What lies past a horizon is not unrelated to us. It is related \emph{through the superframe we are both
subframes of} --- the physics companion's result that a connection between subframes runs up rather than
across --- and its content is accordingly a superframe dimension: \textsc{null} in our frame and
unreadable from here, which is \emph{not the same status as absent}. The missing value was never a
dimension of this frame, so failing to read it discards nothing locally available.
\end{proposition}

\emph{That distinction is the correction, and the entangled case already makes it.} A traced-out partner
is not information thrown away; the purity was never in the subframe. \emph{So what lies beyond a
horizon is real, related, and inaccessible} --- three properties ordinary usage runs together and the
account keeps apart.

\emph{What remains refused is only the reading.} An extrapolation past the horizon is a model of a
superframe dimension made from below, which is a legitimate thing to hold and an illegitimate thing to
call measured. \emph{The prohibition is on the value, not on the existence}, and it costs the practice
nothing, since no measurement inside discriminates between competing values of a dimension that is
\textsc{null} here.

\begin{remark}[Where this could be wrong, and it is testable]\label{rem:bubble}
\emph{This is not idle, and on the corrected reading it is not awkward either.} Searches for signatures
of bubble collisions in the microwave background are searches for a coupling to something past the
horizon. \emph{A detection would refute nothing here.} Measuring projects a superframe dimension into
the subframe's aperture, which is this account's standing description of what measuring does --- so a
found signature is a dimension becoming accessible and not an outside coming into being. \emph{The
aperture widens; the dimension was there.} What it would refute is this paper's assumption about the
present aperture, which is an assumption about us and not about the world.
\end{remark}

\section{The cosmological principle divides}\label{sec:cosmological-principle}
\emph{Isotropy is observed. Homogeneity is inferred, and the inference has a modal leg.} What is measured
is that the sky looks the same in every direction to high precision. What is wanted is that the universe
is the same everywhere, and the step between them is the Copernican principle: that our position is not
special among positions.

\begin{proposition}[The second leg is modal and is declined]\label{prop:copernican}
\emph{Our position is not special} compares this position against positions we do not occupy. The base
paper's commitment is that what bears on a claim is a demonstration and not an imagining, since
imagining a world costs nothing and settles nothing. \emph{So the inference from isotropy to homogeneity
beyond the horizon is not available on this account} --- not because nothing is out there, but because
its value is a superframe dimension and the inference reads one from below. What survives is isotropy,
which is measured, together with homogeneity within the horizon, which is also measured.
\end{proposition}

\emph{The field's own vocabulary already concedes this.} Homogeneity is called an assumption, not a
result, and it is called that by people with no interest in this account. \emph{What is added here is
not the suspicion but the reason}: it is not an assumption because the measurement is hard, but because
the quantity it asserts stands in no relation to the frame asserting it.

\section{The measure problem is the no-prior-free proposition}\label{sec:measure}
\emph{This is the case where the account does real work}, and it is the case cosmology already knows is
broken.

Eternal inflation produces unboundedly many pocket universes. To extract a prediction --- what a typical
observer sees, how likely a given constant is --- one needs a probability measure over them. \emph{The
theory does not supply one.} Different regularisations give different answers and some give absurd ones,
and the difficulty is named and open.

\begin{proposition}[The measure problem is ill-posed, not unsolved]\label{prop:measure}
A measure over all pockets requires a standpoint from which all of them are counted, and by the
companion's no-prior-free proposition the zero of such an axis is not a member of the space: a frame
standing in no relation to what it counts draws no distinction and returns nothing. \emph{So the
quantity being sought does not exist to be approximated.} The regularisations disagree because they are
supplying, by stipulation, a denominator the theory cannot supply --- and by the Narrativium companion's
distinction a stipulated conversion carries exactly the authority of whoever stipulated it, which is why
the answers track the stipulation rather than the physics.
\end{proposition}

\emph{The consequence is the same one the adaptability companion reaches for intelligence.} The
difficulty is not that the measurement is hard but that the control it needs is not constructible, and
an account that kept looking for a better regularisation would be searching for a member of an empty
set. \emph{This is a dissolution and not a solution}, and it predicts that the disagreement between
measures will not converge --- which is a claim about the literature's future rather than about the
world, and is checkable.

\begin{remark}[What this does not do to inflation]\label{rem:inflation}
\emph{Nothing here bears on inflation as physics.} Inflationary models make horizon-scale predictions
that are tested inside a frame and stand or fall on those. What is refused is a specific inferential
move --- probability over an ensemble with no outside --- and a theory can be entirely correct while one
inference drawn from it is malformed. \emph{Conflating the two would be the error this paper is
otherwise about.}
\end{remark}

\section{Anthropic reasoning inherits the same defect}\label{sec:anthropic}
\emph{Typicality arguments are the measure problem wearing a different hat}, and worse than that: the
term they turn on names two different objects, and the argument survives only by sliding between them.

\emph{Read one way, \textbf{universe} means the ensemble} --- all of them, the population a typical
member is typical of. \emph{Read the other, it means what the word ordinarily means}: the observable,
relative universe, the one a frame stands in relation to. Cosmological usage takes the second and infers
with the first.

\begin{proposition}[Typicality equivocates, and each reading fails separately]\label{prop:typical}
On the ensemble reading, a distribution over all of them requires the counting standpoint
Proposition~\ref{prop:measure} refuses, so there is no population and nothing to be typical of. On the
relative reading there is exactly one universe --- the frame's --- and \emph{typicality is undefined
over a set a thing exhausts}. \emph{Neither reading supports the inference}, and they fail for
unrelated reasons, which is why moving between them looks like an answer.
\end{proposition}

\emph{This is stronger than the usual complaint}, which is that the reference class is hard to choose.
The difficulty is not that the class is hard to pick but that on one reading there is no class and on
the other the class has one member.

\emph{The base paper's care about \textbf{everything} is the same care and should be repeated exactly.}
It does not say that everything \emph{is} nothing --- it says the unqualified identity is asserted from a
standpoint at which it does not hold, and that what is true is a claim about \emph{readings}: everything
at once, read from outside, has nothing left over to stand in relation to it and so yields nothing.
\emph{So \textbf{everything} does not name a population whose members could be counted. It names an
operation that returns nothing}, and a distribution cannot be defined over the output of that operation.

\emph{Between the two zeros is where the distinctions live}, which is the base paper's own sentence and
is the whole of what cosmology has to work with: not the ensemble, which reads as nothing, and not a
totality, which does not close --- but this frame, which is where everything measurable is.

\emph{The physics companion reaches the same place from the other direction} and the two should be read
together: there, fine-tuning is dissolved by self-location, since a different seed is a different
observer and there is no trans-seed standpoint from which to compare. Here the same absence removes the
distribution. \emph{One prohibition, met twice.}

\section{Two measurements that disagree}\label{sec:tension}
\emph{A word on the Hubble tension, and it is a word of caution rather than a result.} Early-universe and
late-universe determinations of the expansion rate disagree beyond their stated errors.

\emph{The account says only what it says.} By the adaptability companion's result a measurement is a
property of a four-place arrangement, and two determinations made under different arrangements are two
measurements rather than one measurement made twice --- so comparing them requires an argument that the
moved coordinate does not bear. \emph{That is exactly what the field is doing}, at length and carefully,
which is why this section claims nothing further. \emph{The account does not dissolve the tension, does
not favour either value, and does not predict which coordinate is at fault.} It says the comparison is
the work, which cosmologists established without help.

\section{What is not claimed}\label{sec:not-claimed}
\emph{No cosmological parameter is derived here, and none could be.} No observation is predicted. Nothing
above distinguishes between inflationary models, settles the expansion tension, or bears on dark energy's
equation of state.

\emph{Nor is any of the negative work a criticism of practice.} Homogeneity is already called an
assumption; the measure problem is already called unsolved; the tension is already treated as a
comparison problem. \emph{The account agrees with the field's instincts and supplies reasons for them},
which is worth less than a prediction and more than nothing.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}
\emph{An aperture wider than assumed.} A confirmed signature of a bubble collision, or of any structure
causally beyond the particle horizon, would show a superframe dimension to be accessible from here.
That refutes neither the account nor this paper's reading of what lies outside --- both hold that it is
there --- but it refutes this paper's assumption about the \emph{present aperture}, which is the
load-bearing assumption of the horizon section and is about us rather than about the world.

\emph{Convergence of measures.} If the regularisations of eternal inflation converge on one measure by
an argument internal to the theory, requiring no stipulation from outside, then
Proposition~\ref{prop:measure} is wrong and the problem was hard rather than ill-posed.

\emph{Anisotropy that survives.} Isotropy is the leg this paper keeps. A large-scale anisotropy that
survives scrutiny would remove the observed half of the cosmological principle, and this paper would
have kept the wrong leg.

\emph{Derivation of a seed.} If a cosmological initial condition is ever derived rather than installed
--- from within, with no boundary condition imported --- then the physics companion's seed result and
this paper's dependence on it both fail.
