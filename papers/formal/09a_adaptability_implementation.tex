\begin{abstract}
The operation the adaptability paper describes is built, has been measured on the agent's own corpus,
and has been corrected against itself, that correction being the principal result reported here. It is
also, in one specific and consequential way, in the wrong place. \emph{The algebra is native; the
faculty that uses it is not.}
\end{abstract}

\emph{The standing of what is reported here should be said once and then relied on.} Every figure
below comes from this programme's own pre-registered implementation record and from the working tree
that record describes, neither of which is circulated with these papers, and each is therefore a claim
a reader may decline to credit on exactly the terms Graphic Equalisation's Section~\ref{GE-sec:scope}
sets out. What is offered against that is exactness rather than authority: \emph{the counts in this
paper are generated from the tree rather than transcribed into it}, at commit \countsCommit{} read on
\countsReadOn, by a script kept beside the paper and re-run at every revision, so a reader who does
hold the tree can re-run them and a reader who does not can at least see what was counted.

\section{What is built}\label{sec:built}
The transform algebra is native to the engine. Anti-unification retaining its witness, substitution,
unification, structural similarity and a coverage predicate are all term-level operations, evaluable
inside the rule language rather than beside it. The shape ladder is built on them: decoding against a
held codebook, transformation within the held basis, inference of a dimension, and minting.

The ladder runs as rules. It is a rule fixpoint rather than a loop in the host language --- a residual
mints a quantum, a codebook epoch re-arms decoding, and the re-decode either recognises or does not.
That much is graph-resident and gated.

\section{What has run}\label{sec:run}
\begin{remark}[Transfer by parallelogram completion]\label{rem:parallelogram}
The strongest demonstration takes four of the agent's own rules arranged as a $2\times 2$ over two
independent dimensions. \emph{Parallelogram} is borrowed from the embedding-arithmetic literature and
the adaptability paper's Section~\ref{AD-sec:prior} sets out the lineage, along with the reason that
literature's known critique does not carry over to an exact symbolic operation. Anti-unification over
two corners derives the transform; substitution applies it to the third; the result is a rule
\emph{absent from every distinct recogniser the corpus held} --- $\nRecognisersAtRecord$ of them when
the transfer was measured, on \recogniserRecordOn{} --- which, installed, fires correctly on the missing
cell and on no other. \emph{That one is verified behaviourally.}

$318$ such families exist in the corpus, and $200$ of $200$ sampled transfers are algebraically
lossless, well-defined and novel. \emph{Algebraically is the whole of what that second figure says.}
One transfer was installed and run; the other $199$ establish that the operation is well-defined and
that its output is not already held, and not that the derived rule fires. Reading $200$ of $200$ as
$200$ installations would be reading a stronger result than was taken. \emph{No new term variants, no
graph mirror and no engine change were required} --- the capability was reachable with what already
existed.
\end{remark}
A family here is a represented two-by-two arrangement over two held dimensions,
with three corners supplied and the fourth reconstructed. Under the theory's
Section~\ref{AD-sec:ladder}, this is transformation in a held basis (rung two).
This report supplies no separate measured rung-three instance establishing
inference of a previously unheld dimension. The reported transfer therefore
establishes reusable transformation, not that stronger adaptation claim.



\begin{remark}[The concept-grain faculty, and its own correction]\label{rem:retraction}
At the concept grain the faculty has run against the agent's largest real bridge, $142$ concepts against
$137$, and reported $8{,}575$ analogies.

\emph{Of those, $8{,}253$ were an artefact}: a keyword matching \emph{inside} a longer word, which
accounted for $39.6\%$ of every vocabulary hit in the booted graph. After token-anchoring and a
requirement of structural support the count fell to $321$, of which $78$ are corroborated by a witness
shared across pairs.

That is reported here as the faculty's principal measured result, not as an embarrassment adjacent to
one. A ninety-six per cent retraction is what the correction was worth. \emph{What this paper stands
behind is the withdrawal}, which rests on a stated mechanical defect in the matcher and applies to
every one of the $8{,}253$. It does not stand equally behind the survivors: nobody has read the $321$
and confirmed each, so $321$ is an upper bound with $78$ corroborated inside it, and a reading of them
could move it down again.
\end{remark}

\section{The gap}\label{sec:gap}
\emph{The two halves above do not meet, and the seam is exactly locatable.}

Anti-unification with witness retained appears $\nWitnessInFaculty$ times in the faculty's
host-language module and $\nWitnessRetainingUses$ times across the $\nRules$ rules carried by the
$\nManifests$ seed manifests. The faculty is $\nFacultyLines$ lines of host code, and it \emph{does}
call the native algebra --- so this is not a capability that was built and left unused. It has run, it
has been measured, and its output survives correction.

What it is not is graph-resident. The transform algebra is reachable from the host language and not from
a rule.

\begin{remark}[Why that matters here more than anywhere else]\label{rem:selfrepair}
This programme's boundary on self-repair is sharp, and it is a standing rule of the implementation
record rather than a finding of this paper: \emph{the agent can repair and extend its cognition exactly
where that cognition is graph-native}, because what it can vary, inspect and mint against is graph
data. A faculty in the host language can be used and cannot be improved by the thing using it.

So of all the faculties that could have been left outside, \emph{this is the worst one} --- because it
is the adaptation mechanism. The system's ability to acquire competences it was not given is itself a
competence it cannot acquire. That is not a contradiction, but it is a ceiling, and it is the ceiling
the adaptability paper's ladder (its Section~\ref{AD-sec:ladder}) was built to remove.

The measured retraction above sharpens it. A ninety-six per cent artefact rate was found by an audit
performed \emph{on} the faculty from outside. A graph-resident faculty is the kind of thing this
substrate has repeatedly been made to notice about itself --- it files gaps, retires its own duplicates,
and surfaces what it cannot answer. \emph{The faculty most in need of that treatment is the one that
cannot receive it.}
\end{remark}

\section{What the gap is not}\label{sec:not}
It is not that the algebra is unreachable in principle. Neighbouring operations from the same family
are already invoked by rules, and the census is exact rather than impressionistic --- a match on the
term as a rule actually carries it, not on the term's name appearing somewhere in a file. The coverage
predicate is invoked $\nCoverageUses$ times, across $\nCoverageFiles$ seed manifests, and its open
variant $\nOpenCoverageUses$ times. The \emph{witness-discarding} anti-unification --- the classical
least general generalisation, which returns the generalisation and throws away the substitutions that
recover its inputs --- is invoked $\nWitnessDiscardingUses$ times, across $\nWitnessDiscardingFiles$
manifests. Structural similarity is invoked $\nStructuralSimilarityUses$ times.

\emph{Unification is invoked $\nUnificationUses$ times, and substitution $\nSubstitutionUses$.} The
substitution operation's name does occur as text in $\nSubstitutionNamedFiles$ manifests, none of which
invoke it --- which is what a looser search returns, and is why this census is taken on the term rather
than on the text.

\emph{That is a sharper finding than a bare absence, and it points the same way.} The vocabulary the
rule layer actually holds is the classical one: cover a case, and generalise two structures while
discarding what distinguished them. The two operations that carry a transform --- retaining the witness,
and re-applying it --- are exactly the two that no rule invokes. The adaptability paper's
Section~\ref{AD-sec:prior} states the consequence in its own terms: an lgg that throws away its witness
cannot adapt anything.

It is not that the demonstration is unsound. The parallelogram result stands on its own terms, and its
numbers are reported above with the caveat they deserve: it was demonstrated in analysis scripts rather
than as agent behaviour, so it establishes that the corpus \emph{contains} $318$ derivable rules, not
that the agent derived them.

And it is not a large amount of work in prospect. The absence of new term variants in the demonstration
is the relevant measurement: what would be required is a rule surface over an algebra that already
evaluates, which is the same move this programme has made before and reported.

\section{A second instance of the same seam}\label{sec:reading}
The pattern of Section~\ref{sec:gap} is not confined to analogy, and the clearest other case is the one
most directly about acquisition: reading a scientific paper and minting from what it says.

The path is built and is the ladder pointed at literature. An adapter presents a retrieved document
as a typed graph; the agent navigates a preprint archive, retrieves a paper, and grounds it. Grounding
the abstract yields the paper's \emph{subject} --- and the record's own note on that step is a negative
rather than a success: \emph{grounding to primitive was false}, so the agent knew what the paper was
about and not what it claimed. The body pass exists to close exactly that gap: the claim-bearing
environments are parsed, and each claim is grounded through the construction grammar --- a term as a
genus plus its differentia, recursively toward primitives already held --- carrying a \emph{depth}
alongside the result, so that grounded-to-topic and grounded-to-primitive are distinguishable outcomes
rather than one verdict.

\emph{And where a claim rests on a construction the agent does not hold, that is detected and queued to
acquire.} That is rung four with the residual named: not a failure to read, but a located absence with
a next action attached.

\begin{remark}[Its status, stated exactly]\label{rem:reading-status}
\emph{Two things are built here, only one of them is gated, and they are reported apart because
reporting them together would credit the second with the first's standing.}

The grounding faculty the path stands on --- taking a definition in prose and descending it toward
primitives already held --- is gated. Five tests cover it, two of them confirming that its seed is
load-bearing and installs only through the loader, so the faculty is not an artefact of a script
arranging its own inputs. \emph{What those tests exercise is the faculty on prose fed to it.} They do
not exercise the retrieval, the abstract-and-body split, or the depth distinction.

\emph{Those are built and are not gated.} The retrieval path and the body pass exist and their arc is
recorded in their own source; no test drives them. \emph{And no results document exists for either
half.} There is no figure for how many claims ground to primitive against topic, none for how often an
un-held construction is correctly detected, none for what happens to the queue. So the status is a
gated faculty, an ungated path built on it, and no measurement of the arc end to end --- and this paper
reports it at that status rather than at the one the machinery invites.

And the seam is the same one. The grounding faculty is host-resident, with a single seed referencing
it. So the two capabilities this programme has for acquiring what it was not given --- transfer between
held rules, and reading claims out of a document --- \emph{are both real, both only partly gated, and
both sit outside the boundary within which the system can repair itself}.
\end{remark}

\section{The contrast the record supplies}\label{sec:contrast}
The equality of solved task sets does not establish that held-content proposals
were consulted and found useless. Proposal-source attribution for that run is
not supplied here, so a consultation or wiring failure remains an alternative
explanation. The regime interpretation is consistent with the null, not
identified by it.

One further measurement belongs here, because it is uncomfortable in the right direction.

Program synthesis in this substrate is \emph{graph-native} in the way analogy is not: its enumerator is
a term, and the decisions --- propose, evaluate, accept --- are seed rules. And its clearest result
about held content is a null. Running the loop with the acquired content and running it stripped
produced $54$ of $257$ in both arms, and not merely as equal totals: \emph{the two arms returned
byte-identical result sets}, the same tasks solved and the same tasks missed.

So the record contains both of these at once. \emph{The faculty that is graph-native produced a clean
null about the value of what it held; the faculty that is not produced the strongest positive result
in the corpus, in analysis scripts.} Neither observation rescues the other, and the pair is reported
together because taking either alone would misrepresent the record --- the first is not a reason to
doubt the second, and the second is not a reason to discount the first.

The reading that survives both is narrow, and the adaptability paper's Remark~\ref{AD-rem:plural}
states its general form: capability has plural sources selected by regime, so which faculty happens to
be graph-resident says nothing about which one carries a given domain. \emph{Being graph-resident is
what makes a faculty improvable by the system carrying it, and it is not what makes a faculty work.}
The gap in Section~\ref{sec:gap} is a ceiling on self-repair, and this section is the reminder that
removing it would not by itself buy a capability.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}
Each commitment below is marked \textsc{[shape]} or \textsc{[detail]}, in the sense of Graphic
Equalisation's Section~\ref{GE-sec:falsifiability}. A \textsc{[detail]} failing is a revision: the
account survives with that mechanism replaced. A \textsc{[shape]} failing costs \emph{range} rather
than a mechanism, because what fails is something the substrate is claimed to require or to permit ---
so the account does not hold where it claimed to, which is the more serious of the two and is still a
boundary rather than an annihilation.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The two transform-carrying operations are absent from the rule layer.}
Fails on the exhibition of any seed rule invoking witness-retaining anti-unification \emph{or}
substitution. Both counts are $0$ across the $\nRules$ rules of the $\nManifests$ manifests, taken at
commit \countsCommit{} on \countsReadOn, and are a command rather than a judgement.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Self-repair is bounded by graph-nativeness.} Fails if the agent can be
shown to improve a host-language faculty by its own operation rather than by an author's --- which
would remove the ceiling this paper reports and much of its point.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The $318$ families are algebraically derivable and novel.} Fails if a
sampled transfer is found to be lossy, ill-defined, or to reproduce a recogniser already in the corpus.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{A transfer that passes the algebra fires.} Fails if a derived rule that
is lossless, well-defined and novel, once installed, does not fire on its missing cell or fires
elsewhere. One case has been run; this commits the other $317$.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The corrected analogy count is the defensible one.} Fails if
token-anchoring and the structural-support requirement can be shown to discard genuine correspondences
rather than artefacts, which would make $321$ an over-correction rather than a correction --- or, in
the other direction, if reading the $321$ finds a material fraction of them spurious, which would make
$321$ an upper bound that has not yet been brought down.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The body pass distinguishes topic from primitive.} Fails if, on a paper
whose claims rest only on constructions the agent already holds, the body pass grounds no claim to
primitive --- in which case the depth carried alongside the result is recording a distinction the
machinery does not in fact draw.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Scope note (not a falsifier).} The parallelogram result is about the corpus, not the
agent. No claim is made that the agent derived those rules, and a reader who extracts one has found an
error rather than a result.
