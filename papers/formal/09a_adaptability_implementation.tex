\begin{abstract}
The operation the companion describes is built, has run at scale, and has been measured, including a
large correction against itself. It is also, in one specific and consequential way, in the wrong place.
\emph{The algebra is native; the faculty that uses it is not.} Every count below was obtained by
running a command against the tree.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is built}\label{sec:built}
The transform algebra is native to the engine. Anti-unification retaining its witness, substitution,
unification, structural similarity and a coverage predicate are all term-level operations, evaluable
inside the rule language rather than beside it. The shape ladder is built on them: decoding against a
held codebook, transformation within the held basis, inference of a dimension, and minting.

The ladder runs as rules. It is a rule fixpoint rather than a loop in the host language --- a residual
mints a quantum, a codebook epoch re-arms decoding, and the re-decode either recognises or does not.
That much is graph-resident and gated.

\section{What has run}\label{sec:run}
\begin{remark}[Transfer by parallelogram completion]\label{rem:parallelogram}
The strongest demonstration takes four of the agent's own rules arranged as a $2\times 2$ over two
independent dimensions. \emph{Parallelogram} is borrowed from the embedding-arithmetic literature and
the companion sets out the lineage, along with the reason that literature's known critique does not
carry over to an exact symbolic operation. Anti-unification over two corners derives the transform; substitution applies it
to the third; the result is a rule \emph{absent from all $2{,}851$ distinct recognisers in the corpus}
which, installed, fires correctly on the missing cell.

$318$ such families exist in the corpus, and $200$ of $200$ sampled transfers are algebraically
lossless, well-defined and novel. \emph{No new term variants, no graph mirror and no engine change were
required to demonstrate this} --- the capability was reachable with what already existed.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The concept-grain faculty, and its own correction]\label{rem:retraction}
At the concept grain the faculty has run against the agent's largest real bridge, $142$ concepts against
$137$, and reported $8{,}575$ analogies.

\emph{Of those, $8{,}253$ were an artefact}: a keyword matching \emph{inside} a longer word, which
accounted for $39.6\%$ of every vocabulary hit in the booted graph. After token-anchoring and a
requirement of structural support the count fell to $321$, of which $78$ are corroborated by a witness
shared across pairs.

That is reported here as the faculty's principal measured result, not as an embarrassment adjacent to
one. A ninety-six per cent retraction is what the correction was worth, and the corrected number is the
one this paper stands behind.
\end{remark}

\section{The gap}\label{sec:gap}
\emph{The two halves above do not meet, and the seam is exactly locatable.}

Anti-unification with witness retained appears \textbf{seven times} in the faculty's host-language
module and \textbf{zero times} across the $3{,}843$ rules carried by the $805$ seed manifests. The
faculty is $1{,}241$ lines of host code, and it \emph{does} call the native algebra --- so this is not a
capability that was built and left unused. It has run, it has been measured, and its output survives
correction.

What it is not is graph-resident. The transform algebra is reachable from the host language and not from
a rule.

\begin{remark}[Why that matters here more than anywhere else]\label{rem:selfrepair}
This programme's boundary on self-repair is sharp and is stated elsewhere in it: \emph{the agent can
repair and extend its cognition exactly where that cognition is graph-native}, because what it can vary,
inspect and mint against is graph data. A faculty in the host language can be used and cannot be
improved by the thing using it.

So of all the faculties that could have been left outside, \emph{this is the worst one} --- because it
is the adaptation mechanism. The system's ability to acquire competences it was not given is itself a
competence it cannot acquire. That is not a contradiction, but it is a ceiling, and it is the ceiling
the companion's ladder was built to remove.

The measured retraction above sharpens it. A ninety-six per cent artefact rate was found by an audit
performed \emph{on} the faculty from outside. A graph-resident faculty is the kind of thing this
substrate has repeatedly been made to notice about itself --- it files gaps, retires its own duplicates,
and surfaces what it cannot answer. \emph{The faculty most in need of that treatment is the one that
cannot receive it.}
\end{remark}

\section{What the gap is not}\label{sec:not}
It is not that the algebra is unreachable in principle. Neighbouring operations from the same family are
already used in rules --- coverage in twelve seed files, unification in eleven, substitution in three.
The vocabulary is live; the one operation that carries the transform is the one no rule invokes.

It is not that the demonstration is unsound. The parallelogram result stands on its own terms, and its
numbers are reported above with the caveat they deserve: it was demonstrated in analysis scripts rather
than as agent behaviour, so it establishes that the corpus \emph{contains} $318$ derivable rules, not
that the agent derived them.

And it is not a large amount of work in prospect. The absence of new term variants in the demonstration
is the relevant measurement: what would be required is a rule surface over an algebra that already
evaluates, which is the same move this programme has made before and reported.

\section{The contrast the record supplies}\label{sec:contrast}
One further measurement belongs here, because it is uncomfortable in the right direction.

Program synthesis in this substrate is \emph{graph-native} in the way analogy is not: its enumerator is
a term, and the decisions --- propose, evaluate, accept --- are seed rules. And its clearest result
about held content is a null. Running the loop with the acquired content and running it stripped
produced $54$ of $257$ in both arms, \emph{on the identical set of tasks}, with the difference measured
at zero repeatedly.

So the record contains both of these at once. \emph{The faculty that is graph-native produced a clean
null about the value of what it held; the faculty that is not produced the strongest positive result
in the corpus, in analysis scripts.} Neither observation rescues the other, and the pair is reported
together because taking either alone would misrepresent the record --- the first is not a reason to
doubt the second, and the second is not a reason to discount the first.

The reading that survives both is narrow, and the companion states its general form: capability has
plural sources selected by regime, so which faculty happens to be graph-resident says nothing about
which one carries a given domain. \emph{Being graph-resident is what makes a faculty improvable by the
system carrying it, and it is not what makes a faculty work.} The gap in
Section~\ref{sec:gap} is a ceiling on self-repair, and this section is the reminder that removing it
would not by itself buy a capability.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The transform algebra is absent from the rule layer.} Fails on the
exhibition of any seed rule invoking witness-retaining anti-unification; the count is zero across
$3{,}843$ rules at the stated revision and is a command, not a judgement.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Self-repair is bounded by graph-nativeness.} Fails if the agent can be
shown to improve a host-language faculty by its own operation rather than by an author's --- which
would remove the ceiling this paper reports and much of its point.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The $318$ families are derivable and novel.} Fails if a sampled transfer
is found to be lossy, ill-defined, or to reproduce a recogniser already in the corpus.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The parallelogram result is about the corpus, not the agent.} This paper
commits to the negative: no claim is made that the agent derived those rules, and a reader who extracts
one has found an error rather than a result.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The corrected analogy count is the defensible one.} Fails if
token-anchoring and the structural-support requirement can be shown to discard genuine correspondences
rather than artefacts, which would make $321$ an over-correction rather than a correction.
\end{enumerate}
