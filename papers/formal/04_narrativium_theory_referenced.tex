\begin{abstract}
This paper develops Narrativium as a measurable, frame-relative capacity for consequential deformation. It is not a conserved substance and is not identical to optionality, energy, effort, action, momentum, or success. It measures which consequential deformations remain reachable from a configuration in a specified frame and deformation class, and assigns a discrete measure only where the corresponding capacity dimension is instantiated.

A crucial distinction separates measurement from expenditure. Narrativium $N$ is a state measurement made at a configuration; cost $c$ attending a realised transition is a flow. Spending causes change but does not determine the sign of the subsequent measurement. A transition may spend Narrativium and leave the resulting frame with more, less, or the same measured capacity. Narrativium may also change, appear, or cease to be defined without any transition of the represented graph when a containing frame changes the relations that make a deformation class reachable.

Because distinct frames need not share a comparison dimension, no frame-free total is defined; cross-frame accounting is nevertheless licensed through an explicitly instantiated conversion relation $\kappa$, subject to an invariance test that rejects conclusions surviving only one admissible choice of $\kappa$. The deformation a frame reaches may be its own container, and a section on cross-frame deformation sets out how a subframe loads, bends, breaks, or provokes the extension of its superframe's change grammar.

Combining Narrativium with Narrative yields a revision horizon only where both the capacity dimension and the relevant revision-cost dimension exist on a common comparison scale. The horizon is a controllability boundary, not an immediate failure boundary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Dependencies and scope}\label{sec:dependencies}

\paragraph{Reading for the claim.}
As in the foundational paper, the components used here are individually established and cited as such; the claim is their arrangement, which is not reachable by evaluating those components separately, since they are stipulated to be known. The composition's own falsifiability commitment is stated in the foundational paper and is inherited here.

Graphic Equalisation supplies realised transition, frame-relative individuation, deterministic collapse, and the distinction between a missing dimension and a zero value. Seldon Manifolds supplies reachable future geometry. Narrative Momentum supplies first-class history-bearing structure and directional persistence.

Narrativium asks:
\[
\boxed{\parbox{0.88\textwidth}{\centering what consequential deformation is reachable from this configuration, in this frame, for this deformation class?}}
\]

The closest established mathematical neighbours are controllability in control theory \citep{kalman1960control}, viability and reachable-set theory \citep{aubin1991viability,aubin1990survey}, and capability-oriented accounts that distinguish resources from what those resources make possible \citep{sen1985commodities}. The present contribution is the frame-typed synthesis, the explicit separation of measurement from expenditure, and its use in Narrative revision.

\begin{remark}[Standing of the quantity]\label{rem:narrativium-standing}
Narrativium is a first-class quantity \emph{only in frames that instantiate the relations defining it}, and its absence from a frame is not a deficiency of that frame's dynamics --- the standing remarks of Seldon Manifolds, applied to this quantity.

The claim of this paper is therefore conditional in form: \emph{where} a frame represents these relations, the following quantity is well-defined and behaves as stated.
\end{remark}

\section{Deformation classes and dimensions}\label{sec:deformation-class}

Narrativium is not defined without a deformation class. Let $\mathcal X$ be a class of consequential deformation and $T$ a target relation or region.

\begin{definition}[Reachable deformation set]\label{def:reachable-deformation}
\[
\mathcal R_{W:G}^T(\mathcal X)
=
\left\{
\Delta_{\mathcal X}(G,Y):
\begin{array}{l}
Y\text{ is reachable under the stated}\\
\text{deterministic transition and boundary relations}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
\end{definition}

This is a reachable-set construction in the broad control-theoretic sense \citep{kalman1960control,aubin1991viability}, but the set is explicitly typed by frame and deformation class.

\begin{definition}[Narrativium dimension]\label{def:n-dimension}
The Narrativium dimension for $(W:G,\mathcal X,T)$ exists iff the frame represents a measure over distinguishable members or regions of $\mathcal R_{W:G}^T(\mathcal X)$. If that relation is absent, Narrativium for the class is undefined:
\[
N_{W:G}(\mathcal X,T)=\Null.
\]
\end{definition}

Thus $\Null\neq0$, the dimension/value typing stated in full in Seldon Manifolds: where the dimension exists, zero may be a legitimate measured value; where it does not, there is no value to compare.

Terminal outcomes may be included or excluded depending on $\mathcal X$; the choice must be explicit.

\section{Narrativium}\label{sec:narrativium}

\begin{definition}[Narrativium]\label{def:narrativium}
Where the Narrativium dimension exists,
\[
\boxed{
N_{W:G}(\mathcal X,T)
=
\mu_{\mathcal X}
\left(
\mathcal R_{W:G}^T(\mathcal X)
\right),
}
\]
where $\mu_{\mathcal X}$ is a frame- and grounding-supplied discrete measure over distinguishable reachable deformation.
\end{definition}

The measure is integer-indexed at operative resolution. Different frames and deformation classes need not share a unit, origin, or scale. A grounding may map the integer index into an arbitrary dimensional scale.

\begin{remark}
Narrativium does not claim that all control-theoretic reachability measures are integer-valued in their conventional formulations. The claim is that the distinctions required by this substrate can be represented discretely; continuous measures may be used as groundings or effective descriptions.
\end{remark}

\section{Narrativium is not Muchness}\label{sec:not-muchness}

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\Much=\text{how much distinguishable future remains},\\[2pt]
N=\text{how much consequential deformation is reachable}.
\end{array}
}
\]

This parallels the classical distinction between the size of a state/viability region and controllability over that region \citep{kalman1960control,aubin1991viability}. A system may have high Muchness and low Narrativium or the reverse.

The distinction is also analogous to Sen's separation between possession of commodities and substantive capability: what matters is not merely what is present, but what states or functionings are actually reachable with it \citep{sen1985commodities}. This is an analogy, not an identity.

\section{Measurement and expenditure}\label{sec:measurement-expenditure}

\begin{definition}[Expenditure]\label{def:expenditure}
For a realised transition
\[
G\xRightarrow[T]{}Y,
\]
let
\[
c_{W:G}(G\xRightarrow[T]{}Y)
\]
be the grounding-supplied expenditure attending that transition, where the corresponding cost dimension exists.
\end{definition}

Measurement and expenditure are different types:
\[
\boxed{
c>0
\;\nRightarrow\;
N_{\mathrm{after}}<N_{\mathrm{before}}.
}
\]

\begin{proposition}[No general conservation law]\label{prop:no-conservation}
The substrate implies no equation
\[
N_{t+1}=N_t-c.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[No general path integration]\label{prop:no-path}
Past expenditures cannot in general be summed to recover a later Narrativium measurement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Spend-and-gain]\label{prop:spend-gain}
A positive-cost transition may produce a configuration with greater measured Narrativium on the same instantiated comparison dimension.
\end{proposition}

This is ordinary in control: expenditure can move a system into a state from which a larger region becomes reachable. The distinction between resource consumption and reachable capability is therefore structurally familiar even though the present terminology is new.

\section{Feasibility}\label{sec:feasibility}

Where a grounding instantiates compatible cost and Narrativium dimensions, it may define a comparison
\[
\boxed{
c_{W:G}(G\xRightarrow[T]{}Y)
\preceq
N_{W:G}(\mathcal X,T).
}
\]

This is a same-configuration feasibility gate, not a balance equation. Classical controllability asks whether a state is reachable under the available input structure \citep{kalman1960control}; viability theory asks whether trajectories can remain within constrained regions \citep{aubin1991viability}. Narrativium generalises the same shape to a frame-relative deformation class without importing a universal metric.

Feasibility is what gates spend-and-gain (Proposition~\ref{prop:spend-gain}). A transition that would yield greater Narrativium is realisable only where its cost is affordable \emph{up front}, $c\preceq N$; so a net-positive transition can remain unreachable below the entry threshold, since the transition is gated by a comparison the configuration does not yet satisfy. Change is paid for and yet can end in more Narrativium than it began with --- but only for a frame that can afford the entry, which is the capital-threshold or bootstrapping structure, and part of why the horizons of Section~\ref{sec:horizon} bind. It also makes the substrate's cost economics not necessarily depletive: paying to write (Graphic Equalisation, cost of the write) is an entry cost, not a guaranteed net loss, since no conservation law (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-conservation}) forces the resulting capacity below the prior one.

\section{Conferral, revocation, and dimensional change}\label{sec:conferral}

\begin{proposition}[Conferral and revocation]\label{prop:conferral}
Narrativium may change while the represented graph does not:
\[
\Delta g=0
\]
while the value of an existing Narrativium dimension changes, or while that dimension itself is instantiated or ceases to exist in the containing frame.
\end{proposition}

Standing, licence, recognised authority, credit, access, or world realisability can change what a graph can consequentially deform without changing the graph itself. This is structurally close to sociological accounts in which social capital exists in relations and institutional recognition rather than as a substance stored inside an actor \citep{bourdieu1986forms,coleman1988social}.

If a licence is revoked, for example, the relevant action-capacity dimension may disappear rather than merely taking a very negative or zero value. The NULL/value distinction is therefore load-bearing here.

\section{No cross-frame accounting}\label{sec:cross-frame}

What follows is not a separate stipulation but the founding axiom of Graphic Equalisation applied to
this quantity. That axiom holds that every distinction, measurement and represented quantity is made
in some frame, and there is no view from nowhere; a frame-free total would be a measurement made from
none, which it denies. The results below are therefore inherited rather than posited, and the same
derivation would give the corresponding results for any other quantity this programme defines.

If $G$ is individuated by $W_1$ and $W_2$, then
\[
N_{W_1:G}
\quad\text{and}\quad
N_{W_2:G}
\]
are independently typed quantities.

\begin{proposition}[No cross-frame sum]\label{prop:no-sum}
No frame-free addition
\[
N_{W_1:G}+N_{W_2:G}
\]
is defined unless some containing grounding explicitly instantiates a common comparison dimension.
\end{proposition}

This is stronger than saying that units differ. The common dimension itself may be absent.

\subsection*{Conversion is possible, and it is a choice}

The proposition above forbids a \emph{frame-free} sum. It does not forbid accounting across frames, which is performed constantly and usefully --- by markets, by triage, by cost--benefit analysis, by any decision weighing incommensurable goods. What it requires is that such accounting proceed through an explicitly instantiated conversion, and the theory does not supply one.

\begin{definition}[Conversion relation]\label{def:conversion}
A conversion $\kappa_{W_1\to W_2}$ is an instantiated relation mapping deformation measured in one frame's dimension onto another's. Where it exists, cross-frame accounting is well defined \emph{relative to $\kappa$}:
\[
N_{W_1:G}\;+_{\kappa}\;N_{W_2:G}
\]
is a quantity in $W_2$'s dimension, and not a frame-free total.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Principled and stipulated conversions]\label{rem:kappa-kinds}
Two cases must be kept apart. Where a grounding establishes that both frames measure the same underlying quantity, $\kappa$ is fixed by that grounding and the conversion is principled --- as thermochemical calories to joules. Where no such grounding exists, $\kappa$ is a \emph{stipulation}: legitimate, usable, and carrying exactly the authority of whoever stipulated it. Interpersonal comparison of utility is the standard instance; the classical result is not that comparison is impossible but that it requires a weighting no theory of preference supplies.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A worked principled conversion]\label{rem:emc2}
Mass--energy equivalence is the paradigm case, and it is instructive for three reasons rather than one. First, the common dimension had to be \emph{established}: before special relativity, mass and energy were separately conserved quantities with no common comparison dimension --- exactly the condition Proposition~\ref{prop:no-sum} describes --- and the grounding that identifies them supplies $\kappa$ with rate $c^{2}$. Second, that rate is constant, in contrast with Remark~\ref{rem:nonconstant-kappa}, so the two cases together exhibit both kinds of principled conversion. Third, and most usefully, the surrounding structure runs the invariance test of Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-invariance} as a matter of course: in
\[
E^{2}=(pc)^{2}+(mc^{2})^{2},
\]
$E$ and $p$ are frame-relative while $m$ is invariant, so a conclusion resting on energy alone is frame-dependent and one resting on rest mass survives the change of frame. The invariant is what identifies which conclusions were about the system rather than about the description.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The rate need not be constant]\label{rem:nonconstant-kappa}
A conversion may exist and still not be a scale factor. Mechanics supplies the example inside a single grounding, developed in Narrative Momentum (Remark, ``Two currencies for one alteration''): the impulse and energy prices of one alteration of motion stand in a ratio that depends on $v$. A conversion that varies with state is still a conversion; it is simply not a unit change, and treating it as one is a common route to a spurious total.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Invariance test for cross-frame claims]\label{prop:kappa-invariance}
A conclusion drawn from cross-frame accounting is admissible only where it is invariant under the legitimate alternatives to $\kappa$. If the conclusion changes when $\kappa$ is replaced by another conversion the grounding equally permits, the conclusion is an artefact of the conversion rather than a fact about the frames.
\end{proposition}

This is the same gauge condition the companion construction paper imposes on relative weighting, applied one level up. The prohibition worth carrying is therefore narrower and more usable than ``no cross-frame accounting'': \textbf{convert if you must, but state $\kappa$, and check whether the conclusion survives changing it.} A silent conversion is unauditable, and an unstated one is usually a unit change assumed where a stipulation was required.

\section{Replenishment modes}\label{sec:replenishment}

Different groundings may increase Narrativium by structurally different mechanisms.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,style=nextline]
\item[Acquisition.] A transition changes the configuration so that additional deformation becomes reachable.
\item[Conferral.] Another frame instantiates or enlarges a relation such as authority, access, standing, or credit \citep{bourdieu1986forms,coleman1988social}.
\item[Work against a field.] In a mechanical grounding, work changes configuration and therefore the later set of mechanically reachable states; classical potential energy is a standard configuration-dependent quantity \citep{goldstein2002mechanics}.
\item[Cessation.] Some groundings can regain future capability through a represented no-intervention interval. This is not a ``null action'': the no-intervention alternative is an instantiated alternative with consequences.
\end{description}

Their differences are evidence against treating Narrativium as one conserved substance.

\begin{remark}[The gain of the loop, and the one place it is already named]\label{rem:supercritical}
Proposition~\ref{prop:spend-gain} says a spend \emph{may} return more than it cost. The obvious next
question is what happens when that is iterated, and the answer wants a name for the ratio: the gain of
the loop, the Narrativium a spend returns per unit spent. Below one the capacity decays and the
configuration settles toward the equilibrium it can never quite occupy; at one it is sustained; above
one each spend funds more than the next spend requires and the quantity compounds. That last condition
is overshoot, and this states it more sharply than a rate does --- \emph{overshoot is not rapid change,
it is change whose products exceed their cause in number}, which is why it produces capacity in excess
of what any situation demanded and why the excess is then available for something other than the
purpose that generated it.

Nuclear engineering names exactly this ratio and calls the three regimes subcritical, critical and
supercritical, and the correspondence is close enough to import one non-obvious constraint. A
supercritical process is controllable only where some fraction of its effect arrives slowly enough for
a regulator to act between cause and consequence; in fission that fraction is small and it is the whole
reason the reaction can be held at all. Transferred, the claim is that \emph{a compounding loop with no
slow fraction cannot be regulated, only contained or allowed} --- and regulation here is the handler of
the theory paper's own account, which is held structure and paid for.

Two things about that ratio, and neither is a shortfall. It is \emph{observed} and never set. Nuclear
engineering already supplies the numbers for fission --- the multiplication factor, the cross-sections,
the delayed fraction --- and nothing here re-derives them or could. That is the standing relation
between this account and the domains it ranges over rather than a gap in it: a physical theory is an
embodiment of the structure described here and not a competitor to it, so the constants belong to the
embodiment and always did, and a framework that produced them would be a rival physical theory, which
this is not. What is supplied instead is the class the quantity belongs to --- that a fission
multiplication factor and a spend-and-gain ratio are one kind of thing, a loop gain, with the same
three regimes and the same regulation constraint. \emph{This is the framework the rules live in, not a
re-derivation of the rules.}

Nor is linearity claimed, or constancy: the ratio may vary with the configuration it is measured on,
exactly as $\kappa$ does (Remark~\ref{rem:nonconstant-kappa}), and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-conservation} there is no conserved quantity whose flow it would measure. What
is bought is the threshold and the direction. That purchase has a price and it is better stated than
hidden --- a claim pitched at the class of a quantity is harder to falsify than one carrying a value,
which is why the theory paper marks each of its commitments for whether it bites on shape or on detail.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Where this quantity comes from being spendable at all]\label{rem:overshoot-lead}
The remark that follows arrived in the theory paper's section on classification, which was the wrong
home for it: it is about capacity in excess of what a situation requires, which is this paper's
subject and not that one's. It is reproduced here unchanged in substance, and read alongside
Remark~\ref{rem:supercritical} it is what a gain above unity produces.

One term in it needs reconciling with the substrate paper, which holds that a substrate law must be
stated in $\Delta$ rather than in a rate. The \emph{rate} at issue below is $\Delta$ per the frame's
own closure cycle, and a frame's cycle is its own unit rather than an external clock, so this is an
quantity whose inputs are in-frame and not smuggled. That is half of the reconciliation; the other
half is the substrate paper's: the \emph{quotient} is not itself an in-frame quantity, because a ratio of two integers is
not generally an integer and a frame's own coordinates are the integer lattice. A rate is accordingly
in-frame in provenance and cross-frame in type --- a map between two readings the frame holds, which is
exactly where non-integer structure belongs. Read that way the two papers are consistent without either
conceding: the substrate carries no rate, a frame measuring its own change against its own cycles
constructs one, and what it constructs is a map rather than a held coordinate.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Overshoot is a rate, not a shape --- and it is where agency comes from]\label{rem:overshoot}
Equalisation is a balance point: the state at which what a structure meets no longer forces it to
write against what it holds. Reaching it is not the only thing a lineage can do at it, and the other
option is the one that matters.

A lineage \emph{overshoots} when its rate of change carries it past that point --- when it goes on
changing after the environment has stopped requiring it to. That is not a defect and not
specialisation, which an earlier version of this remark wrongly made it. It is momentum in the rate
itself: the machinery that produced change does not stop because the residual did, and a standing
structure that includes a rate carries the rate forward, which is the subject of the Narrative
Momentum companion one grounding over.

What overshoot produces is \emph{capacity in excess of requirement}, and excess capacity is spendable
in three ways of increasing consequence. It can fit an environment not yet met --- structure arising
under one demand turning out to serve another \citep{gouldvrba1982}. It can be spent on the current
environment rather than on the organism, which is an organism altering the conditions it is selected
by \citep{odlingsmee2003}. And it can be spent selecting which environment to be in, which is also
already named: \emph{niche choice}, one of three modes in a published framework whose other two are
construction and conformance \citep{trappes2022}. The three-way division above is close to theirs and
descends from the same source.

What this account adds is only the ordering --- that the three are one capacity spent at increasing
remove from the organism --- and the suggestion that at sufficient accumulation the third amounts to
self-direction. That last step is the one not found in the literature, and it should be taken
carefully: the agency-in-biology programme grounds agency in organisational closure and normativity
rather than in surplus, and there is a recent and pointed argument that the agency perspective has no
research programme at all \citep{difriscogawne2024}. Constituting agency out of unspent capacity is a
position in a live dispute, not a summary of one.

Two ends, and the second matters more. Overshoot is expensive: excess capacity is structure that does
no work yet, so it is ill-formed until the day it is not, and most of it is never spent. Most
overshoot is waste, and most lineages that overshoot go extinct rather than becoming anything. And
nothing here is directional --- overshoot is undirected excess that is sometimes usable afterwards,
and agency is one thing accumulated usable excess can amount to, not a destination the process is
heading for. Read as a trajectory this would be orthogenesis, which the account has no room for,
since the substrate supplies no goals for anything to be heading toward.

It is tempting to read that as two stable answers to a stable environment --- settle at the balance
point, or carry rate past it --- and the temptation should be resisted, because the first is not
available. \emph{Equilibrium is a limit and not a state.} Reaching it would require an environment
that stopped, and environments do not: an environment is other frames, which are running; a frame is
a subset of its container, so its own persistence is a fact in that container; and at any grain where
anything is individuated something is arriving. Total absence of forcing would require the container
to be doing nothing, which is a limit rather than a condition environments occupy.

That is deliberately not identified with the empty limit this account reaches for perfection and for
the view from nowhere, though an earlier version of this passage did identify them. Those are
properties of a frame; equilibrium is a relation between a frame and its container. The shapes may
be related and the relation is not argued here.

So what a stable-looking environment offers is not settling but a \emph{rate of approach}, and the
difference between the two answers is how fast a lineage closes on a target that keeps moving. One
closes slowly and spends little; the other carries rate past the point the environment is currently
requiring, pays for capacity that does nothing yet, and holds the means to survive a change of
conditions or to cause one. Neither arrives. The horseshoe crab is usually offered as the settled case, and the specialist measurements say
otherwise --- which is a correction that strengthens the reading above rather than embarrassing it,
since under it nothing settles. The
four-hundred-and-fifty-million-year figure belongs to the \emph{lineage}; measured shape stasis in
xiphosurids is Late Jurassic, on the order of a hundred and fifty million years, with developmental
stasis older and body size increasing throughout \citep{bicknell2021}. And the clade did not settle:
xiphosurids invaded non-marine environments independently at least five times and twice radiated into
novel morphospace \citep{lamsdell2016}.

So the group is not an example of equilibrium attained. It is an example of equilibrium
\emph{approached slowly and never reached}: long intervals in which environments rarely required a
write, a form that kept changing anyway, and repeated episodes of exactly the excess-spending the
other rate describes. That is the prediction rather than an exception to it, and the received
version --- four hundred and fifty million years of a fixed shape --- is the one the account would
have had trouble with. What did continue underneath was demonstrated for these animals as a
\emph{decoupling} --- large numbers of molecular characters distinguish even these morphologically
conservative organisms, though the same study measures a moderate slowdown in sequence evolution
rather than an ordinary rate \citep{avise1994} --- and is now a stronger claim than point substitution: three
whole-genome duplications are specific to the group \citep{nong2021,castellano2025}.

The other answer has a worked instance, and it is one where the received story was wrong in exactly
the way this account predicts. Fish did not grow legs in order to walk. Limbs with digits appear in
animals that were still aquatic and still breathing through gills: \emph{Acanthostega} had eight
digits on a limb \citep{coatesclack1990} and retained internal gills
\citep{coatesclack1991,clack2012}, and digits and putative digits are now described inside a fin that
still bore lepidotrichia, in an animal that still bore lepidotrichia \citep{cloutier2020}. The structure
preceded its \emph{effective} terrestrial use --- not its possibility, since the capacity for
terrestrial locomotion is argued to have arrived with limbs themselves \citep{dickson2021}, and the
known \emph{Acanthostega} material is juvenile and exclusively aquatic \citep{sanchez2016}, so
nothing here is claimed about the adult animal. Legs were not needed. What the lineage had was \emph{change} ---
rate carried forward in its standing structure --- and legs are what that change produced. Land became
available to them afterwards.

And the sequence was not a ramp, which is the part that matters most for the reading offered here. \emph{Qikiqtania wakei}, a Late Devonian elpistostegalian, has a pectoral fin specialised for swimming
and lacking substrate-support features, which its describers read as a secondary return to open water
close to the origin of limbed vertebrates \citep{stewart2022}. A lineage near that origin specialised
back toward the water --- not, on this evidence, one shown to have held substrate-support capacity
and then lost it, since the taxon sits more basally than \emph{Tiktaalik} in the same analysis. That is the
disclaimer above made concrete --- most overshoot is not spent on anything --- and it is the clearest
available evidence against reading any of this as a direction the process was heading in.

What that instance is about is the half of the process \emph{before} selection, and saying so places
this account rather than merely correcting a story. \emph{Natural selection is half of evolution.}
Variation is generated and variants are differentially retained; neither half alone produces
anything, and there is nothing to select from until something has been generated.

That is a claim about billing rather than about power. Selection's evidential standing is not in
question and nothing here reduces it. What is in question is that the theory is named after one half
while the other enters as a premise --- \emph{given heritable variation} --- so that the structure of
what is generated is treated as an input to explanation rather than part of it. If generation has
structure of its own, the shape of what evolves depends on it as much as on what is retained, which
is the substance of a live dispute rather than a neutral observation: an extended synthesis arguing
that developmental bias, plasticity, niche construction and inheritance beyond the gene deserve equal
standing, against a position that they are already accommodated \citep{laland2015}. This account
takes the first side, and takes it as a position rather than as settled.

The gap is old and named. Natural selection explains the survival of the fittest and not the
\emph{arrival} of the fittest, which is de~Vries's formulation and has stayed in use for over a
century \citep{devries1905,wagner2014}. It is not neglected --- developmental constraint, evolvability
and the biased structure of available variation are all active, and variation is known not to be
isotropic: lineages move more readily along some directions than others, and the direction is a fact
about the organism rather than about the pressure \citep{schluter1996}. This account belongs on that
side of the line and has nothing to add to the selection side.

The mechanism this account would offer there is named already, and the name is the same one: a
lineage that has been changing does not stop when selection does. Maternal inheritance makes a
population continue to evolve for many generations after selection ceases, sometimes in the direction
selection opposes, and the effect is described as a form of
\emph{evolutionary momentum} in the follow-on treatment \citep{kirkpatricklande1989,landekirkpatrick1990}.
A related effect has been measured in the field, where responses in red squirrels were influenced by
the previous generation's selection as well as the current one --- under selection that was ongoing
rather than ceased \citep{mcadamboutin2004}. Rate itself is likewise treated as an inherited, autocorrelated quantity in
molecular evolution \citep{thorne1998}, and whether evolutionary patterns arise from variational
properties of traits or lineages, or instead from ecology, is stated as the open question by the people working on it
\citep{love2021}.

So this is not a gap and nothing here fills one, and an earlier version of this passage claimed a
remaining novelty that the momentum paper has since withdrawn: the published account's carrier is the
maternal phenotype, which is present structure doing the work, so it holds the position this account
holds rather than a rival to it. What the correspondence supplies is a grounding, not a contribution.

\emph{Need does not generate structure, and cannot}: a need for legs is a relation to legs, and there
are none yet to be related to. Need filters what has been produced, which is exactly selection's
work. The producing is the part above, and if change were proportional to what is required nothing
new would ever appear, since every change would be a closer fit to a demand already present.
\emph{Novelty requires overshoot} --- structure has to exist before anything can find a use for it, so
the capacity that looks wasteful is the only thing that has ever produced anything not already
implied.

That inference is not new either, and one version of it holds in simulation. Search directed at an
objective can be actively prevented from reaching it by that objective, and on two benchmark tasks
--- maze navigation and biped walking --- search for novelty alone outperformed it
\citep{lehmanstanley2011}. Those are evolved controllers rather than organisms, so this transfers a
result across domains and does not supply biological evidence. Redundant capacity released from
immediate requirement is the textbook biological route to novel function \citep{ohno1970}, and
complexity arising with no adaptive cause is an established position in molecular evolution
\citep{stoltzfus1999,lukes2011}. What is thin rather than absent is the tie to an overshooting
\emph{rate} specifically.

Three qualifications. Morphological stasis is not the absence of evolution and only the former is
claimed. The mechanism of stasis \emph{in general} is contested --- stabilising selection
\citep{estesarnold2007} and ephemeral divergence with gene flow \citep{futuyma2010}, whose title names
constraint as the other live account, are the developed positions --- but there is no
horseshoe-crab-specific three-way contest, and an earlier version of this remark implied one, and
misdescribed the study that measured the group: it argues habitat and developmental constraint and
\emph{also} endorses stabilising selection explicitly, including in its conclusion. It fits no
evolutionary-mode models at all, which is worth knowing beside \citet{hunt2025} below. And settling is not safety: \emph{Limulus} is presently threatened by harvesting and
habitat loss, which is the environment moving faster than any trade was struck for.

A caution about stasis generally, since the remark leans on it. The punctuated reading is not the
settled state of the evidence: a recent assessment finds stasis favoured in fewer than half of
examined cases, with meandering random walks or more complex models accounting for most of the rest, which is why the
citation above is worded as what the record \emph{was read as} showing \citep{hunt2025}.
\end{remark}


\section{Narrative revision cost}\label{sec:revision-cost}

\begin{definition}[Narrative revision-cost dimension]\label{def:revision-cost-dimension}
For a specified material revision $\Delta\Narr$, a revision-cost dimension exists only if the revision itself is represented as a reachable deformation class and the frame supplies a compatible cost relation over candidate interventions.
\end{definition}

If the revision is not expressible or the cost relation is not instantiated, then
\[
C_{\mathrm{rev}}(\Delta\Narr)=\Null,
\]
not $+\infty$.

\begin{definition}[Minimum Narrative revision cost]\label{def:revision-cost}
Where the revision-cost dimension exists,
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{rev}}
(\Delta\Narr\mid G_t,\Gamma_t,W)
=
\min
\left\{
c_W(u):
u\text{ can realise the required }\Delta\Narr
\right\}.
}
\]
\end{definition}

Because the substrate is discrete, $\min$ is the natural operator on a finite or well-founded represented candidate class. A grounding with a continuous effective description may use an infimum, but that is not required by the substrate.

\section{The Narrative horizon}\label{sec:horizon}

\begin{definition}[Narrative horizon]\label{def:horizon}
For a specified revision class, a Narrative horizon exists only where both
\[
N_{W:G}(\mathcal X,T)
\quad\text{and}\quad
C_{\mathrm{rev}}(\Delta\Narr)
\]
are instantiated on a common comparison dimension. The horizon is the equality relation
\[
\boxed{
N_{W:G}(\mathcal X,T)
=
C_{\mathrm{rev}}(\Delta\Narr).
}
\]
\end{definition}

The three cases are therefore relational rather than ratio-based:
\[
\begin{aligned}
N &\succ C_{\mathrm{rev}}
&&\Rightarrow \text{the stated revision remains reachable with residual capacity},\\
N &= C_{\mathrm{rev}}
&&\Rightarrow \text{the frame lies on the Narrative horizon},\\
N &\prec C_{\mathrm{rev}}
&&\Rightarrow \text{the stated revision exceeds current reachable capacity}.
\end{aligned}
\]

If either dimension is NULL, none of these comparisons is licensed. In particular, an absent revision class is not ``infinitely expensive''; it is not in the comparison domain.

\begin{proposition}[Post-horizon fragility]\label{prop:post-horizon}
If
\[
N\prec C_{\mathrm{rev}}
\]
on an instantiated common comparison dimension, the specified Narrative revision is not reachable in the present frame under the stated deformation class.
\end{proposition}

The horizon is a controllability boundary, not a failure boundary. This connects naturally to viability theory, where loss of membership in a viability kernel can precede the later constraint violation that makes failure externally visible \citep{aubin1991viability,aubin2011newdirections}.

\section{Horizon and delayed collapse}\label{sec:delayed-collapse}

Let $t_H$ be Narrative-horizon crossing and $t_C$ observable material collapse or divergence in the same frame.

\begin{hypothesis}[Delayed failure]\label{hyp:delayed-failure}
For system classes that can continue routine operation after losing access to a necessary adaptation,
\[
\boxed{
t_H<t_C
}
\]
occurs in a substantial class of failure trajectories.
\end{hypothesis}

The delay exists because existing Narrative may remain compatible with the containing graph after the capacity to revise it has been lost. This is structurally related to resilience theory's distinction between present persistence and the size of disturbance a system can absorb before reorganising \citep{holling1973resilience}, although the quantities are not identified.

\section{Power and agility}\label{sec:power-agility}

Because neither a universal origin nor a universal unit is assumed, agility is not defined by a substrate-level ratio $N/C_{\mathrm{rev}}$.

Where a grounding supplies a common ordered dimension, define agility ordinally by the margin between available Narrativium and required revision cost:
\[
\boxed{
A_{\Frame}
=
\operatorname{Margin}_{\Frame}
\left(
N_{W:G},
C_{\mathrm{rev}}(\Delta\Narr)
\right).
}
\]

Only the ordering of this margin is substrate-relevant unless the grounding supplies more structure. Because Narrativium is deformation-class typed, a frame may possess enormous capacity to deform external structure while being unable to materially rewrite its own established Narrative.

\section{Cross-frame deformation: acting on the superframe}\label{sec:cross-frame-deformation}

The external structure a frame deforms may be its own \emph{container}. A subframe alters its superframe only by changing the part of it that it is --- Graphic Equalisation carries as a falsifiable commitment, a pattern rather than a strict universal restriction, that there is no reach across a gap, outward effect being propagation from one's own subset --- so a subframe's Narrativium acts on its superframe as a cross-frame deformation, sorted by the superframe's change grammar $\Gamma_{W_c}$ (the change face of $W$'s grammar, from Graphic Equalisation): the changes $\Gamma_{W_c}$ admits cohere into the superframe, and the rest are residual.

\paragraph{Loading.} The primary lever is to inject \emph{expensive admissible} changes. These become change-load on the superframe, and by the physics companion's change-budget --- a frame's change capacity is finite, so change consumed by one demand is change unavailable for another --- the re-cohering this load compels consumes throughput at the expense of the superframe's own proper time: the subframe paces and shapes its container by what it feeds it. The mechanism is a cost transfer --- the subframe pays to \emph{create} the change, and the superframe pays for the writes its own re-cohering rules must then make (sorting itself is free; it is the re-cohering that is written) --- and the two prices need not match, so a cheap trigger can compel an expensive response. The leverage is that ratio, and it is how a small contained frame moves a large container at all. It requires little understanding of the container: any expensive admissible change will do.

\paragraph{Bending and breaking.} Loading is not the only lever. The others come from understanding the superframe's grammar, and each exploits the gap between the grammar the superframe operates on as \emph{believed} and as \emph{actual}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Bending} exploits the actual grammar being looser than intended: a move that genuinely passes $\Gamma_{W_c}$ yet achieves what the superframe never meant to allow --- letter against spirit. Nothing is violated; the specification was too generous.
\item \textbf{Breaking} exploits the actual grammar failing its own specification: a move the intended grammar would reject but a flaw in enforcement admits --- a change that should be residual, cohering anyway. Breaking can reach $\Gamma_{W_c}$ itself, admitting a change that modifies the grammar against its own specification.
\item \textbf{Minting} is the sanctioned counterpart, and it is not an exploit at all: a residual the superframe cannot admit may be absorbed by $\Gamma_{W_c}$ \emph{extending} to admit it (Graphic Equalisation --- minting is itself a rule). A subframe's residual routinely provokes exactly this, and it is where the substrate says recognition and growth meet. Load, bend, and break work against the container's grammar; minting works with it.
\end{itemize}
Understanding is the enabler and the ordering for the three adversarial levers. Load, bend, and break rise in the understanding of $\Gamma_{W_c}$ they demand and in how far they violate it; the more of the superframe's grammar a subframe holds, the more edges it can bend and flaws it can break. This is the exploitation face of modelling one's container: capacity over a superframe scales with how much of its actual grammar the subframe understands, and it is why the more of its container a frame models, the less it is merely subject to it. The superframe's only defence is to close the gap --- tighten the actual grammar toward the intended against bending, fix enforcement against breaking --- and exploit against hardening is a continuous contest, since the gap between intended and actual is never fully closed.

\begin{proposition}[The cross-frame relation is asymmetric in kind]\label{prop:cross-frame-asymmetry}
This is the reverse leg of Graphic Equalisation's higher-frame principle. There, a containing frame holds first-class quantities \emph{about} the graph it contains --- the container represents the contained. Here the contained \emph{affects} its container while unable to fully represent it. Container knows contained; contained shapes container. The operation is the same self-similar one either way (Graphic Equalisation); what is asymmetric is representational \emph{capacity} --- the container \emph{may} hold the contained whole where it instantiates the relations to do so, and the contained cannot.
\end{proposition}

\section{Groundings and prior art}\label{sec:groundings}

\paragraph{Control and viability.}
Controllability asks what states can be reached under admissible inputs \citep{kalman1960control}; viability theory asks what constrained states can continue to be maintained \citep{aubin1990survey,aubin1991viability}. Narrativium is closest to these traditions mathematically, but is explicitly frame-relative, deformation-class typed, and separated from expenditure.

\paragraph{Capability approach.}
Sen's capability approach distinguishes resources or commodities from the substantive set of states and activities they make achievable \citep{sen1985commodities}. Narrativium shares that structural separation while making it a dynamical, frame-relative reachable-deformation measure.

\paragraph{Potential energy.}
Classical mechanics provides a special grounding in which configuration-relative potential energy participates in a conserved energy accounting under appropriate assumptions \citep{goldstein2002mechanics}. That special accounting must not be promoted to a law of Narrativium generally.

\paragraph{Social capital.}
Bourdieu and Coleman both treat social capital as relationally conferred capability embedded in networks and institutions \citep{bourdieu1986forms,coleman1988social}. This is a direct structural precedent for Narrativium changing because a containing frame changes its relations to the represented graph.

\paragraph{Resilience.}
Holling's distinction between stability and resilience provides a predecessor for the idea that routine performance can remain intact while the capacity to absorb or adapt to disturbance has already contracted \citep{holling1973resilience}. The Narrative horizon gives that shape a specific comparison between available deformation and revision requirement.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}

\noindent\emph{On ``a claimed class'' and ``a claimed grounding''.} Several commitments below are
indexed to a class or a grounding rather than stated universally, and that is a real weakness unless
the index is fixed in advance: a counterexample can otherwise be excluded after the fact by declining
to have claimed that class, which makes the commitment untestable by the party holding it. The
discipline this paper adopts, stated here so it can be held to it, is that \emph{a class counts as
claimed once it appears in a grounding section of this corpus}, and the claim is then owed for every
member of it. Nothing may be added to or removed from a class in response to a result. Where no class
has been named the commitment is not yet live, and the honest reading of such an entry is that it
states what \emph{would} be risked rather than what currently is.

\noindent\emph{On the tags.} Each commitment below is marked \textsc{[shape]} where failing it costs
this grounding its \emph{reach} --- the account would no longer apply to the domain, or would apply
only by giving up what it was brought here to explain --- and \textsc{[detail]} where failing it costs
a specific mechanism while the grounding stands. The substrate paper has carried these marks for some
time and the groundings did not, which had the discipline applied where the risk is smallest: the
substrate risks little by design and the groundings carry the exposure. They were assigned in a single
pass under the rule just stated, and are the least-audited judgement in this document --- a reader who
thinks one is marked in the flattering direction is disagreeing with a fast call, not an argued
position.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Deformation-class typing.} Fails if one scalar value can represent all deformation classes without losing distinctions where one capability is exhausted and another remains.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Measurement/expenditure distinction.} Fails if every grounding requires expenditure to equal the fall in measured Narrativium.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Spend-and-gain.} Fails if positive expenditure leading to greater measured future capacity is structurally impossible in every grounding.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Conferral/revocation.} Fails if Narrativium can change only through transition of the represented graph.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No frame-free accounting.} \emph{(This is Axiom~$-1$ in this paper's
vocabulary. Worth noting that the substrate paper carries no commitment for that axiom directly, so
the axiom's exposure lives in entries like this one and in the mind companion's ``no frame is
privileged'' --- which are the same risk in two places, not two risks.)} Fails if distinct individuating frames necessarily supply a common Narrativium dimension and unit without explicit grounding. Note that this does not forbid conversion (Definition~\ref{def:conversion}); it forbids an unconverted total.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Conversion invariance.} Fails for a claimed cross-frame result if the result is not invariant under the alternative conversions the grounding equally permits (Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-invariance}). A result surviving only one admissible $\kappa$ is a property of that choice.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Narrative horizon.} Fails if a certified revision whose minimum compatible cost exceeds current Narrativium is nevertheless realised under the same frame, deformation class, and accounting.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Delayed failure.} Fails for a claimed system class if Narrative-horizon crossing never precedes observable divergence.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Cross-frame deformation is admissibility-gated.} Fails if a subframe can deform its superframe by a change the superframe's \emph{actually enforced} grammar rejects --- that is, if any deformation \emph{coheres into} the container other than by loading it, bending it, breaking it, or provoking it to mint. The wording matters: a graph-originated delta is already a change in its container rather than something submitted to it (Graphic Equalisation's residual result), so what is gated is absorption and not arrival (Section~\ref{sec:cross-frame-deformation}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Narrativium is not a tank. It is a frame-relative measurement of consequential deformation reachable from a configuration:
\[
\boxed{
N
=
\mu(\text{reachable deformation})
}
\]
where the corresponding dimension exists.

Spending changes configuration; the resulting configuration is measured again. It may have more capacity, less capacity, the same capacity, or a differently instantiated capacity dimension. Other frames may alter that dimension without changing the represented graph at all.

Combined with Narrative, Narrativium supplies a revision horizon only where available capacity and required revision cost are jointly comparable. That horizon marks the point at which a necessary future adaptation can become unreachable even though present behaviour still appears normal.
