## A. "Three things are posited" and "two postulates" in consecutive paragraphs

l.736–738: "Three things are posited past it and none of them is free:
transformation, so that something does the happening; an economics, so
that a change has a price; and realisation, so that what is admissible
becomes what occurs." l.741–742: "So what the companions add to this
paper is the admission that things happen, two postulates named in their
ledger, and past those the bets…" The reader has to work out that
*transformation* is the admission and *economics* is the destroy/preserve
distinction of l.732–733. **Fix:** one clause at l.741: "the admission
that things happen — transformation — and the two postulates its ledger
names, realisation and the destroy/preserve distinction that is the
economics, and past those the bets…"

---

## B. The empirical bound belongs in commitment 2

l.587–592: "What would bear on it is a demonstration that this world has
time or cause in its floor — *which is empirical and not modal* … Finding
one would bound the account rather than refute it." Commitment 2
(l.918–923, *Time, cause, rule and an observer are separable*) says it
"fails if the account of change built on this one could not be
constructed without folding one of them back into the base" — a
construction test only. The body names a second way the same claim is
met, empirically, and the commitment does not carry it. **Fix:** one
sentence in item 2: "It is also bounded, not failed, by a demonstration
that this world has time or cause in its floor (Section~\ref{sec:graph}):
the claim would then hold of worlds without such a floor and this would
not be one of them."
