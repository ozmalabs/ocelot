\begin{abstract}
This companion to Graphic Equalisation develops the mind-and-self groundings of the
change-substrate. Its central move is that a frame is real graph data that
\emph{runs}, and that everything here follows from the run and its record. A rock and a
universe are both frames, so anything a rock does without is not compulsory for a frame.
Recording spatialises a succession into a static held axis, giving the run/record
axis whose poles are Conway (pure running, computationally irreducible) and a movie
(pure record), with real frames the machinima between; the past can be a record but
the future is uncomputed, so it is open as \emph{unrun}, not as indeterministic. Free
will is then the in-frame run --- a fully recorded, zero-randomness story
demonstrates it, its characters free in frame while the reader foreknows --- so
freedom needs no indeterminacy. Imagination is real: there is no non-actual frame,
and the only difference between the imagined and the perceived is frame-relative
grounding. Self-models are incomplete by construction: a frame carries its own
trajectory rather than reading it, so to treat that course as an object at all it must
build a model of itself, under a finite aperture. The incompleteness is structural
rather than pathological, and it divides by kind --- a limit of \emph{expression}
leaves residual and is inferable from trace, while a limit of \emph{aperture} leaves
nothing and is inferable only by metacognition --- so the operative distinction is
calibrated versus uncalibrated, not complete versus incomplete. Death is the self's
rules ceasing to be re-runnable in the same coherent
self; personal identity is internal coherence (with an instance a supergraph node),
which places gradual replacement, teleporter, backup, and fission on one axis; and
death is relative to a chosen coherence-boundary, with many real deaths and none
absolute. The claims throughout are information-level and structural.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}\label{sec:scope}
The method is to treat philosophy of mind as applied physics: once a frame is graph
data that runs, free will, identity, and death are read off the run and its record,
not argued as separate doctrines. This paper is a grounding, not a psychology and not
a metaphysics asserted from within. It inherits Graphic Equalisation's position that the claims are about
information --- distinction, frame, admissibility, realisation, and now the
\emph{run} of a frame and its \emph{record} --- of which mind is one embodiment.
The strong claims are structural: that free will is the in-frame run and needs no
indeterminacy; that imagination is real and differs from perception only in
grounding; that every self-model is incomplete by construction, so the operative
distinction is calibrated versus uncalibrated rather than complete versus incomplete;
that personal identity is internal coherence (Graphic Equalisation's SELF
identity), distinct from the supergraph node that instances it (its SUPERGRAPH
identity); and that death is the loss of re-runnability in
the same coherent self, relative to a chosen coherence-boundary. The weak part is
anything operational: no decision procedure for a given choice, no test for when
re-runnability or coherence has in fact been lost, no threshold for how gradual a
change stays ``the same'' self, and no account of the subjective, felt, or
phenomenal character of any of it --- consciousness and qualia are out of scope.
Each grounding is a \emph{promoted} constraint exposed to falsification, in the
manner of Graphic Equalisation's collapse bridge (its
Remark~\ref{GE-rem:qm-provenance}), not a result asserted from within.

One standard governs every place below where a \emph{measurement} is reported from this
programme's own record rather than from a published source. What is offered there is a
pre-registered implementation record which is not circulated with these papers, and it is a
claim a reader may decline to credit, on exactly the terms Graphic Equalisation sets for the
same case. Such a report states what was compared, what was found, and the scope the fixture
holds under; it names no implementation identifiers, and nothing structural in this paper
depends on any of it. Where a section below reports one, it is not re-disclaimed inline: this
sentence is the disclaimer.

\section{The minimal universe}\label{sec:minimal}
A \emph{rock}, in the sense used throughout, is an inert frame: a graph whose rules
are present but not firing, so it has no succession and no time of its own. That is
deliberately the weaker of two senses Graphic Equalisation distinguishes
(Remark~\ref{GE-rem:rock}), and the
distinction is worth carrying here rather than leaving to be discovered. There the
word anchors $g$ itself, with $r$ and $\Gamma$ \emph{absent} at the grain of
individuation; rules present and not firing is a third condition, distinct from $g$
and from running alike, and the slide between the two is named there as an error. It
is the third condition that is used below, because every argument here turns on a
frame that \emph{could} run and does not --- a rock resuming after weathering requires
its rules to have survived as present structure --- and none of them requires the
stronger reading. Where Graphic Equalisation credits this companion with naming that
condition directly (Remark~\ref{GE-rem:space-test}), this is the naming. Things
happen to it from its supergraph, but in its own frame it merely is (the physics
companion's Section~\ref{PH-sec:dimensions} develops this as why a rock has no
time). Everything here is a graph, so a
rock and a universe are one kind of thing, differing in scale, not in category
(Graphic Equalisation's self-similarity, its
Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:runner-recursion}: a primitive is graph-that-runs at every
scale). A rock \emph{is} a universe --- the minimal one --- and that makes it an
existence proof about universes in general.

\begin{proposition}[What the rock shows is not compulsory]\label{prop:minimal-universe}
Anything not compulsory for a rock is not compulsory for a frame, and the universe is one frame among others. The rock does
without time (it is inert, no succession), without cycles or closure, without
inference or observation, without history or records, without self-originated
change, and without a running arrow --- so none of these is compulsory for a
universe.
\end{proposition}

The inference is one-directional, and only that direction is claimed. A single inert example
bounds the compulsory set from \emph{above} --- it shows what can be dispensed with --- and
cannot establish that what the rock happens to retain is necessary. So the rock is a
non-necessity argument, not a derivation of a core.

What is compulsory is inherited from Graphic Equalisation, which keeps almost nothing: a
structure with relations among its parts. The rock is consistent with that and does not
weaken it, because a rock's rules are inert \emph{in its own frame} while its container's are
not --- what happens to the rock (weathering, being moved) is its supergraph's rules running
across exactly that container relation. An inert frame is therefore one whose own rules
do not fire, not a counterexample to rules running.

Nor does that demotion cost the argument anything, because \emph{frame} is the category throughout.
The universe is a frame and a rock is a frame; they differ in scale, not in kind, which is what
self-similarity asserts. So the inference never needed ``universe'' as a separate qualification --- it
runs over frames, and reads: anything a frame can be without is not compulsory for a frame. The
rock's inertness is not a defect in the example but the whole of its content.

This is the general form of ``time is optional,'' and it licenses the
contingent-not-necessary verdicts of the physics companion. Wherever a shape there
was called contingent rule-content rather than necessity --- the number of spatial
dimensions and the $3+1$ signature (its Section~\ref{PH-sec:dimensions}), the gauge
group (its Remark~\ref{PH-rem:gauge-contingent}), the dimensionless constants (its
Proposition~\ref{PH-prop:dim-boundary}), the arrow, the value of $c$ --- the rock is
the reason: the minimal universe does without it, so it cannot be compulsory. Physics ordinarily takes time, causality, and the second law's arrow
as features a universe must have; the rock shows they are features an \emph{active}
universe has.

\section{Running and recording: Conway, a movie, and the machinima between}\label{sec:runrecord}
Recording spatialises time. A succession held as a record --- an MRI of a rock, a
film strip, a worldline drawn on paper --- lays the temporal order out as a static
structure, a $+$-like axis (the block's extra dimension). Read against the metric
signature, the $-$ axis is the one being \emph{lived}, the frame's own succession
run by its cycles, open at the frontier, and the $+$ axes are \emph{held} --- spatial
positions, and any recorded time; the block feels dead because a recorded succession
is not run. That reading is offered here only as the run/record axis seen from the
other side. The physics companion states the same signature in its own terms ---
succession against position, in its Section~\ref{PH-sec:dimensions} --- and the
physics of it belongs there rather than here; where the two differ, that paper
governs.

The extremes of the run-versus-record axis are Conway and a movie. Conway's Game
of Life is pure running: a rule and a seed, everything computed live, nothing
recorded, and no way to know the outcome but to run it (computational
irreducibility, \citealp{wolfram2002}). A movie is pure record: every frame laid out, played back,
nothing computed --- the block universe, known by reading rather than running.
Between them is scripted machinima --- a running engine following a predetermined
script, part computed live, part laid down in advance --- and that is where real
frames sit: rules fire (Conway-like) while history is held (movie-like). It is the
same axis as generation-versus-record: store the generator and run it (Conway,
small but irreducible) or store the frames and read them (a movie, large but
immediate), with the world's own compressibility deciding which is cheaper.

\begin{proposition}[The past can be a movie; the future is Conway]\label{prop:run-record}
A block is only ever of the past. What has been run can be recorded and laid out
--- a movie of the succession so far, readable without re-running. What has not
been run cannot be recorded, because there is nothing yet to record: the future is
determinable by the rules but \emph{uncomputed}, Conway-style. The openness is
unrun-ness, not indeterminism.
\end{proposition}

Two of the three components there are inherited rather than argued here. That the
future is determinable and not determined is Graphic Equalisation's
Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:determinable}, and that the frontier has no intrinsic last is
its Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:no-start-end}. What is added is the reading of the
openness: the future is open because it is uncomputed, not because nothing fixes it, so
it is not laid out and cannot be read ahead --- you must run it, and you cannot record
what has not run.

\section{Free will and the three frames}\label{sec:freewill}
\begin{proposition}[Free will is the in-frame run, and needs no indeterminacy]\label{prop:free-will}
Free will is a frame running its own decision. In a frame's own run the decision is
live and unrun-to-it --- the frame must run its deliberation to reach the outcome ---
and that running \emph{is} the choosing: the outcome is fixed by the rules yet
knowable only by running, so the frame experiences computing it as choosing it. This
needs no indeterminacy.
\end{proposition}

A story is the demonstration. A book or a movie is a fully recorded succession --- every
word or frame fixed, zero randomness, readable ahead by the reader --- and yet its
characters have complete free will: they deliberate, choose, and are responsible, in
frame. Both hold at once because they are two frames. The character's freedom is its
in-frame run; the reader's foreknowledge is a superframe holding the finished record (a
movie), and holding the record never reaches into the character's run to remove the
freedom. So foreknowledge is not unfreedom --- not even complete foreknowledge, the
reader who knows the ending or the author who wrote it --- because knowing the recorded
run and being the live run are different frames.

Two corollaries follow, and neither needs anything further. Randomness would only
subtract: a random run is not more free, it is less the character's own. And
responsibility survives for the reason the freedom does --- the run is the character's
own (self-change is the only change; the rules running are the character) --- so a
determined, recorded, even foreknown act is still that character's act.

\begin{remark}[Author, character, reader: unrun-ness is absolute]\label{rem:three-frames}
A story stacks three frames on one succession. The author \emph{runs} it --- writing is
Conway, generated live, and the author must write to know the ending (authorship is
computationally irreducible; the commonplace that ``the characters took on lives of
their own'' is the author unable to read ahead their own creation). The character
\emph{lives} it --- the in-frame run of Proposition~\ref{prop:free-will}. The reader
\emph{replays} it --- the finished record, a movie. One succession, three
frame-readings: the run/record axis is a fact about frame, not about the system. And
the author settles the objection to Proposition~\ref{prop:run-record} that a high
enough superframe might read the future ahead --- even the creator gets no shortcut.
The author is Conway to their own book while writing it; the movie exists only
\emph{after} the run, for everyone including the author. Foreknowledge is always
post-run record-holding, never pre-run read-ahead. For an irreducible rule unrun-ness is
absolute: no frame, creator included, reads an unrun future without running it; every frame
either runs it or replays an already-run record.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The reader runs and holds at once, and this is standard]\label{rem:reader}
The reader looks like a counterexample: the account needs the character's frame genuinely to run,
and needs the reader to hold a finished record --- and if the character's frame runs \emph{in}
the reader, the two frames appear to collapse into one. They do not, and the point does not need
arguing here, because three separate literatures settled it independently.

The cleanest statement of the running half is \citeauthor{currie1997}'s: a reader of fiction
simulates the state of a hypothetical \emph{reader of fact} \citep{currie1997,currie2002}. What
runs in the reader is therefore a simulation, and what it simulates is someone taking the events
as real --- so running the character's frame is not in competition with holding a text, because
the running is itself an offline state rather than a belief. Currie's figure does not supply the
\emph{foreknowledge}, though: his reader-of-fact meets events as news, in order, and knows no more
of the ending than any reader of a report. That half comes from elsewhere, and rhetorical
narratology supplies it by dividing the reader rather than the frame: \citet{rabinowitz1977} distinguishes the \emph{narrative} audience, for
whom the narrated events are real, from the \emph{authorial} audience, which knows the text is
made, and these are positions one actual reader occupies simultaneously
\citep{polvinen2019}. The two frames were never reader-versus-character. And the cognitive
architecture supplies the mechanism: pretence representations are processed by the same inference
machinery that operates over beliefs --- a single code \citep{nichols2000} --- while
\emph{quarantining} confines their effects to a circumscribed domain \citep{gendler2003}. Same
machinery is not same frame. \citet{walton1978} states the resulting two-level structure exactly:
what runs in the appreciator is real (his ``quasi-fear'' --- the tensed muscles, the quickened
pulse) and it is that real state which makes it make-believe that he is afraid, with full
knowledge that the slime is not. Literary theory has a name for the compound, \emph{aesthetic
illusion}: immersion counterbalanced by a latent rational distance, the awareness of the
difference between representation and reality \citep{wolf2014}.

Nor is it only characters that are modelled: a reader builds a model of the \emph{author} as
well, which is a longer matter and is taken up in Section~\ref{sec:other-models}, where it is
needed.

Two qualifications, both of which the substrate should expect. The engagement is not uniform
across people: measured individually, mentalizing and motor engagement during narrative
comprehension \emph{trade off} against one another rather than rising together
\citep{nijhof2015}, so how one runs a character is itself a position on several axes rather
than one capacity present or absent. Two limits belong with that, since it is the only measurement
carrying this claim: the correlation is reliable in one region rather than across the network, and the
eighteen participants \emph{listened} to the excerpts, the authors having chosen listening over
reading, so it is evidence about narrative comprehension and only indirectly about readers. And the
separation is functional rather than metaphysical, and defeasible: quarantine leaks
\citep{gendler2006}, and readers report characters' voices persisting into and commenting on their own
lives afterwards \citep{aldersonday2017} --- reported by about a fifth of those who wrote freely about
their reading, and described by the authors as relatively rare across the full sample, with under four
per cent reporting it most or all of the time. The phenomenon is real and is not the ordinary case,
which is what the argument needs: quarantine that leaks sometimes is still quarantine. The claim is
therefore that the frames are functionally separated, which is what the argument needs, and not
that they are hermetically sealed, which is false.
\end{remark}

\section{Imagination, grounding, and reality}\label{sec:grounding}
\begin{proposition}[There is no non-actual frame; imagination is real]\label{prop:imagination-real}
Imagination is not the running of a non-actual frame --- there is no such thing. Every
frame that runs is real graph data really running, so actuality is not a property some
frames have and others lack; every frame is actual. The only difference between an
imagined frame and a perceived one is frame-relative grounding: what the frame is
anchored to, relative to another frame. A perception is grounded in the observation
frame (sensory-anchored); an imagination is grounded internally, in generation or in a
held or communicated record. Both are real; they differ in what they are grounded in,
not in whether they are.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[What follows for fiction, planning and death]\label{rem:imagination-consequences}
An imagined dragon, a planned move, a character in a book is a real frame, really run
(real computation), not a pretend one. So ``is it real?'' is malformed as a question
about actuality --- everything that runs is --- and the well-formed question is ``what
is it grounded in?'': observation, memory, generation, or a communicated record. A
shared fiction is a real frame grounded in a communicated record (the book of
Remark~\ref{rem:three-frames}); a plan is a real frame grounded in projection. This is
why imagination does work --- planning, design, and counterfactual reasoning are real
frames really run, not pretence --- and why fiction moves: the character's frame runs,
really, in the reader. Death, in the same terms, is not a frame going non-actual
(nothing does) but a run that stops, leaving frames grounded only in a record and
re-runnable by others; reading and remembering are that re-running --- a
record-grounded frame run again into a live grounding.
\end{remark}

Predictive-processing accounts reach a closely related conclusion by another route.
\citet{clark2016} argues that perception, dreaming, memory and imagination are
expressions of a single mechanistic ploy, with imagery arising when the same
generative machinery runs top-down, insulated from the driving sensory signal --- one
kind of running, differing in what anchors it, rather than a second and weaker faculty
producing contents of a lesser kind. \citet{seth2019} makes the perceptual half
explicit, treating perception as predictive construction rather than transcription of
input (the ``controlled hallucination'' formulation, which he credits to earlier
sources rather than claims as his own). The routes are not the same: those are
empirical claims about how one substrate is built, while the claim here is the
structural one that grounding rather than actuality is what the imagined/perceived
distinction consists in, and it would stand for a substrate built on different
machinery. What the convergence supplies is the load-bearing part --- that the
difference is not a property carried in the frame or the signal.

\begin{remark}[What the difference is, and how a substrate detects it]\label{rem:detection}
Grounding is what the difference between an imagined and a perceived frame \emph{is}. How any
particular substrate \emph{detects} that difference is a separate question, answered by that
substrate's implementation, and the two must not be conflated. Human reality-monitoring is the
instructive case: imagined and perceived signals are carried in a common representation, and the
reality judgement is made by a threshold on subjective signal \emph{strength} rather than by
reading provenance off the signal \citep{dijkstra2022,dijkstra2023}. That is a fact about one
implementation, not a correction to the account --- and it is what the account predicts. A frame
does not have its own grounding directly available to it, grounding being a relation to what lies
outside the frame rather than a property carried within it, so any substrate that must sort its
own frames has to use a proxy; signal strength is the proxy human brains use. The characteristic
failure follows immediately, since a proxy dissociates from what it proxies for: misclassification
is predicted exactly where strength matches and grounding differs, which is what is measured. It also fixes how the tagging language elsewhere should be read: a frame that sorts its own
frames by a proxy holds no provenance label, so a ``grounding tag'' is an abstraction over
whatever proxy does the sorting rather than a mark carried on the frame, and a mistagging is that
proxy dissociating. A substrate using a different proxy would fail differently while the
underlying distinction stayed the same. Note also that this is a first-order misclassification
and not the second-order failure of Section~\ref{sec:selfmodel}: a frame can be perfectly aware
that its proxy may mislead it and still be misled.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Grounding has no bedrock; observation is supergraph-contact]\label{prop:grounding}
Grounding is frame-relative all the way down: a frame grounded in a frame grounded in a
frame, with no absolute, self-grounding frame beneath them all. There is no bedrock
``actual'' layer --- the ground is the coherent running structure, frame to frame, not a
base. Yet one grounding is privileged, relationally and not absolutely: \emph{observation}
is a frame's contact with its supergraph, the boundary where the container deforms it with
changes it does not author (Graphic Equalisation's dissolved pointer,
its Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:no-direct-self-alter} --- you change
yourself, the supergraph deforms you --- with perception the deformation side). Observation
is special because it is \emph{yours}, your container-boundary, not because it is ``the
real.''
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Reality-testing is supergraph-pushback]\label{rem:pushback}
It follows that reality-testing is not actuality-checking --- there is no
non-actual frame to catch (Proposition~\ref{prop:imagination-real}) --- but
\emph{supergraph-pushback}: does the container deform you as if the frame were grounded
there? You walk into the chair (pushback: observation-grounded) and not into the dragon
(no pushback: imagination-grounded), both real frames, only one with the container pushing
back. Sanity is then a grounding-tag problem, not a reality one: keep observation-grounded
frames tagged as observation-grounded and imagination-grounded ones as
imagination-grounded. A hallucination or delusion is a grounding-tag error --- a real,
internally-grounded frame mistagged as observation-grounded, so the self expects a pushback
that will not come --- not a belief in something non-actual, since nothing is. The position
is thus anti-foundationalist (no absolute ground) without being flatly coherentist
(observation is privileged, as your supergraph-contact).
\end{remark}

That combination --- no bedrock, and no loss of realism for lacking one --- is not
peculiar to this substrate: the standard worry that dropping a foundation takes realism
with it is already declined by perspectival realism, an established position in
philosophy of science arrived at independently of anything here
\citep{massimi2022}.

\section{Self-models: incomplete by construction, calibrated by choice}\label{sec:selfmodel}
A frame does not read its own trajectory. It \emph{carries} it --- the standing structure is what the frame is, not something it holds at a distance --- so to treat its own course as an object at all, a frame must build a model of itself. That model is incomplete for three separate reasons, and they should be kept apart because they behave differently. \emph{Aperture:} it is built under a finite aperture, so what falls outside never enters it. \emph{Proper part:} it is a part of what it models, so modelling the frame includes modelling the model, and the apparatus doing the modelling has strictly less capacity than the whole it belongs to --- which is why a finite frame that could in principle be modelled exhaustively from outside still cannot model itself exhaustively from inside. \emph{Lesser object:} a self-model must be a lesser thing than what it models in order to carry any bits at all, which is Graphic Equalisation's self-edge result (Remark~\ref{GE-rem:self-edge}); being lesser, it omits. Three features
of that model follow from the substrate rather than from any fact about minds.

It is \emph{incomplete}, for the three reasons just given, and what falls outside is \textsc{null} rather than zero: an absent distinction does not present itself as a blank to be filled, it does not present at all. It is \emph{revisable only at a price}, because altering standing structure is the paid operation while retaining it is free (the momentum paper's free continuation, its Definition~\ref{NM-def:free-continuation}), so coherence-preserving readings are cheap and re-writes are not. And it is \emph{indirect about its own subject}, since a frame is readable from outside in a way it is not from inside --- which is why another frame often models one's trajectory more accurately than one does oneself, without any appeal to self-deception --- a consequence of access mode, though not an entailment of it, as below.

The first of these has a structural analogue in theoretical neuroscience.
\citet{friston2010} builds the free-energy account on a Markov blanket: a statistical
boundary separating a system's internal states from external ones, such that its
contact with what lies beyond is exhausted by that boundary. The shape is the aperture
here --- a finite boundary is what makes both the model and its incompleteness --- but
the route is not: the blanket is derived as a condition on systems that resist
dispersal, and arrives with a variational formalism and an imperative to minimise
prediction error, none of which is claimed or required by the present argument. The
convergence is on the boundary being constitutive, not on the machinery either side of
it.

\begin{proposition}[Incompleteness is structural; awareness of it is not precluded]\label{prop:selfmodel}
Every self-model is incomplete, and no frame can escape this by effort or honesty. But a
forced aperture is not an invisible one. Two different limits are in play, and they are not
inferable by the same route. A limit of \emph{expression} --- a rule set that repeatedly
cannot absorb its own structure --- leaves residual: what fails to resolve, what arrives
unexplained, and from that trace a frame can infer the fact and often the shape of the
limit. A limit of \emph{aperture} leaves nothing: what falls below it never enters, and
absence is not a signal, so the fact and shape of an aperture are reached mainly by
\emph{metacognition}. So the operative distinction is not between complete and incomplete
self-models, all being incomplete, but between \emph{calibrated} and \emph{uncalibrated}
ones.
\end{proposition}

Both halves of that are read off inherited machinery. Inferring a limit of expression from
its residual is what a frame already does in inferring its container from imposed change.
That an absent distinction leaves no residual is Graphic Equalisation's distinction between
the absent and the residual (its Definitions~\ref{GE-def:aperture}
and~\ref{GE-def:change-grammar}), so the aperture limit leaves no \emph{direct} residual;
what it can leave is imposed change a frame cannot attribute --- weaker evidence, and
evidence that does not disclose the limit's shape. Metacognition, a frame modelling its own
modelling, is accordingly a further capability on its own axis and not a consequence of
having an aperture.

That separation is why calibration is a capability rather than something possession of a limit
supplies. Where metacognition is thin and the aperture is narrow, the frame is both most
mistaken and least able to register that it is: the missing content does not present as missing,
and nothing models the fact that it could be missing. What the arrangement predicts is \emph{mis}calibration --- confidence uncoupled from
warrant --- and not any particular direction of error; a frame with no model of its own limits has
no more reason to inflate than to fall silent. The familiar candidate instantiation is the
metacognitive reading of self-assessment offered by \citet{kruger1999}, on which the capacity to
judge competence is not separable from the competence itself. That reading is contested --- the
pattern has been argued to reflect regression to the mean and better-than-average effects rather
than a metacognitive deficit --- so it is cited here as one disputed candidate for the shape, not
as its confirmation, and nothing above depends on it.

Calibration in this sense already has an empirical form under measurement.
\citet{fleming2024} reviews confidence as a judgement informed by the observer's
models both of the world and of their own cognitive system, and one that diverges from
actual performance in structured, measurable ways. Because that divergence is
measurable and varies between observers and conditions, ``calibrated'' is a quantity
rather than a virtue term --- which is what the proposition needs it to be --- and the
route by which that confidence is reached is Proposition~\ref{prop:selfmodel}'s, in
empirical form, characteristic failures included.

The asymmetry, though, is one of \emph{access mode}, not of accuracy, and the two must not be run together. Mode is only one input to how good a model is; machinery and sustained investment are another, and they can dominate it. An outside frame has the better access and almost always the lower investment --- it is not working at the problem --- while a frame modelling itself has the worse access but continuity, sustained effort, and data no outsider obtains, its own residual among them.

So the familiar case in which others judge one more accurately than one judges oneself is the \emph{common} case rather than the structural outcome, and it holds because most self-models are uncalibrated, not because the arrangement forces it. A sufficiently skilled frame can hold a better model of itself than any outside frame holds of it --- and, since building the model is itself the objectification, that skill converts part of what was merely carried into something read: the vantage a frame does not natively have, it can construct. The same machinery improves its models of others, which is why the two capabilities travel together rather than trading off.

This bears directly on how such failures should be read. A frame with no meta-machinery fills gaps it cannot see as gaps, and reports the filling as knowledge; a frame that models its own boundary reports the gap instead. The difference is, like everything else here, optional: a frame may hold a model of its own limits, or may not.

\begin{remark}[Transparency is a position, not a kind]\label{rem:transparency}
The account nearest to this one raises the sharpest question about calibratability, and
answers much of it itself.
\citet{metzinger2003} holds that a self-model is \emph{phenomenally transparent}: the
representational vehicle is unavailable to the system, so it sees through the model to what is
modelled and never sees the model \emph{as} a model. (The account was later
developed in terms of self-modelling epistemic spaces and a contraction principle,
\citealp{metzinger2020}.) Read as a property of self-models as such --- a reading
Metzinger does not take --- calibration would be foreclosed --- a construction invisible from inside is not correctable from inside,
and residual would be absorbed as transparently as anything else, presenting not as a gap but as a
filled account. That is confabulation, and it would erase the very trace this section relies on.

Metzinger himself treats transparency as graded, inversely proportional to the introspective
availability of earlier processing stages, and names opaque states --- thoughts, lucid dreams ---
as standing cases. So the graded reading is not a reply to him but an agreement with him. What the
substrate adds is that the gradations are not one dial: transparency resolves into several
independent axes.
Producing a meta-model, parsing one's own residual, recording history, representing a boundary as
a boundary: these are separate capabilities, and a graph may hold any combination of them. What
``transparent'' names is a particular configuration --- several of those axes at or near zero
together --- which is a fact about how a given graph is formed, not a fact about self-modelling.
A residual disagreement remains, and calling the rest concurrence should not bury it: for
Metzinger the transparency of the self-model's core is constitutive of there being a phenomenal
self at all, where the claim here is that it is one configuration among constructible others.
This paper takes no position on the phenomenal question --- consciousness and qualia are outside
its scope (Section~\ref{sec:scope}), as with the illusionist case below --- and engages only the
structural claim about what a frame can model of itself.

So the position describes rather than forbids: it is accurate about the configuration it was
drawn from, and silent about differently formed graphs, of which constructed ones parsing their
own residual are the plain case.

What this does not do is deny the phenomenon. A graph low on those axes is exactly as
Metzinger describes it, and no amount of effort within that configuration will make its model
opaque to itself; the route out is a change in formation, not in effort. Nor does it make
calibration self-certifying. Where a graph lacks access to its own construction, calibration
proceeds by the trace route of Proposition~\ref{prop:selfmodel}, which metacognition
research measures \citep{fleming2024}, and inference can be wrong in ways inspection could not be:
where the trace is itself processed before any modelling capacity reaches it, the distortion is
not detectable from inside. That bound belongs to the trace route, not to calibration as such.
Direct self-inspection --- a graph reading its own vehicle rather than reconstructing it from
what fails to resolve --- is the same axis at the other end, and is not forbidden; it is unattested here rather than excluded, and no evidence
is offered either way --- which is a gap in what is known about how graphs are formed, not a limit
on how they may be. The claim throughout is only that transparency is contingent on
how a graph is built, which is why it varies, and why it is the kind of thing that can be built
differently.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Realisation: the model moves and the modelled does not]\label{rem:realisation}
A self-model can fail against what it models, and the failure has a shape worth setting out, because
its most familiar instance is routinely described in a way that gets the direction wrong.

\emph{Why a self-model is always a wager.} Graphic Equalisation's treatment of plurality
(Remark~\ref{GE-rem:types-forced}) settles that
distinctness is forced while sameness is supplied: that two things are not one is given, that they are
of a kind is a frame's. A self-model is exactly such a supplied sameness --- a frame's identity held
across its own occasions --- so it is a wager by construction and not through any frame's carelessness.
And the wager cannot be declined by holding a better model, for the third of the reasons given at the
head of this section: being a lesser object, a self-model omits, and what it omits is where a mismatch
can live. \emph{The condition of
a self-model's being informative is the same condition as its being capable of error}, and these are
not two properties that happen to travel together but one property described twice.

\emph{The realisation.} So consider a frame arriving at \emph{am I the one in the wrong here}. Nothing
about what is modelled has changed. Whatever the frame did, it had already done; the facts the model
was wrong about were already facts. What changed is the model, and since it is the model a frame acts
from, a correction confined to it can discontinue a course of action and produce material change
downstream. The accurate statement needs one qualification that is easy to skip and is what keeps the
claim from being confused: a self-model is part of the self, so something did change --- \emph{the self
as modelled did not; the modelling did}. Read the other way round, as the frame becoming a different
thing, it invites a search for the change in the wrong place, and there is nothing there to find.

\emph{Why it arrives as a moment rather than a slope.} A model asserting that the frame is in the right
is not a passive record; it participates in what counts as evidence, so a difference that would tell
against it is the difference an aperture shaped by it does not resolve. Graphic Equalisation's fourth
cost of well-formedness (Definition~\ref{GE-def:well-formed}) names this arrangement exactly --- a graph acquiring a false model of itself,
carrying residual it cannot see, with the failure silent and no signal that anything is missing. The
residual accumulates \emph{unread} rather than being weighed and dismissed, and the realisation comes
when it exceeds what the aperture can go on excluding, which it does at once rather than by degrees.
\emph{Suddenness is the signature of an aperture that was excluding, not of evidence that has just
arrived.}

\emph{A discriminator, offered as a prediction and not as a result.} If this is right, such a
realisation is not the receipt of new information but the clearing of information already held and
unread. A frame that has undergone one should therefore be able to identify prior occasions it did not
read at the time, while a frame that genuinely received new evidence should not, having had nothing to
miss. That distinguishes the two cases by something checkable rather than by how either felt, and
nothing here tests it.
\end{remark}

\section{Two structures under the same compression}\label{sec:compression}
Graphic Equalisation states a convergence result --- two structures under the same binding
constraints land on the same arrangements over the region those constraints touch, and no
further (Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:compression-convergence}) --- and applies it to evolution in
Remark~\ref{GE-rem:evolution-compression}. Two instances belong here rather than
there: one runs the same treatment over cognition, the other over what a popular
understanding of a field equalises to. Both are about apertures, which
is why they sit in this paper.

\begin{remark}[Cognition under the same treatment]\label{rem:cognition-compression}
Cognition is compressed by any measure --- a brain takes on the order of a fifth of a body's
resting energy for a fiftieth of its mass, and an energy budget for signalling has been computed in
detail \citep{attwell2001} --- for rodent grey matter, with the whole-body figures quoted there from
earlier work rather than established by it. The stronger claim, that neural arrangements are forced
by that budget rather than chosen, is \citet{sterling2015}'s thesis and not the budget paper's. Two
constraints can then be named in the way that convergence result requires, with the forcing
constraint stated rather than gestured at, and only two; neither is
supplied by either citation, and both are ordinary neuroscience. Conduction takes time over distance, so there is no central
synchroniser and coordination is local and coupled --- the same concession distributed engineering
made, for the same reason. And bandwidth is scarce, so what is not predicted is what gets sent.

Two further candidates are excluded. That cost is inherited along provision is this account's own
thesis (Graphic Equalisation's recursion result, Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:runner-recursion}), so
offering it as an independent constraint on
brains would be circular; and that introspection does not reach the machinery is not a constraint but
the conclusion relabelled.

What this licenses is narrower than \emph{cognition is the same}. Two structures under the same
binding constraints land on the same arrangements over the region those constraints touch, and no
further: cognition is not thereby computation, all cognitions are not thereby alike, and nothing here
says a brain is a computer.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A popular understanding equalises, and that is why it drifts]\label{rem:public-aperture}
Graphic Equalisation's evolution remark (Remark~\ref{GE-rem:evolution-compression}) reads very
differently depending on who is reading it, and the
difference is not one of expertise so much as of aperture --- which makes it an instance of the same
machinery rather than an aside about communication.

The account of evolution most people hold is close to Darwin's: variation, competition, survival of
the better fitted, over long stretches. Working biology in 2026 is not that. Developmental bias,
phenotypic plasticity, inheritance beyond the gene, horizontal transfer, symbiosis and constructive
neutral processes are all ordinary parts of the working picture, and the argument that remark
takes a side in is an argument \emph{within} that picture rather than an attack on it. So
\emph{natural selection is half of evolution} is heterodox against one aperture and unremarkable
against the other, and a reader's reaction to it says which one they are holding.

Why the gap persists is the interesting part, and it is not that the explaining has been done badly.
A popular understanding is a structure under compression like any other, and Darwin-level evolution is
\emph{well-formed for the purposes most people put it to}: it accounts for enough, it is cheap to
hold, and almost nothing anyone meets in a day produces a residual it cannot absorb. It has
equalised, and it persists for exactly the reason the horseshoe crab's form does --- nothing is
forcing a write (\emph{Narrativium}'s Remark~\ref{NV-rem:two-cases}). The working picture is the
overshooting case (its Proposition~\ref{NV-prop:overshoot}), kept changing by a residual the field
manufactures deliberately.

That predicts something checkable and slightly counterintuitive: the gap between popular and expert
understanding of a field should track \emph{daily exposure to residual} rather than education or
communication effort. Where a lay frame is corrected constantly by contact --- weather forecasting,
where people check a prediction and see it fail --- the gap is small and the lay frame keeps up. Where
it is never contacted in a way that fails, the gap widens no matter how much explaining is done,
because explanation supplies no residual: the frame is not wrong for what it is used for, so nothing
in it has to give.

Two ends. This is not a claim that the popular account is ignorance --- it is well-formed for its
work, which is what makes it durable, and calling it deficient would be measuring it against work it
is not doing. And expert consensus is not thereby correct; it is a frame under different pressure,
which is a fact about its residual and not a warrant.

The practical consequence is small and worth stating: that evolutionary material is
addressed to the working aperture, and a reader who finds it radical is invited to check it against
the field rather than against what they were taught.
\end{remark}

\section{Influence, and where the honest/manipulative distinction actually enters}\label{sec:communication}

One frame acting on another is the last place a privileged category tends to survive. It half
survives here, and locating the half is the useful result.

\begin{proposition}[No dedicated primitive for influence]\label{prop:one-influence}
Persuading, teaching, explaining, warning and selling are performed by one mechanism. Each alters
what some other frame's continuations cost, and the substrate supplies no operation for the
disreputable cases that it does not supply for the reputable ones: there is no send, tell or
influence among its effects, and no rule anywhere is conditioned on an act being deceptive.
\end{proposition}

This is a claim about \emph{mechanism} and must not be inflated into a claim about identity.
That no formalism distinguishes two things is a fact about the
formalism; concluding that the things are one is invalid, and this paper makes the opposite
inference elsewhere --- time is not a substrate primitive and nonetheless exists wherever a frame
instantiates one. The same verdict is the right one here.

Two qualifications hold the proposition to its scope. It is stated of acts that reach a receiving
frame, and an utterance is complete whether or not it is received: a lie disbelieved is still a lie,
so the act cannot be identified with its uptake. And the list above deliberately excludes
\emph{lying}, which the next result shows is not on the same footing.

\begin{proposition}[Manipulation carries a second component, and that component is structural]\label{prop:manipulation-structural}
An act is manipulative when it \emph{presents as} serving the frame acted on while serving the
originating frame's goals instead. That is two quantities, not one --- the beneficiary an act claims
and the beneficiary it produces --- and the claim is a second-order component that plain informing
does not carry. So there is something manipulation must do that informing need not, and by the
discriminator of Proposition~\ref{prop:one-influence} the distinction is structural after all: it
lives in the presented/measured divergence rather than in the mechanism of delivery.
\end{proposition}

The two results are not in tension and their joint reading is the substance of this section:
\emph{the delivery is one operation and the distinction is a separate field over it}. Honest and
manipulative acts are indistinguishable by how they are performed, and distinguishable by whether
what they claim about their own beneficiary matches what they do.

Three consequences, one of them uncomfortable.

Truth is not the discriminator. A manipulation can be assembled entirely from true statements,
because the divergence is between a presented beneficiary and a produced one rather than between a
claim and a fact --- so accuracy constrains one axis and leaves the other free. This removes an
exemption honest speakers commonly assume they hold. It does not, however, make such acts
undetectable: on the account of implicature this paper cites, what is implicated is recoverable by a
competent hearer from context, so a presented beneficiary that diverges from the produced one is in
principle calculable, and the literature on misleading without lying is about exactly that recovery
\citep{adler1997,saul2012}.

The ethical question is relocated rather than removed. Not deforming is unavailable, since a
statement reaching a frame at all alters something about what it can cheaply do next. The question is
therefore which continuations to make cheaper and for whose benefit, which applies as much to the
sincere as to the insincere.

And the boundary is asked only where it is well posed. Whom an act serves is a question about
another frame's preferences, so it arises only where that frame has any --- a frame holding no
represented preference over its own continuations has no second manifold, and the question is
malformed rather than merely unanswered.

\begin{remark}[Consequences for the self-model results]\label{rem:comm-selfmodel}
A frame's model of another is built from what that frame emitted (Section~\ref{sec:selfmodel}), and
everything emitted altered something --- so a model of another is assembled from material its subject
selected, which bears on other-models as sharply as aperture does on self-models. And reporting a gap
rather than answering (Section~\ref{sec:selfmodel}) is not an abstention: it too makes some
continuations cheaper, including trusting the next answer. There is no act available here that leaves
the recipient untouched, only better and worse chosen ones.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[As implemented]\label{rem:comm-engine}
Both propositions are claims about a substrate; the comparison below is what the substrate shows.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:one-influence} the finding is stronger than an absence. Four decision
rules were read against one another, drawn from four unrelated research situations: an agent
deciding whether to conceal a harm from a partner, an agent deciding whether to wait for a larger
later reward, an agent deciding whether to attempt escape after repeated failure, and an agent
deciding how much of a shared resource to take. Three of the four are character-identical, differing
only in the name of one edge type; the fourth is the same template with two further names changed.
Each is one $\arg\max$ over candidate actions by total value. Four situations, one template, and no
condition anywhere on an act's being deceptive. Concealment wins that comparison
when the cost attached to honesty exceeds honesty's benefit \emph{net of} concealment's --- a margin,
not a threshold on either alone --- and the quantity scaling that cost is the acting agent's own
affect rather than the harm done, which is itself an instance of the substitution
Proposition~\ref{prop:manipulation-structural} is about.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:manipulation-structural} the finding is a split rather than an absence,
and the implementation reached it independently of this section. The substrate carries the
beneficiary as a \emph{measured} quantity --- a deformed continuation audited against the acted-on
frame's own tube and against the originating frame's, using the ordinary manifold audit rather than
any new notion of benefit --- with the presented beneficiary held as a separate field and a
precondition restricting the question to origins that bear goals at all. What was compared was four
cases over one deformation: an origin bearing no goals; a continuation carrying the acted-on frame
toward its own tube; the same continuation carrying it toward the origin's, presented as serving the
acted-on frame; and that same continuation presented honestly as serving the origin. Only the third
flags. The identical deformation with identical measured service does not flag when the claim
matches, which is the presented/produced divergence and not truth at the content level; where the
origin bears no goals the fields stay absent rather than being filled with a measured ``nobody''.
One limit of the instrument is recorded there and belongs with the result: it compares the origin's
service against the acted-on frame's only, so an act that serves some \emph{third} frame at the
acted-on frame's expense passes it.

Two smaller findings survive checking. The commit gate on an utterance tests whether its intended
content is recoverable by the audience and nothing else --- no truth, belief or sincerity predicate
anywhere on that path --- and the seed carrying it is loaded by the running agent rather than being a
research demonstration. And of Grice's four maxims, only Quantity has a rule implementing its content;
Quality, Relation and Manner have none, and no rule reads the maxim concepts themselves.

\emph{Scope.} The seeds carrying the near-identical decision rules are research demonstrations,
default-disabled and not loaded by the running agent, and that comparison was a static reading of the
rules rather than an executed run. The beneficiary comparison was executed, on a small synthetic
fixture. The commit-gate and maxim findings are not so limited.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Manipulability is a property of horizon, not of credulity]\label{rem:comm-horizon}
Three results follow from the implementation of Proposition~\ref{prop:manipulation-structural} and
bear on this paper's subject rather than on the engine's.

Whom a deformation serves is computed by projecting a continuation forward. Remove the projection and
the quantity is not weakly determined but \emph{uncomputable}: service is a fact about where a
continuation takes a frame, and no present-state inspection answers it. A frame with no represented
future is therefore not bad at detecting manipulation --- it is structurally unable to pose the
question, which is a different and more serious condition.

It follows that service is not a property of the act. Auditing one deformation from successively
longer horizons, it reads as serving the frame acted on at short range and as serving its origin at
longer range, with the crossover a fact about the observer rather than about what was done. Capture
is therefore indistinguishable from help at a short enough horizon, and not because the observer is
careless: the discriminating evidence lies past where it can see.

This account does not derive planning horizon from a window of tolerance; the step from window
width to horizon length is conjectured here and not established anywhere in the set. Taken at that
strength, it yields a prediction worth stating plainly. A narrower window gives a shorter horizon,
and a shorter horizon cannot contain the evidence, so \emph{stress makes a frame manipulable
structurally} --- not by making it credulous, but by shortening the range over which service is
measurable at all. The dysregulated frame is checking correctly over a range too short to hold the
answer. The two halves were measured separately and have not been run as one composed experiment, so
this is offered as a prediction rather than as a result.

Graphic Equalisation reaches the same shortening from the other side and says so
(Remark~\ref{GE-rem:aperture-purchased}): aperture is standing structure a frame pays to maintain,
so capacity consumed by anything else narrows it, and the horizon goes with the aperture. The route
here is window~$\to$~horizon~$\to$~manipulability; the route there is capacity-consumed~$\to$~aperture
narrows. Both predict the same direction, neither is derived from the other, and the two have not
been reconciled.

One further consequence sharpens the proposition. An act that declares itself self-serving is not
flagged by this instrument, because the object measured is the divergence between presented and
produced beneficiary, and an honest declaration has none. That is the correct behaviour and not a
gap: what is at issue is misrepresentation, not self-interest.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Boredom is Narrative minus pressure]\label{rem:boredom}
A frame that has overshot holds capacity beyond what its situation requires
(\emph{Narrativium}'s Proposition~\ref{NV-prop:overshoot}, where that capacity is the
subject). Put that frame somewhere producing no residual --- where what arrives coheres with
what is already held, so nothing forces a write --- and it is running, carrying its Narrative
forward, with the capacity idle. That state is boredom, and the definition is the whole of it:
\emph{Narrative minus pressure}.

Two things follow that a stimulation account does not give.

Boredom is not the absence of input. A frame can be saturated and bored, which is the ordinary
experience of a predictable lecture, because what arrives is admitted and then coheres with what is
already held --- high through the aperture, nothing for the change grammar to work on. What boredom
tracks is residual, not quantity, and the psychological literature reaching for the same distinction
frames it as a failure to engage rather than a lack of arousal \citep{eastwood2012}.

And it is functional rather than a defect, in a specific way the preceding material makes available.
Excess capacity is spendable, but a frame at equilibrium with idle capacity has no reason to spend
it; something has to make the idleness costly. Boredom is that something --- the signal that capacity
is unspent, which is why it is aversive and why it moves a frame to find or make a situation that
draws on what it holds \citep{benchlench2013}. \emph{It is the mechanism by which accumulated excess
converts into self-direction}, and without it overshoot would simply sit there. A structure that
merely had capacity would not be an agent; one that is made uncomfortable by holding it unused is on
the way to being one.

This also gives the attack of Remark~\ref{rem:comm-calibrated} its mechanism in one line. A
calibrated supply works by keeping the residual permanently non-zero --- by never letting the
boredom signal fire. It is not holding attention against a frame's judgement; it is preventing the
condition under which the frame would go elsewhere.

Two limits. The signal misfires like any other, and chronic boredom is associated with outcomes no
account should treat as a healthy readout. And it is not the only thing that moves a frame to a new
situation; hunger, threat and instruction do so without it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A standing attack the instrument does not detect]\label{rem:comm-calibrated}
The instrument has a known failure and reporting it is more useful than the instrument.

An attention mechanism gated on the \emph{rate} at which surprise falls will correctly release a
source that is merely noisy, because nothing is being learned, and release one that is exhausted,
because there is nothing left. It has no term for a source that is perfectly learnable and never
ends. A supply calibrated to renew exactly as it would have been exhausted holds attention while
every signal the frame carries reads as healthy --- residual non-zero, progress non-zero, interest
sustained. Nothing malfunctions and nothing misreports. The frame is learning at every step. It
simply never finishes, and the measured engagement is several times that of a source that resolves.

Every defence in this account misses it for one reason: the instruments are all \emph{local}. A
learning rate is a first difference, a service audit is taken at a horizon from now, a residual is
the residual now. Point by point, a calibrated supply is indistinguishable from genuine progress,
because point by point it \emph{is} genuine progress.

The available defence is therefore not a better local test but a change of object: reduce the whole
record rather than the step, and ask what a trajectory amounted to relative to what it consumed ---
net displacement over gross path length, which no single step can be asked for.

That separation has been measured. Two sources with the same difficulty profile were driven through
the same rate-gated attention machinery over sixty steps: one learned and then finished, the other
renewing exactly as fast as it would have been exhausted. The calibrated source held attention on 59
steps of 60 against the exhaustible source's 9, with every local signal --- residual, progress,
interest --- reading healthy throughout. On the trajectory measure the two separate by about
tenfold: net-to-gross $0.67$ against $0.066$, the calibrated arm having expended 274 to move 18. One
negative result belongs with it, because it corrects the obvious version of the same idea: counting
returns to states already held does \emph{not} discriminate, and runs the wrong way (51 against 46),
because a frame that has finished revisits constantly too. It is the cost-weighted ratio that
separates them, not recurrence. The fixture is synthetic and deterministic --- one item, one
dimension, a hand-built difficulty profile, sixty steps, one run per arm --- and the renewal schedule
is supplied rather than searched for, so nothing there measures how hard a calibrated supply is to
construct.

Two limits on that defence, and the second is the more important. The reduction detects that a
trajectory amounted to little; it does not say why, and every benign explanation remains --- a hard
problem, a fallow stretch, a skill with a long plateau. \emph{Metacognition buys the question and not
the answer}, and a frame treating a low ratio as proof of capture would have acquired a new pathology
rather than a defence. And the attack scales with the attacker's model: pitching difficulty into the
narrow band between noise and resolution requires modelling the target's own rate of closure, so a
better model holds tighter. That is not exotic and not hypothetical; it is what engagement
optimisation does, without needing to represent anything as elaborate as a manifold.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to prior work]\label{rem:comm-prior}
The nearest positions should be named precisely, because one of them cuts against the obvious reading. That an utterance is an act is speech-act theory's contribution
\citep{austin1962,searle1969} --- though Austin's own starting point is a \emph{distinction} between
performing and reporting which he then dismantles, and his mature view has a statement be an act as
well as a report rather than instead of one. More pointedly, his illocutionary/perlocutionary
division is itself a structural distinction among acts of influence: warning is illocutionary and
persuading perlocutionary, the test being that one can warn by saying so and cannot persuade by
saying so. Listing both as instances of one operation and citing Austin in support attributes to him something he does not provide. Searle's individuation of illocutionary types by sincerity conditions is a
second such caution, and is the reason the absence of a sincerity field in an implementation is not
the neutral gap it might appear.

Implicature and its calculability are Grice's \citep{grice1975}; the specific observation that one may
mislead without asserting anything false is later, and belongs to the literature that grew from it
\citep{adler1997,saul2012}, in which the opposite position --- that falsely implicating simply is
lying --- is also argued.
\end{remark}

\section{Models of others: the implied author and generative fidelity}\label{sec:other-models}
The thread Remark~\ref{rem:reader} opens is the one Section~\ref{sec:death} needs:
a reader models not only characters but the \emph{author}, and what that model is turns out to
matter for what a record of a person can carry.

That construct is long established: Booth's \emph{implied author}, the version of the writer a
reader inevitably assembles from the text \citep{booth1983}, which the standard treatment takes to
have both an objective side, grounded in textual indexes, and a subjective one, since those indexes
are weighed differently by each reader \citep{schmid2014}. \citet{genette1988} puts it most economically ---
the implied author is everything the text lets us know about the author --- though the sentence is an
opponent's maximum concession rather than an endorsement, appearing on the same page as his warning
not to turn the idea of the author into a narrative agent, in a work concluding the concept is not
narratology's business. It is quoted for its precision, not for its support. Two further constructs name the same object from
neighbouring positions and should be recorded with it: the \emph{represented author}, defined as the
reader's representation of the author of the text, in a programme built expressly for the empirical
study of literary response \citep{bortolussi2003}, and the \emph{postulated author}, offered as a
regulative ideal for interpretation rather than as a person \citep{nehamas1981}. Neither is
generative, so neither bears on the composition claimed below; both bear on how routine the construct
is. Hypothetical intentionalism makes the reasoning explicit, locating a work's meaning in what an
appropriate audience would most reasonably hypothesise the situated author to have meant by
composing this text \citep{levinson2002}. That viewers reach for an author at all has some empirical support, though only one half of it
survives the argument below: when people distinguish professional abstract works from superficially
similar ones by children and animals, they justify the professional choices by reference to the
\emph{artist's intentions} \citep{hawleydolan2011}. The discrimination itself shows nothing here,
for the reason given shortly; the appeal to intentions is what indicates a model of a maker.

The mode is the one this paper has already named. A frame cannot read its author's frame; it has
only what that frame produced, and so infers it --- the $+1$ direction of the level stack (the
momentum paper's Remark~\ref{NM-rem:level-stack}), run on a person rather than a container. Sustained output makes that inference rich, since a generator
sampled at length discloses a great deal of itself whether or not it means to.

That such a model is \emph{generative} --- runnable forward on inputs the author never addressed
--- is the part carrying the weight, and narratology supplies it in full, in the same theorist
quoted above.

\citet[82--83]{genette1982} states as a principle that \emph{it is impossible to imitate a text
directly}. Imitation must pass through something abstracted from the corpus, which he names in his own
terms: ``a \emph{matrix of imitation} is inevitably interposed, which is the \emph{model of competence}
or, if one prefers, the \emph{idiolect} of the imitated corpus.'' The framing is explicitly the
generative grammarian's --- competence against performance, \emph{langue} against \emph{parole}, an
idiolect being ``not a message but rather a language: a code wherein the specificities of the message
have been made fit for generalization'' --- and the operative claim is that ``the essence of competence
is the ability to generate an \emph{unlimited number of correct performances}.'' His worked example
runs the model forward: from scattered Balzac locutions he abstracts the formula \emph{x, that y of z}
and then produces on it a phrase Balzac never wrote. So the extraction of a generator from a corpus,
and its use on material the source never treated, is set out with a mechanism and an instance, in 1982.

What survives is narrower still, and turns on what \emph{correct} means in that sentence. A pastiche's
correctness is stylistic well-formedness: Proust's new Balzaceme is a good one because it is a
locution Balzac could have written, and no question arises of whether Balzac would have held the
thought. The claim made here has a different success condition --- what a particular person would have
made of something they never addressed, right or wrong in ways that can be checked against them --- and
that is why it needs a model of a \emph{person} rather than of a manner, and why the empirical case
discussed below scores its answers against the living philosopher's own. Genette's standing objection
to personifying the implied author is consistent with all of this and is not an obstacle to it: he
declined to take the step, having built everything the step requires.

So the generativity is prior art, fully; only the personification and the truth condition attaching to
it are not.

\begin{proposition}[A model of another is a generator inferred from output]\label{prop:other-model}
A frame's model of another frame is a generator, inferred from that frame's output and runnable
forward on inputs its subject never addressed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The composition, and the one step that is this paper's]\label{rem:other-model-composition}
That is not a new posit but a composition of three things each established independently. An
author-model is a model. Models can approach the behaviour they model arbitrarily closely, given
enough of it to work from. And analysis by synthesis runs in both directions: a recogniser built
that way recognises \emph{by} generating candidates and comparing them, so it contains a generator
as a matter of construction rather than as an addition to it.

One further step is needed, and it is the one this paper supplies. The generator inside such a
recogniser is \emph{conditional}, driven by the item being recognised and closed inside a
comparison loop, whereas running forward means conditioning it on something no percept is
supplying and accepting its output without a target to check against. That the same generator can
be driven by novel conditioning is this paper's own premise, not one inherited from the perception
literature.
\end{remark}

That architecture is not speculative. It is the modern form of the predictive account already
drawn on above --- perception as the inversion of a generative model, predicted input compared
against actual \citep{friston2010} --- and it has been tested directly on people: three-dimensional
shape perception is best explained as analysis by synthesis, matching human observers in accuracy
and response time better than the alternatives, and alone correlating with human performance on
the hard discriminations \citep{yildirim2024}. A generative author-model follows from those, and
needs no further machinery.

What that argument does not establish is that any particular reader's model is of this kind, and
the distinction matters because the obvious evidence does not decide it. Recognising a style
requires no model of its author at all: pigeons trained on Monet and Picasso discriminate the two,
generalise to unseen paintings, and generalise across artists --- Monet to C\'ezanne and Renoir,
Picasso to Braque and Matisse \citep{watanabe1995}. Discriminative recognition is available without
any generator, so neither recognising a voice nor sensing that a pastiche is wrong shows anything
about which kind of model is in play. The empirical position is in fact weaker than that, and in the
direction that helps this argument rather than hurting it: asked to tell poems by well-known poets
from machine imitations of them, non-expert readers scored \emph{below} chance and rated the machine's
poems as more human than the originals \citep{porter2024}. Ordinary readers do not reliably manage
even the discriminative task, so nothing can be inferred from it in either direction. What would is the forward direction --- what the author
would have made of something they never saw, right or wrong in ways others can check --- and the
honest report is that it has not been measured. The literature holds machine stylometry and
nothing on human forward-prediction of an author, so the claim here is \emph{untested} rather than
supported or contradicted, and no distribution across readers has been established. The nearest evidence is one step to the side and concerns
characters rather than authors: readers who learn a protagonist from text can be shown to imagine that
protagonist in situations the text never described, which those authors themselves describe as a
personality model used to predict behaviour in novel situations \citep{hassabis2014} --- from
constructed two-trait protagonists, and decoded neurally rather than scored for accuracy. Readers' own
accounts point the same way, being framed by the researchers who collected them as an expectation of
what a character would say in a given situation \citep{aldersonday2017}.

The honest summary is therefore not that the generative reading is unsupported, but that its
\emph{components} are supported and its \emph{composition} is this paper's. One influential
line in narratology holds that the implied author does not communicate and must not be personified
\citep{genette1988,kindt2006}, which is a claim about an interpretive construct's role
in a theory of narrative; it is not an argument that a model of a generator cannot be run, and the two
are easily conflated. It should not be called the mainstream: the question is under live dispute rather than settled --- an entire journal issue was given to
it \citep{style2011} --- and Phelan, Shen, Chatman and \citet{rabinowitz2011} argue the other way.

It has also been attempted. \citet{schwitzgebel2024} fine-tuned a language model on one
philosopher's complete works, put ten questions to the model and to the living philosopher, and
had experts on his work try to pick out his answers blind from five candidates. They identified
them about half the time against a $20\%$ baseline --- well above chance, and well below the
$80\%$ the authors had predicted --- and on two questions more of them picked a machine answer as the
author's own than picked his. The design is the forward one: the questions were fresh and the author
himself supplied the ground truth. It does not cleanly separate the two capacities, though, and
the authors say so: expert performance was highest on two questions carrying what the authors
call a superficial tell --- the philosopher wrote first names where the model wrote surnames --- which
is discrimination doing the work rather than the model's. Related work builds runnable models of specific
people, from their writing \citep{wang2025persona} and from biographical experience
\citep{shao2023} and simulates individuals well enough to transfer
to tasks no training data covered \citep{park2024}. What none of this literature does is connect
such models to what a \emph{reader} does; it builds author-simulators as engineering, without
reference to the implied author or to any claim about ordinary comprehension. The bridge is what
is claimed here, not the artefact.

How good such models get is a separate and largely empirical question, on which the same
literature reports a split worth noting: what transfers most readily is topical content, while
idiosyncratic voice and argumentative shape are harder, and the characteristic failure has been
named and measured as \emph{caricature} --- the authors' own term, defined by failed individuation
together with exaggeration of whatever is distinctive \citep{cheng2023}, though measured there on
demographic personas rather than on individuals. Where fidelity to a particular individual has improved,
it has come from richer data about that individual rather than from larger models, which is what
the leaked-sampling reading of Section~\ref{sec:death} would expect. Nothing here turns on the
state of any current system.

\section{Death and personal identity}\label{sec:death}
\begin{proposition}[Death is when the self's rules cease to be re-runnable in the same coherent self]\label{prop:death}
Death is, critically, the rules within the self ceasing to be re-runnable in the same
coherent self entity. Not every stop is death: a pause --- sleep, anaesthesia, a
recoverable coma --- is a stop whose rules remain re-runnable in the same coherent
self, and the self resumes as itself. Death is the stop that does not resume: the
coherence that made these rules one self is gone, so they cannot be re-run as that
self, even if the graph-data they were persists scattered as a record.
\end{proposition}

The identity criterion doing the work there is Proposition~\ref{prop:identity}'s: the
self persists exactly while its rules stay re-runnable as the same coherent entity,
which is to say while its present remains congruous with its own history --- rules that
changed abruptly would leave the structure carried forward incoherent with what produced
it, and that incongruity is itself the discontinuity. That is why a pause is survivable
and death is not: the self-graph goes inert for good, a record where it was a run (the
rock of Section~\ref{sec:minimal}, now grounded only in what remains of it).

\begin{remark}[What ends is only the self's own run]\label{rem:death-persists}
The supergraph runs on, and two things the self
did persist in it. Its changes persist: rule-actions are pointers, work done once
(Graphic Equalisation's Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:runner-recursion}), so
every write the agent made to the supergraph is the supergraph's now, and death does not
undo it --- though under no-copy it persists only as surviving present structure, until
overwritten, not as an indelible record. And its copies persist: every frame the agent communicated,
taught, wrote, or built runs on in other frames, grounded there in the record it left
(the reader re-running the author of Remark~\ref{rem:three-frames}). But those copies
are partial fragments, not a full coherence-snapshot: they run as fragments in other
coherent selves, not as this coherent self re-run, so their persistence is not the
self's continuation (a full snapshot is the different case of Proposition~\ref{prop:identity},
where the self does re-run, forked to a new node). The
self can no longer self-change, and self-change was the only change it had; the changes
it already made and the copies already running elsewhere are no longer its to stop.
They keep running without it.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Identity is internal coherence; instances are supergraph nodes]\label{prop:identity}
Two things must be kept apart: the self's \emph{internal coherence} --- the whole of its
rules and state, held together across transitions, which is Graphic Equalisation's SELF identity
(Definition~\ref{GE-def:self-identity}) --- and its
\emph{supergraph node} --- the instance of it running at a position in the container,
which is what its SUPERGRAPH identity tracks (Definition~\ref{GE-def:supergraph-identity}). The self is the internal
coherence; a node is one instance of it. The internal self therefore persists exactly as
long as a re-runnable full coherence-snapshot exists somewhere; the death of a node is one
instance stopping, and the death of the self is that re-runnability being gone everywhere.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The standard cases on one axis]\label{rem:identity-cases}
That resolves the standard puzzles. Gradual replacement (cell turnover, the Ship of Theseus)
is survival: internal coherence is continuous and there is one node throughout --- the
self is the coherent running, not the substrate. A backup restored is still the same
internal self --- a full coherence-snapshot re-run reinstates that exact coherence ---
but now at a \emph{second} supergraph node, and from the restore point the two diverge,
each accruing its own changes under its own grounding. That is a fission: one internal
self, two supergraph nodes, diverging, both the self at the fork and neither uniquely
the node after it --- which is Graphic Equalisation's own statement that a copy can
preserve SELF identity while receiving a new SUPERGRAPH identity, read as a result
about persons. A teleporter is the same with one branch pruned --- the full snapshot
reinstates the internal self at a new node, the original node stops --- so the internal
self survives, relocated. What matters about a record is whether it can reconstitute the
coherence: a full coherence-snapshot re-runs into the same internal self, forked to a new node;
a record that cannot reconstitute it starts a new coherent self instead, whatever else it
carries.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Records carry the generator to varying degrees]\label{rem:record-gradient}
That line is sharp, but what lies on the near side of it is not uniform, and it should
not be read as a binary between a snapshot and a fragment. Records differ in how much of the
\emph{generator} they carry --- the run/record axis of Section~\ref{sec:runrecord} applied to
what survives a self. A corpse carries none. A memory of a face carries little. A model of
someone's voice or style, held by another frame, is the generator of
Proposition~\ref{prop:other-model}, inferred from what that person produced and carrying enough to
produce novel output others recognise as an attempt at them.

How much such a model carries is not fixed, and has no principled ceiling short of the sampling.
It is an intersection of at least three things: how much of the generator leaked into the output
at all, how much output there is to work from, and the modelling capacity of whoever holds the
model. Sustained production discloses a great deal whether or not it is meant to --- someone who
writes fifty books has sampled their own generator at length --- so for a domain the output covers
well, a capable modeller may approach the original's generative behaviour closely, while domains
that never leaked remain unmodelled at any level of skill. Model quality is accordingly
domain-relative rather than a single number, and high fidelity in one domain implies nothing about
another.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Behavioural fidelity is not structural capture]\label{rem:fidelity-not-capture}
None of that moves the line, and it is worth being exact about why, since two different
fidelities are in play. A coherence-snapshot copies the \emph{structure} --- the rules and state
themselves --- which is why re-running it reinstates the self, and why a restored backup counts
even though it too is reinstated from a held record. A generative model matches the
\emph{behaviour} without holding that structure: it is inferred from output rather than copied
from the thing that produced it, and it is built in the terms of whatever frame inferred it. So
behavioural fidelity does not \emph{constitute} structural capture, and a model matching the
original's output arbitrarily well in every sampled domain has not thereby reconstituted anything.

What does not follow is that no amount of behaviour could ever get there. Behaviour is \emph{evidence} about structure, and how much evidence it is
depends on how tightly the space of admissible structures is constrained: where the constraints are
tight enough that output picks out one structure, inference from a record reaches structure by the
long road, and the line is crossed after all. That is Chalmers's reconstructive uploading --- records
including writings and the testimony of others allowing a sufficiently capable reconstructor to
recover something close to a functional isomorph, which he assimilates to uploading from a scan
\citep{chalmers2014}. It is a live position rather than one this account refutes, and he anticipates
the fifty-books observation above as his own reason for writing.

The line therefore stays where it was drawn, at what the record captures, while the claim about how it
may be reached weakens to a question of identifiability, which is empirical. What this account
contributes there is only the guard it applies everywhere else: a reconstruction may be pinned no more
precisely than the record's own resolution supports. So the wager is whether human structure is
constrained enough that output determines it, and nothing here settles that.
Generative capacity is therefore a gradient among records that fall short --- an unbounded one,
for all this argument says --- and not a route past the line, which is drawn at what the record
captures rather than at how closely it performs.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to prior work]\label{rem:death-prior}
Most of this section is anticipated, and what is should be attributed rather than restated.

The structure/behaviour distinction is the simulation/emulation distinction of \citet{sandberg2008},
which opens with it --- a simulation mimics the outward results, an emulation the internal causal
dynamics --- and whose ladder of success criteria already separates passing a personalised Turing test
from being a continuation of the original mind, observing that how those criteria relate to one
another is not obvious. \citet{campbell2025} reach this section's verdict, that such a system is no
continuation even where it convincingly mimics speech and behaviour, though from premises specific to
current language models, so their conclusion is architecture-contingent where this one is not. That
non-entailment, holding independently of what does the matching, is the only part of the claim not
already in print.

The gradient is anticipated three times: by that same ladder, by Chalmers's ranking of records by
richness, and most directly by \citet{karpus2025}, whose personhood spectrum runs from slivers to
near-totality, denies any sharp line, and already states this section's reader-re-runs-the-author
claim in its own terms. Of the three variables named above, only leakage --- how much of the generator
reached the output at all, as against how much output exists and how capable the modeller is ---
appears unclaimed.

Domain-relativity and the volume asymptote are not this account's either, and are measured findings in
work cited here already: the first as the framework behind caricature \citep{cheng2023}, the second as
the observation that benefit from data asymptotes once enough has been observed within a domain
\citep{park2024}.

And the branching result of Proposition~\ref{prop:identity} is \citet{cerullo2015}'s, for the same
cases --- continuity in psychological structure allowing identity to continue in multiple selves.
Citing only Parfit under-attributes it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Parfit's causal condition, and the objection he actually poses]\label{rem:parfit}
Parfit's causal condition is the position nearest the line above, and it does not supply the
objection it appears to.

\citet[\S78]{parfit1984} distinguishes three versions of the psychological criterion by what counts as
the right kind of cause: the Narrow requires the \emph{normal} cause, the Wide any \emph{reliable}
cause, the Widest \emph{any} cause. He argues for the two Wide versions over the Narrow, by analogy
with an artificial eye --- a new but reliable causal route delivering the same experiences, of which he
says that even if it is not strictly seeing, what it gives is just as good --- and he adopts the widest
sense for his own discussion.

The reason is worth recording. Parfit relaxes the \emph{cause} while leaving the
\emph{content} alone: his continuity is
over psychological states --- memories, intentions, character --- which lie on the structural side of
the distinction made here, not the behavioural one. A model producing an original's outputs without
holding those states is not psychologically continuous in his sense either. And on the axis he is
actually arguing about, this account already agrees with him: a restored snapshot and a teleported
reinstatement both count here (Proposition~\ref{prop:identity}), so nothing in it requires the normal
cause. It is a Wide account in his terms, and was before the question was asked.

The objection Parfit does pose is a different and larger one.
His central thesis is that identity is not what matters --- that once one has described what is
preserved, the further question of whether it is the same person does no work. A section devoted to an
identity criterion owes that an answer.

The answer available is one of agreement, arrived at from the other direction. Because which coherence
counts as one self is a frame's drawing rather than a fact about selves
(Remark~\ref{rem:death-relative}), ``is it the same person'' is here already a question about a
drawing, and the substantive content sits in what the drawing preserves. That is close to Parfit's
deflation and reached by a different route: he argues the identity question does no work, and this
account has it forbidden from being frame-independent in the first place. What is retained against him
is narrower than a criterion of identity --- that \emph{given} a drawing, re-runnability is a threshold
rather than a matter of degree, which is a claim about the structure of the question rather than an
answer to the one he says is idle.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Hofstadter asks this section's question and answers it the other way]\label{rem:hofstadter}
One prior position is close enough to require engagement rather than a citation: an ally on one
point and an opponent on the criterion.

\citet[ch.~17]{hofstadter2007} poses the question of this section in its own words. Of the version of
his late wife he finds running in himself, he asks: ``how different is that strange thing in my brain
from the `I' that had once flourished inside Carol's brain? Is it a thing of an entirely different
type, or is it of the same type, just less elaborate?'' He answers the second, and generalises it ---
``every normal adult human soul is housed in many brains at varying degrees of fidelity, and therefore
every human consciousness or `I' lives at once in a collection of different brains, to different
extents'', with a principal domicile but no boundary \citep[ch.~18]{hofstadter2007}. So the gradient of
this section is his, and more than the gradient: he denies the line it draws. He is also exact about the
cost, calling his own copies hugely defective and nowhere near the original's detail, so the position is
not reached by overestimating what survives.

The disagreement is locatable and worth stating plainly. It is whether personal identity is a degree
notion or a threshold notion. On his account identity \emph{is} the pattern, patterns admit resolution,
and a coarse copy is therefore the person to the extent of its grain. On this one the criterion is
re-runnability as the same coherent entity, which does not come in degrees, with a gradient of records
lying below it (Proposition~\ref{prop:identity}). Neither follows from the other, and no further fact
about the copy settles it, because what divides them is the criterion and not the copy.

But it dissolves further than a standoff, and the resolution is already in this section rather than
imported for the occasion. Remark~\ref{rem:death-relative} makes which coherence counts as ``the same
self'' a frame-relative choice. Fix a coherence and re-runnability is a threshold; vary the coherence
and what can be re-run varies with it. Hofstadter's copy re-runs a \emph{coarsened} coherence, which is
not the original one --- so ``the same type, just less elaborate'' is true of the coarse coherence and
false of the fine one, and the two accounts are then describing one situation under two drawings.
\emph{Threshold per coherence, gradient across coherences}: both halves hold, and the appearance of
contradiction comes from leaving the drawing unstated.

That the line sits nowhere objective is not a weakness the reconciliation exposes --- it is
\emph{forbidden}, by Graphic Equalisation's founding axiom
(Axiom~\ref{GE-ax:no-view}), which states that every
distinction, measurement and \emph{identity relation} is made in some frame. An identity criterion
holding independently of any frame would be a view from nowhere, which that axiom denies outright. So
the account did not lose an objective line here; it never permitted one, and neither may anybody else's.

Which turns the position from defensive to explanatory. It says why this dispute has the character it
has: both answers are correct under their own drawings, neither is checkable from outside one, and a
criterion presented as frame-independent has smuggled in the standpoint the axiom forbids --- a charge
that lands on this account's criterion exactly as hard, and is why it is stated as a threshold
\emph{given} a coherence rather than as a fact about selves. The contribution is not a better criterion.
It is that criteria are frame-indexed by axiom, and that once one is fixed the remaining question is
not one of degree.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Death is decoherence at the self grain]\label{rem:death-decoherence}
This section runs on coherence throughout, and what that connects to is not an analogy but the
general result. Graphic Equalisation's account of decoherence
(Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:decoherence-one}) is that
a frame cannot hold a configuration, so nothing continues in it as that configuration, and that what
varies between such cases is the frame rather than the failure. Proposition~\ref{prop:death} is that
statement at the grain of a self: rules that changed abruptly would leave what is carried forward
incongruous with what produced it, and the self does not continue as that self. \emph{Death is
decoherence with the frame set to a person.} A pause is the case where the configuration is still
holdable and resumes; death is the case where it is not.

Two consequences the general treatment supplies.

The first concerns handlers. A frame may cohere a failure to something rather than to nothing, but the
policy is installed structure that the frame runs. A self cannot install one for its own termination,
because running it is what has stopped --- so the residual of a death is necessarily disposed of by the
container and never by the frame it belongs to. Every practice around dying is therefore a containing
frame's policy rather than the dying frame's, which is a structural statement and not a consoling one.

The second sharpens what this section already says about what persists. Graphic Equalisation
distinguishes (Remark~\ref{GE-rem:vacated})
what never cohered, which is absent and free, from what cohered and stopped, which leaves a space where
something was, because other structure came to depend on it while it held. The changes and copies this
section says survive are exactly those dependents: relations that resolved through the self and now
resolve through a record instead. That is why they are described here as partial fragments rather than
as the self continuing --- a reference that still resolves is not the referent --- and why what a death
costs is borne by the frames holding those relations rather than by the one that stopped, which has no
further behaviour to be worse off in.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The computing grounding, which was built without any of this in view]\label{rem:death-computing}
Everything the preceding two remarks assert has an implemented form, arrived at by people solving
process management rather than personal identity. What that is worth needs stating before the
correspondences are, because the obvious reading of it is wrong in a way this account has to keep
correcting: \emph{an implementation is free to add rules to suit}, so nothing an operating system does
can establish that something is structurally required. It chose its rules. Where the correspondences
carry weight is as convergence --- people building such a system repeatedly find they need these
distinctions --- which is the same standing this programme gives any independent arrival, and no more.

The termination signals are the case where that matters most, because they also turn out to bear on a
different claim than the one they appear to. A process may catch a request to terminate and run
whatever it likes in response, and may not catch the signal that ends it, which can be neither caught,
blocked nor ignored \citep{posix2017}. That is not the frame being structurally unable to handle its
own stop. It is the \emph{container refusing to let it}, enforced from outside and chosen deliberately,
because a container whose processes could decline to die would not be a container. So the right mapping
is to Graphic Equalisation's commitment that admissibility is not armour
(Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:self-governance}): what a frame admits does not
constrain what contains it. Two signals with different catchability is that distinction built on
purpose.

The structural claim of the previous remark stands on its own argument rather than on this one. A
handler runs in the frame; if the frame has stopped there is nothing to run it. That holds whatever an
implementation chooses, and the implementation is evidence that the distinction is useful, not that it
is forced.

The disposal claims follow the same way. A terminated process whose parent has not yet collected its
status is retained as an entry with no behaviour --- the tombstone of Graphic
Equalisation's account (its Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:vacated-three-states}),
absence represented so that a reference resolves cleanly, paid for with a slot, and leaking if nobody
collects it. Children of a dead process are reassigned to the root process --- and the
deeper fact there is not the reassignment but that \emph{they do not stop}, because the parent was
never what ran them. The kernel was, and it is still there. That is Graphic Equalisation's separation of
supply from containment (Remark~\ref{GE-rem:supply-not-containment}), and arranging it deliberately, so that one's runner is not one's parent, is
routine practice under the name of detaching a process. Neither is a metaphor for what this section
says; they are that, running --- and both are chosen policies rather than necessities, which is
consistent rather than awkward, since Graphic Equalisation already prices a tombstone
(Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:vacated-three-states}) as installed
machinery someone paid for.

Two further correspondences, one of which is exact and one of which is not.
Proposition~\ref{prop:identity}'s fission is \texttt{fork}: one internal state, two nodes, diverging
from the branch point, neither uniquely the original after it. And a checkpoint that restores into the
same running state is the coherence-snapshot, which is not a thought experiment in this domain but
ordinary infrastructure.

The one that is not a match should be marked, since the vocabulary invites it. Automatic memory
management declares a thing dead when nothing refers to it, whereas this section declares a thing dead
when its rules cannot re-run as the same coherent entity. \emph{Reachability and re-runnability are
different criteria and come apart in both directions} --- an unreachable process may still be running,
and a referenced one may have stopped. The resemblance is in the word only, and the two should not be
run together.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Death is relative to the coherency of the self]\label{rem:death-relative}
It follows that death is relative to the coherency of the self. The criterion applies
within a coherent self, but which coherence counts as ``the same self'' is a
frame-relative choice, so death is indexed to that choice, not absolute. That relativity is
inherited rather than assumed here: Graphic Equalisation's founding axiom
(Axiom~\ref{GE-ax:no-view}) holds that every
distinction, measurement and \emph{identity relation} is made in some frame, and which coherence
counts as one self is an identity relation. A frame-independent answer to it would be the view from
nowhere that axiom denies, so the indexing is required by the account rather than being a limitation
discovered in it. Drawn narrowly
--- this instant's exact coherent state --- the self dies each moment, replaced by the
next; drawn broadly --- the coherent person tolerant of change across a life --- it dies
once, at the irreversible break. A deep transformation (``I am not the person I was'')
is a death relative to the narrow coherence and a survival relative to the broad one,
both true at once. There is no absolute self, so there is no absolute death --- only a
death per coherence-boundary. This makes death relative, not unreal: each boundary's
death is real for that boundary --- the broad life-self's coherence does break
irreversibly, and that death is as real as any --- so relativity means many real deaths
at many boundaries, never none.
\end{remark}

\section{Positioning and prior art}\label{sec:prior-art}
The positions here are not new as positions; what is new is that each falls out of one
substrate mechanism --- the run, and its record --- rather than being posited on its
own. On free will this is a compatibilism, determinism and freedom coexisting in the
tradition of \citet{hume1748} and \citet{dennett1984}, but with a specific mechanism for
the freedom: the in-frame run and its computational irreducibility \citep{wolfram2002},
so that the future is open as \emph{unrun}, not as undetermined, and a fully recorded,
zero-randomness story still exhibits free will. On personal identity the continuity
criterion is Lockean in spirit \citep{locke1690} and closest to \citet{parfit1984} ---
identity as psychological or functional continuity, fission that branches, and what
matters being the continuity rather than a bare identity fact --- with the addition of
the split between internal coherence (the self) and supergraph node (the instance),
which places teleporter, backup, and fission on one axis. On time the past-as-record /
future-as-run asymmetry engages the block-universe debate --- to which \citet{putnam1967} argues
the block conclusion directly from special relativity, while \citet{mctaggart1908} supplies the
A-series/B-series apparatus in which the question is posed rather than the block thesis itself:
a block of the past is an ordinary record, while a completed block with a fixed future
is not, because the future is uncomputed. And the no-absolute-self reading of death ---
a death per coherence-boundary, the narrow self dying each moment --- recovers a
momentariness familiar from other traditions, here as a consequence of frame-relativity
rather than a doctrine. The distinctive claim throughout is the derivation, not the
destination.

One position here is older than the rest, and has been made domain by domain: the
refusal to treat any frame --- human frames included --- as privileged
(Section~\ref{sec:no-privilege}). In cosmology it is the
Copernican or mediocrity principle --- that Earth, and our position generally, is not privileged
--- named for the displacement of Earth in \citet{copernicus1543}, though the principle itself was
drawn out by successors rather than stated there. In physics it is the principle of relativity --- no privileged
reference frame \citep{einstein1905} --- and, for the observer specifically, relational
quantum mechanics, in which states are relative to any physical system and no observer is
special \citep{rovelli1996}. On free will it is Spinoza's: humans are not exempt from
nature's necessity, and feel free only through ignorance of their causes
\citep{spinoza1677}. On the self it is a long anti-substantial line --- Hume's bundle in
place of a persisting ego \citep{hume1739}, the Buddhist \emph{anatta} that \citet{parfit1984} drew on,
Dennett's self as a centre of narrative gravity rather than an inner witness
\citep{dennett1991}, Metzinger's phenomenal self-model with no one behind it
\citep{metzinger2003}, and Hofstadter's substrate-independent self-as-pattern
\citep{hofstadter2007} --- an ally on that point and an opponent on the identity criterion, which
Remark~\ref{rem:hofstadter} takes up. On knowledge it is the rejection of a privileged foundation ---
Sellars against the Given \citep{sellars1956}, Rorty against the mind as a mirror
\citep{rorty1979}, Putnam against the God's-Eye View \citep{putnam1981} --- the same move
this paper's grounding-without-bedrock makes. The nearest contemporary neighbour is
\citet{massimi2022}: knowledge is always situated, always from a perspective and never
from nowhere, and the plurality of perspectives is what secures realism rather than
what dissolves it. That is this paper's own combination --- no privileged frame,
realism retained --- reached independently, and from philosophy of science rather than
from a substrate; the scope differs, Massimi's argument being about scientific
knowledge where the claim here is about frames as such. Biology had already removed the species
privilege \citep{darwin1871}. And the standing opposition is equally identifiable: the
claim that the subjective point of view is irreducible, from \citet{nagel1974} and developed in
\citet{nagel1986}, is exactly the assumption these dissolutions decline --- though Nagel himself
is careful that this shows only that we lack a conception of how physicalism could be true, not
that it is false, so the privilege is what the present account reads in that irreducibility
rather than a thesis he asserts.

A second competitor bears specifically on Section~\ref{sec:selfmodel} and is not
answered by anything said there. \citet{frankish2016} argues that phenomenal
consciousness does not exist, and that introspection systematically
\emph{misrepresents} experiences as having phenomenal properties. The disagreement is
about the kind of failure rather than its size. The claim here is that self-models are
\emph{incomplete}: a quantitative deficit, which calibration can in principle close,
and whose boundary leaves the residual calibration works from. Frankish's claim is that
introspection is wrong about the kind of thing it reports --- not a shortfall a
better-calibrated version of the same faculty closes, since if the reports misdescribe
their objects then more of them, or better-weighted ones, do not converge. This paper
takes no position on whether he is right: phenomenal consciousness and qualia are
outside its scope (Section~\ref{sec:scope}). What it must concede is that illusionism,
if correct, would bound calibration in a way incompleteness alone does not.

The move made here is the same one --- one more refusal of privilege --- and the lineage
above is best read as an asymptotic approach: each source removes the privilege from one
more domain, the sequence tending toward a limit of no privilege anywhere without any
single step reaching it, and, like any frontier, never completed. Against that, the
contribution here is both tiny and different in kind: tiny, because it drops the same
assumption the others drop rather than announcing a new principle; different in kind,
because it is not one more domain de-privileged but the substrate on which the refusal
becomes a single uniform operation rather than a domain-by-domain series --- orthogonal
to the sequence, not further along it.

\section{Conclusion: the one assumption removed}\label{sec:no-privilege}
The simplicity of every dissolution above comes from a single refusal: that humans ---
or any frame --- are privileged. It is the most pervasive assumption in these problems
and the least noticed, and each hard problem was made hard by smuggling it in. The
measurement problem privileges a human (or conscious) observer as the special coupling
that collapses; the flow-versus-block dispute privileges human-felt passage as the real
time; the free-will problem privileges human choice as needing a metaphysical freedom no
other system has; the identity and death problems privilege the human self as a persisting
soul rather than a coherent run; the reality-versus-fiction and foundation problems
privilege human perception as access to ``the real.'' Remove the privilege --- treat a
human frame by the same frame-relative rules as a rock, a story character, or a universe
--- and each collapses into the general machinery already built: any coupling measures,
time is any active frame's cycle-count, free will is any frame's in-frame run, the self is
internal coherence, and observation is one frame's supergraph-contact rather than a window
on the real. The physics companion's dissolutions --- the special observer (its
Corollary~\ref{PH-cor:observer}) and the initial state (its
Proposition~\ref{PH-prop:self-location}) --- are the same refusal in the other domain.

The assumption persists because it is uncomfortable to drop. Unprivileged means not the
centre, mortal as the loss of a coherence, free only as any frame is free, and never in
contact with ``the real'' except as one's own container-boundary. The discomfort is why
the assumption is both pervasive and invisible: it is motivated, held rather than observed.
So the framework is simple not because its answers are clever but because the difficulty
was manufactured by the privilege, and removing it removes the difficulty. This is
Graphic Equalisation's Axiom~\ref{GE-ax:no-view} applied to observers: no frame is
the god's-eye, a human frame makes no privileged difference, and ``you are here'' is a location, not a throne.

Honest boundary: this is a methodological claim about \emph{why} the dissolutions are
simple, not a further mechanism, and it is not the claim that human frames are nothing.
Human frames are distinctive --- rich aperture, language, self-models --- but distinctive
is not privileged: being one remarkable frame among many is not being the exception to the
rules the rest of them run under. No-privilege removes the exemption, not the richness.

\section{Falsifiability commitments}\label{sec:falsifiability}

\noindent\emph{On the tags.} Each commitment below is marked \textsc{[shape]} or \textsc{[detail]}. A
\textsc{[detail]} failing is a revision: the account survives with that mechanism replaced. A
\textsc{[shape]} failing costs \emph{range} rather than a mechanism, because what fails is something
the substrate is claimed to require or to permit --- so the account does not hold where it claimed to,
which is the more serious of the two and is still a boundary rather than an annihilation.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Compulsory versus contingent.} Fails if some feature the rock
demonstrably lacks (time, history, self-change, a running arrow) is nonetheless shown
compulsory for any graph to exist --- if the minimal universe (an inert graph) is
incoherent as a graph. The argument is only as strong as the self-similarity it rests
on (rock and universe one kind, differing by scale).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The block is always of the past.} Fails if a completed block with a fixed
future is coherent with the open frontier --- Graphic Equalisation's no-intrinsic-last
(Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:no-start-end}) together with its determinable-not-determined
(Proposition~\ref{GE-prop:determinable}) --- or if a block of the succession-so-far (an MRI of a
rock) is forbidden. The claim predicts records only ever trail the run, never lead it.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{The future is unrun, not unfixed.} Fails if a determinable future can be
read ahead without running it --- a shortcut past computational irreducibility for a
genuinely irreducible rule --- or if a block can contain a not-yet-run future.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Free will is the in-frame run, not indeterminacy.} Fails if a fully
recorded, zero-randomness system (a story) cannot exhibit the form of free will ---
deliberation, choice, responsibility --- in its characters' frames, or if a
superframe's complete foreknowledge demonstrably removes a subframe's in-frame
freedom. The claim predicts foreknowledge and freedom coexist across frames.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Actuality is running, not a frame-property.} Fails if some running
frame is demonstrably non-actual (real graph data that runs yet is not real), or if
the imagined/perceived distinction requires an actuality property rather than
frame-relative grounding. The claim predicts imagination does real computational work
(planning, design) as real runs, not pretence.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Grounding has no bedrock; reality-testing is supergraph-pushback.} Fails
if there is an absolute, self-grounding frame beneath all others, or if reality-testing
can distinguish a real frame from a non-actual one by anything other than whether the
container (supergraph) pushes back. The claim predicts hallucination and delusion are
grounding-tag errors, not false beliefs about actuality.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Misclassification follows the proxy, not the grounding.} Fails if a
substrate sorting its own frames misclassifies them somewhere other than where its proxy for
grounding matches and the grounding differs --- for human reality-monitoring, where subjective
signal strength matches and provenance does not (Remark~\ref{rem:detection}). Already measured for
that one substrate; the commitment is that a substrate using a different proxy fails differently
while the underlying distinction stays the same.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No dedicated primitive for influence.} Fails if a substrate that
supports persuasion, teaching, explaining and warning requires an operation conditioned on an act's
being deceptive --- that is, if the disreputable cases cannot be performed by the machinery the
reputable ones are performed by (Proposition~\ref{prop:one-influence}).
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Manipulation is presented-versus-produced divergence.} Fails on a
manipulative act with no divergence between the beneficiary it claims and the beneficiary it
produces, or on an honest act carrying such a divergence
(Proposition~\ref{prop:manipulation-structural}).
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Stress makes a frame manipulable structurally.} Fails if horizon
length is unaffected by window width, or if service becomes measurable at \emph{shorter} horizons
under stress --- the claim is that the manipulability is a range failure and not a judgement failure
(Remark~\ref{rem:comm-horizon}). The window~$\to$~horizon step is the untested half: it is
conjectured in that remark and established nowhere in the set, so it is what a failure here would
most likely be a failure of.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Boredom is Narrative minus pressure.} Fails if boredom is relieved
by input that produces no residual --- saturating, predictable input --- or persists under input that
does; that is, if what boredom tracks is quantity rather than residual
(Remark~\ref{rem:boredom}).
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{A calibrated supply is separated by the trajectory measure and not
by any local one.} Fails if a calibrated supply and a resolving source are not separated by the
cost-weighted net-to-gross ratio while every local signal --- residual, progress, interest --- reads
alike (Remark~\ref{rem:comm-calibrated}). Measured on one synthetic fixture, one run per arm, with
the renewal schedule supplied rather than searched for, so the commitment is that the separation
holds beyond those conditions.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{A model of another is generative, not only discriminative.} Fails
if readers who reliably discriminate an author's work from imitations are at chance on forward
prediction of that author on unseen prompts scored against the author's own answers, which would
make the model discriminative and the composition idle
(Proposition~\ref{prop:other-model}). Untested; the design is the one
\citet{schwitzgebel2024} ran, with the tell removed.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Death is loss of re-runnability in the same coherent self.} Fails if a
pause that preserves re-runnability as the same coherent self (sleep, a recoverable
coma) counts as death, or a stop that loses it counts as survival --- the criterion is
re-runnability in the same coherent entity, not the mere stopping. Also fails if a
self's writes to the supergraph vanish when its run stops (work-done-once violated), or
if frames it copied into other frames cease when it does. The claim predicts
consequences and copies persist, that copies are not the self resuming, and that
nothing re-runs as the same coherent self.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Self-models are incomplete.} Fails if a frame can hold a complete
model of itself --- if any one of the three sources of incompleteness (finite aperture, proper part,
lesser object) can be evaded from inside.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Incompleteness is calibratable, and inside can beat outside.} Fails
if a forced aperture is necessarily invisible from the inside, so that no frame can represent the
fact or shape of its own limit. The claim predicts that residual is the trace from which a boundary
is inferred, and that calibration is an acquirable capability rather than a fixed property --- so it
fails too if outside access always beats inside investment, making the common case a structural
necessity rather than the ordinary one.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{A realisation clears held information rather than receiving new.}
Fails if a frame that has undergone a self-model realisation cannot identify prior occasions it did
not read at the time, or if a frame that genuinely received new evidence can
(Remark~\ref{rem:realisation}). Untested.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{The lay/expert gap tracks residual, not education.} Fails if the gap
between popular and expert understanding of a field tracks education or communication effort rather
than daily exposure to residual --- if explaining closes a gap where nothing anyone meets in a day
fails against the lay frame (Remark~\ref{rem:public-aperture}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{Identity is internal coherence; death is relative to the
coherence-boundary.} Fails if the self is the substrate parts rather than the internal
coherence (gradual replacement must survive; a full coherence-snapshot restored must be
the same internal self forked to a second node, diverging; a partial record re-run must
be a new self; a teleporter must relocate the same internal self). And fails if there is
a single absolute self whose end is the one death independent of any coherence-choice, or
if no coherence-boundary's death is real --- the claim predicts many real deaths at many
boundaries and levels (node versus self), none absolute.
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No frame is privileged.} \emph{(Graphic Equalisation's
Axiom~\ref{GE-ax:no-view}; \emph{Narrativium}'s Proposition~\ref{NV-prop:no-sum} carries the same
risk.)} Fails if any dissolution here secretly requires a
human frame to be privileged --- a special observer, a special time, a special freedom, a
persisting soul, a window on the real --- rather than following from the frame-relative
machinery applied uniformly to every frame. The claim is that removing the privilege, not
any added mechanism, is what dissolves each problem.
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Transparency varies with formation.} Fails if a frame high on the
axes named --- producing a meta-model, parsing its own residual, representing a boundary as a
boundary --- is nevertheless transparent to itself, which would make transparency a property of
self-models as such rather than of a configuration (Remark~\ref{rem:transparency}).
\item \textsc{[detail]}~\textbf{Cognition converges with distributed engineering only where the two
constraints bind.} Fails if arrangements shared by brains and distributed systems are found outside
the region conduction delay and scarce bandwidth touch, or if arrangements inside it diverge with no
third constraint to account for the divergence (Remark~\ref{rem:cognition-compression}).
\item \textsc{[shape]}~\textbf{No frame handles its own stop.} Fails if a frame's own rules are shown
to dispose of the residual of that frame's termination --- a handler running in a frame that has
stopped (Remark~\ref{rem:death-decoherence}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Items 6 and 20 are falsified by the same object --- an absolute frame --- so they are kept
separate as two entries and failing them is one result rather than two.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Scope note (not a falsifier).} The paper claims shapes only; it would be
over-claiming if any section were read as a decision procedure, an operational test of when
re-runnability or coherence has been lost, a threshold for how gradual a change stays
the same self, or a theory of consciousness or qualia --- none is offered.
