# Writing standard — communication on the substrate

**Status: binding for prose about this work.** Apply it to formal papers, book
chapters, outreach, proposals, abstracts, captions, and editorial comments.
It governs the prose around a result; it does not erase the distinctions among
formal, book, and public registers. `../book/BOOK_VOICE.md` remains the
chapter-level contract and supplies its own non-negotiables.

## The governing idea

Communication is a relation between a reader, a claim, and a path through the
claim. In the project's terms, prose is an arrangement with an admissibility
grammar: each sentence changes what the reader can hold; a broken transition
leaves residual. The work therefore has two writing obligations:

1. **Make the claim and its limit legible.** State what is being claimed, what
   earns it, and what would change it. Do not replace a commitment with a mood
   of caution.
2. **Make continuation cheaper than reconstruction.** A reader should normally
   have more reason to take the next step than to abandon the path and rebuild
   the argument alone. That is narrative momentum in prose: not a trick, and
   not withholding the answer, but a sequence in which each step creates a
   question the next step is positioned to settle.

Coherence is not a cosmetic property. It is the main constraint on this body of
work, because the arrangement spans scales, disciplines, and registers. Build
it deliberately at every level. Narrative is how the reader carries the
arrangement without having to hold the whole thing at once.

## The core posture: committed hard, held soft

Write the claim in its strong, testable form. Then hold it softly by naming its
scope, conditions, counterweights, and falsifier exactly. A hard line is a
probe: its break location is information. A hedge that only makes a sentence
harder to disconfirm is lost information.

- Say **what follows**, **what is contingent**, and **what would fail**.
- Do not write `may`, `might`, `perhaps`, `arguably`, `it seems`, or similar
  fog in place of a boundary.
- When a result is conditional, write the condition: “given X, Y follows,” not
  “Y may follow.”
- When a tendency has counterweights, name them. Do not convert a real
  asymmetry into a fixed outcome.
- When an earlier move was too broad, state the loss, restore what carried the
  work, and continue. An overshoot is a measurement when its loss is read; it
  is not an occasion for apology.

**Do not apologise. Ever.** The work is neutral. Replace apology language with
an account of the object: what changed, what the former version lost, what is
now supported, and what remains open. “The earlier formulation excluded X” is
useful. “Sorry, this was unclear” is not.

## Coherence is scaffolded and pathed

A coherent document has visible architecture before it has elegant sentences.
Every scale carries a path.

### Whole document

- Open with the reader’s payoff: the problem, the claim, and why it matters
  together. Do not make the reader earn the subject of the document.
- Give the reader the familiar result or question first; give the machinery
  that makes it necessary second. This is not simplification. It is dependency
  order from the reader’s side.
- Establish a small set of load-bearing terms, re-use them consistently, and
  keep the notation budget low. A term that does not carry later work does not
  belong in the opening.
- Arrange sections so that each answers a question raised by the preceding
  section. The section boundary must say what is now held and what is needed
  next.
- Make the claim’s evidential path inspectable: premise → mechanism → result →
  boundary or falsifier. Do not replace that path with a pile of citations,
  examples, or assertions.
- End at the live boundary: what would falsify, revise, or limit the result.
  A conclusion that merely repeats the claim is a dead end.

### Section and paragraph

- Give every section one job and say that job early.
- Start a paragraph from what the reader already holds; end by changing what
  they hold. The final sentence should create the exact need served by the next
  paragraph, section, figure, or proof.
- Keep one motion per paragraph: a claim, a contrast, a mechanism, an example,
  or a consequence. Split a paragraph when its transition cannot be named.
- Introduce a term before relying on it. Re-introduce it in ordinary language
  when the reader has travelled far enough that the connection would otherwise
  be lost.
- Put a number, formal result, table, or citation where it resolves a question
  the prose already created. Inventory without a question is not evidence of
  coherence.

### Sentence and clause

- Keep the agent, action, and consequence visible. Prefer active, ordinary
  syntax when it does not lose precision.
- Prefer shorter causal paths. If a sentence has several logical steps, make
  the dependency explicit or split it.
- Prefer casual language where it does not decrease coherence. “What this
  buys,” “the catch,” and “here is the problem” often carry the relation more
  cleanly than ceremonial prose.
- Preserve formal language where it is the compact name of a distinction. Do
  not replace a needed term with friendliness; translate it once, then use it.

## Narrative is a service to the reader

The work should invoke narrative in the reader. All else equal, stopping should
cost more than continuing because continuing promises a nearby resolution, not
because the prose hides information.

1. **Give people what they want first.** Start with the familiar problem,
   concrete consequence, result, failure, or scene that made the question
   worth asking.
2. **Then give them what they need.** Bring in the definitions, mechanism,
   prior art, formalism, and caveats at the point where the reader now needs
   them to settle the question.
3. **Withhold mechanism, never the claim.** State the result plainly. Let the
   reader want to know how it was earned; do not make them guess what is being
   asserted.
4. **Keep every promise short-range.** A question raised in a paragraph should
   receive a local answer or an explicit signpost. Long suspense without a
   receipt is debt, not momentum.
5. **Use failure, residual, and retraction as motion.** A result that broke an
   expectation tells the reader why the next distinction exists. Do not hide
   nulls or corrections in appendices or footnotes.

Narrative is not marketing. It cannot inflate evidence, manufacture urgency, or
turn uncertainty into drama. It earns continuation by making the shape of the
problem clearer at each step.

## Attribution, references, and novelty

This work is a radically simple arrangement of parts science has already made.
That is a neutral fact, not a defensive posture. Attribute generously and size
the contribution honestly.

- Lead with what is established, borrowed, or shared. The claim is the
  arrangement and the work it does, not ownership of its components.
- Prefer “recover” to “derive” where a known result is reproduced within the
  framework given contingent rule-content. Do not turn an admitted selection
  into a necessity.
- Do not explain a reference merely because it is a reference. Explain it only
  when its origin, analogy, or prior result carries a structural step in the
  present path.
- Names such as *Seldon manifolds* and *Narrativium* need no parenthetical
  origin story when they are simply the local names of already-grounded terms.
  Explain their lineage where that lineage performs conceptual work; otherwise
  let the term do its job.
- Cite the source that supplies the mechanism, result, or terminology actually
  used. Do not use a citation as decoration or as a substitute for saying what
  follows.
- Never argue for the author’s standing. State the relevant record once where
  it matters, then let the evidence carry the claim.

## Register discipline

The same path can be written at different resolutions. Do not mix them by
accident.

- **Formal papers:** statement first, explicit assumptions, definitions,
  mechanism, proof or evidence, scope, falsifier. The prose must still path the
  reader; formality does not licence theorem-first fog or passive indirection.
- **Book chapters:** use the `BOOK_VOICE.md` inversion: scene and plain claim
  together, then the earned mechanism. Formal content is relocated to named
  statements or the citable paper, never silently discarded.
- **Outreach and talks:** give the concrete result and stakes first, then the
  smallest amount of theory needed to make it true. Do not substitute metaphor
  for the mechanism.
- **Editorial, review, and project communication:** name the finding, affected
  claim, evidence, and next operation. Critique the object rather than
  apologising for it or narrating personal emotion around it.

## Revision gates

Before releasing prose, run these checks.

1. **Path check:** Can a new reader state the opening claim, the question each
   section answers, and the final boundary without reconstructing missing
   links?
2. **Continuation check:** Does every major question receive a nearby answer,
   signpost, or visibly earned next step? Remove suspense that has no payload.
3. **Coherence check:** For every paragraph transition, name the relation:
   consequence, contrast, mechanism, example, scope, or falsifier. Add the
   relation or split the paragraph when it cannot be named.
4. **Commitment check:** Replace foggy hedges with a condition, a counterweight,
   a range, or a falsifier. Check both ends of every claimed gradient; a
   calibrated limit is not an absent limit.
5. **Contribution check:** Mark what is inherited, what is arranged, and what
   this document actually establishes. Remove novelty theatre.
6. **Reference check:** Delete origin explanations and citations that do not
   carry coherence; retain and explain the ones that do.
7. **Tone check:** Delete apologies and ceremonial padding. Keep the most casual
   wording that preserves the exact relation.

The test is simple: a reader should leave with a claim they can state, a path
they can retrace, and a boundary they can use to disagree with it. That is
communication adequate to the theory.
