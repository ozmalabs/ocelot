# 10a — Cost-Floor Compression, the AI Wedge, and Civilisational Transition: A Conditional GE Assessment — research notes

**Stage:** 1 research record only. This record supports an unreleased formal application paper. It does not establish a universal GE result, a realised graph substrate, an empirical collapse forecast, a geological epoch, a political prescription, or a claim that any particular civilisation is already in transition.

**Question.** For a named outcome and population, when does the audited cost of delivering that outcome move toward a measured feasible lower envelope; how do AI and other technologies alter that pressure; and what evidence distinguishes successful adaptation from transferred costs, exclusion, backlog, terminal discontinuity, or successor-regime change?

**Short result.** The evidence supports rapidly falling cost at selected performance levels in AI, major long-run productivity and input substitution in U.S. agriculture, and digitally enabled reduction of selected logistics/trade frictions. It does not support a universal law that all costs approach zero, that lower API price equals lower social cost, that automation necessarily reduces jobs or labour income, or that a civilisational transition is inevitable. The defensible result is a measurement contract: a fall in the audited, outcome-normalised cost of a task can place pressure on work-, ownership-, price-, and legitimacy-mediated arrangements, but the direction of adaptation depends on distribution, physical burden, governance, reliability, and human/institutional capacity.

**Epistemic rule.** “Cost approaches the floor” is not a numerical claim until the task, outcome, performance threshold, payer, spatial and temporal boundary, allocation rule, and externalities are stated. A list price, productivity statistic, platform narrative, or GE term does not supply these conditions by itself. Sources below are diagnostic evidence about parts of the question; none asks or settles the proposed GE/application question.

**Two lenses.** The first lens is analytical. For a named regime $R_0$, its current manifold $\mathcal M(R_0)$ is the declared state space of reproduction arrangements, ledger variables, and measured constraints; “manifold” is classificatory language here, not a physical-manifold claim. Given the GE transition premise, this lens maps terminal discontinuity and viable non-isomorphic successor regimes without ordering them.

The second lens is interpretive. The continuance lens recorded for this paper orders civilisational continuance above terminal discontinuity. Conditional on continuance, it orders trajectories by reduced measured human suffering $S^H$ and continued human participation $P^H$ in the system. Under that lens, continuance is the best outcome, and lower $S^H$ with continuing $P^H$ is the ideal direction. This ordering is a declared value input: it does not follow from GE, cost compression, or the analytical partition. It requires its own definitions of civilisation, humanity, suffering, participation, population, horizon, and measurement threshold; another lens leaves the analytical classification unchanged.

## 1. Claim discipline

| Item | Permitted use | Not established or licensed |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-floor pressure | Repeated, boundary-harmonised evidence that the audited cost of a declared successful outcome approaches its observed feasible lower envelope. | Zero cost, a universal physical floor, a claim that every sector has the same floor, or a conclusion that all residual scarcities disappear. |
| AI/LLMs | A dated change in model-specific performance, tariff, throughput, or deployment configuration. | Agency, consciousness, AGI, autonomous replacement, one stable capability curve, or total task cost. |
| Agriculture/logistics | Historical evidence that productivity, inputs, and selected transaction frictions can change substantially. | Costless food/transport, a global causal estimate of AI, or a forecast of unlimited physical output. |
| Economic-system pressure | A hypothesis that access, income, ownership, demand, and legitimacy may require remeasurement when task costs and task composition change. | The certain destruction of markets, wage labour, property, or any named economy. |
| GE transition fork | A conditional classification of a declared current regime after a separately defended non-persistence premise. | A proof that the premise holds, a dated collapse, a moral ranking of successors, or a mandated policy. |
| GE graph substrate | A separately tested possible technical accelerator. | An existing implementation, a demonstrated efficiency gain, or evidence for the fork. |

### 1.1 Levels that must not be collapsed

1. **Task:** model/inference, tool use, workflow, reliability, retry, and audit costs.
2. **Organisation:** procurement, integration, staffing, training, authorisation, repair, and accountability.
3. **Population:** employment, income security, access, care, status, bargaining power, and agency.
4. **Market/institution:** prices, ownership, competition, taxation/transfers, demand, public services, law, and legitimacy.
5. **Physical system:** energy, water, land, materials, grid capacity, emissions, waste, and local burden.
6. **Regime/civilisation:** a named set of arrangements that reproduces the preceding levels over time.

A decrease at one level does not assign a decrease at another. A cheaper model can reduce an organisation’s information cost while increasing verification work, data-centre load, platform dependence, or exclusion. A task’s price can fall while household access or a farm’s input exposure worsens.

**Existence is not goodness.** A regime can continue through extraction, coercion, exclusion, and externalisation without fair governance, legitimacy, or equitable access. Those conditions are not prerequisites for a system to exist, nor antecedents of the terminal/successor classification. They are measured only when they belong to the declared outcome or viability vector. A successor regime can therefore be viable in the narrow declared sense while being harmful, unequal, or unacceptable; this record does not endorse it.

## 2. Evidence record

### 2.1 AI is a visible cost wedge, not the whole process

Stanford HAI’s *AI Index Report 2025* reports that inference cost at a performance level comparable to GPT-3.5 declined by more than **280-fold** between November 2022 and October 2024 [S1]. It also reports one-year gains on particular difficult benchmarks, including MMMU (+18.8 percentage points), GPQA (+48.9), and SWE-bench (+67.3). This supports a rapidly moving frontier for particular benchmark/performance configurations. It does **not** provide total application cost, an all-workflow reliability result, a causal productivity estimate, or a social-outcome forecast.

A named commercial tariff is only one input to the task cost. Current provider pricing can differ by input/output, cache use, throughput tier, batch delay, region, tool calls, and context length. An announced DeepSeek V4.1-Flash pricing/deployment change illustrates why cache, active-parameter assumptions, runtime, and tariff time must be recorded; by the document’s display date, it is not evidence of an independently audited realised social saving [S2]. It cannot be used to infer a universal LLM rate.

### 2.2 Agriculture: transformation under residual constraints

USDA ERS reports that U.S. farm output nearly tripled from 1948–2023 (+1.52% annually) while aggregate inputs grew only +0.12% annually; total-factor productivity grew +1.40% annually [S3]. Across the period, labour input declined 76% and land input 28%, while intermediate inputs rose 109%. Agriculture is the clearest historical example of a primary activity whose labour requirement and input composition have changed: historical series report that the majority of the labour force in France, the United Kingdom, Italy, and the Netherlands worked in farming after the agricultural revolution [S13]. That is country evidence, not a reconstructed share for every human population or a single global historical estimate. Together, the records show that a physical production system can undergo large labour, productivity, and input-composition change. They do not supply a reconstructed all-in economic-cost series or show zero-cost food: intermediate inputs include energy, chemicals, services, and other embodied burdens; neither source measures global food access nor identifies automation/AI as the cause.

### 2.3 Logistics and commerce: transaction-friction changes are partial

OECD analysis finds that digital connectivity and trade-facilitation measures, including automated border processes, are likely to have greater trade-enhancing effects for parcel trade than traditional trade [S4]. Its 2025 supply-chain work treats AI/digital tools as possible efficiency and resilience inputs while retaining data, verification, and environmental conditions [S5]. These sources support an account of reduced selected coordination and transaction frictions. They do not license an Amazon-specific generalisation, costless logistics, or a claim that delivery labour, transport energy, warehousing, regulation, and material infrastructure disappear.

### 2.4 Distribution, demand, ownership, and legitimacy

The ILO estimates that about one quarter of global employment is potentially exposed to generative AI, while judging job transformation more likely than replacement in most cases [S6]. Exposure is not realised displacement or a wage forecast. Separately, the ILO reports a 1.6 percentage-point decline in global labour-income share from 2004–2024 and lists capital-augmenting technology/automation, globalisation, and weaker bargaining power among structural influences [S7]. This does not isolate AI as a cause; it does justify measuring the distribution of gains rather than treating aggregate productivity as population access.

The UK CMA identifies risks to fair, open, and effective competition in foundation-model markets [S8]. The IEA reports that local acceptance of data centres can depend on local benefits and fair allocation of affordability/environmental costs [S9]. These are constraints on the transition pathway, not evidence that concentration or rejection is inevitable.

### 2.5 Physical floor, rebound, and externality boundary

IEA’s *Energy and AI* gives examples in which AI may improve operations while also documenting rebound risks: lower autonomous-vehicle cost can shift travel demand and increase use [S10]. It estimates that certain AI-supported fault detection can reduce outage durations 30–50% and that grid-management tools could unlock capacity, but these are conditional use cases. IEA’s 2026 update states that data-centre electricity demand roughly doubles from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030 in its central outlook, while per-task energy efficiency can decline in aggregate consequence when use expands and workloads become more intensive [S9]. Therefore no token or labour saving establishes a lower total physical cost.

### 2.6 Anthropocene transition/end as an analytical umbrella

“Anthropocene transition/end” is used here as an analytical umbrella for possible nonlinear reorganisation of coupled civilisational–Earth-system conditions. “Singularity,” “Lyapunov-horizon collapse,” and “phase change/transition” are rough descriptions of the question, not demonstrations of a physical singularity, a settled mechanism, a formal ratified epoch, a known endpoint, or the end of human influence/geology. The label must name observables, scale, and horizon. The status of an Anthropocene Epoch remains distinct: the 2024 ICS decision rejected formalisation of the proposed epoch [S11].

## 3. Measurement contract

For unit $u$, outcome $o$, configuration $q$, and interval $i$, record:

| Symbol | Meaning | Required observations | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| $C^{I}$ | Information/inference cost | Model/version, context/cache, tools, latency, tariff, hardware where relevant. | One API price as social cost. |
| $C^{Q}$ | Coordination cost | Handoffs, training, procurement, delay, conflict resolution, interoperability. | Treating a model output as coordinated action. |
| $C^{B}$ | Embodied cost | Energy, water, hardware/materials, logistics, facility/infrastructure inputs. | Treating information as immaterial. |
| $C^{V}$ | Verification/repair cost | Error rate, retries, appeal, audit, incident response, maintenance. | Benchmark score as field reliability. |
| $C^{G}$ | Governance/authorisation cost | Legal review, consent, accountability, due process, legitimacy, oversight. | Calling these costs unnecessary by definition. |
| $C^{E}$ | Allocated externality cost | Payer, location, time, environmental/social burden, allocation and uncertainty. | Omitting displaced burden. |
| $F_{u,o}$ | Feasible lower envelope | Repeated successful configurations on a common outcome/access/reliability scale. | A universal or zero floor. |
| $A$ | Delivered adaptation | Audited outcome delivery, access, repair, and distribution. | Announced investment or purchased tooling. |
| $H$ | Residual harm/induced demand | Rework, failures, exclusion, rebound, concentration, externalities. | Assuming a cost saving has no countereffect. |
| $B$ | Outcome backlog | Stable outcome-scaled unmet requirement after all terms are recorded. | News volume or a rhetoric index. |

The core accounting convention is

\[
C^{\mathrm{aud}}=C^I+C^Q+C^B+C^V+C^G+C^E,
\qquad
B_{i+1}=\max\{0,B_i+\Delta D_i-A_i+H_i\}.
\]

A cost-compression observation is usable only if the recorded configuration delivers the same declared outcome and if the boundary prevents counting a transferred burden as a saving. The variables may be partially observed; in that case the result is a request for measurement, not numerical proof.

**MDL–cost-floor grounding.** Here an outcome $o$ is a declared shape or shape trajectory, not an invariant. A grammar $\gamma$ is a declared repertoire of generative relations/operations, making a shape space $\Sigma_\gamma$ and a grammar-relative lower envelope $F^\gamma_{u,o}$. A new grammar $\gamma'$ can alter both. It records a floor step only through a declared shape comparison map $\Pi_{\gamma\to\gamma'}$ for which $F^{\gamma'}_{u,\Pi(o)} < F^\gamma_{u,o}$: the grammar changes what shape can be generated and at what audited cost.

Rissanen’s MDL criterion minimises a sufficient model description plus its residual data description, $L(M)+L(D\mid M)$ [S12]. Under the GE reading, this is the same compression pressure as cost-floor pressure at account and realised-transformation levels. MDL concerns an account and its grammar; $C^{\mathrm{aud}}$ concerns the realised information, coordination, embodied, verification, authorisation, and externality transformations of shapes. The two expressions are not numerically equal. The correspondence requires a declared descriptive-to-realised grammar map and remains frame-, boundary-, and substrate-relative. MDL is neither evidence that an audited cost falls nor evidence for a grammar step or transition premise; it supplies the structural GE grounding of the compression operation.

## 4. Conditional GE transition ledger

Let $R_0$ be a named regime with identity/reproduction arrangements $\mathcal I_0$ and a declared minimum viability vector $v$. The GE transition premise $G(R_0)$ says that, under measured cost-floor pressure, $R_0$ cannot continue indefinitely while retaining all of $\mathcal I_0$. This is a theory-facing premise to be argued independently; the sources in this record do not establish it.

If $G(R_0)$ holds and post-transition trajectories are classified exhaustively as either failing or meeting $v$, then: (a) failure to meet $v$ is terminal discontinuity; (b) continued viability without retaining the full identity of $R_0$ is a successor regime. This is a conditional partition, not an estimate of probability or date. The successor is not assumed good; its possibility space is analytically open because it is not required to retain every current arrangement.

A GE graph substrate may be considered only as a further scenario: if a realised implementation independently lowers $C^{\mathrm{aud}}$ at the same task outcome, reliability, access, and externality boundary, it shifts one cost condition. It does not prove $G(R_0)$, net adaptation, or a branch of the partition.

## 5. Falsifiers and evidence gaps

**Cost-floor pressure is falsified for a named task** if harmonised audited cost does not decline, a supposedly lower-cost configuration fails the outcome/reliability/access condition, a lower cost is merely displaced to an omitted payer or time, or a comparable alternative delivers the result at lower total cost.

**The economic-pressure hypothesis is falsified locally** if access, income security, agency, demand, competition, and legitimacy remain adequate on declared measures despite the stated task transformation. It is not falsified merely because a headline price falls.

**The terminal/successor partition is inapplicable** if $G(R_0)$ is not supplied, the viability vector is undefined, or the classification fails to cover relevant trajectories.

**Key gaps.** There is no single longitudinal dataset of audited all-in task cost across AI, agriculture, logistics, public services, and households; no source here identifies a universal floor; and no cited result decides whether a named civilisation is nearer terminal discontinuity or a successor regime.
