# Relational Information Ethics: review and related work

**Research dates:** 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-11. **Purpose:** Support the Paper 11 argument and identify its dependencies. This is a review record, not a completed derivation or an exhaustive priority search.

## 1. Contribution to establish

> Ethical cost follows from informational transformations and their consequences for affected frames, without an additional ethical postulate or observer assigning value. The cost exists in the affected relations. Measurement describes it; decisions apply relative weightings to it.

This is stronger than keeping accounting separate from decision weights. The theory proposes that ethical cost occurs before and independently of evaluative recognition. Unobserved loss, an unreported burden, and a consequence assigned zero importance can still have cost. The derivation must establish that no act of evaluation constitutes that cost.

Keep occurring cost, measured account, and decision valuation distinct. Frame-indexed measurement conditions identify which relation and quantity an account describes; they do not imply that an evaluator creates the consequence. Relational attribution does not require a separate observer to confer value, and this claim introduces neither a view from nowhere nor a universal scalar.

The contribution also includes the actual functions for calculating the account: it is implementable. A further interpretation is that humans and other animals express the underlying informational principle through compression into dispositions, affect, heuristics, and practices. Explicit reflection can describe and revise ethical behaviour whose operation does not require prior philosophical reasoning. Differences in compression and weightings can explain differing decisions without requiring identical implementations.

State three separate claims: a formal derivation, executable calculation functions, and a compression interpretation of ethical behaviour. The present argument does not recover a brain's precise representations or algorithms, and that recovery is not required to establish the contribution. Its value can be assessed through coherent accounting, calculability, explanatory scope, and useful comparisons. Computability alone is not the distinction from all related work: empowerment, side-effect penalties, and computational moral-choice models also have operational functions. The proposed contribution combines the specific informational derivation and attributed account with its explanatory interpretation.

“No additional requirement” excludes a new moral axiom, biological privilege, sentience gate, represented-preference requirement, or ability to complain as a condition for counting an instantiated consequence. The series' existing conditions for defining quantities still apply: a frame, distinguishable alternatives, an attribution boundary, and an instantiated comparison dimension. They must not conceal an evaluative population filter or a preferred ordering.

The contribution needs a derivation from the existing definitions. Defining a capability-loss measure and calling it ethical cost establishes a vocabulary, but does not yet establish that the full account follows from those foundations. The paper should give the dependency chain and address consequences that a simple option count misses. Relative weightings can remain decision inputs, as proposed; a unique universal decision ordering is not part of that claim.

## 3. Closest primary related work

The comparisons below are interpretations of the cited sources. Full relevant passages were checked for the central information-ethics, choice, empowerment, and entropy-force comparisons. The reachability paper and informational agency/individuality comparisons were checked against author/publisher abstracts and metadata; they warrant closer mathematical comparison during the formal draft.

### 3.1 Information ethics: the direct philosophical predecessor

**Luciano Floridi (1999), “Information ethics: On the philosophical foundation of computer ethics.”** Information ethics extends consideration beyond biological subjects through an informational conception of entities and their moral claims. This precedes both the information-ethics framing and opposition to biological privilege. Paper 11's proposed distinction is deriving cost without introducing a further moral-worth principle. [Publisher and DOI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1010018611096).

**Luciano Floridi and J. W. Sanders (1999), “Entropy as Evil in Information Ethics.”** This is an especially close formal comparison. Section “Entropy-based Information Ethics” supplies moral laws and an entropy structure reflecting a prior assessment of good and bad configurations. Its context-sensitive ordering should not be identified with Shannon entropy or universal thermodynamic disorder. Paper 11 should compare exactly where its cost dimensions come from and whether any comparable evaluative structure enters before decision weighting. [Author text](https://sites.units.it/etica/1999_2/floridi/index.html), [the decisive section](https://sites.units.it/etica/1999_2/floridi/node8.html).

### 3.2 Cybernetic choices: the direct options-and-ethics predecessor

**Heinz von Foerster, “Ethics and Second-Order Cybernetics.”** His choice-expansion imperative connects ethics to future options and includes the observer in the system. The text presents ethical commitment through decisions on questions it regards as undecidable; it does not derive a universal cost account from a channel model. Paper 11 shares the relational concern and option geometry while proposing to derive consequences before decision priorities. Cite the 2003 collected version consistently; the archived text describes the 1990 address. [Primary text](https://pangaro.com/hciiseminar2019/Heinz_von_Foerster-Ethics_and_Second-order_Cybernetics.pdf), [collected volume](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/b97451).

### 3.3 Empowerment: the direct mathematical predecessor

**Alexander S. Klyubin, Daniel Polani, and Chrystopher L. Nehaniv (2005), “Empowerment: A Universal Agent-Centric Measure of Control.”** Empowerment uses action-to-sensor channel capacity to quantify potential control without a task-specific reward. It supplies a rigorous information-theoretic comparison for reachable futures. Its quantity depends on sensors, actuators, channel, and horizon. Maximising an agent's own empowerment is a motivation proposal; calculating empowerment is not itself a rule assigning priorities among affected agents. [Institutional record](https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/empowerment-a-universal-agent-centric-measure-of-control/), [primary paper](https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/282/1/901241.pdf).

**Christoph Salge and Daniel Polani (2017), “Empowerment As Replacement for the Three Laws of Robotics.”** This explicitly brings information theory into robotic ethics using human, robot, and transfer empowerment. Sections 4.3–4.4 discuss weighting and human priority; the authors do not present the proposal as sufficient for complete ethical behaviour. Paper 11 must acknowledge this close overlap. Its proposed difference is a substrate-independent account without an imposed human-serving mission, with weights applied after attributed costs are derived. [Primary article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2017.00025/full).

### 3.4 Reachability and attainable utility: direct accounting predecessors

**Victoria Krakovna, Laurent Orseau, Ramana Kumar, Miljan Martic, and Shane Legg (2018; revised 2019), “Penalizing side effects using stepwise relative reachability.”** Separates baseline choice from deviation measure and studies undesirable incentives from both. This is a direct precursor for deriving side effects through lost reachability. Its proposed penalty guides a reinforcement-learning objective. Paper 11 should test whether its own baseline and loss construction reproduces interference or overcompensation incentives when used for decisions. [Author-deposited paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01186).

**Alexander Matt Turner, Dylan Hadfield-Menell, and Prasad Tadepalli (2020), “Conservative Agency via Attainable Utility Preservation.”** Penalises changes in the capacity to optimise auxiliary rewards alongside a primary reward. This already separates impacts on future capabilities from a specific main task. It remains a designed objective and penalty scheme. Paper 11 should compare its full attributed account, including burdens to other frames, with the attainable-utility proxy. Already cited in Paper 02. [Primary paper and publication record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09725).

### 3.5 Informational significance and individuality: foundations without biological gates

**Artemy Kolchinsky and David H. Wolpert (2018), “Semantic information, autonomous agency, and nonequilibrium statistical physics.”** Defines semantic information through correlations causally necessary to maintaining existence, using counterfactual interventions and a low-entropy viability criterion. Applies to living and nonliving physical systems. This supports deriving significance through dynamics rather than externally supplied semantic labels; its viability criterion is not a complete account of costs across affected frames. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08053).

**David Krakauer, Nils Bertschinger, Eckehard Olbrich, Jessica C. Flack, and Nihat Ay (2020), “The information theory of individuality.”** Develops information-based ways of identifying individuals across organisational scales. Relevant to attribution and continuity without biological membership. Choosing a partition and resolving overlapping individuals still require explicit treatment; individuality is not itself a moral ranking. [Publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12064-020-00313-7).

### 3.6 Further mathematical and evaluative comparisons

**Claude E. Shannon (1948), “A Mathematical Theory of Communication.”** Supplies uncertainty and channel-capacity machinery. Its communication measure does not by itself determine the relevant affected population, causal counterfactual, or interpersonal priorities. State which additional structure is already inherited from GE. [Primary paper](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/yry/readings/general/shannon1948.pdf).

**W. Ross Ashby (1956), *An Introduction to Cybernetics*.** Variety and requisite variety connect available distinctions to regulation. Useful for distinguishing a capability measure from a rule to increase every option. [Author archive text](https://ashby.info/Ashby-Introduction-to-Cybernetics.pdf).

**A. D. Wissner-Gross and C. E. Freer (2013), “Causal Entropic Forces.”** Proposes a force favouring future path entropy and demonstrates adaptive behaviours, including cooperation, in simple systems. The proposed dynamical bias and path distribution are specific premises; adaptive behaviour is not an ethical derivation. Compare paths versus endpoints and stochastic entropy versus controllable reachability. [Author copy](https://alexwg.org/publications/PhysRevLett_110-168702.pdf).

**Amartya Sen (1980), “Equality of What?”** The capability approach makes available ways of living central to evaluation instead of treating goods or utility alone as sufficient. It supplies an important conceptual comparison for actual versus nominal options. Its evaluative focus on human capabilities differs from the proposed derivation across informational frames. [Author publication record](https://sen.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/equality-what), [primary lecture](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/sen80.pdf).

### 3.7 Human and animal behaviour: context for the compression interpretation

**Jonathan Haidt (2001), “The emotional dog and its rational tail: A social intuitionist approach to moral judgment.”** Proposes that moral judgments commonly arise through intuitions, with reasoning often serving social explanation and justification. Supports treating explicit reasoning as an operation that need not originate every judgment. This is an explanatory model, not evidence for the present informational functions specifically or a claim that reasoning never changes judgment. [Primary publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11699120/), [author manuscript](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jhaidt/articles/alternate_versions/haidt.2001.emotionaldog.pub022-manuscript.pdf).

**Felix Warneken and Michael Tomasello (2006), “Altruistic helping in human infants and young chimpanzees.”** Reports instrumental helping in very young children and chimpanzees, with species and task differences. Motivates behaviour that does not require articulated ethical theory and provides tasks involving another individual's blocked or attainable goal. Helping establishes a behavioural observation; it does not establish an absence of cognition, a particular moral motive, or the proposed calculation mechanism. [Primary publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16513986/).

**Julen Hernandez-Lallement and colleagues (2020), “Harm to Others Acts as a Negative Reinforcer in Rats.”** Rats reduced selection of a food lever when it also shocked another rat. Responses varied between individuals, with prior experience, and with reward trade-offs; anterior cingulate deactivation reduced harm avoidance. This is a particularly useful comparison for consequences and different weights operating without articulated ethics. It provides behavioural and causal evidence, but does not show that rats calculate a general reachable-futures account. [Author institution and primary abstract](https://pure.knaw.nl/portal/nl/publications/harm-to-others-acts-as-a-negative-reinforcer-in-rats/).

**Molly J. Crockett and colleagues (2014), “Harm to others outweighs harm to self in moral decision making.”** Human choices trade money against painful shocks to self or others. Computational models estimate harm aversion, providing a concrete precedent for common outcome dimensions with different decision parameters. The proposed informational derivation should connect to such measurable trade-offs rather than treating all disagreement as qualitative or unmeasurable. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4260587/), [publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25404350/).

**Jeroen M. van Baar, Luke J. Chang, and Alan G. Sanfey (2019), “The computational and neural substrates of moral strategies in social decision-making.”** Gives an integrative computational model of reciprocity decisions and compares its strategies with neural data. A direct predecessor for an implementable descriptive model; its authors also provide code. Paper 11 needs to explain what its informational derivation adds to existing fitted social-preference models. [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09161-6), [authors' code](https://github.com/jeroenvanbaar/MoralStrategiesFMRI).

**Jim McGetrick and colleagues (2023), “Perceived reward attainability may underlie dogs' responses in inequity paradigms.”** An important control case: responses attributed to inequity aversion can also depend on perceived reward availability, and responses did not distinguish a dog partner from a box in the relevant comparison. The proposed mechanism needs controls separating cost to another frame from personal opportunity and cue-based expectation. This limits what a behaviour alone establishes; it does not settle whether animals can behave ethically. [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-38836-w).

These behavioural comparisons were checked against primary abstracts, publication metadata, and accessible article material. They motivate and constrain possible compressed expressions of the principle; they do not identify a biological implementation. The formal theory's value does not depend on recovering that implementation. Where a particular compression proposal makes quantitative predictions, new choices under independently constrained parameters can assess that extension. Behavioural fit and the validity of the underlying derivation are separate questions.

### 3.8 Graded moral consideration and perceived informational organisation

**Heather M. Gray, Kurt Gray, and Daniel M. Wegner (2007), “Dimensions of Mind Perception.”** Perceived experience and agency both related to valuing entities, but avoiding harm related more strongly to experience, while punishment judgments related more strongly to agency. This is direct evidence for graded, multidimensional consideration. It supports investigating attributed informational organisation; it also challenges treating intelligence or action repertoire as a sufficient single measure. Perceived capacities and actual organisation must be measured separately. [Primary author copy](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/DANWEGNER/pub/Gray%2C%20Gray%2C%20%26%20Wegner%20%282007%29.pdf).

**Aurélien Miralles, Michel Raymond, and Guillaume Lecointre (2019), “Empathy and compassion toward other species decrease with evolutionary divergence time.”** Reports a relationship between human responses to other species and evolutionary distance. Relevant to the proposed trend, but phylogenetic proximity is a competing explanation or proxy, not a measure of informational complexity by definition. Checked against the publisher's indexed record; a full methods comparison remains for drafting. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-56006-9).

**Hoang Nguyen / YouGov (2024), “Market insights into people's food choices in the US and Great Britain.”** Reports self-identified vegetarian and vegan diets at 6% and 2% in Great Britain, and 3% and 2% in the US. This supports vegetarian prevalence exceeding vegan prevalence in those surveyed populations, with different gaps. Welfare is one reported motivation alongside environmental and health considerations. The prevalence ordering does not isolate an informational mechanism or establish a worldwide ratio. [Survey organisation's report](https://yougov.com/articles/48355-us-uk-dietary-trends).

The hypothesis to test is that attributed informational organisation and reachable capacities predict treatment, after accounting for familiarity, resemblance, relationship, and competing consequences. Distinguish a larger derived loss from a greater decision weight. Vegetarian-versus-vegan choices additionally compare different actions affecting the same animals; use them to examine killing, ongoing burden, and indirect effects rather than as a simple ranking of entity complexity.

### 3.9 Violent video games: historical concern and the cost of the depicted act

**US Senate Committee on Governmental Affairs (1995), activities report for the 103rd Congress.** Records hearings on 9 December 1993, 4 March 1994, and 29 July 1994 concerning violent games and ratings. The first included testimony about links between depicted violence and actual youth violence. This documents the historical controversy; it is not scientific evidence establishing that link. [Primary congressional report, S. Rept. 104-27](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-104srpt27/html/CRPT-104srpt27.htm).

**Tilo Hartmann and Peter Vorderer (2010), “It's Okay to Shoot a Character: Moral Disengagement in Violent Video Games.”** Two experiments examined guilt, negative affect, and enjoyment. Familiarity and some contextual framing affected responses; several other manipulations, including nonhuman appearance and distorted consequences, did not produce the predicted effects. Directly relevant to informational meaning and compressed category treatment, with results that prevent an unrestricted claim that any added context must change moral response. The existing moral-disengagement explanation and the proposed informational interpretation require comparison, not automatic identification. [Author institution and primary abstract](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/its-okay-to-shoot-a-character-moral-disengagement-in-violent-vide/).

These comparisons were checked against the congressional report and primary abstracts/metadata. The application concerns the ethical cost of the depicted act. An expendable encounter NPC can have no persistent relationships, individual development, or continuing futures beyond its scripted role. Where those continuations are absent in the baseline, the depicted death cannot remove them. Contrast the actual loss with the removal of a persistent companion and its connected futures. Temporary capability, resource, and player consequences remain part of the account where instantiated.

This is a difference in consequential structure, not merely a difference in assigned concern. A violent image does not establish that the informational losses associated with terminating a continuing agent have occurred. Equally, a player's indifference cannot make existing losses disappear. Aggression and behavioural-transfer studies address another question and have been removed from this application and its source list.

## 4. Proposed derivation and checks against the predecessors

1. **Inherit informational structure:** relational existence → situated frames and provision → admissible transitions and their grounded costs → aligned reachable continuations. Show that the resulting consequential relations exist without evaluative observation or assigned moral value.
2. **Derive attribution:** identify where the transformation changes an instantiated dimension. Do not require goals, consciousness, or a reporting channel before accounting for that change.
3. **Derive the account:** represent the occurring costs through lost and gained distinctions, resource differences, path consequences, and repair liabilities. Use the existing grounding for each measure and avoid duplicate charging across levels of provision. An absent or inadequate account does not imply an absent consequence.
4. **Establish the information-theoretic content:** compare reachable-distinction measures with channel capacity and path information. Prove any identification under its actual conditions.
5. **Apply decision weightings:** evaluations can vary while the fixed causal account remains unchanged. Weights do not erase incurred consequences.
6. **Specify executable functions:** provide complete grounding inputs, operations, numeric outputs, and handling of missing information. A formula depending on an unspecified reachability or measurement oracle is not a complete implementation.
7. **Develop the compression interpretation:** distinguish actual consequences from available information, compressed dispositions, and weights. Specify what a proposed compression preserves or loses. Assess explanatory usefulness without requiring recovery of a neural algorithm; constrain parameters independently where an extension makes quantitative predictions.

### A concrete mathematical comparison

For a finite deterministic action-to-observation channel `Y=f(U)` with `m ≥ 1` reachable distinguishable outputs:

```text
E = max_p(U) I(U; Y) = max_p(U) H(Y) = log2(m).
```

The first equality is the empowerment construction. Determinism gives `H(Y | U)=0`; an entropy upper bound gives `H(Y) ≤ log2(m)`; choosing one input for each reachable output with equal probability attains the bound. Thus an action that restricts a six-output channel to three outputs loses one bit of potential control under these conditions. It also removes three particular outputs. These are different descriptions with different units, and both require the declared channel and distinctions.

This comparison is not a proof that all cost is empowerment. Equal capacities can conceal replacement of valuable or causally distinctive outcomes; noise can reduce reliable control without changing the support set; temporary restriction can disappear at the endpoint; terminating the channel is not a valid `log2(0)` case. The general account must handle those cases through its inherited machinery.

### Required examples

- An affected frame incurs a burden while representing no preference over continuations.
- The payer cannot signal; recognition changes while the historical consequence remains fixed.
- No evaluator recognises or assigns value to the consequence; subsequently introducing a reporting layer changes available knowledge without creating the cost.
- A reversible deprivation episode restores local state but has a nonzero instantiated path/resource account.
- One agent gains control by reducing another's: both entries remain before weighting.
- Distinct outcomes replace each other with equal cardinality or channel capacity.
- A collective and its components share a provision bill: attribution avoids charging the same bill repeatedly.
- The human continuance lens from Paper 10a selects among accounts without defining which embodiments can bear costs.
- A specified game world exposes persistent character state, relationships, and reachable interactions. An action changes those relations whether or not the player notices or values the change; graphical appearance and game score are separate quantities. Save/reload and regeneration distinguish terminal restoration, historical consequences, and repair cost.
- A companion's death can matter greatly while a thousand enemy deaths matter little to a player. Develop this as an example of informational individuation, shared history, anticipated futures, and compression into interchangeable categories. Separate the entity's operative organisation, the player's representation, and decision weights. The example explains differing assigned significance without equating an ignored consequence with zero occurring cost.
- Establish the cost difference before considering player salience: an expendable NPC can lack the persistent organisation and continuing futures that a companion's removal actually destroys. Its purpose as a target is evidence about its structure only when the implemented continuations confirm it.

## Whole-system accounting and moral injury (revision 2)

Costs borne by an actor or witness must be included alongside effects on an external recipient. Awareness can causally alter an affected system without becoming an external value assignment.

## Operative valuation and self-model rejection (revision 3)
