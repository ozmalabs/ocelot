# Relational Information Accounting

## Calculable informational cost, reachable futures, and ethical behaviour

**Scope correction for revisions 2–3:** The foundational framework is whole-system accounting. Ethics becomes an accounting frame for consequences omitted by narrower accounts; those consequences already belong to the system under the relational ontology. The formal manuscript includes actors, witnesses, internal enabling relations, and moral injury. It also develops the distinction between an already operative numerical valuation and a self-model that rejects explicit acknowledgement, with conditional calculation functions and primary prior work. The original ethical framing below is retained as the development record and is superseded where it identifies ethics with accounting.

**Status:** Argument plan used for the completed first draft of [Paper 11](../formal/11_relational_information_ethics.tex). See the [plan review](11_relational_information_ethics_plan_review.md) for corrections made during drafting. This file retains the development record; the formal manuscript states the current argument.

**Central claim:** Ethical cost exists in the informational consequences of interaction: the burdens and capability losses an action imposes on affected structures. Its existence requires no observer to recognise it or assign it value. The paper derives functions for calculating an account of that cost. Decisions evaluate the account through relative weightings, priorities, and constraints, made explicit in the model. Those weightings need not be consciously articulated by the acting system.

**Foundational lens:** Distinguish the cost that occurs, the measurement that represents it, and the valuation that informs a decision. An unobserved or unvalued consequence can still impose cost. An observer's recognition can change knowledge of that cost; it does not bring the cost into existence. The derivation must establish this stronger claim, beyond independence from an evaluator's chosen weights.

**Contribution to establish:** Ethics from informational relations, with no additional ethical postulate or eligibility requirement. The existing substrate supplies affected frames, transformations, reachable futures, and the costs of sustaining or changing them. Ethical cost is derived from that machinery; relative weightings enter decisions. No biological status, sentience test, represented preference, or externally supplied moral rule is required before an instantiated consequence can enter the account. Substrate independence means that the same derivation procedure applies across embodiments; the quantities depend on their actual capabilities and costs.

**Meaning of “no additional requirement”:** No new ethical axiom is added to the informational foundations. Frame, boundary, baseline, horizon, and measurement conditions are inherited conditions for defining any quantity in this series. A complete derivation must identify those dependencies and show that none introduces an undisclosed moral selection rule. This is a claim to establish in the paper, not a theorem already proved by this outline. It does not claim that ordinary Shannon theory alone already supplies a complete ethical account.

### Three contributions

1. **Derivation:** Ethical cost follows from the existing informational machinery without a further ethical prerequisite or an observer assigning value. Its existence is a property of affected relations.
2. **Calculation:** The paper supplies the actual functions for calculating that cost, with typed inputs, outputs, attribution, and uncertainty. An implementer can compute an account and then vary decision weightings while holding it fixed.
3. **Compression hypothesis:** The derived informational principle can be expressed through compressed dispositions, affect, heuristics, and social practices in humans and other animals. The theory identifies the underlying principle; it does not identify the precise functions, representations, or neural operations a brain executes.

The principal contribution is a derivation from first principles with functions that make its quantities calculable. Its value includes coherent accounting, explicit trade-offs, explanatory connections, and implementation in a declared grounding. Establishing that contribution does not require recovering a biological implementation. The compression hypothesis supplies a further interpretation of ethical behaviour; evidence can support its explanatory adequacy without identifying a brain's exact algorithm. Earlier empowerment and computational moral-choice models are also implementable, so the distinction rests on this particular derivation and account.

## Proposed abstract

Actions alter what affected structures can do, what they must expend, and which futures remain reachable. This paper proposes to derive ethical cost from those informational transformations without adding a separate ethical postulate or requiring an observer to assign value. The cost occurs in the affected relations; a measurement represents it and a decision weights it. The account records lost reachable capabilities, imposed resource burdens, and effects along the action history, with newly available capabilities recorded alongside them. Its comparison conditions are inherited from the informational framework. A consequence does not depend on the affected frame having a represented preference or being able to report it. Decisions evaluate the derived account through relative weightings, so disagreement about a decision need not imply disagreement about its costs. Biological, computational, simulated, and collective embodiments use the same derivation. The paper develops independence from evaluative observation, weighting independence, representation invariance under preserved structure, and historical accounting through restoration or termination. It compares the derivation with information ethics, empowerment, and measures of side effects based on reachability.

The paper provides functions that make the account calculable in a declared grounding. It further proposes that human and animal ethical behaviour can arise as compressed expressions of the underlying informational principle, before or without explicit ethical reflection. Differences in compression, information, and relative weightings can produce differing treatment and decisions. This interpretation concerns the principle expressed through behaviour; recovering the precise biological implementation is outside the present claim.

## 1. Consequences before decisions

Open with a protective intervention that restricts one agent's actions and restores another's options. Both effects enter the account. The justification of the intervention requires a decision rule over that account.

State the foundational proposition:

> Ethical cost is constituted by burdens and losses of reachable capability in affected relations. It exists whether or not an observer recognises or values it. Ethical accounting measures those consequences under specified comparison conditions; ethical decisions apply relative weightings and constraints to the account.

Explain the role of the word *ethical*: it names consequences borne through informational relations, whose cost is derived from that structure. Establish why the cost follows from the inherited machinery, without a morally privileged population or an evaluator conferring its ethical status. A restriction can carry a derived cost and still be selected under the decision weightings. An expansion can be recorded without an unconditional instruction to maximise it.

### 1.1 Occurrence, measurement, and valuation

| Layer | What determines it | Role of an observer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occurring cost | The action, affected relations, lost continuations, and imposed burdens | No evaluative observer is required for the consequence to occur. |
| Measured account | A representation of those relations under specified comparison conditions | An observer or instrument can estimate, calculate, bound, or fail to detect the cost. |
| Decision valuation | Relative weights, priorities, and constraints over consequences | An evaluator selects or expresses a decision; this does not constitute the underlying cost. |

Relational existence remains inherited: cost belongs to situated affected relations. Independence from an observer assigning value does not require a view from nowhere or a frame-free numerical scalar. A recipient or provider is part of the consequential relation, even if it has no evaluative representation of that relation.

Demonstrate an imposed burden that is neither reported nor recognised. Subsequently introducing a passive measurement or judgment changes the account available to an observer while leaving the already specified interaction fixed. If observation itself intervenes in the interaction, include that intervention and its consequences as another action.

**Section transition:** Identify affected frames using the existing relational definitions.

## 2. Relational existence and affected frames

### 2.1 Existence supplies no category-based ranking

Inherit the relational floor from Paper 00. Biological, computational, simulated, and represented structures are instantiated through relations. Their causal roles and capabilities require examination in their own right.

An imagined agent is represented in an existing structure. Whether that structure instantiates the agency attributed to the imagined agent is a further question. The existence of a representation alone does not establish an independently acting or experiencing subject.

### 2.2 Inherit attribution; introduce no additional ethical gate

Use the frame, continuity, capability, and provision relations already defined in the series. Attribute a consequence wherever the relevant relation and dimension are instantiated. The term *agent* may describe an acting frame, but action selection is not a prerequisite for bearing a resource burden or losing a reachable continuation.

Self-report, biological membership, similarity to a human, consciousness, and represented preference are not eligibility requirements. Where experience, distress, or a preference dimension is instantiated, account for its consequences through the same machinery. Unknown experience does not turn a known capability loss into zero; an uninstantiated dimension remains undefined rather than being invented.

Collective and component agents require an explicit attribution boundary. The same physical burden cannot be counted twice merely because it appears in accounts at both levels.

**Derivation obligation:** Trace every attribution condition to an existing informational definition. Demonstrate costs to a frame with no represented preference and no reporting channel. Separate Paper 01b's goal-dependent beneficiary comparison from the broader account of incurred consequences.

## 3. The comparison contract

Introduce one accounting contract containing:

- The action `a` and baseline alternative `b`, including the policies they change.
- The affected agents and the rule for tracking their continuity.
- The causal boundary and exogenous conditions held fixed between alternatives.
- The horizon and operative resolution.
- A common capability-outcome space for each agent, with an alignment between alternatives.
- The measure of distinguishable capabilities and the resource units.
- Rules for attributing shared burdens and recording uncertainty.

The baseline can be continuation, non-intervention, or another available action. Non-intervention has consequences and requires an account. Costs must always carry their baseline index; they are not absolute numbers.

The outcome space represents what the agent can actually reach under specified resources and support. Nominal permission alone does not establish reachability. An imposed increase in the price of access can reduce capability before a formal permission changes.

The contract specifies what a particular account measures; it does not create the underlying affected relations or their consequences. The same interaction can have differently resolved or bounded accounts. A counting measure over defined distinctions is different from a preference ordering over those distinctions. If the measure itself encodes desirability, that evaluative assumption must be disclosed.

**Boundary:** Without an alignment or an instantiated measure, the corresponding numerical comparison is undefined. This does not establish that the underlying consequence is absent or costless. Absence of measurement, failed recognition, and zero measured cost are distinct claims.

## 4. Deriving the cost account

### 4.1 Lost and newly available capabilities

Write `R_i(a)` and `R_i(b)` for agent `i`'s reachable capability outcomes under the action and baseline, evaluated under the same accounting contract. Define:

```text
Lost_i(a | b) = R_i(b) \ R_i(a)
Gained_i(a | b) = R_i(a) \ R_i(b)

L_i(a | b) = μ_i(Lost_i(a | b))
G_i(a | b) = μ_i(Gained_i(a | b))
```

The derivation proceeds from the causal model to each reachable set, aligns the outcomes, takes the differences, and applies the declared measure. Retain the identities of lost and gained capabilities alongside their magnitudes.

Equal cardinalities do not establish unchanged capability. An action can remove two distinct options and introduce two others; the account records both changes.

### 4.2 Imposed resource burdens

Record the resource consequences for each agent in their grounded units: alteration work, maintenance requirements, and expenditures caused by the action. Let `K_i(a | b)` be the signed difference from baseline in each declared resource component. A positive component is an added burden; a negative component is a saving.

Actual expenditure and increased access requirements are different quantities. Report both where relevant. Avoid adding unlike units or charging the same expenditure once as a resource burden and again as a repair expense.

### 4.3 Repair and reversibility

An endpoint account alone is incomplete. A reversible episode can impose temporary capability loss, alteration work, maintenance burden, or represented distress even when the final local state matches the baseline. Retain the causal action history and derive each instantiated path quantity. For example, a time-indexed loss profile `L_i(t; a | b)` can accompany the terminal loss; an integral or discrete sum requires an inherited clock, alignment, and accumulation rule. These entries are not automatically in the same units as terminal loss.

Paper 02b's restored deprivation episode is a required case: byte-identical restoration cannot establish a zero path account. Its arousal variable alone also cannot establish suffering or its magnitude. Where a historical difference has no remaining operative consequence, its record describes what happened; it is not an extra force acting on the restored present (Paper 01, `prop:no-implicit-historical-state`).

Where repair is represented, derive its feasibility and minimum required resources from paths that restore a declared capability condition. Specify what counts as restoration: recovering a count alone is insufficient if the lost capability was distinctive.

If no restoration path exists within the declared boundary, record repair as unreachable. If a numerical resource minimum is not attained, use an infimum and report that limitation. A repair estimate is a liability estimate, not an expenditure already incurred.

### 4.4 The complete account

The cost account for agent `i` contains its added burdens, lost capabilities, path consequences, and repair liabilities. Gains and resource savings accompany the account as separate entries. The assessment retains the family of agent accounts and its causal attribution record.

No universal scalar is introduced here. A scalar cost is defined only where the grounding supplies the required units and conversion relations.

**Derivation obligation:** Resource burdens must be computed or measured separately. The loss of a reachable distinction alone does not establish a thermodynamic erasure cost.

### 4.5 Establish the information-theoretic step

Set difference and a declared measure establish an accounting construction. To support the contribution, derive its informational content and show why the chosen dimensions follow from the existing substrate, rather than inserting a preferred capability metric as a new ethical premise.

Compare the series' reachable-distinction measure with empowerment: `E_i = max_p(u) I(U_i; Y_i)` for the action-to-observation channel under declared conditions. In a finite deterministic channel with `m ≥ 1` reachable distinguishable outputs, capacity is `log2(m)`: conditional output entropy is zero, output entropy is bounded by `log2(m)`, and an input distribution producing uniform outputs attains the bound. This supplies a concrete information-theoretic result, attributed to the empowerment literature.

For nested deterministic outcome sets with positive cardinalities, lost capacity is `log2(|R_i(b)| / |R_i(a)|)`. For general changes, retain the identities of lost and gained outcomes; equal capacities can conceal substitution. Noisy channels require the transition probabilities, and possible outcomes need not all be controllable. Treat empowerment as a candidate comparison, not an automatic identification with Narrativium or the complete ethical account. Empty continuation, termination, and absent channel dimensions require separate treatment: no valid channel capacity is obtained by writing `log2(0)`.

### 4.6 Executable calculation

Supply a function specification that is sufficient to implement the account. Each function must resolve to inherited graph operations or a declared measurement procedure; naming an unknown oracle is not an implementation.

| Function | Inputs | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reachable(model, alternative, frame, contract)` | Transitions, alternative, affected-frame identity, horizon, support conditions, and outcome quotient | Reachable outcome set, or a certified bound/unknown result |
| `capability_changes(R_action, R_baseline, measure)` | Aligned sets and an instantiated measure | Lost/gained outcome identities and their magnitudes |
| `resource_difference(run_action, run_baseline, attribution)` | Grounded path/resource records and provision attribution | Signed differences in each resource unit |
| `path_account(run_action, run_baseline, contract)` | Aligned trajectories or scenario families and instantiated path dimensions | Temporary losses, gains, and other path consequences |
| `repair(model, target, contract)` | Present condition, restoration target, admissible repair transitions, resource ordering | Feasible paths and minimum/infimum resource requirements, or unreachable/unknown |
| `ethical_account(model, action, baseline, contract)` | The preceding inputs and attribution relations | The family of typed affected-frame accounts and provenance |
| `evaluate(account, decision_parameters)` | Fixed account, relative weights, priorities, and constraints | Decision scores, ordering, or admissible choices |

For a finite enumerable grounding, show the actual calculation:

```text
for each frame affected in either alternative:
    R_a = reachable(model, action, frame, contract)
    R_b = reachable(model, baseline, frame, contract)
    lost = R_b - R_a
    gained = R_a - R_b
    L = measure(lost)
    G = measure(gained)
    K = attributed_resource_difference(action, baseline, frame)
    P = aligned_path_account(action, baseline, frame)
    Q = feasible_repair_account(model, frame, lost, contract)
    retain(frame, lost, gained, L, G, K, P, Q, provenance)
return retained_accounts
```

Define traversal, support, path alignment, and repair search in the formal grounding, then give complete numeric inputs and outputs. The finite example in Section 7.1 computes `(L_A, G_A)=(3,0)`, `(L_B, G_B)=(0,3)`, and `K_B=1` declared resource unit. Its terminal capability entries and intervention resource entry are determined; path and repair entries need their own supplied data.

Distinguish an exact finite computation from estimation over an unbounded or partially observed system. Report approximation, search limits, and undefined dimensions in the output. Implementability does not imply efficient exact calculation for every possible grounding.

## 5. Results of the derivation

### Proposition 1 — Independence from decision weightings

**Premises:** The causal model, alternatives, agency attribution, comparison contract, and cost derivation are fixed. Evaluator weightings enter only the subsequent decision rule.

**Conclusion:** Changing those weightings leaves the derived account unchanged.

**Proof path:** The account's defining operations contain no decision-weight parameter. Changing that parameter changes the valuation applied to the account, not its arguments or outputs.

**Boundary:** If preferences change behaviour, policies, or resource provision, they change the causal model. That is a different transformation requiring a new account.

### Proposition 1a — Independence from evaluative observation

**Premises:** The interaction, affected relations, and comparison conditions are fixed. Introducing or removing an evaluator's recognition or assigned value does not intervene in those relations.

**Conclusion:** The structural losses and imposed burdens are unchanged whether or not an evaluator recognises, measures, or values them. A measurement may remain unavailable or uncertain while the consequence occurs.

**Proof path:** Derive the affected transitions, lost continuations, and resource burdens from the substrate without an evaluator-recognition or value-assignment predicate. Show that adding such a predicate to a reporting layer changes no consequential relation. This proof must establish independence of the cost's occurrence, not merely omit a weight variable from a reporting formula.

**Boundary:** A changed measurement resolution can produce a different account of the same interaction. Observation that changes behaviour or resource provision changes the interaction and requires its own account. Relational attribution remains essential; a separate observer conferring value does not.

### Proposition 2 — Invariance under preserved structure

**Premises:** A bijective representation change preserves agent identity, transitions, capability distinctions, measures, resource quantities, and attribution.

**Conclusion:** The corresponding cost accounts are equal under that mapping.

**Proof path:** A bijection preserves set difference; the preserved measures and resource maps preserve the entries derived from it.

**Boundary:** Changing embodiment can change resource costs or reachable capabilities. Substrate independence means a common procedure; equality of accounts requires the stronger preservation premises.

### Proposition 3 — Agent termination does not erase its account

**Premises:** The attribution rule retains agents affected in either alternative, including an agent whose continuation the action terminates.

**Conclusion:** Terminating an agent cannot remove its losses merely by removing it from the post-action census.

**Proof path:** The relevant comparison population is retained from the alternatives and causal record. Where termination leaves no continuing-agent outcomes, those baseline outcomes remain in the loss set.

**Boundary:** This is an attribution result. It does not equate a finite capability count with a universal price for death.

### Proposition 4 — Robust comparison under monotone valuation

**Premises:** Two actions are compared in compatible dimensions. One imposes no greater burdens or losses and supplies no fewer gains in every declared component. The decision rule is monotone in those directions.

**Conclusion:** That action is weakly preferred by every such rule. A strict improvement gives strict preference only when the rule responds strictly to at least one improved component.

**Boundary:** Crossing accounts need further priorities or weightings. This result does not manufacture a unique ordering where the accounts trade off.

## 6. Decisions and relative weightings

Show a weighted decision rule as one possible use of the account. Each weight must include any necessary unit conversion and identify whose account it evaluates. Then contrast its requirements with decision procedures based on constraints, priorities, or negotiated consent.

Explain three consequences:

1. Different weightings can select different actions from the same account.
2. A decision can justify imposing a cost without making that cost disappear.
3. Benefits to one agent do not cancel another agent's losses before an aggregation rule explicitly performs that operation.

Treat consent as a consequential relation: consent can change authorisation, coordination, distress, and later behaviour. It also has a specified role in the decision rule. Consent alone does not remove a lost capability from the account.

**Proof boundary:** Decisions supply relative weightings, as in the proposed contribution. If reciprocity or agency preservation is asserted to follow from the informational machinery, prove that implication from inherited premises. If it is a chosen decision priority, place it in the weighting or constraint rule. Do not make either a new prerequisite for defining ethical cost.

## 7. Worked cases

### 7.1 A fixed account with different decisions

Use a finite toy model with aligned outcome sets and counting measure:

| Alternative | Agent A's reachable outcomes | Agent B's reachable outcomes | Additional intervention resource |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline continuation | `{a1, a2, a3, a4, a5, a6}` | `{b1}` | 0 |
| Protective restriction | `{a1, a2, a3}` | `{b1, b2, b3, b4}` | 1 declared unit borne by B |

The action removes three capabilities from A, adds three to B, and imposes one resource unit on B. Under a declared conversion, an illustrative score to minimise is:

```text
J = 3w_loss,A + w_resource,B − 3w_gain,B
```

Its sign depends on the decision weights. The account remains three lost capabilities, three gains, and one resource unit. Equal weights across agents or dimensions require an explicit justification.

This example demonstrates the distinction between accounting and decision. It supplies no empirical measurement or general verdict about protective restriction.

### 7.2 Necessary restrictions and traps

Compare immediate optionality with longer-horizon capability. Restricting entry to a trap can remove an immediate option while preserving later escape or viability. Show both entries and the horizon dependence.

### 7.3 Automation and delegation

An action reduces one agent's operating burden while creating dependencies, verification work, or exposure for others. Track those effects through the causal boundary, including instantiated effects borne by the automated frame and its providers.

### 7.4 Simulated and biological agents

Use isomorphic capability dynamics to demonstrate common accounting. Then change resource provision or support conditions to demonstrate why different embodiments can have different costs.

### 7.5 Termination, replacement, and repair

An action creates a replacement while terminating an existing agent. Preserve both identity accounts. Show why aggregate capability growth does not itself establish restoration of the terminated agent's capabilities.

### 7.6 Restoration and absent preferences

Use Paper 02b's deprivation/restoration case to distinguish terminal, path, and resource accounts. Then remove the affected frame's preference representation and reporting channel. Derive the remaining instantiated consequences without requiring either to be restored as an ethical prerequisite.

### 7.7 Video games: consequential structure without assigned value

**Central example:** The death of one companion can be deeply meaningful to a player while the deaths of a thousand enemies carry little meaning. The operation named “killing” and its frequency do not determine the player's valuation. Examine the informational structure in which each event occurs.

First establish the underlying cost difference. Specify an expendable encounter NPC whose role ends in its scripted removal, with no persistent individual development, ongoing relationships, or continuing futures beyond that encounter. Where the baseline contains no such continuations, none are lost by the displayed “death.” Any temporary capability, resource, or player consequences remain separately accountable; the image of killing does not instantiate the losses ordinarily associated with terminating a continuing agent.

Contrast that with a companion whose removal closes persistent relationships and distinctive future interactions. Those losses can reside in the character, the player-character relation, and other connected structures. The low-cost NPC case concerns what is actually absent from the affected structure, not merely a player's indifference. Designed purpose alone is not a proof of zero cost; inspect the instantiated continuations and burdens.

A companion can be individually represented through a shared history, distinctive interactions, dependencies, and anticipated futures. Its death changes that specific relational structure. Enemies can instead be represented as interchangeable instances of a compressed category, carrying little individually retained history or anticipated relationship. Many deaths can then register as repetitions of one event type. This is the compression interpretation to develop; the example does not establish that every player or game has those representations.

Give two versions of the same encounter: in one, an opponent is an interchangeable target; in the other, the player has learned that opponent's history, formed a relationship, and acquired expectations of future interaction. Track what information and relations actually change and what becomes available to the player. Analyse the resulting difference in salience and decision valuation without requiring the game to attach a different numerical reward or articulate a moral rule.

Distinguish the informational organisation of the affected game entity from the structure held about it by the player. Individuation can change the latter even when the former is fixed. This illustrates how compressed representation and relative weights can produce sharply different valuations. Low player salience does not establish zero occurring cost for the affected entity; the cost's existence remains independent of that valuation.

Use a deliberately specified game world as an inspectable example. A persistent character holds relationships, learned state, and a set of admissible future interactions. An action terminates that character and closes some of those interactions. The affected relations and transition changes are available in the game state; their occurrence does not require the player to notice them, care about them, or judge the action wrong.

Run the comparison in three stages:

1. **Calculate the change:** Compare reachable continuations and grounded resource/path effects before and after the action under a fixed baseline. Attribute effects separately to the character, other game entities, the player, and provision systems where their dimensions are instantiated.
2. **Vary recognition:** Give one player access to the changed relationships and continuations while another cannot see them. Hold the underlying action and world state fixed. The consequential relation persists while their available accounts differ.
3. **Vary valuation:** Hold the measured account fixed and change the player's priorities: completion, experimentation, attachment, or avoiding disruption. Their decisions can differ without changing the already specified cost.

Distinguish graphical similarity, narrative description, and actual instantiated state. A rich description can change a player's estimate or attachment without changing the character's operative capabilities. Conversely, a visually simple entity may hold persistent relationships and capabilities whose destruction produces an extensive account. This provides an explicit comparison between appearance and informational organisation.

Include save/reload, replacement, and procedural regeneration. Exact restoration can remove a terminal difference while retaining the occurred episode's path/resource account. Creating a similar character need not restore the original relations or identity. Cheap reconstruction changes repair requirements; it does not establish that the preceding action had no consequences.

The example demonstrates computable changes and their independence from recognition or assigned importance within a defined informational system. The proposed ethical interpretation follows through the same derivation as elsewhere, with no exemption based solely on simulation. Do not infer subjective suffering from a scripted animation, or treat a game's scoring convention as the definition of ethical cost.

## 8. Measurement and disagreement

Separate four sources of disagreement:

- **Causal:** Which consequences does the action produce relative to baseline?
- **Representational:** Which capabilities and agents does the accounting contract distinguish?
- **Epistemic:** Which entries are established, estimated, bounded, or unknown?
- **Evaluative:** Which weightings, priorities, and constraints determine the decision?

For uncertain causal outcomes, report scenario-specific accounts or bounds. An expected account requires a declared probability model. Information gain for an evaluator and capability gain for an affected agent must have separate entries.

Do not claim that a defined quantity is already known. Specify the observations, counterfactual model, and resource measurements needed to estimate it. Examine sensitivity to baseline, horizon, resolution, and attribution.

### 8.1 Compression and ethical behaviour

State the hypothesis:

> Ethical behaviour can arise as a compressed expression of the informational consequences of interaction and their relative weighting. Explicit ethical reasoning can articulate, inspect, and revise that expression; the underlying principle does not depend on its prior articulation.

The formal account describes actual consequences and provides functions for calculating them. A biological disposition need not reconstruct the complete account on each occasion. Learned or evolved responses may retain distinctions relevant to recurrent interactions while omitting detail, shortening horizons, or combining effects into an action tendency. Affect, attachment, habits, and social practices are candidate forms of such compression; this paper does not establish their exact neural encoding.

Keep four objects distinct: the derived account, the information available to a situated system, its compressed representation or disposition, and its relative decision weightings. A compressed response can express sensitivity to cost without storing the equations as explicit symbols. It can also omit, misestimate, or misallocate consequences. These limitations leave the underlying account defined.

**Compression bridge to develop:** Identify which consequential distinctions a proposed compression retains, which it merges, and how those losses affect its decision scope. Use finite examples where a reusable disposition approximates decisions from the fuller account under specified weights. A proposed compression is a substantive explanatory hypothesis, not a license to reinterpret every observed behaviour as a successful expression of the theory.

Human ethical reflection need not originate every behaviour later described as ethical. Helping, harm avoidance, and cooperation in young humans and other animals provide relevant explanatory comparisons. Different species can express related principles through different compressions and weights. The paper need not assert that animals never reason or that all cooperative behaviour has a moral motive.

**Assessment at the claimed level:**

- Evaluate the first-principles derivation for coherence and whether the functions calculate the declared quantities.
- Evaluate explicit compressed examples for what they preserve and how accurately they support the chosen decisions.
- Use behavioural patterns to assess the plausibility and explanatory usefulness of particular compression proposals, without requiring a recovered neural implementation.
- Where an extension makes quantitative behavioural predictions, constrain its compression and weights and compare its predictions with alternatives. Failure limits that extension; it does not by itself invalidate an independently established accounting result.
- Treat differing information, compression, and weighting as possible explanations with declared scope, rather than selecting them after every observation solely to secure agreement.

See the related-work review for human harm-aversion models, early helping, and animal harm avoidance. These provide context for the compression interpretation. Neural evidence may inform a future implementation account, but recovery of that account is not a prerequisite for the theory's present contribution.

### 8.2 Graded consideration across entities

Use the companion-versus-enemies game example to motivate informational individuation as well as attributed capacity. A singular, richly held relationship can receive far greater weight than many instances compressed into one category. This concerns the organisation of represented relationships and anticipated continuations, not merely the amount of description or the number of deaths. The player's compressed account can omit consequences that remain present in the fuller relational account.

Add a second descriptive hypothesis:

> Human moral consideration tends to increase with the informational organisation and reachable capacities people attribute to an entity, moderated by their relationship to it and their weighting of the consequences.

“Informational organisation” must resolve to the series' actual measures of held distinctions, causal organisation, and reachable capabilities. Raw bit count, Shannon uncertainty, physical size, and human resemblance are not interchangeable measures. The hypothesis describes a tendency in treatment; it adds no category-based eligibility requirement to the cost account.

Separate two possible mechanisms. A richer reachable structure may lose more measured capability under an action. An evaluator may also assign greater weight to the affected entity or to particular dimensions. Greater protection alone does not identify which mechanism produced it. Actual consequences, perceived consequences, and weights remain separate.

Use graded treatment of humans, apes, other mammals, fish, invertebrates, and plants as candidate observations, with population and context specified. Do not present dietary taboos as universal. Compare perceived mind and evolutionary relatedness with measured informational organisation rather than using either as its definition.

Vegetarian and vegan prevalence supplies context, not a direct complexity measurement. The distinction also concerns different uses of the same animals: killing, maintaining production, and indirect supply-chain burdens can receive different estimates and weights. Dietary decisions include access, habit, taste, health, and social cost. Account for the full action rather than inferring zero ethical cost from a food category or inferring a mechanism from prevalence alone.

**Discriminating test:** Hold the action and its consequences as comparable as possible, vary evidence about an entity's organisation or reachable capabilities, and measure whether treatment changes as the model predicts. Independently vary resemblance, familiarity, and relationship to identify their effects on estimates and weights. Include entities with high attributed experience but low action-selection capacity; a one-dimensional intelligence ranking may fail there.

### 8.3 Violent-game controversy: depicted killing and actual loss

Use the 1990s public controversy as a historical application, with the US hearings of December 1993 and 1994 as documented examples. The question for this paper is what a depicted act removes from the affected informational relations. A “violent game” classification describes content; it does not calculate that loss.

For an expendable NPC with no instantiated continuing futures or durable relational structure to lose, the apparent killing can be ethically almost trivial under the derived account. Its design as a target is relevant insofar as that role exhausts its actual organisation and continuations. A persistent companion can carry substantially different consequences even when its death uses the same visual operation. Count the lost futures and imposed burdens rather than importing a cost from the label “killing.”

Keep actual cost and player response distinct. Trivial structural loss can explain trivial concern without positing failure to recognise a substantial ethical cost. Conversely, indifference cannot erase losses that are instantiated. The account supplies the distinction without a blanket ethical exemption for games or an assumption that all animated deaths carry the losses of a continuing person.

Research on game framing and guilt can inform the discussion of recognition and valuation. It does not determine the underlying cost. The application here concerns the ethical account of the game action itself.

### Closing claim

The proposed derivation makes ethical cost an informational consequence of interaction, requiring no additional ethical postulate or observer to assign value. Cost occurs in affected relations; measurement describes it and decisions apply relative weightings to it. The account records who bears a burden, which capabilities disappear, which become available, what occurs along the path, and what repair requires. The completed paper must establish this derivation from the inherited definitions and state the comparisons and quantities it actually determines.

Providing the calculation functions makes this account implementable. The further interpretation is that human and animal ethical behaviour can express the underlying informational principle through compression, with different available information and weights. The contribution concerns the derived principle and its explanatory value; identifying the precise biological implementation is outside the present claim.
