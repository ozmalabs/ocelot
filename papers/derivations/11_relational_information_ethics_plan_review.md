# Paper 11: review of the argument plan

## Outcome

The plan supports a complete paper. Revision 2 reframes it as **Relational Information Accounting**, with ethics as an accounting frame and moral injury as a substantive case. Its strongest contribution is an attributed, calculable account of informational consequences that exists independently of recognition and precedes decision weights. The manuscript develops that contribution and states the compression interpretation as a separate explanatory hypothesis.

## Changes made while drafting

1. **Expose the derivation.** Begin with actual transitions, continuing identity, admissible continuations, and grounded resource expenditure. Derive loss and gain by set difference. Recover the finite information capacity result with a proof. Do not treat Shannon entropy alone as a theory of ethics.
2. **Make observer independence a causal claim.** A passive evaluator's presence, report, or judgement cannot change the affected transition relation. Measurement choices affect an account's scope and resolution; they do not instantiate the consequence. An observer who intervenes creates a different causal case.
3. **State the philosophical commitment precisely.** The foundational object is whole-system accounting of informational consequences; ethics becomes an accounting frame for consequences omitted by narrower accounts. The user's correction supersedes the first draft's identification of the account itself with ethics. Structural mathematics establishes the account's properties; it does not prove every rival conception of ethics impossible or supply a compulsory universal decision rule. No additional moral eligibility condition enters the calculation.
4. **Retain identities and types.** Equal numbers of reachable futures can conceal substitution. Keep loss and gain separately, retain affected identities after termination, and separate resource units, continuing capability, path exposure, and repair estimates. Overlapping claims do not justify double counting a shared expense.
5. **Supply executable functions.** Provide bounded graph traversal, outcome comparison, resource differences, time profiles, deterministic capacity, repair search, and evaluation. Inputs declare the causal model and measurement contract. Unknown quantities remain unknown.
6. **Resolve the game case.** An encounter character with no persistent continuations can have zero loss at the continuing-future boundary. This does not imply zero intermediate path or resource cost. Purpose of design alone cannot establish the result. A persistent companion can lose actual continuing relations even before a player evaluates them. The historical example concerns what a depiction removes, without introducing aggression or media-transfer research.
7. **Make compression inspectable.** Give conditions under which a compressed record preserves a decision and a bound when it approximates one. Animal helping and human intuition support the possibility of ethical behaviour without explicit ethical reasoning; they do not identify a neural implementation.
8. **Attribute the closest predecessors.** Floridi and Sanders, empowerment and robotic ethics, relative reachability, attainable utility preservation, and computational moral-choice models are substantive prior work. The contribution is this derivation and arrangement, rather than the first information-based or executable ethics proposal.
9. **Keep alignment bounded.** Compare representative human-feedback and constitution-based approaches with consequence accounting. Do not generalise those examples to all alignment research; reachability and empowerment already provide overlaps.
