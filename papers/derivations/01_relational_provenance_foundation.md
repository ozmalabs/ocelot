# Relational provenance as a foundational principle

## Placement and status

The author's principle, **provenance is inferred, not inherent**, is now stated near the beginning of Graphic Equalisation, immediately after the relativity-of-grounding axiom and its scope clarification. It is a relational definition and an evidence-relative consequence, not a new origin primitive or an additional axiom. It belongs here because causal dynamics, frames, and inference are introduced by this paper. `00_is.tex` deliberately supplies none of that machinery; its source was therefore not changed.

The definition separates actual causal provenance from a frame's attribution of it. Records, bodily traces, generated explanations, and observed effects can constrain an attribution. They do not supply a source identity independently of their relations to what they purport to record. This does not deny actual causal histories, erase losses of evidence, or make every local origin unknowable.

## Formal consequence

For a declared candidate-history family, evidence map, and provenance distinction, collect all provenance values compatible with the available evidence. Two histories with identical evidence and different provenance prevent a unique evidence-based attribution. A singleton compatible set fixes the requested distinction under the declared model; an empty set signals incompatibility with that model. Neither case establishes completeness of the candidate-history family.

The proposition has an explicit proof. Its non-identifiability result is mathematical under its assumptions. The relational account does not add an empirical claim that all actual histories leave indistinguishable traces or that deliberate actions cannot be locally identified.

## Prior work and contribution framing

At the author's clarification, the principle is explicitly framed as an established concept given derivational backing by Graphic Equalisation. The evidence-relative proposition is identified as a standard identifiability argument stated in the framework's terms. Neither the concept nor that argument is claimed as a new discovery.

Primary sources verified and cited in the foundational status remark:

- Johnson, M. K., Hashtroudi, S., and Lindsay, D. S. (1993). *Source monitoring*. Psychological Bulletin 114(1), 3–28. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.3). The original article describes source attribution from memory characteristics and judgment processes, including both relatively automatic and deliberate judgments. The first two pages of the author-hosted scan were rendered and read through OCR.
- Shpitser, I., and Pearl, J. (2008). *Complete Identification Methods for the Causal Hierarchy*. Journal of Machine Learning Research 9, 1941–1979. [Original article record](https://jmlr.org/papers/v9/shpitser08a.html). The paper characterises identification and non-identification of causal queries from information at different levels under graphical models.
- Buneman, P., Khanna, S., and Tan, W.-C. (2001). *Why and Where: A Characterization of Data Provenance*. Database Theory — ICDT 2001, LNCS 1973, 316–330. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-44503-X_20), [author-hosted manuscript](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/opb/papers/ICDT2001.pdf). It distinguishes contributing source data from source locations for query-derived outputs.

These are related precedents with different scopes, rather than claims that each paper states the entire ontology. Graphic Equalisation provides the relational derivation and connects applications. The same distinction is explicit in the mind and decision papers; both cite the established source-monitoring framework. “Inferred” is not equated with conscious deliberation.
